# The Lingua Onde method

Lingua Onde follows the method of the Assimil courses: assimilation through
a short text a day, heard and repeated aloud, understood through your own
language, first passively and then actively. The first two courses of each
language follow the structure of Assimil's own books ("senza sforzo" /
"sans peine" and "Perfectionnement"). The third, Mastery, is Lingua Onde's
own extension to C2, built on the same principles. All lessons, recordings
and notes are original: only the teaching structure is shared.

Each language has three courses, studied one after the other:

| Course | Level | Lessons | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | first words → B2 | 100 | Assimil beginner course |
| Perfectionnement | B2 → C1 | 70 | Assimil Perfectionnement |
| Mastery | C1 → C2 | 125 | Lingua Onde's own |

A course is written for the speakers of one language at a time (`bases:` in
its files): Spanish is written first for Italian speakers.

## 1. Foundation (A1 → B2), 100 lessons

### The ordinary lesson (six days out of seven)

1. **Heading**: the lesson number spelled out in the target language
   ("Lección primera"), learnt along the way, and the title.
2. **Dialogue**: a short scene from one ongoing story with recurring
   characters. Beside each sentence, the translation in your language, with
   the word-for-word sense in brackets where the languages differ:
   "Buon giorno (buoni giorni)".
3. **Figured pronunciation** (lessons 1–50): under each sentence, the sentence
   spelled as a reader of your language would read it aloud, following a
   fixed key for each pair of languages (`content/<lang>/pronunciation.md`).
4. **Pronunciation observations** (mostly in the early lessons): the new or
   tricky sounds of the lesson, compared with your language.
5. **Notes**: numbered, each starting from a word or phrase of the dialogue:
   grammar met in use, vocabulary, idiom. The grammar is never taught
   up front: you meet it first, the notes explain it, the revision lesson
   puts it in order.
6. **Exercise 1 · Translate**: about five new sentences, made only of words
   you know, to translate into your language. Its correction is the
   translation.
7. **Exercise 2 · Fill in the missing words**: a sentence in your language,
   the same in the target language with words missing, one dot for each
   missing letter. Its correction is the full sentence.
8. **Cultural note** (some lessons): a short paragraph about life, customs or
   history linked to the lesson.

How to study it (one lesson a day, about half an hour):

1. Listen to the dialogue without reading: you tune your ear.
2. Listen again while reading the translation, then the original; repeat
   each sentence aloud in the pause, imitating the voice.
3. Read the dialogue aloud with the translation, and the figured pronunciation.
4. Read the pronunciation observations and the notes. Do not learn by heart.
5. Do the two exercises and compare with the corrections.
6. Read the dialogue again with the translation hidden, and listen to it
   once more without the text.
7. Reread and listen to the two lessons before.

The early recordings are slow; the pace grows towards natural speed. One
lesson a day is the rhythm: more is not better. A hard lesson can be gone
over again the next day ("go over this lesson again tomorrow").

### The revision lesson (every 7th lesson: 7, 14, 21...)

It brings nothing new. It is studied for as long as a new lesson.

1. **Grammar recap**: numbered sections that put in order the grammar met in
   the six lessons before (with the lessons named), in more depth than the
   daily notes: tables of forms, rules, examples you already know.
2. **Revision dialogue**: a new dialogue made only of what the six lessons
   taught. After each line, the lessons its words come from: "(1, 3)". You
   listen to it and repeat it, then read it with the translation.

### The active wave (from lesson 50)

Lessons 1–49 are the passive phase: you take the language in. From lesson 50
every day has two parts:

1. the new lesson, studied as usual;
2. the **active wave** of the lesson 49 places earlier: lesson 50 brings back
   lesson 1, 51 brings back 2, ... 100 brings back 51. You see its dialogue
   and its exercise 1 in your language only, say each sentence aloud in the
   target language (and may type it), then reveal it and compare. The old
   recording and text stay at hand. Revision lessons come back too, with
   their grammar.

After lesson 100 the active wave goes on, one lesson a day, until lesson 100
itself has been rebuilt.

## 2. Perfectionnement (B2 → C1), 70 lessons

Assimil's Perfectionnement keeps the beginner course's frame for a learner who
already has the basics:

- **Texts**, not only dialogues: articles, letters, stories, radio pieces,
  speeches, literary pages, in many registers and accents; longer and at a
  natural pace. The story's characters still run through them.
- **Translation** beside the text; no figured pronunciation (word stress is
  marked in the notes where it is irregular).
- **Notes** that explain and also widen: related words, idioms, register,
  the subjunctive and the irregular verbs in depth, prepositional and
  conjunctive phrases, culture (cinema, painting, history, humour, mentality).
- **Exercise 1 · Translate** and **Exercise 2 · Fill in**, both on the
  lesson's vocabulary; the corrections are not the only good answers.
- **Revision every 7th lesson**: not elementary grammar again, but the
  finer points and how sentences are built, with short practice whose
  answers close the lesson.
- **The same study routine**, with one more step: read each paragraph aloud,
  then repeat it without looking until you reproduce it faithfully.
- **Active wave**: from lesson 50 the lesson 49 places earlier comes back
  (50 → 1, ... 70 → 21), then the rest one a day after lesson 70: its text
  and exercise 1 from your language into the target language.

## 3. Mastery (C1 → C2), 125 lessons

Assimil stops at C1. Mastery keeps the method's core (a long text a day, heard
and read closely; notes; translation; delayed active reconstruction; revision
every 7th lesson) and changes what is asked of you, because at C2 the goal is
no longer to understand and say correct sentences but to master meaning,
register and effect.

- **Texts**: long, authentic-style originals at natural speed: essays,
  opinion columns, literary prose and verse, speeches, radio and podcast
  talk, debates, professional and academic writing, regional speech.
- **Notes**: register and its shifts, pragmatics (what is meant, not said),
  irony and humour, cultural and historical references, idioms and
  collocations, precision of word choice, rhetoric and style.
- **Exercise 1 · Translate**: demanding passages into your language, where
  the exact nuance matters.
- **Exercise 2 · Fill in**: collocations, idioms and connectors in context.
- **Open tasks**: one or two a lesson: summarise, paraphrase, change the
  register, answer the argument, write the letter, give the talk. There is no
  single right answer: you write or say yours, then compare it with a model
  answer and tick a self-check list (meaning, register, structure, idiom,
  effect).
- **Revision every 7th lesson**: style, register and rhetoric of the week in
  order, with a revision text.
- **Mastery wave** (from lesson 50, 49 places back, as before): you read the
  old text in your language and recreate it in the target language: the
  same meaning, tone, register and effect, not word for word. Then you set
  your version against the original with the self-check list. Exercise 1
  comes back the same way. You can also go sentence by sentence.

## Across the courses

- Spaced review of the dialogues' sentences (Review, Daily sentences) and a
  search across every sentence of a language (Review, Search) stand in for
  the book's index.
- Notes, corrections and the figured pronunciation are written in the
  learner's language.
- Every built-in lesson passes `lingua_onde --check` before it is added.
