# The Lingua Onde method

Lingua Onde follows the method of the Assimil courses: assimilation through
a short text a day, heard and repeated aloud, understood through your own
language, first passively and then actively. The first two courses of each
language follow the structure of Assimil's own books ("senza sforzo" /
"sans peine" and "Perfectionnement"). The third, Mastery, is Lingua Onde's
own extension to C2, built on the same principles. All lessons, recordings
and notes are original: only the teaching structure is shared.

Each language has three courses, studied one after the other:

| Course | Level | Lessons | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | first words → B2 | 100 | Assimil beginner course |
| Perfectionnement | B2 → C1 | 70 | Assimil Perfectionnement |
| Mastery | C1 → C2 | 125 | Lingua Onde's own |

A course is written for the speakers of one language at a time (`bases:` in
its files): English is written first for Italian speakers, then Spanish.

What follows was checked against the books themselves (Lo Spagnolo, German
with ease, Espagnol perfectionnement, Perfectionnement Allemand): their
layout, lesson lengths, notes, exercises, revision lessons and second wave.

## 1. Foundation (A1 → B2), 100 lessons

### The ordinary lesson (six days out of seven)

1. **Heading**: the lesson number spelled out in the target language
   ("Lección primera"), learnt along the way, and the title.
2. **Dialogue**: a short scene complete in itself, with its own title, often
   witty, and its own people: two voices marked only by a dash, or a name
   that appears in this lesson alone. There is no continuing story; at most
   a scene runs over two lessons ("..., continued"). Most scenes end with a
   twist or a joke. Length grows slowly: lesson 1 has three to five short
   lines, around lesson 20 about eight, from about lesson 40 twelve to
   fifteen. A line can also be a stage direction ("A little later:").
   Beside each sentence, the translation in your language, with the
   word-for-word sense in brackets where the languages differ: "Buon giorno
   (buoni giorni)".
3. **Figured pronunciation**: under each sentence, the sentence spelled as a
   reader of your language would read it aloud, following a fixed key for
   each pair of languages (`content/<lang>/pronunciation.md`). The whole line
   in lessons 1–28; from lesson 29 only the words new in that lesson, the
   rest left out, as German with ease does.
4. **Pronunciation observations** (mostly in the early lessons): a few
   "remember" points on the new or tricky sounds of the lesson, compared
   with your language.
5. **Notes**: four to eight, numbered, each on a word or phrase of the
   dialogue: what it means and how it works, compared with your language,
   often with a related everyday expression or two. They point back to
   where something was met ("we saw it in lesson 1") and ahead to where it
   will be explained ("the revision lesson will show this verb"). The
   grammar is never taught up front: you meet a form several times before a
   note explains it, and the revision lesson puts it in order.
6. **Exercise 1 · Translate**: five items to translate into your language.
   In the first lessons they are words and phrases of the dialogue
   ("Good morning! A lesson. Please."), then whole new sentences made of the
   lesson's words and earlier ones. Its correction is the translation.
7. **Exercise 2 · Fill in the missing words**: a sentence in your language,
   the same in the target language with words missing, one dot for each
   missing letter. Its correction is the full sentence.
8. **Cultural note** (at the end of many lessons): a paragraph about life,
   customs, places, history or the language, linked to the scene.

Every four to six lessons a line from the author closes the lesson
("From the author"): encouragement, "a third of the course done", a word
before a long lesson or the revision lesson, the announcement of the active
wave in lesson 49 and its instructions in lesson 50.

Later in the course some lessons are short descriptive texts without
speakers (a place, a festival, the language itself), more of them towards
the end, and lessons 99–100 are the author's farewell: how to keep in touch
with the language once the book is closed. The whole map, lesson by lesson,
is in `work/PATTERNS.md` (measured in the books).

How to study it (one lesson a day, about half an hour). Every lesson of the
three courses goes the same way:

1. Listen to the dialogue without any text, repeating each sentence aloud in
   the pause, imitating the voice.
2. The same, reading the translation in your language.
3. The same, reading the original.
4. Read the dialogue aloud in the original, with the translation and the
   figured pronunciation.
5. Read the pronunciation observations and the notes. Do not learn by heart.
6. Do the two exercises and compare with the corrections.
7. Read the dialogue once more, the translation hidden.
8. Listen without the text one last time, repeating each sentence.
9. Read and listen to the dialogues of the two lessons before.

The early recordings are slow; the pace grows towards natural speed. One
lesson a day is the rhythm: more is not better. A hard lesson can be gone
over again the next day ("go over this lesson again tomorrow").

### The revision lesson (every 7th lesson: 7, 14, 21...)

It brings almost nothing new. It is studied like any other lesson, with the
grammar recap in place of the notes and no exercises.

1. **Revision dialogue**: about ten short lines made only of what the
   lessons so far taught. After each line, the lessons its words come from:
   "(1, 3)".
2. **Grammar recap**: a few lines of encouragement (do not learn rules by
   heart; doubts are normal), then six to eight numbered sections that put in
   order the grammar met in the six lessons before, in more depth than the
   daily notes: full tables of forms, the rule, examples you already know,
   and a few completions (all the numbers, the whole verb). Early on,
   pronunciation and stress have their own sections. A closing line marks
   the end of the week.

### The active wave (from lesson 50)

Lessons 1–49 are the passive phase: you take the language in. From lesson 50
every day has two parts:

1. the new lesson, studied as usual;
2. the **active wave** of the lesson 49 places earlier: lesson 50 brings back
   lesson 1, 51 brings back 2, ... 100 brings back 51. You see its dialogue
   and its exercise 1 in your language only, say each sentence aloud in the
   target language (and may type it), then reveal it and compare. The old
   recording and text stay at hand. Revision lessons come back too, with
   their grammar.

After lesson 100 the active wave goes on, one lesson a day, until lesson 100
itself has been rebuilt.

## 2. Perfectionnement (B2 → C1), 70 lessons

Assimil's Perfectionnement keeps the beginner course's frame for a learner who
already has the basics:

- **Texts**, each complete in itself and of a different kind from day to
  day: a humorous reflection that plays with one word and all its idioms, a
  dialogue, a portrait of a city or region, a short story, a joke, a
  character sketch, a piece on customs, a historical or artistic figure,
  the wider world where the language is spoken. Humour runs through them
  and shows how its speakers think. About fifteen to twenty numbered
  sentences, at a natural pace. No continuing story.
- **Translation** beside the text; no figured pronunciation (word stress is
  marked in the notes where it is irregular).
- **Notes**, ten to fifteen, that explain and also widen: idioms and set
  phrases with their literal sense, related words, register, the
  subjunctive and the irregular verbs in depth, prepositional and
  conjunctive phrases, culture (cinema, painting, history, humour,
  mentality).
- **Exercise 1 · Translate** and **Exercise 2 · Fill in**, five sentences
  each, on the lesson's vocabulary; the corrections are not the only good
  answers.
- **Revision every 7th lesson** ("revision and finer points"): not
  elementary grammar again, but numbered sections on finer points and how
  sentences are built, with tables, and short practice whose answers close
  the lesson. It has no dialogue.
- **The same study routine** as Foundation.
- **Active wave**: from lesson 50 the lesson 49 places earlier comes back
  (50 → 1, ... 70 → 21), then the rest one a day after lesson 70: its text
  and exercise 1 from your language into the target language.

## 3. Mastery (C1 → C2), 125 lessons

Assimil stops at C1. Mastery keeps the method's core (a long text a day, heard
and read closely; notes; translation; delayed active reconstruction; revision
every 7th lesson) and changes what is asked of you, because at C2 the goal is
no longer to understand and say correct sentences but to master meaning,
register and effect.

- **Texts**: long, authentic-style originals at natural speed, each complete
  in itself: essays,
  opinion columns, literary prose and verse, speeches, radio and podcast
  talk, debates, professional and academic writing, regional speech.
- **Notes**: register and its shifts, pragmatics (what is meant, not said),
  irony and humour, cultural and historical references, idioms and
  collocations, precision of word choice, rhetoric and style.
- **Exercise 1 · Translate**: demanding passages into your language, where
  the exact nuance matters.
- **Exercise 2 · Fill in**: collocations, idioms and connectors in context.
- **Open tasks**: one or two a lesson: summarise, paraphrase, change the
  register, answer the argument, write the letter, give the talk. There is no
  single right answer: you write or say yours, then compare it with a model
  answer and tick a self-check list (meaning, register, structure, idiom,
  effect).
- **Revision every 7th lesson**: style, register and rhetoric of the week in
  order, with a revision text.
- **The same study routine** as Foundation, with the open tasks after the
  two exercises.
- **Mastery wave** (from lesson 50, 49 places back, as before): you read the
  old text in your language and recreate it in the target language: the
  same meaning, tone, register and effect, not word for word. Then you set
  your version against the original with the self-check list. Exercise 1
  comes back the same way. You can also go sentence by sentence.

## Across the courses

- Spaced review of the dialogues' sentences (Review, Daily sentences) and a
  search across every sentence of a language (Review, Search) stand in for
  the book's index.
- Notes, corrections and the figured pronunciation are written in the
  learner's language.
- Every built-in lesson passes `lingua_onde --check` before it is added.
