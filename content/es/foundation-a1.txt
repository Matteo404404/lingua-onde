== 1 A1
numeral: Lección primera
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* pri*mé*ra
title: En el bar
title.en: At the bar

dialogue:
- ¡Buenos días!
  en: Good morning! (Good days!)
  pron.en: ¡*boué*noss *di*ass!
- Buenos días. ¿Qué le pongo?
  en: Good morning. What can I get you? (What [for] you do I put?)
  pron.en: *boué*noss *di*ass. ¿ké lé *pon*go?
- Un café con leche, por favor.
  en: A white coffee, please. (A coffee with milk, for favour.)
  pron.en: oun ca*fé* con *lé*tché, por fa*bor*.
- ¿Algo más?
  en: Anything else? (Something more?)
  pron.en: ¿*al*go mass?
- Sí, una tostada con tomate.
  en: Yes, a [slice of] toast with tomato.
  pron.en: si, *ou*na toss*ta*da con to*ma*té.
- Aquí tiene.
  en: Here you are. (Here [you] have.)
  pron.en: a*ki* *tié*né.
- Muchas gracias.
  en: Thank you very much. (Many thanks.)
  pron.en: *mou*tchass *gra*ciass.

sounds.en:
1. Under each sentence you find the figured pronunciation: read it as if it were English, with the conventions of the pronunciation guide. The syllable that carries the stress is in bold, in every word of two or more syllables; words of one syllable have none. Every e is written é, so that you never leave it silent, and u is written ou, as in soup: *boué*noss *di*ass.
2. Spanish vowels are short and clear, and they never change: an unstressed vowel keeps its full sound and is never swallowed into the "uh" of English. Leche is *lé*tché and tomate is to*ma*té, with the last é sounded. An s is always the s of see, never the z of rose, so the figured line writes ss inside a word: *mou*tchass.
3. In Madrid a c before e or i is said with the tongue between the teeth, like the th of think: gracias is *gra*ciass. The v of favor is a b (fa*bor*), and the r is a single quick tap of the tongue, like the tt of "better" said fast.

notes.en:
1. Buenos días: good morning, literally "good days", in the plural. It is said until lunch, which in Spain is at about two o'clock, so the Spanish morning is a long one. The afternoon greeting comes later in the course.
2. ¿Qué le pongo?: what the barman says to every customer, literally "what [for] you do I put?". Notice the upside-down question mark: Spanish opens every question with ¿ and every exclamation with ¡, so that you know from the first word how the sentence will sound.
3. Un café con leche: every Spanish noun is either masculine or feminine, and "a" is un before a masculine noun and una before a feminine one: un café, una tostada. Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a feminine, but café shows there are others: learn each noun with its un or una. A café con leche is half coffee, half hot milk: the Spanish white coffee.
4. Por favor: please, literally "for favour". It can go at the end of the sentence, as here, or at the beginning.
5. ¿Algo más?: anything else? Algo means "something" or "anything", and más means "more". Más carries a written accent, which also tells you where the stress falls when a word has more than one syllable.
6. Aquí tiene: here you are, literally "here [you] have". The barman does not say "you": the ending of the verb is enough, and Spanish usually leaves the person out. We will come back to this in the next lesson. Aquí, here, is stressed on its last syllable, as its written accent shows: a*ki*.
7. Muchas gracias: thank you very much, literally "many thanks". Mucho, "much, many", agrees with its noun: gracias is feminine and plural, so it becomes muchas.

ex1:
- ¡Buenos días!
  en: Good morning!
- Un café, por favor.
  en: A coffee, please.
- ¿Algo más?
  en: Anything else?
- Una tostada con tomate.
  en: Toast with tomato.
- Aquí tiene.
  en: Here you are.

ex2:
- [Buenos] [días].
  en: Good morning.
- Un café [con] [leche], por favor.
  en: A coffee with milk, please.
- ¿[Algo] [más]?
  en: Anything else?
- [Muchas] [gracias].
  en: Thank you very much.

culture.en:
Many Spaniards have breakfast at the bar, standing at the counter on the way to work or in a break in the middle of the morning. The classic order is the one in the dialogue: a café con leche and a tostada, a slice of toasted bread with grated tomato and olive oil, or with butter and jam. A small black coffee is un café solo, and a cortado is a black coffee "cut" with a dash of milk. Nobody minds a short order: two or three words and por favor are polite enough, and you usually pay at the end, at the counter. To ask how much it is, say: ¿Cuánto es?

== 2 A1
numeral: Lección segunda
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* sé*goun*da
title: ¿Cómo te llamas?
title.en: What's your name?

dialogue:
- Hola. ¿Cómo te llamas?
  en: Hello. What's your name? (How yourself do [you] call?)
  pron.en: *o*la. ¿*co*mo té *ya*mass?
- Me llamo Fiona. ¿Y tú?
  en: My name is Fiona. (Myself [I] call Fiona.) And you?
  pron.en: mé *ya*mo *fio*na. ¿i tou?
- Yo soy Javier. ¿Eres inglesa?
  en: I'm Javier. Are [you] English?
  pron.en: yo soï Ha*biér*. ¿*é*réss in*glé*ssa?
- No, soy escocesa. De Edimburgo.
  en: No, I'm Scottish. From Edinburgh.
  pron.en: no, soï éssco*cé*ssa. dé édim*bour*go.
- Yo soy de aquí, de Madrid.
  en: I'm from here, from Madrid.
  pron.en: yo soï dé a*ki*, dé ma*drid*.
- Encantada.
  en: Pleased to meet you. (Delighted, said by a woman.)
  pron.en: éncan*ta*da.
- Encantado.
  en: Pleased to meet you. (Delighted, said by a man.)
  pron.en: éncan*ta*do.

sounds.en:
1. The j of Javier is a sound English does not have: a rough breath at the back of the throat, like the ch of Scottish loch. The figured line writes it H: Ha*biér*. Notice the v too, said b, as in lesson 1.
2. The h of Spanish is always silent: hola is *o*la. The double l is said like the y of yes: llamas is *ya*mass, llamo *ya*mo.
3. Remember the stress rule. Without a written accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the next-to-last syllable: éssco*cé*ssa, *ya*mo. A word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last: ma*drid*. A written accent breaks the rule and shows where the stress goes: *co*mo.

notes.en:
1. ¿Cómo te llamas?: what's your name? Literally "how do you call yourself?". The answer is me llamo, "I call myself": me llamo Fiona, my name is Fiona. Cómo, how, has a written accent in a question.
2. ¿Y tú?: and you? Y means "and", and tú is the "you" for one person you talk to as a friend: a young person, a colleague, a member of your family. At a language exchange everybody says tú. Strangers in a shop or older people are usually spoken to with usted, as the barman did in lesson 1. Tú has a written accent; tu without one means "your".
3. Yo soy Javier: I am Javier. Soy, "I am", and eres, "you are", come from ser, "to be". Spanish does not need the yo: the ending of soy already says "I". Javier uses it because he is setting himself against Fiona, "and me, I'm Javier"; when she answers she simply says soy escocesa. English cannot leave out its "I"; Spanish leaves it out most of the time.
4. ¿Eres inglesa?: are you English? A question needs no change of order in Spanish: eres inglesa, "you are English", becomes a question with the question marks and the voice rising at the end.
5. Inglesa, escocesa: words of nationality agree with the person: a man is inglés or escocés, a woman inglesa or escocesa. In Spanish they take no capital letter. The written accent of inglés goes away in inglesa: the word now ends in a vowel, so the stress falls on the next-to-last syllable by the rule, in*glé*ssa.
6. De Edimburgo, de Madrid: de means "from" (and also "of"). Soy de Madrid: I'm from Madrid.
7. Encantada, encantado: pleased to meet you, literally "delighted". Like the words of nationality, it agrees with the person who says it: Fiona says encantada, Javier encantado.

ex1:
- Hola, me llamo Fiona.
  en: Hello, my name is Fiona.
- ¿Cómo te llamas?
  en: What's your name?
- Soy escocesa.
  en: I'm Scottish. (a woman speaking)
- ¿Eres de Madrid?
  en: Are you from Madrid?
- Encantado.
  en: Pleased to meet you. (a man speaking)

ex2:
- ¿[Cómo] te [llamas]?
  en: What's your name?
- [Me] [llamo] Javier.
  en: My name is Javier.
- ¿[Eres] inglesa?
  en: Are you English?
- Soy [de] Madrid.
  en: I'm from Madrid.

culture.en:
Spaniards move to first names and to tú very quickly: two people introduced at a party or a bar say tú from the first sentence, as Fiona and Javier do. When they are introduced, women, and a man and a woman, greet each other with dos besos, two light kisses, one on each cheek, even at a first meeting; two men usually shake hands. In Madrid you will also find many intercambios, evenings in a bar where Spaniards and foreigners meet to swap languages: half an hour in English, half an hour in Spanish. They are the best place to say, for the first time and to a real person: Encantado, or Encantada.

== 3 A1
numeral: Lección tercera
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* tér*cé*ra
title: ¿Qué tal?
title.en: How are things?

dialogue:
- Buenos días. ¿Qué tal?
  en: Good morning. How are things? (What so?)
  pron.en: *boué*noss *di*ass. ¿ké tal?
- Regular. He dormido muy mal.
  en: So-so. I slept very badly. (So-so. I have slept very badly.)
  pron.en: régou*lar*. é dor*mi*do moui mal.
- ¿Estás cansado?
  en: Are you tired?
  pron.en: ¿éss*tass* can*ssa*do?
- Sí, estoy muy cansado.
  en: Yes, I'm very tired. (Yes, [I] am very tired.)
  pron.en: si, éss*toï* moui can*ssa*do.
- ¿Quieres un café?
  en: [Do] you want a coffee?
  pron.en: ¿*kié*réss oun ca*fé*?
- Sí, por favor. Uno grande.
  en: Yes, please. [A] big one. (Yes, please. One big.)
  pron.en: si, por fa*bor*. *ou*no *gran*dé.

sounds.en:
1. The r at the start of a word is rolled, like the rr, though the figured line writes it r: regular is said with a fluttering tongue, not with the single tap of the English "better" said fast.
2. Qu is a k before e and i, and the u is silent: quieres is *kié*réss. The ie is one syllable, with the stress on its first part, as the bold shows.
3. A d between vowels is said with the th of this, soft and short, as in dormido and cansado: the tongue touches the back of the teeth and the breath never stops.

notes.en:
1. ¿Qué tal?: how are things? Literally "what so?", and it is a set phrase, not a question about one thing. The answer is usually a short one: regular, so-so, or muy bien, very well, as we will hear in lesson 5.
2. Regular: so-so, not the English "regular". It is a false friend: in Spanish it means "so-so" or "average", and it is never a praise of something that is steady or normal.
3. He dormido: I slept, literally "I have slept". Spain uses this form for last night when it is still morning, because today has not finished yet; a time that is over, like yesterday, takes another form, which comes much later. We will come back to this form in lesson 18.
4. Estoy cansado: I'm tired. Estar is the second "to be" of Spanish. Soy, from lesson 2, says who you are; estoy says how you are today. Cansado agrees with the person: a woman says estoy cansada. The revision lesson puts ser and estar side by side.
5. ¿Quieres un café?: do you want a coffee? Spanish has no word for the "do" of the English question: the verb quieres, "you want", is the question, and the voice rises at the end.
6. Uno grande: "one big", with no un before it. Uno stands alone when the noun has already been named (un café, uno grande). Grande has one form for both genders: un café grande, una tostada grande.
7. Muy: very, as in muy cansado and muy mal. Muy never changes, and it goes before an adjective or an adverb; mucho, as in muchas gracias, is for nouns and "a lot".

ex1:
- Estoy muy cansado.
  en: I'm very tired. (a man speaking)
- ¿Quieres uno grande?
  en: Do you want a big one?
- He dormido mal.
  en: I slept badly.
- ¿Quieres una tostada grande?
  en: Do you want a big toast?
- Estoy muy mal.
  en: I feel very bad.

ex2:
- [Estoy] muy cansado.
  en: I'm very tired.
- ¿[Estás] muy mal?
  en: Are you feeling very bad?
- ¿[Quieres] un café?
  en: Do you want a coffee?
- [Uno] grande, por favor.
  en: A large one, please.
- [He] dormido mal.
  en: I slept badly.

== 4 A1
numeral: Lección cuarta
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* *couar*ta
title: ¿Dónde está el metro?
title.en: Where's the underground?

dialogue:
- Perdone, ¿dónde está el metro?
  en: Excuse me, where's the underground? (Pardon, where is the metro?)
  pron.en: pér*do*né, ¿*don*dé éss*ta* él *mé*tro?
- ¿El metro? Está allí, a la derecha.
  en: The underground? [It's] over there, on the right. (The metro? Is there, to the right.)
  pron.en: ¿él *mé*tro? éss*ta* a*yi*, a la dé*ré*tcha.
- ¿Está lejos?
  en: Is [it] far?
  pron.en: ¿éss*ta* *lé*Hoss?
- No, no. Cinco minutos.
  en: No, no. Five minutes.
  pron.en: no, no. *cin*co mi*nou*toss.
- Muchas gracias.
  en: Thank you very much. (Many thanks.)
  pron.en: *mou*tchass *gra*ciass.
- De nada.
  en: You're welcome. (Of nothing.)
  pron.en: dé *na*da.

sounds.en:
1. The d of nada, between two vowels, is the th of this, soft and short: *na*da, said with the tongue touching the back of the teeth, not the d of "dog".
2. The j of lejos is the Scottish ch of loch, the rough breath of lesson 2: *lé*Hoss. Remember it with the H of Javier, not with the English j of "jot".
3. The r of derecha is a single tap, as in lesson 1: the tongue flicks once against the roof of the mouth. The ch that follows is the tch of church: dé*ré*tcha.

notes.en:
1. Perdone: excuse me, said with usted, the form for an adult you do not know, which the barman of lesson 1 used with his customer. The friendly form, to someone you talk to as a friend, is perdona. The two forms are one of the first things to keep apart in Spain.
2. ¿Dónde está el metro?: where is the underground? Dónde has a written accent because it is a question word, as cómo did in lesson 2. Está is the form of estar for a place: where something is, not who or what it is, which is ser.
3. El metro: the, masculine. El is used for masculine nouns, la for feminine ones: el metro, la derecha. Metro ends in -o, and most such nouns are masculine, but the rule is a guide and the article tells you the gender.
4. Está allí, a la derecha: over there, on the right. Allí is "there" for something further away than aquí, here. A la derecha is literally "to the right"; its opposite is a la izquierda, to the left.
5. ¿Está lejos?: is it far? Lejos means far, and cerca, near, is its opposite. The word for "it" is not said: the form está already tells you that the thing is far, so Spanish leaves the pronoun out.
6. No, no. Cinco minutos.: no is said twice to mean "not at all". Minutos is the plural of minuto, with the -s we will look at in the revision lesson. Cinco is five.
7. Muchas gracias, de nada: thank you very much, and you're welcome, literally "of nothing". The answer to gracias is de nada; you will hear it all over Spain.

ex1:
- Perdone, ¿dónde está el café?
  en: Excuse me, where is the café?
- La tostada está aquí.
  en: The toast is here.
- El metro está lejos.
  en: The underground is far.
- Cinco minutos, por favor.
  en: Five minutes, please.
- No, no. El metro está allí.
  en: No, no. The underground is over there.

ex2:
- ¿Dónde [está] el metro?
  en: Where is the underground?
- [Muchas] gracias.
  en: Thank you very much.
- El metro [está] allí.
  en: The underground is over there.
- [Cinco] minutos.
  en: Five minutes.
- [De] nada.
  en: You're welcome.

culture.en:
Madrid opened the first underground line in Spain in 1919, between Sol and Cuatro Caminos, and el metro is still the way most people get around the city. Its sign is a red diamond with the word METRO across it, and many of its stations take their names from squares, streets and saints: Sol, Callao, Santo Domingo. Ask a stranger for the way, and you will be told a la derecha or a la izquierda, and often how many minutes it is: ¿Está lejos? Cinco minutos.

== 5 A1
numeral: Lección quinta
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* *kin*ta
title: ¿Hablas inglés?
title.en: Do you speak English?

dialogue:
- ¿Hablas inglés?
  en: [Do] you speak English?
  pron.en: ¿*a*blass in*gléss*?
- Sí, un poco. ¿Eres inglés?
  en: Yes, a little. Are [you] English?
  pron.en: si, oun *po*co. ¿*é*réss in*gléss*?
- No, soy irlandés. Vivo en Dublín.
  en: No, I'm Irish. [I] live in Dublin.
  pron.en: no, soï irlan*déss*. *bi*bo én dou*blin*.
- ¿Y qué haces en España?
  en: And what do you do in Spain?
  pron.en: ¿i ké *a*céss én éss*pa*gna?
- Estudio español en Madrid.
  en: [I] study Spanish in Madrid.
  pron.en: éss*tou*dio ésspa*gnol* én ma*drid*.
- ¡Hablas muy bien!
  en: You speak very well!
  pron.en: ¡*a*blass moui bién!
- Gracias. Hablo un poco.
  en: Thanks. [I] speak a little.
  pron.en: *gra*ciass. *a*blo oun *po*co.

sounds.en:
1. The ñ of España is written gn and sounds like the ni of onion: éss*pa*gna, not the n of "Espana".
2. The -s at the end of hablas is always sounded, as the s of see and never the z of rose: *a*blass. The h of hablar is silent, and so is the h of hablo.
3. The b of hablo, between two vowels, is made with the lips barely touching, so that the air leaks through: *a*blo. The v of vivo is said the same way, as the v of favor was in lesson 1.

notes.en:
1. ¿Hablas inglés?: do you speak English? Hablas is the form of hablar, to speak, for the person you talk to as a friend (tú). The ending -as says "you", so Spanish does not need the pronoun, as in lesson 2 with soy. A question is the same order as a statement, with the voice rising at the end.
2. Sí, un poco: yes, a little, literally "a bit". Un poco is the way Spaniards say they speak a little, and it is used for anything in small measure: un poco de español, a little Spanish. The English "a" of "a little" is the un of the Spanish.
3. ¿Eres inglés?: are you English? Inglés is the nationality and also the name of the language, and both take no capital letter in Spanish. The feminine is inglesa, as in lesson 2.
4. Soy irlandés. Vivo en Dublín: I'm Irish, and I live in Dublin. Irlandés is the Irish man, irlandesa the Irish woman: it changes in the same way as inglés. Vivo is the form of vivir, to live, and vivir follows the same pattern as hablar, with the ending -o for I. In is en, and Dublín takes its stress on the last syllable, as its accent shows.
5. ¿Y qué haces en España?: and what do you do in Spain? Haces is the form of hacer, to do or to make, for tú. Qué haces is the question you ask someone about their work or their studies, and the verb carries it. We will meet hacer in full in lesson 15.
6. Estudio español: I study Spanish. Estudiar, to study, is another verb of the -ar pattern: estudio, estudias, estudia. Español, Spanish, is the language, with no capital letter.
7. ¡Hablas muy bien!: you speak very well! Muy bien is very well, and muy is very, as in lesson 3.
8. Gracias. Hablo un poco: thanks. I speak a little. The modest reply is the Spanish way: the speaker is praised and answers that they only speak a little, even when they speak well.

The pattern of the three verbs so far is simple. The -ar verbs: hablo, hablas, habla; estudio, estudias, estudia. The -ir verbs: vivo, vives, vive. Only the ending changes, and the ending tells you who is speaking. We will put them side by side in the revision lesson.

ex1:
- Hablo inglés y español.
  en: I speak English and Spanish.
- Vivo en España.
  en: I live in Spain.
- Hablo un poco de español.
  en: I speak a little Spanish.
- Estudio inglés en Dublín.
  en: I study English in Dublin.
- ¿Eres de España?
  en: Are you from Spain?

ex2:
- [Vivo] en Dublín.
  en: I live in Dublin.
- [Estudio] español en Madrid.
  en: I study Spanish in Madrid.
- ¿Eres [de] Madrid?
  en: Are you from Madrid?
- No, [soy] irlandés.
  en: No, I'm Irish. (a man speaking)
- Un [poco] de inglés.
  en: A little English.

culture.en:
In Spain the language is called español, the name used everywhere in the world, and also castellano, from Castile, the old kingdom whose speech became the standard. Both names are correct, and in Madrid you will hear both. Where another language is spoken as well, as Catalan is in Barcelona and Basque in the north, people often say castellano to mark the difference, and the word español is the one used across the Spanish-speaking world. A quick "hablo un poco" will get you a warm reply.

== 6 A1
numeral: Lección sexta
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* *séss*ta
title: Dos chocolates
title.en: Two hot chocolates

dialogue:
- Buenas tardes. ¿Qué le pongo?
  en: Good afternoon. What can I get you? (What [for] you do I put?)
  pron.en: *boué*nass *tar*déss. ¿ké lé *pon*go?
- Dos chocolates con churros, por favor.
  en: Two hot chocolates with churros, please. (Two chocolates with churros, for favour.)
  pron.en: doss tchoco*la*téss con *tchou*rross, por fa*bor*.
- ¿Para dos personas?
  en: For two people?
  pron.en: ¿*pa*ra doss pér*sso*nass?
- No, para una.
  en: No, for one.
  pron.en: no, *pa*ra *ou*na.
- ¿Dos chocolates para una persona?
  en: Two hot chocolates for one person?
  pron.en: ¿doss tchoco*la*téss *pa*ra *ou*na pér*sso*na?
- Sí: uno para hoy y otro para mañana.
  en: Yes, one for today and another for tomorrow.
  pron.en: si: *ou*no *pa*ra oï i *o*tro *pa*ra ma*gna*na.

sounds.en:
1. The ch of chocolates and churros is the tch of church, never the sh of "ship": tchoco*la*téss, *tchou*rross.
2. The rr of churros is rolled, the tongue fluttering behind the teeth, while the r of para is one quick tap, like the tt of American "better". The two sounds are different letters, and they are different sounds.
3. The ñ of mañana is the ni of onion, as in España in lesson 5: ma*gna*na.

notes.en:
1. Buenas tardes: good afternoon. It is said after lunch, until the evening, when buenas noches takes over. This is the afternoon greeting promised in lesson 1: buenos días for the morning, buenas tardes for the afternoon.
2. ¿Qué le pongo?: what can I get you? It is the same question as the barman asked in lesson 1: le is the usted form, for the person you serve. You will hear it in most bars and churrerías.
3. Dos chocolates con churros: two hot chocolates with churros. Dos is two, and the noun after it takes the plural -s: chocolate becomes chocolates. Churros are fried strips of dough, and they are already plural, as the -s shows. From one to ten: uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez; tomorrow's revision lists them up to twenty.
4. ¿Para dos personas?: for two people? Para means "for" in the sense of the one a thing is meant for, and it is one of the two Spanish words for the English "for"; por is the other, and we will come to it in lesson 14. Persona, person, is feminine: una persona, dos personas.
5. No, para una: no, for one. Una stands for the feminine persona, left unsaid, so it is una and not uno.
6. Uno para hoy y otro para mañana: one for today and another for tomorrow. Otro means "another" and it takes no un before it: otro chocolate, never un otro. Uno and otro are a pair: the one and the other. Hoy is today and mañana is tomorrow, with the ñ of the ni of onion.

ex1:
- Dos churros, por favor.
  en: Two churros, please.
- Mañana estudio español.
  en: Tomorrow I'm studying Spanish.
- Un café para una persona.
  en: A coffee for one person.
- Buenas tardes. ¿Qué tal?
  en: Good afternoon. How are things?

notes.en:
Six lessons have gone by, and nothing in them has to be learnt by heart. This lesson is not a test: it puts in order what you have already read, heard and said. If a part seems too much, go on; it will come back in the lessons ahead, a little clearer each time. What matters is being understood, not being perfect.

1. Pronunciation (lessons 1, 2, 3, 5, 6). The five Spanish vowels are pure and short, and they never change, stressed or not. The figured line writes them as follows.

| Vowel | Figured | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a | a | gracias (*gra*ciass) |
| e | é | leche (*lé*tché) |
| i | i | sí (si) |
| o | o | hola (*o*la) |
| u | ou | uno (*ou*no) |

The c before e and i, and the z, are the th of think: gracias, lección. Ll and y are the y of yes: llamo (*ya*mo). The h is silent: hola, hablo. The j, and the g before e and i, are the rough H of Javier (Ha*biér*), as in lejos (*lé*Hoss). The ñ is gn, the ni of onion: España (éss*pa*gna). The single r is a tap, as in para, and the rr is rolled, as in churros. An s inside a word is always ss, never z: hablas (*a*blass).

2. Stress and the written accent (lessons 1, 2, 4, 5). Without a written accent, a word ending in a vowel, n or s is stressed on the next-to-last syllable; a word ending in any other consonant is stressed on the last. The accent shows where the stress goes when the rule does not fit.

| Word | Stress | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| escocesa | éssco*cé*ssa | vowel ending: next-to-last |
| Madrid | ma*drid* | consonant ending: last |
| cómo | *co*mo | written accent |
| dónde | *don*dé | written accent, question word |
| aquí | a*ki* | written accent, last syllable |

The accent on question words (cómo, dónde, qué) is always written. Tú, with an accent, means "you", and tu without one means "your"; sí, with its accent, means "yes".

3. Gender: un, una, el, la (lessons 1, 2, 4, 5). Every noun is masculine or feminine, and "a" and "the" agree with it. Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine, but café is masculine without ending in -o, so the article, not the ending, tells you the gender.

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| un café | una tostada |
| el metro | la derecha |
| inglés | inglesa |
| irlandés | irlandesa |

Nationality words agree with the person: a man is inglés, a woman inglesa. Persona, person, ends in -a and is feminine: una persona. The adjective grande has one form for both: un café grande, una tostada grande.

4. The plural (lessons 3, 4, 6). The plural adds -s after a vowel and -es after a consonant. The article el and la becomes los and las.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| chocolate | chocolates (lesson 6) |
| minuto | minutos (lesson 4) |
| tostada | tostadas |
| inglés | ingleses |
| el, la | los, las |

Dos is two, and the noun after it is plural: dos personas. Inglés loses its written accent in the plural: ingleses ends in -s and is stressed on its next-to-last syllable, which the rule already covers.

5. The subject pronouns (lessons 1, 2, 5, 7). The subject pronoun is dropped unless it contrasts or stresses: soy inglés, but yo soy Javier, where yo answers someone else. So it is in ¡yo también!, me too, where también, also, follows the yo it agrees with.

| Person | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| I | yo | I |
| you (a friend) | tú | you |
| you (a stranger, formal) | usted | you |
| he, she | él, ella | he, she |
| we | nosotros, nosotras | we |
| you (all, Spain) | vosotros, vosotras | you (all) |
| you (all, most of the world) | ustedes | you (all) |
| they | ellos, ellas | they |

Usted takes the verb form of he and she: ¿es usted de Madrid?, usted habla muy bien. Vosotros is "you all" among friends and family in Spain; ustedes is used for several people everywhere else, and in formal settings in Spain.

6. Ser and estar (lessons 2, 3, 4, 5, 7): the full present. Ser is for who and what someone is, where they come from and their nationality; estar is for where something is, how someone is today, and the -ing forms we will meet later.

| Person | ser | estar |
|---|---|---|
| I | soy | estoy |
| you (a friend) | eres | estás |
| he, she, you (usted) | es | está |
| we | somos | estamos |
| you (all, Spain) | sois | estáis |
| they, you (all) | son | están |

Examples from the lessons: soy inglés (lesson 5), es usted de Madrid (lesson 7), estoy cansado (lesson 3), está allí (lesson 4). The two verbs are not interchangeable: soy cansado calls you a tiresome person, and estoy cansado says you are tired today.

7. The present of hablar, vivir and estudiar (lessons 5, 6, 7). Hablar and estudiar are -ar verbs, vivir is an -ir verb, and comer, to eat, is an -er verb. The ending tells you the person, so the pronoun is left out.

| Person | hablar | vivir | estudiar | comer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | hablo | vivo | estudio | como |
| you (a friend) | hablas | vives | estudias | comes |
| he, she, you (usted) | habla | vive | estudia | come |
| we | hablamos | vivimos | estudiamos | comemos |
| you (all, Spain) | habláis | vivís | estudiáis | coméis |
| they, you (all) | hablan | viven | estudian | comen |

Completions: Yo ___ (vivir) en Madrid: vivo. Ellos ___ (hablar) inglés: hablan. In the last line of this scene, hablamos is the we form of the same table.

8. Numbers 0 to 20 (lessons 1, 4, 5, 6). Uno becomes un before a masculine noun and una before a feminine one; the other numbers do not change.

sounds.en:
1. The gu of guardia keeps its u before the a, and it is said as an ou glide: *gouar*dia, the w of "war" with the vowel after it.
2. The ll of calle is the y of yes, as in lesson 2: *ca*yé ma*yor*. The z of plaza is the th of think, written ç: *pla*ça.
3. Hay is said aï, with the i closing the vowel as the y of "eye": aï *ou*na. Notice the h, silent, in hay and hoy.

notes.en:
1. Hay: there is and there are both say hay, for something that is there but not yet known to the listener: hay una farmacia, hay una cola. It never changes its form, so the number of things makes no difference.
2. Una in hay una en la plaza stands for farmacia, which is feminine, and so it is una and not uno, as in lesson 6. Spanish does not need to repeat the noun: the one is left out.
3. Está cerrada: estar is used for a state, open or closed, and the -a agrees with farmacia. Hoy es domingo uses ser, as it does for the day of the week and for the time (we will come back to the time in lesson 9).
4. Otra: another, feminine, with no una before it, as otro was with no un in lesson 6: ¿hay otra? is "is there another one?".
5. De guardia: literally "of guard", the English is "on duty". It is the chemist's that is on duty when the others are shut.
6. Está lejos: estar again, this time for where something is, and lejos, far, from lesson 4. Spanish has no word for "it" here, so the English puts one in: [it] is far.
7. Una cola muy larga: a very long queue. Larga is the feminine of largo, long, and it is a false friend: "large" is grande (lesson 3). Muy, very, goes before it, as in lesson 3.
8. Empieza: from empezar, to start. Its e becomes ie when the stress falls on it, a change we will meet again in other verbs. For now, empieza aquí is simply "it starts here".

- Estoy en la cola.

sounds.en:

2. The ll of llego is the y of yes, as in lesson 2: *yé*go. The g before o is the hard g of go, not the rough H of lesson 2.
3. The c of cuando is the hard k of cat, not the th: *couan*do. In empieza the stress falls on the middle syllable, ém*pié*ça, as the rule for a word ending in a vowel says.

notes.en:
1. Son las nueve y cuarto: the time is said with son and las, "they are the nine and quarter". Only one hour takes es: es la una, "it is one o'clock". From two onwards it is son las dos, son las tres, and so on.
2. Y cuarto is "quarter past", and y media is "half past". The third form, menos cuarto, is "quarter to": we will see the whole time, with the table, in the revision lesson.
3. ¡Las nueve y cuarto!: the time repeated back in surprise, as an English speaker would say "a quarter past nine!" with a rise in the voice.
4. Tengo que estar en clase: tener que plus an infinitive means "to have to", and it is the English "I have to be". Tengo is the first person of tener, to have: tengo, tienes, tiene. Estar here is the infinitive, "to be" in a place.
5. A las nueve: "at nine". The word a goes before the time, and the las is the same las as in the hour: a las nueve, as in son las nueve.
6. Empieza is the form of empezar, to start. Its e changes to ie in the stem, a change we will come back to, as the verbs of this kind are met.
7. Llegas and llego, from llegar, to arrive: llego, "I arrive"; llegas, "you arrive" for a friend. Spanish does not need the yo or the tú, as in lesson 2.
8. ¿Por qué? and porque: the question is two words with an accent on qué, and the answer is one word with no accent, "because". El profesor soy yo: literally "the teacher am I". The yo goes last to stress who it is, and the verb agrees with yo; English says "I'm the teacher".

- Son las dos.

ex2:

  en: What time is it?

- La clase [empieza] a las nueve.
  en: The class starts at nine.

  en: Why?

Spain keeps the clock of Central Europe, one hour ahead of Britain. Lunch is late, usually from about two in the afternoon, and dinner comes later still.

4. Voy a estudiar: voy a with an infinitive is "I'm going to study", the same pattern as English. Estudiar is the infinitive of the verb of lesson 5.
5. Vienes conmigo: vienes is the tú form of venir, to come. Conmigo means "with me" and contigo, in Voy contigo, means "with you": con, "with", joins to mí and to ti in one word.
6. No puedo: poder, to be able, has its o changed to ue in the stem, as in puedo. We will come back to these stem changes.
7. Espera, "wait", is an order, from esperar, to wait. Esperar also means "to hope", so the sense comes from the sentence. We will meet orders in full in lesson 16.
8. ¿De qué?: literally "of what?", the English is "what's it in?" and it is a question about the subject of the exam. De cocina answers it.

1. Este es el salón, esta puerta: este, "this", goes with a masculine noun such as salón, and esta with a feminine one such as puerta. The ending follows the noun, and it is the noun that decides, not the speaker.
2. Spanish has three words where English has two: este, this, near me; ese or esa, that, near you; aquel or aquella, that over there, far from both of us. Esa es la cocina is "that's the kitchen", and aquella ventana is the window over there.
3. Esto is this with no noun after it, for a thing not yet named, as in the last line. Eso is the same for that.
4. ¡Qué pequeño!: qué with an adjective is "how small!", and the exclamation carries its own accent on qué. Pequeño agrees with the thing it describes.
5. Mucha luz: mucho means "a lot of" and agrees with the noun, so it is mucha with luz, which is feminine. Tiene is the form of tener, to have, for he, she or it: tengo for I, as in lesson 9.
6. Del vecino: de with el becomes del, as a with el became al in lesson 11. The word de also shows whose a thing is: "the neighbour's".
7. Piso is a flat in Spain, and the same word is a floor of a building: el primer piso is the first floor. The floor you walk on is suelo, which comes later.

sounds.en:
1. The ñ of caña is the ni of onion, as in lesson 5: *ca*gna.
2. The j of joven is the rough H of Javier in lesson 2: *Ho*bén. The v is said b, as in lesson 1.
3. The ei of treinta is the ey of they, closing on an i: *tréïn*ta. The t has no puff of air after it, as the p and t of the key say.

notes.en:
1. ¿Qué va a tomar?: with usted, the verb takes the form of he or she, va, from ir. Usted is the formal you, and the question needs no pronoun.
2. ¿Qué vas a tomar?: with tú, the verb takes its own form, vas. The English says "what will you have?" both times, so Spanish is the only place where you can hear which you is meant.
3. Perdona and perdone are the same word in the two forms: perdona for tú, perdone for usted. It is the ending of the verb that tells you which one the speaker has chosen.
4. Tengo treinta años: tener, to have, is used for age, "I have thirty years". Soy treinta is wrong. The same tener is used for hunger and for cold, which we will meet later.
5. Treinta is thirty. From twenty to a hundred the tens are veinte, treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa and cien for one hundred; the numbers between them join with y: treinta y uno, cuarenta y cinco.
6. Una caña is a small glass of draught beer, and vino tinto is red wine. Señor and señora are "sir" and "madam", and joven is "young"; said to a waiter or a shop assistant, it is "young man" or "young lady".
7. Buenas noches is said once it is dark, where buenas tardes was for the afternoon in lesson 6.
8. ¿Y tu amigo?: tu, without an accent, is "your", as in lesson 7. With its accent, tú is the "you" of the verbs in lessons 2 and 5.

  en: What will you have?

  en: I'm thirty.

  en: A red wine, young man.

  en: And your friend?

culture.en:
In Spain tú is used almost everywhere today, among friends, young people and colleagues. Usted is kept for older people, for formal settings and to show respect, and the phrase hablar de usted means to speak to someone in that way.

notes.en:
1. Era, iba, hacías, tenías: the imperfect, the past of description and habit, "was", "used to" and "was ... -ing". Era is ser and iba is ir; the others follow the regular endings.
2. The endings: -aba for -ar verbs (llamaba, "rang" or "used to ring"), and -ía for -er and -ir verbs (tenía, vivía, hacía). In -ía the í is stressed and is a syllable of its own; in -aba the stress falls on the first a: ya*ma*ba.

4. Más pequeño is "smaller", and tantos coches is "so many cars": tanto agrees with the noun, so it becomes tantos with the plural coches.
5. Padres means "parents", not "fathers": the word for father is padre.
6. Hija is "daughter", but it is also said as a fond address to a granddaughter or a young woman, as here.
7. The other past, the one for an event that happened once, is the preterite, and it comes in lesson 29.

- Antes había muchas personas.
  en: There used to be lots of people.
- Iba al Retiro los domingos.
  en: I went to the Retiro on Sundays.
- Mi abuela vivía en Madrid.
  en: My grandmother lived in Madrid.
- No tenías móvil.
  en: You didn't have a mobile.
- Era más pequeño.
  en: It was smaller.

ex2:
- Abuelo, ¿cómo [era] Madrid antes?
  en: Grandad, what was Madrid like before?
- No [había] móviles.
  en: There weren't any mobiles.
- Vivía en el piso de [arriba].
  en: She lived in the flat upstairs.

  en: I went to the Retiro with my parents.

  en: I rang her at nine.

culture.en:
El Retiro was a royal park and was opened to the public in 1868, with a lake where you can row a boat and the Palacio de Cristal, the Crystal Palace.

== 18 A1
numeral: Lección dieciocho
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* dié*cio*tcho
title: Se lo he dicho
title.en: I've told her

dialogue:
- ¿Has visto a Marta hoy?
  en: Have you seen Marta today?
  pron.en: ¿ass *biss*to a *mar*ta oï?
- Sí, la he visto esta mañana.
  en: Yes, I saw her this morning. (Yes, her I have seen this morning.)
  pron.en: si, la é *biss*to *éss*ta ma*gna*na.
- ¿Le has dado el informe?
  en: Have you given her the report?
  pron.en: ¿lé ass *da*do él in*for*mé?
- Sí, se lo he dado.
  en: Yes, I've given it to her.
  pron.en: si, sé lo é *da*do.
- ¿Y le has dicho que es para el jefe?
  en: And have you told her it's for the boss?
  pron.en: ¿i lé ass *di*tcho ké éss *pa*ra él *Hé*fé?
- Sí, se lo he dicho.
  en: Yes, I've told her so.
  pron.en: si, sé lo é *di*tcho.
- ¿Y qué ha dicho?
  en: And what did she say?
  pron.en: ¿i ké a *di*tcho?
- Nada. Lo ha leído y se lo ha llevado.
  en: Nothing. She read it and took it away.
  pron.en: *na*da. lo a lé*i*do i sé lo a yé*ba*do.
- ¿Y no se lo ha dado al jefe?
  en: And hasn't she given it to the boss?
  pron.en: ¿i no sé lo a *da*do al *Hé*fé?
- No. La jefa es ella.
  en: No. She's the boss. (No. The boss is she.)
  pron.en: no. la *Hé*fa éss *é*ya.

sounds.en:
1. The ch of dicho is the tch of church: *di*tcho.
2. The h of hoy and the h of he are silent: the word starts on the vowel, as in hablo in lesson 5.

notes.en:
1. The perfect: he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han, then the participle: he visto, he dado, he leído. Regular participles end in -ado for -ar verbs (dado, llevado) and -ido for -er verbs (leído).
2. Some participles are irregular: visto (from ver, to see) and dicho (from decir, to say). You will meet more of them in the revision lesson.
3. Spanish uses the perfect for today and this morning, while the time is still going: esta mañana, "this morning". Mañana on its own is also "tomorrow", and that is a trap for the English speaker. Yesterday takes the other past, which comes in lesson 29.
4. La is "her" and lo is "him" or "it": la he visto, I've seen her; lo ha leído, she has read it. Informe is a masculine word, so the it of the report is lo, not la.
5. Le is "to her", and le plus lo becomes se lo: se lo he dado, I've given it to her. The le changes to se before lo, so the two sounds do not come together.
6. A before a person: ¿has visto a Marta? The a has no sense in English; it marks Marta as the person who is seen.
7. La jefa es ella: a woman who is the boss is la jefa. The subject pronoun is usually dropped, so ella is said only to stress the person: she, and nobody else.

ex1:
- ¿Has visto a Marta?
  en: Have you seen Marta?
- He dado el informe.
  en: I've given the report.
- Lo he leído.
  en: I've read it.
- Nada, gracias.
  en: Nothing, thanks.
- Es para el jefe.
  en: It's for the boss.

ex2:
- ¿Has [visto] a Marta hoy?
  en: Have you seen Marta today?
- Sí, la he [visto] esta mañana.
  en: Yes, I've seen her this morning.
- ¿Le has [dado] el informe?
  en: Have you given her the report?
- Sí, [se] lo he dicho.
  en: Yes, I've told her so.
- Lo ha [leído] y se lo ha llevado.
  en: She has read it and taken it away.

aside.en:
When you have read a sentence, see if you can say it again without looking: that is how you begin to think in Spanish.

== 19 A1
numeral: Lección diecinueve
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* diéci*noué*bé
title: ¿Te gusta el cine?
title.en: Do you like the cinema?

dialogue:
- ¿Te gusta el cine?
  en: Do you like the cinema?
  pron.en: ¿té *gouss*ta él *ci*né?
- Me encanta. ¿Y a ti?
  en: I love it. And you?
  pron.en: mé én*can*ta. ¿i a ti?
- A mí también. ¿Te gustan las películas de miedo?

  pron.en: a mi tam*bién*. ¿té *gouss*tan lass pé*li*coulass dé *mié*do?
- No, no me gustan nada.
  en: No, I don't like them at all.
  pron.en: no, no mé *gouss*tan *na*da.
- ¿Y las comedias?
  en: And comedies?
  pron.en: ¿i lass co*mé*diass?

  en: Not those either. (Neither.)
  pron.en: tam*po*co.

  en: So what do you like?
  pron.en: én*ton*céss, ¿ké té *gouss*ta?

  en: Long films, in black and white, in Swedish...
  pron.en: lass pé*li*coulass *lar*gass, én *blan*co i *né*gro, én *soué*co...

  en: With subtitles?

  en: No, without subtitles. That way I sleep better.
  pron.en: no, sin soub*ti*touloss. a*ssi* *douér*mo mé*Hor*.

sounds.en:
1. The g of gusta is hard, as the g of go, and not the H of Javier in lesson 2.
2. The s of sueco is said s, never z, as in lesson 1, and the ue is said as in huevo in lesson 15: *soué*co.

notes.en:
1. Gustar turned round: me gusta el cine means "the cinema pleases me". The thing liked is the subject, so the verb agrees with it: me gusta el cine, but me gustan las películas.
2. Me encanta works the same way, and it is stronger: "it enchants me", which in English is "I love it".
3. A mí and a ti say who is meant, to stress it: ¿y a ti? is "and you?". A mí también is the answer "me too".
4. También, "too, also", follows a yes; tampoco, "not ... either", follows a no: a mí también, "me too"; tampoco, "not those either".
5. No ... nada: Spanish needs both no and nada round the verb, where English says "I don't like them at all".
6. Dormir changes its o to ue: duermo, "I sleep". The o stays in nosotros and vosotros: dormimos, dormís.

culture.en:
In Spain most foreign films are dubbed into Spanish, dobladas, but the bigger cities also have cinemas that show the versión original, VO, with subtitles.

ex1:
- Me gusta el café.
  en: I like coffee.

  en: Do you like toast?
- A mí no me gusta el tomate.
  en: I don't like tomato.
- Me encanta esto.
  en: I love this.
- Duermo bien los domingos.
  en: I sleep well on Sundays.

ex2:
- Me [gusta] el cine.
  en: I like the cinema.
- Me [gustan] las comedias.
  en: I like comedies.
- A mí [también].
  en: Me too.
- No me gustan [nada].
  en: I don't like them at all.
- Así [duermo] mejor.
  en: That way I sleep better.

== 20 A1
numeral: Lección veinte
numeral.pron.en: lék*cion* *béïn*té
title: Me duele la cabeza
title.en: I've got a headache

dialogue:
- Buenos días, doctora.
  en: Good morning, Doctor.
  pron.en: *boué*noss *di*ass, doc*to*ra.
- Buenos días. ¿Qué le pasa?
  en: Good morning. What's the matter?
  pron.en: *boué*noss *di*ass. ¿ké lé *pa*ssa?
- Me duele la cabeza y me duele la garganta.
  en: My head hurts, and my throat hurts.
  pron.en: mé *doué*lé la ca*bé*ça i mé *doué*lé la gar*gan*ta.
- ¿Tiene fiebre?
  en: Have you got a temperature?
  pron.en: ¿*tié*né *fié*bré?
- Un poco. Y estoy muy constipado.
  en: A little. And I've got a bad cold.
  pron.en: oun *po*co. i éss*toï* moui conssti*pa*do.
- Es un resfriado. Tiene que descansar y beber mucha agua.
  en: It's a cold. You have to rest and drink lots of water.
  pron.en: éss oun réss*fria*do. *tié*né ké désscan*ssar* i bé*bér* *mou*tcha *a*goua.
- ¿Puedo ir a trabajar?
  en: Can I go to work?
  pron.en: ¿*poué*do ir a traba*Har*?
- ¿Quiere ir a trabajar?
  en: Do you want to go to work?
  pron.en: ¿*kié*ré ir a traba*Har*?
- No.
  en: No.
  pron.en: no.
- Entonces no puede.
  en: Then you can't.
  pron.en: én*ton*céss no *poué*dé.

sounds.en:
1. The g of garganta is the hard g of go: gar*gan*ta, and the stress falls on gan.
2. The r at the start of resfriado is rolled, though it is written as one r: réss*fria*do.

notes.en:
1. Me duele la cabeza: doler works like gustar, so the thing that hurts is the subject, "the head hurts me". With a plural it is me duelen, as in me duelen los pies.
2. The part of the body takes el or la, not "my": me duele la cabeza, "my head hurts". Spanish does not need the "my" for the body.
3. ¿Qué le pasa?: with usted, le means "you", and pasar is "happen": "what is happening to you?", which in English is "what's the matter?".
4. Tener fiebre: tener, "to have", is used for fever, as it is for age in lesson 13.
5. Tiene que descansar: tener que with an infinitive is "have to", as in lesson 9, where tengo que estar meant "I have to be".
6. Constipado means "with a cold". The English word "constipated" is estreñido, a false friend to avoid.
7. El agua: agua is a feminine word, but it takes el in the singular, because the a is stressed. The adjective stays feminine, so it is mucha agua.

culture.en:
In Spain the family doctor is the médico de cabecera, and you see them at the centro de salud, the local health centre.

ex1:
- No me duele nada.
  en: Nothing hurts.
- No tengo fiebre.
  en: I haven't got a temperature.
- Tengo que descansar.
  en: I have to rest.
- Puedo beber agua.
  en: I can drink water.
- No puedo ir hoy.
  en: I can't go today.

ex2:
- ¿Te [duele] la cabeza?
  en: Does your head hurt?
- ¿Qué le [pasa]?
  en: What's the matter?
- Es un [resfriado].
  en: It's a cold.
- Tiene que [descansar].
  en: You have to rest.
- ¿Tiene [fiebre]?
  en: Have you got a temperature?

notes.en:
Six lessons have gone by, and nothing in them has to be learnt by heart. This lesson is not a test: it puts in order what you have already read, heard and said. If a part seems too much, go on; it will come back in the lessons ahead, a little clearer each time. Doubts are normal, and every table here is a reminder rather than a rule to memorise.

1. Object pronouns (lessons 15, 18, 19). They go before the conjugated verb, and they join on to an infinitive or a command.

| Pronoun | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| me | me | ¿Puedes traerme pan? (15) |
| te | you (tú) | ¿Te gusta el cine? (19) |
| lo | him, or it (masculine) | lo ha leído (18) |
| la | her, or it (feminine) | la he visto (18) |
| le | to him, to her, to you (usted) | ¿Le has dado el informe? (18) |
| nos | us | |
| os | you all (Spain) | |
| los | them (masculine) | |
| las | them (feminine) | |
| les | to them | |

Le plus lo becomes se lo: se lo he dado, "I've given it to her" (18). Joined: dímelo (15), levántate (16).

2. Reflexive verbs (lesson 16). The pronoun comes back to the subject, and it goes before the verb. Levantarse in the present: me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, os levantáis, se levantan. Ducharse and vestirse follow the same pattern: me ducho (16), me visto (16), and vestirse changes its e to i.

3. The tú command (lessons 15, 16). For -ar verbs, the command is the tú form without its s: levantas becomes levanta, and mirar's miras becomes mira (15). The command di, "tell", is irregular, and with the pronouns it becomes dímelo (15). With a pronoun joined, a written accent keeps the stress where it was: levanta, levántate (16).

4. The imperfect (lesson 17). The regular endings are these: -aba for -ar verbs and -ía for -er and -ir verbs. The imperfect means "was", "used to" and "was ... -ing".

| Person | llamar | tener | ser | ir | ver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | llamaba | tenía | era | iba | veía |
| you (tú) | llamabas | tenías | eras | ibas | veías |
| he, she, you (usted) | llamaba | tenía | era | iba | veía |
| we | llamábamos | teníamos | éramos | íbamos | veíamos |
| you (all, Spain) | llamabais | teníais | erais | ibais | veíais |
| they, you (all) | llamaban | tenían | eran | iban | veían |

Había is the imperfect of hay, one form for "there was" and "there were" (17). Vivía (17) and tenías (17) follow the same endings.

5. The perfect (lessons 18, 21). Haber in full: he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han, then the participle. The participle is -ado for -ar verbs (dado, llevado) and -ido for -er verbs (leído). The irregular participles met so far are visto (from ver) and dicho (from decir): he visto (18), se lo he dicho (18).

6. Gustar, encantar and doler (lessons 19, 20). The thing that pleases or hurts is the subject, so the verb agrees with it.

| | gustar | encantar | doler |
|---|---|---|---|
| one thing | me gusta | me encanta | me duele |
| more than one | me gustan | me encantan | me duelen |

With a mí, a ti, a él, a ella, a usted, a nosotros, a vosotros and a ellos, the person is named to stress it: a mí también (19), ¿y a ti? (19). The pronoun le goes with a él, a ella and a usted; les goes with a ellos and a ellas.

7. Tener que and hay que (lessons 9, 20). Tener que with an infinitive is "have to": tengo que estar (9), tiene que descansar (20). Hay que, "one must", is one form with no subject: hay que descansar, "you have to rest".

The week closes here: the forms of these six lessons come back in every lesson that follows, and each time they come a little more easily.
