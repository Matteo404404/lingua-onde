notes.en:
Six lessons have gone by since the last revision, and nothing in them has to be learnt by heart. This lesson puts in order what you have read, heard and said in lessons 22 to 27. If a part seems too much, go on; it will come back in the lessons ahead, a little clearer each time. Doubts are normal, and every table here is a reminder rather than a rule to memorise.

1. Saber and conocer, and the personal a (lessons 18, 22). Both mean "to know", but they are not interchangeable.

| | saber | conocer |
|---|---|---|
| I | sé | conozco |
| you (tú) | sabes | conoces |
| he, she, you (usted) | sabe | conoce |
| we | sabemos | conocemos |
| you (all, Spain) | sabéis | conocéis |
| they, you (all) | saben | conocen |

Only the yo forms are irregular: sé (with its accent, so it is not se), conozco. Sé dónde vive, "I know where he lives" (22); conozco a Pedro, "I know Pedro" (22). A goes before a person who is the object, as in ¿has visto a Marta? (18), but not before a place: conozco Madrid (22).

2. Comparisons (lessons 23, 26, 28). Spanish compares with más and menos, and English uses "-er" or "more".

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| más + adjective | more, or the -er of "cheaper" | más baratas (23), más grande (28) |
| menos + adjective | less | menos grande que aquella |
| más ... que | ... -er than | más barata que esta (28) |
| menos ... que | less ... than | menos grande que aquella |
| tan ... como | as ... as | tan grande como esta |
| mejor, peor | better, worse | mejores que las de Murcia (23), peores (23) |
| el mejor, la mejor, las mejores | the best | las mejores (23) |
| mayor, menor | older, younger (of people) | mi hermano mayor |

Mejor and peor already mean "better" and "worse", so no más goes before them. Una talla menos (26) is "one size smaller", where menos stands alone.

3. Estar and the -ando or -iendo form (lesson 24). It says what is going on now: estoy trabajando, "I am working".

| Verb | Form | Met in |
|---|---|---|
| -ar: trabajar, hablar, gritar | trabajando, hablando, gritando | 24 |
| -er: hacer | haciendo | 24 |
| -ir: escribir | escribiendo | 24 |

Complete with the gerund: Estoy ___ (leer), "I am reading": leyendo. Están ___ (dormir), "they are sleeping": durmiendo, where the o becomes u. Estás ___ (pedir), "you are asking for": pidiendo.

4. Por and para (lessons 27, 28). Both can mean "for", and they are not swapped.

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| para | where you are going | un billete para Sevilla (27) |
| para | for whom | ¿Qué tiene para ella? (28) |
| para | a time limit | para hoy (27) |
| por | the price you pay | setenta euros por un billete (27) |
| por | the way through | pasa por Córdoba (27) |
| por | the part of the day | por la mañana (27) |
| por | a place near you | por aquí, "round here" (8) |

5. Dates and numbers to 1,000 (lessons 25, 28). After one hundred, cien is used alone, and ciento is used before another number.

| Number | Spanish | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | cien | cien euros |
| 101 | ciento uno | ciento un euros |
| 1,000 | mil | mil euros |

Complete: Son ___ euros, "a hundred euros": cien. Es el ___ de febrero, "the twenty-eighth of February": veintiocho. Son ciento ___ euros, "a hundred and one euros": un, as uno becomes un before a noun. Son ___ euros, "a thousand euros": mil.

6. Possessives (lessons 10, 21, 22, 26). Mi, tu and su go before the thing owned; mío, tuyo and suyo come after it, or stand alone, and agree with it.

| Owner | Before the noun | After or alone |
|---|---|---|
| I | mi hermana (10) | mío, mía (esa es mía, 26) |
| you (tú) | tu piso (21) | tuyo, tuya |
| he, she, you (usted) | su novia (22) | suyo, suya |

Su can mean his, her or your (usted), so the sentence must settle it, as su novia does in lesson 22. Complete: Esa bufanda es ___, "that scarf is mine": mía.

7. Pronouns with two verbs (lessons 26, 28). The pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, or joins on to the infinitive, and the infinitive then keeps its stress with an accent.

| Before the verb | Joined to the infinitive |
|---|---|
| me la puedo probar (26) | puedo probármela |
| la voy a comprar | voy a comprarla |
| le va a gustar (28) | va a gustarle |

The week closes here: these forms come back in every lesson that follows, and each time they come a little more easily.

2. Vez means "time", as in one occasion; its plural is veces, and tres veces is "three times".
3. Todavía no is "not yet", and nunca is "never". Both go with the perfect, as in nunca la has visto, "you have never seen it".
4. Hace with a time means "ago": hace cinco años, "five years ago". The verb is in the preterite, fui, because that time is over. The rule for Spain: the perfect for a time not over (hoy, alguna vez), the preterite for one that is (ayer, el año pasado).
5. Año pasado is "last year", and pasado is the word for "past" or "last".
6. Había is the imperfect of hay, "there was" or "there were", as in lesson 17. Entrada here means a ticket for a place.
7. Ya las he comprado: ya with the perfect means "already", and the pronoun las goes before the verb, as in lesson 26.
8. Comprar is "to buy". Its perfect, he comprado, means "I have bought", as in lesson 18.

notes.en:
Six lessons have gone by since the last revision, lessons 29 to 34. Nothing here needs to be learnt by heart. This lesson puts in order the past tenses and the commands that came up in them. If a part seems too much, go on; it will come back in the lessons ahead, a little clearer each time. Doubts are normal, and every table here is a reminder rather than a rule to memorise.

1. The regular preterite (lessons 29, 30, 32). The ending tells you the person, and the verb's family decides which ending: -ar verbs or -er and -ir verbs.

| Person | -ar: llegar | -er or -ir: perder, volver | Met in |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | llegué | perdí | 29, 30 |
| you (tú) | llegaste | perdiste | 30 |
| he, she, you (usted) | llegó | perdió | |
| we | llegamos | volvimos | 32 |
| you (all, Spain) | encontrasteis | perdisteis | 32 |
| they, you (all) | llegaron | perdieron | |

Verbs ending in -gar and -car change their spelling in the I form: llegué, not llegé, so the g stays hard (lesson 30).

2. The irregular preterites met (lessons 29, 32). These verbs change their stem and take their own endings, not the regular ones.

| Verb | Forms | Met in |
|---|---|---|
| ir and ser | fui, fuimos; fue (he, she, you usted) | 29, 32 |
| hacer | hiciste, hicisteis | 29, 32 |
| decir | dijisteis | 32 |
| estar | estuve | 29 |
| tener | tuvimos | 32 |
| poder | pudimos | 32 |

Fui can be "I went" or "I was": the sentence decides, as in lesson 29.

3. Perfect or preterite? (lessons 18, 28, 30, 31). In Spain the perfect is for a time that is not over, and the preterite is for a time that is over. English is less strict, so the rule is worth learning.

| Time word | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| esta mañana (this morning) | perfect | Esta mañana se ha llevado una (28) |
| alguna vez (ever) | perfect | ¿Has estado alguna vez en Granada? (31) |
| ayer (yesterday) | preterite | ¿Por qué llegaste tarde ayer? (30) |
| hace cinco años (five years ago) | preterite | Fui hace cinco años (31) |
| el año pasado (last year) | preterite | Fui hace dos años y el año pasado (31) |

4. Indirect pronouns and se lo (lessons 18, 33). These pronouns say to whom something is done, and they come before the verb.

| Pronoun | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| me | to me | me das (33) |
| te | to you (tú) | te los pida (33) |
| le | to him, to her, to you (usted) | ¿Le has dado el informe? (18) |
| nos, os, les | to us, to you (all), to them | |

Before lo or los, le and les become se: pídeselos, "ask him for them" (33), and se los he pedido, "I have asked him for them" (33). Pedir means "to ask for" something, as in pídeselos. Preguntar means "to ask" a question, a different verb.

5. The tú command (lessons 33, 34). For the command to a friend, -ar verbs take -a and -er or -ir verbs take -e: pela, echa, bate, fríe (34). Pronouns join on the end, and the stress keeps its place, so échalos has an accent. Irregular commands met: pon (34), di, in dile (33), and da, in dale (34). The others, haz, ve, sal, sé, ten and ven, are not in the dialogues yet.

6. Hace with a time, "ago" (lesson 31). Hace takes the preterite, because the time is over: hace cinco años, "five years ago"; hace un año, "a year ago" (31).

That is the week: the preterite for what is over, the perfect for what is not, and the tú command to tell someone what to do.

1. The imperfect or the preterite? (lessons 29, 36). The imperfect is for the scene and for what went on; the preterite is for what happened, once, and ended.

| Use | Tense | From the dialogue |
|---|---|---|
| a scene that was going on (English "was raining") | imperfect | Llovía mucho (line 3) |
| what you were doing when something happened (English "was waiting") | imperfect | Yo esperaba el autobús (line 3) |
| one event that is over (English "arrived") | preterite | Ella llegó (line 5) |
| one act that is over (English "said") | preterite | Dije gracias (line 7) |

2. The irregular preterites (lessons 29, 32, 40). Each group has its own stem, and all of them share the same endings: -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron. The I form and the he or she form carry no written accent: tuve, tuvo.

| Group | Stem | Verbs | I | you (tú) | he, she, you (usted) | we | you (all) | they |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| u | tuv-, pud-, pus-, estuv-, sup- | tener, poder, poner, estar, saber | tuve, pude, puse, estuve, supe | tuviste, pudiste, pusiste, estuviste, supiste | tuvo, pudo, puso, estuvo, supo | tuvimos, pudimos, pusimos, estuvimos, supimos | tuvisteis, pudisteis, pusisteis, estuvisteis, supisteis | tuvieron, pudieron, pusieron, estuvieron, supieron |
| i | hic-, quis-, vin- | hacer, querer, venir | hice, quise, vine | hiciste, quisiste, viniste | hizo, quiso, vino | hicimos, quisimos, vinimos | hicisteis, quisisteis, vinisteis | hicieron, quisieron, vinieron |
| j | traj-, dij- | traer, decir | traje, dije | trajiste, dijiste | trajo, dijo | trajimos, dijimos | trajisteis, dijisteis | trajeron, dijeron |

Ir and ser share one preterite: fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron. Fui is "I went" or "I was", and the sentence decides. The j group has no i in its ending: dijeron, not dijieron.

3. Soler, "to usually" (lesson 37). Soler is followed by an infinitive, and its stem changes to ue except in the we and you-all forms (solemos, soléis).

| Person | Form | From the dialogue |
|---|---|---|
| I | suelo | Yo a las diez suelo estar en la cama |
| you all (Spain) | soléis | ¿A qué hora soléis cenar? |

Spanish puts suelo before the infinitive; English puts "usually" after "am". The other forms (sueles, suele, solemos, suelen) follow the same pattern.

sounds.en:

notes.en:
1. ¿Ha visto a alguien? is "Have you seen anyone?". Alguien is a person, so it takes the a that comes before a person who is the object, as in ¿has visto a Marta? (lesson 18). The perfect ha visto is used for tonight, a time that is not over yet.
2. No he visto a nadie is "I haven't seen anybody", and it is the double negative: Spanish puts the no before the verb and keeps the second negative after it, where English would use only one. A second negative does not cancel the first.
3. Nadie and nada are the pair for people and things: "nobody" and "nothing". Alguien and algo are the same pair in a question: ¿ha oído algo?, "did you hear anything?".
4. Oído is the past participle of oír, "to hear", so he oído is "I have heard".
5. Ninguna is "none", the feminine form of ninguno, and it refers to la puerta. Before a masculine noun, ninguno becomes ningún, and the revision lesson has the table.
6. Nunca comes before the verb with no second no: nunca sale, "he never goes out". Tampoco, in the reply to ¿y ninguno...?, means "not them either".
7. Usted es el portero de noche, ¿no?: ¿no? at the end asks for a yes, like the English "aren't you?".
8. Duermo is dormir, o becoming ue, as in lesson 19. Por la noche is "at night", and the porter is answering what he does at night.
