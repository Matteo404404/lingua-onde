title: Ojalá les toque
title.en: Let's hope they win

dialogue:
- Perdone, ¿es esta la cola de la lotería?
  en: Excuse me, is this the queue for the lottery?
  pron.en: … loté*ri*a

  en: Yes. How long have you been in the queue?
- Dos horas.
  en: Two hours.

  en: And how many décimos are you buying?
  pron.en: … *dé*cimoss
- Veinte. Para todos mis compañeros.
  en: Twenty. For all my colleagues.
  pron.en: *béïn*té … compa*gné*ross
- ¡Ojalá les toque!
  en: I hope they win!
  pron.en: oHa*la* léss *to*ké
- Ojalá.
  en: I hope so.
- ¿Y usted no juega?
  en: And don't you play?
  pron.en: … *Houé*ga
- Yo nunca juego.
  en: I never play.
  pron.en: … *Houé*go
- ¿Y entonces?
  en: So?
- Si les toca a todos y a mí no, ¿qué hago? ¿Voy a trabajar solo?
  en: If they all win and I don't, what do I do? Am I going to work on my own?
  pron.en: … *to*ca … *a*go …
- ¿Y qué va a hacer?
  en: And what are you going to do?
- Pues compro uno más. Para mí.
  en: Well, I'll buy one more. For myself.
  pron.en: … *com*pro …

notes.en:
1. Ojalá means "I hope", and it is followed by the subjunctive: ¡Ojalá les toque! is "I hope it falls to them", which in English is "I hope they win".
2. The present subjunctive has its own endings. Verbs in -ar take -e in the third person (toque from tocar, hable from hablar), and verbs in -er and -ir take -a (coma from comer, viva from vivir). Only the third person is needed for now.
3. Tocar, said of a prize, means that it falls to someone. Les toque is the subjunctive after ojalá, because the wish is about them.
4. Si les toca a todos y a mí no is a real possibility, so Spanish uses the present, toca, with si, not the subjunctive. The subjunctive is for wishes, doubts and what is wanted, as in quiero que, which you met in lesson 50.
5. Para mí, "for me", has an accent on the í, so that it is not mistaken for mi, "my".
6. ¿Voy a trabajar solo? uses ir a with the infinitive, as in lesson 11, and it asks "am I going to work on my own?".

culture.en:
El Gordo is the Christmas lottery, drawn on 22 December, when the children of the San Ildefonso school sing the numbers. Each whole ticket is divided into ten décimos.

  en: I hope they win!

  en: I hope he speaks English!

  en: Well, I don't play.

  pron.en: éss*cou*tchamé … a*o*rra … mi*tad* … *pa*ga

  en: Well, I don't get it.
  pron.en: … én*tién*do
- Cuando seas mayor, lo entenderás.
  en: You'll understand it when you're older.
  pron.en: … énténdé*rass*
- ¿El qué?
  en: What, exactly?
- Que te digo esto para que aprendas.
  en: That I'm telling you this so that you learn.
  pron.en: … *di*go … a*prén*dass

  en: And what am I supposed to learn?
  pron.en: … aprén*dér*

  en: Not to spend the pocket money in one day.
  pron.en: … gass*tar* …
- Vale, papá.
  en: Okay, Dad.
  pron.en: *ba*lé pa*pa*

  pron.en: … si*lén*cio
- Cuando seas joven, lo entenderás.
  en: You'll understand it when you're young.

notes.en:
1. Cuando seas mayor is a time still to come, so Spanish uses the subjunctive after cuando. A habit takes the present, as in cuando llego a casa, ceno ("when I get home, I have dinner"), where nothing is still to come.
2. Para que tengas algo: para que, "so that", is followed by the subjunctive. Tengas is the tú form of tener in the subjunctive, and it is irregular, with the same stem as tenga; seas, the tú form of ser, follows the same pattern with sea.
3. Lo entenderás is the future of entender, "you will understand", with lo before it. The future ending -ás is the one from lesson 43.
4. ¿El qué?: "what, exactly?", a colloquial way to ask what someone means. The answer starts with que: (lo que entenderás es) que te digo esto...
5. A no gastar la paga: aprender takes a before an infinitive (aprender a ahorrar); with no in front it is "not to spend".
6. Joven, "young", is the opposite of mayor, and it takes the same seas, because it follows the same cuando.

title: El tren ya había salido
title.en: The train had already left

  en: Horrible. I got to the platform and the train had already left.
  pron.en: o*rri*blé … an*dén* … sa*li*do
- ¿Y qué hiciste?
  en: And what did you do?
- Llamé al hotel, pero ya habían dado mi habitación.
  en: I phoned the hotel, but they had already given away my room.
  pron.en: ya*mé* … a*bi*an … abita*cion*
- ¡Qué faena! ¿Y dónde dormiste?
  en: What a nuisance! And where did you sleep?
  pron.en: … fa*é*na … dor*miss*té
- En un banco de la estación. Hasta las cinco.
  en: On a bench in the station. Until five.
  pron.en: … *ban*co …
- ¿Y llegaste a casa?
  en: And did you get home?
- Por fin, a las nueve de la noche.
  en: At last, at nine in the evening.
  pron.en: … fin …
- ¿Y tu marido te esperaba?
  en: And was your husband waiting for you?
  pron.en: … ma*ri*do …
- Eso creía yo.
  en: That's what I thought.
  pron.en: … cré*i*a …
- ¿Y qué pasó?
  en: And what happened?
- Cuando llegué, mi marido ya se había comido mi cena.
  en: When I got in, my husband had already eaten my dinner.
  pron.en: … co*mi*do … *cé*na

notes.en:
1. El tren ya había salido is the pluperfect, "the train had already left": había, the imperfect of haber, followed by the past participle salido. It is used for an event that came before another past event.
2. Ya goes before había, as in ya había salido and ya se había comido: it is the English "already", and it sits between the subject and the verb.
3. Dado, the participle of dar, stays the same after había and habían, whatever the noun is, because the participle that follows haber never changes its ending.
4. Esperaba is the imperfect, "was waiting", which sets the scene. We met it in lesson 36, where the preterite was used for what happened.
5. Se había comido: comerse is "to eat up", with the pronoun se attached to comer. The se goes before había, never between había and comido.
6. ¡Qué faena! Faena means a job or task, and in Madrid it is used as an exclamation for "what a nuisance!".

culture.en:
Renfe runs most of Spain's trains, and the Cercanías are the commuter trains around the big cities, Madrid among them.

ex1:
- Cuando llegué, el tren ya había salido.
  en: When I arrived, the train had already left.
- Yo ya había comido.
  en: I had already eaten.
- Yo esperaba en el andén.
  en: I was waiting on the platform.
- Ya se había comido la cena.
  en: He had already eaten the dinner.
- Ya habían dado mi habitación.
  en: They had already given away my room.

ex2:
- Cuando [llegué], el tren ya había salido.
  en: When I arrived, the train had already left.

  en: My husband had already eaten the dinner.
- Llamé al hotel, pero ya [habían] dado mi habitación.
  en: I called the hotel, but they had already given away my room.
- Yo [esperaba] en la estación.
  en: I was waiting at the station.
- Ya [habían] comido.
  en: They had already eaten.

  pron.en: … *ra*to

  en: Not even for a drink?

  en: I'm not running tomorrow.
  pron.en: … *co*rro …

notes.en:
1. Es importante que descanses: after es importante que, Spanish uses the subjunctive. For tú, -ar verbs take -es, so descansar gives descanses, and the -e of the third person from lesson 51 becomes -es with tú.
2. Es mejor que comas: for tú, -er verbs take -as, so comer gives comas. The pattern is the same as in lesson 51, with the tú ending.
3. Hace falta que duermas: hace falta, "it is needed", takes que and the subjunctive. Dormir changes its o to ue, as in duermes, so the subjunctive is duermas.
4. No salgas is the negative command, "don't go out". It uses the subjunctive with no in front, so it is the same form as the subjunctive you have just met: salgas.
5. Ni a tomar algo: ni means "not even", as we saw in lesson 50, and it comes at the front of the phrase.
6. Yo no corro mañana is in the present indicative, because it states a plan, not a wish or an obligation. Corro is the first person of correr, "to run".

The subjunctive will not be learnt in a week: you are meeting it, and that is enough.

notes.en:
1. Se me ha olvidado is the involuntary se: the forgetting happens to me, so Spanish says the thing has forgotten itself on me. The verb agrees with the thing forgotten, not with me.
2. Tampoco is the opposite of también: it means "not that either", and it answers the question by itself.
3. No te preocupes: preocuparse is "to worry", and in the negative command for tú the pronoun goes between no and the verb. The verb is the subjunctive form, so preocuparse gives preocupes.
4. No mires atrás: mirar takes -es for tú in the negative command, as salgas did in lesson 55. Atrás means "back", as in "behind".
5. Pararse is "to stop", and in no te pares the te comes after no, as with preocuparse. Completo means "full" here, not "complete": there are no beds left.
6. Llamaste is the preterite of llamar, "did you call", for yesterday, a time that is over: the same tense as in lesson 29.
