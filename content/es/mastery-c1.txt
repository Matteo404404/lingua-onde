== 1 C1
numeral: Lección primera
title: ¿Nos tuteamos?
title.en: Shall we use tú?

dialogue:
- Hoy, en España, casi todo el mundo se trata de tú, y el usted ha quedado para unos pocos casos.
  en: Today, in Spain, almost everyone uses tú with almost everyone, and usted has been kept for a few cases.
- El banco me tutea en la aplicación del móvil, y el supermercado, en el ticket de la compra.
  en: The bank says tú to me in its phone app, and the supermarket does so on the receipt.
- Nadie se extraña ya de ello, y sin embargo hace unas décadas esa misma carta habría empezado por «Estimado señor».
  en: Nobody is surprised by this now, and yet a few decades ago that same letter would have begun «Estimado señor».
- Mis abuelos decían usted a sus propios padres, y lo hacían sin sentir distancia alguna, como quien menciona el tiempo que hace.
  en: My grandparents said usted to their own parents, and felt no distance in doing so, the way one mentions the weather.
- Mi madre trataba de usted a la portera hasta que las dos se hicieron amigas en la cola del mercado, y desde entonces fue de tú, para siempre.
  en: My mother addressed the concierge as usted until the two of them became friends in the queue at the market, and from then on it was tú, for good.
- Lo que cambió no fue la gramática, sino la idea de cercanía que cada cual quiere mostrar.
  en: What changed was not the grammar but the idea of closeness that each person wants to show.
- El tú se impuso como forma de igualdad, y la igualdad tiene la ventaja de llegar a veces sin que nadie la haya pedido.
  en: Tú spread as a form of equality, and equality has the advantage of sometimes arriving without anyone having asked for it.
- El usted, en cambio, dice algo que el tú no dice: que el otro tiene su espacio y que uno lo reconoce.
  en: Usted, by contrast, says something tú does not: that the other person has their own space, and that one acknowledges it.
- Lo digo sin nostalgia, porque también he recibido el usted de personas que me querían mucho, y lo sentí como una muestra de respeto.
  en: I say this without nostalgia, because I too have received usted from people who were very fond of me, and I took it as a sign of respect.
- Lo que de verdad me sorprendió fue el primer usted que alguien me dirigió a mí.
  en: What really surprised me was the first usted that someone addressed to me.
- Fue en la farmacia de mi barrio, a los veintisiete años, y tardé unos segundos en darme cuenta de que hablaban conmigo.
  en: It was at the chemist's in my neighbourhood, when I was twenty-seven, and it took me a few seconds to realise they were speaking to me.
- El farmacéutico, de mi edad más o menos, me preguntó: «¿Le pongo una bolsa?», y yo no supe qué contestar.
  en: The pharmacist, roughly my age, asked: «Shall I put it in a bag for you?», and I did not know what to say.
- Hasta entonces era yo el que tuteaba; de pronto alguien me hablaba desde lejos, como si hubiera llegado de golpe a una edad que no había notado que se acercaba.
  en: Until then I was the one who used tú; suddenly someone was speaking to me from a distance, as if I had arrived all at once at an age I had not noticed approaching.
- Desde entonces he observado que el usted es más frecuente en las cartas oficiales, en algunas tiendas de barrio y en boca de camareros muy jóvenes.
  en: Since then I have noticed that usted is more common in official letters, in some neighbourhood shops, and from very young waiters.
- Con las personas muy mayores también se usa, como una señal de respeto que se hereda y que nadie ha firmado.
  en: It is also used with the very old, as a mark of respect that is passed down and that nobody has signed.
- En América Latina el usted tiene más terreno: en parte de Colombia y en Costa Rica todavía se oye dentro de algunas familias.
  en: In Latin America usted holds more ground: in parts of Colombia and in Costa Rica it can still be heard inside some families.
- Tampoco hay que idealizarlo: en algunas casas, el usted entre padres e hijos era una distancia que nadie había elegido.
  en: Nor should it be idealised: in some households, usted between parents and children was a distance that nobody had chosen.
- Los dos sistemas tienen su lógica, y lo que molesta es el uso que no tiene ninguna.
  en: Both systems have their logic, and what is annoying is the use that has none.
- Cuando el banco escribe «Hola» y después pide que confirme una contraseña, el tú no es cercanía: es una estrategia.
  en: When the bank writes «Hi» and then asks me to confirm a password, the tú is not closeness: it is a strategy.
- La compañía de la luz me tutea con la misma familiaridad con la que me cobra el recibo.
  en: The electricity company says tú to me with the same familiarity with which it charges me the bill.
- Por eso no me molesta el usted de la administración pública, que al menos no pretende ser mi amigo.
  en: That is why the usted of the public administration does not bother me: at least it does not claim to be my friend.
- Lo que sí me molesta es el tuteo de los anuncios, que no deja más salida que aceptarlo.
  en: What does annoy me is the tú of adverts, which leaves no way out but to accept it.
- Hay quien dice que el tú es cosa de modernidad, y quien dice que es cosa de dejadez; yo creo que es, sobre todo, cuestión de quién decide.
  en: Some say tú is a sign of modernity and others that it is a sign of carelessness; I think it is, above all, a question of who decides.
- Por eso creo que lo correcto es preguntar, y no decidir por el otro.
  en: So I think the right thing is to ask, and not to decide for the other person.
- En el trabajo, con los compañeros más jóvenes, nos hemos tuteado sin decidirlo; con los mayores seguimos con el usted sin que nadie lo proponga.
  en: At work, with my younger colleagues, we have ended up using tú without deciding to; with the older ones we have kept usted without anyone proposing it.
- Hace poco, a los sesenta años, le dije a un camarero de veinte años: «Trátame de tú, por favor».
  en: Not long ago, at sixty, I said to a waiter of twenty: «Please use tú with me».
- Y él me contestó: «Como usted quiera, caballero».
  en: And he answered: «As you wish, sir».

notes.en:
1. tratarse de tú (sentence 1): two people address each other as tú, and the reflexive makes the choice mutual. English "you" gives no such choice, so the Spanish verb shows the relationship. The forms themselves are in (Foundation, lesson 13). Example: Nos tratamos de tú desde el primer día, "we have been on tú terms since the first day".
2. tutea (sentence 2): tutear is the verb for addressing someone as tú, and it takes a person as its object. A company can do it too, which is the point of the essay. Example: Mi jefe me tutea, pero yo le digo usted, "my boss says tú to me, but I say usted to him".
3. Estimado señor (sentence 3): the usual opening of a formal letter to a man. A letter to a woman begins Estimada señora, and a letter to a company Estimados señores.
4. decían usted (sentence 4): in many families of the older generation, children said usted to their parents. The sentence describes a habit, not a rule, and it says the habit felt natural.
5. tratar de usted (sentence 5): tratar de usted and tratar de tú are the two set phrases for the two forms of address. A portera is the concierge who looks after a block of flats. Example: La trato de usted, aunque es mi cuñada, "I address her as usted, although she is my sister-in-law".
6. en cambio (sentence 8): it sets one thing against another and sits between commas after the first part, as in (Perfectionnement, lesson 64, note 4). Example: Yo, en cambio, prefiero el tuteo, "I, on the other hand, prefer tú".
7. tardé unos segundos en (sentence 11): tardar en with an infinitive means to take time to do something. Example: Tardé en contestarle, "it took me a while to answer him".
8. le pongo (sentence 12): the pronoun le refers to the usted the pharmacist uses, and it means "for you". Shops say it all day: ¿Le pongo una bolsa?, "shall I put a bag in for you?".
9. como si hubiera llegado (sentence 13): como si takes the imperfect or the pluperfect subjunctive when the comparison is not real. Hubiera llegado is the pluperfect subjunctive. Example: Me habló como si no me conociera, "he spoke to me as if he did not know me".
10. en boca de (sentence 14): said by, coming from the mouth of someone. Example: Esa palabra, en boca de mi abuela, suena natural, "that word, from my grandmother, sounds natural".
11. Latin America (sentence 16): usted is heard more widely in Latin America than in Spain. In Madrid a mother and daughter would say tú to each other; in parts of Colombia and in Costa Rica, usted can still be heard within families. Each country has its own usage, so no single rule covers the region.
12. pretende ser (sentence 21): pretender means to try, or to claim, to be something. It does not mean to fake something, as English "pretend" often does. Example: Pretende saber mucho de vinos, "he claims to know a lot about wine".
13. no deja más salida que (sentence 22): no dejar más salida que means to leave no choice but. Example: Nos deja más salida que esperar, "it leaves us no choice but to wait".
14. cosa de (sentence 23): cosa de followed by a noun means a matter of, or a sign of. Example: Eso es cosa de niños, "that is a child's business", said of something childish.
15. «Como usted quiera» (sentence 27): the formula grants the request in words, as you wish, and keeps usted in the form. The last line gives the choice back to the writer, who asked for it.
16. caballero (sentence 27): the address waiters often use for a man in a restaurant. It goes with usted, and it sounds a little old-fashioned.

ex1:
- Señora Ruiz, le ruego que me disculpe el retraso, aunque el tren se paró media hora en Atocha.
  en: Mrs Ruiz, I beg your pardon for being late, though the train stopped for half an hour at Atocha.
- Tutear a alguien no es un gesto neutro: a veces es una invitación y otras veces, una imposición.
  en: Using tú with someone is never a neutral gesture: sometimes it is an invitation, and sometimes an imposition.
- «¿Ya no te acuerdas de mí?», me preguntó la vecina, a la que siempre había tuteado, y tardé en reconocerla.
  en: "Don't you remember me?" the neighbour asked, a woman I had always addressed as tú, and it took me a while to recognise her.
- A diferencia de mi madre, no me importa que mis alumnos me traten de tú, siempre que sepan dónde está el límite.
  en: Unlike my mother, I do not mind my students addressing me as tú, as long as they know where the limit is.
- «Mire, caballero, la mesa está reservada desde las nueve, pero si quiere esperar a que se libere la barra, con gusto le atiendo.»
  en: "Look, sir, the table has been booked since nine, but if you would like to wait until a place at the bar frees up, I would be glad to serve you."

ex2:
- No tiene [pelos] en la lengua: dice lo que piensa, aunque le cueste algún amigo.
  en: He does not mince his words: he says what he thinks, even if it costs him a friend.
- Lo que me ha contado Sonia no tiene [ni pies] ni cabeza.
  en: What Sonia told me makes no sense at all.
- Lucía, no me [tomes] el pelo: ¿de verdad te ha tocado la lotería?
  en: Lucía, don't pull my leg: has the lottery really come up for you?
- Ayer estuve toda la cena en las [nubes]: no me enteré de nada de lo que hablábamos.
  en: Yesterday I was daydreaming all through dinner: I did not catch a word of what we were talking about.
- Vi a mi antiguo profesor en la calle y [pasé] de largo, sin saludarle.
  en: I saw my old teacher in the street and walked straight past him without greeting him.

== 2 C1
numeral: Lección segunda
title: Dos mensajes, una queja
title.en: Two messages, one complaint

dialogue:
- Hace una semana que el agua cae sobre mi balcón, y no hace falta ser un experto para saber de dónde viene.
  en: For a week water has been falling onto my balcony, and you do not need to be an expert to know where it comes from.
- Viene de la lavadora del piso de encima, que a las seis de la mañana empieza a centrifugar como si fuera un avión.
  en: It comes from the washing machine in the flat above, which starts its spin cycle at six in the morning as if it were an aeroplane.
- El ruido empieza antes de que salga el sol, y dura casi una hora.
  en: The noise starts before the sun is up, and it lasts almost an hour.
- Mi amiga Ana se había mudado al edificio el mes anterior, y lo primero que hice fue escribirle por WhatsApp.
  en: My friend Ana had moved into the building the month before, and the first thing I did was write to her on WhatsApp.
- Hacía años que no vivíamos cerca, así que su mudanza me pareció una buena noticia.
  en: We had not lived near each other for years, so her move seemed to me good news.
- El mensaje salió así: «Tía, no puedo más. La del cuarto pone la lavadora a las seis de la mañana, y el agua me gotea en el balcón desde hace una semana. Es que flipa, en serio.»
  en: The message came out like this: «Tía, I can't take any more. The woman on the fourth floor runs her washing machine at six in the morning, and water has been dripping onto my balcony for a week. It's outrageous, honestly.»
- Lo leí dos veces antes de enviarlo, y me pareció una queja con buen humor, de esas que se hacen entre amigas.
  en: I read it twice before sending it, and it seemed to me a complaint with good humour, the kind one makes between friends.
- No me pareció un texto grosero, solo impaciente.
  en: It did not strike me as rude, only impatient.
- Luego pensé que, para servir de algo, la queja tenía que llegar a quien podía arreglarlo, y eso ya era otra cosa.
  en: Then I thought that, to be of any use, the complaint had to reach the person who could fix it, and that was a different matter.
- Así que escribí al administrador de fincas, que es quien se ocupa en el edificio de todo lo que afecta a las zonas comunes.
  en: So I wrote to the building's administrator, the administrador de fincas, who deals with everything that affects the common areas.
- Con el correo cambié de registro por completo, como quien cambia de zapatos al salir de casa.
  en: For the email I changed register completely, the way one changes shoes on leaving the house.
- En él no había ni un tuteo, ni una exclamación, ni la palabra tía.
  en: It contained no tú form at all, not one exclamation, and not the word tía.
- Nunca había escrito un correo con tanta cortesía, ni con tan poca confianza.
  en: I had never written an email with so much courtesy, or with so little familiarity.
- Escribí: «Estimado señor Medina: le escribo para comunicarle que, desde hace una semana, el agua de una lavadora de la vivienda de encima gotea sobre mi balcón.»
  en: I wrote: «Dear Mr Medina: I am writing to inform you that for a week water from a washing machine in the flat above has been dripping onto my balcony.»
- «Le ruego que lo revise cuanto antes, porque el balcón ya no se puede usar. Quedo a la espera de sus noticias. Un cordial saludo.»
  en: «I would be grateful if you would have it checked as soon as possible, as the balcony can no longer be used. I await your reply. Kind regards.»
- Releí la carta tres veces para quitarle cualquier tono de reproche, y al final la dejé tal cual, con sus «le ruego» y sus «quedo».
  en: I reread the letter three times to strip out any note of reproach, and in the end I left it as it was, with its «le ruego» and its «quedo».
- Un correo sirve para que alguien actúe, y un mensaje, para que alguien se ría; el problema era el mismo, pero cada texto pedía algo distinto.
  en: An email is there so that someone will act, and a message so that someone will laugh; the problem was the same, but each text asked for something different.
- Las dos versiones eran verdad, y lo que cambiaba era la persona a la que iban dirigidas.
  en: Both versions were true, and what changed was the person they were addressed to.
- Dos días después, la respuesta del administrador llegó en tres líneas: un técnico pasaría por la vivienda de arriba el jueves.
  en: Two days later the administrator's reply arrived in three lines: a technician would call at the flat upstairs on Thursday.
- No pedía disculpas, pero tampoco era necesario: el tono de la respuesta era el de mi carta.
  en: It did not apologise, but that was not necessary: the tone of the reply was the tone of my letter.
- Me daba las gracias por el aviso, y firmaba sin ninguna frase de más.
  en: He thanked me for letting him know, and signed without a single sentence too many.
- Ana no había contestado ni al mensaje ni a la llamada que le hice después, y yo empezaba a pensar que se había ofendido.
  en: Ana had answered neither the message nor the call I made afterwards, and I had begun to think she was offended.
- Repasé mentalmente lo que le había escrito, y no encontré nada que pudiera ofenderla.
  en: I went over in my mind what I had written to her, and found nothing that could offend her.
- El jueves, mientras el técnico subía las escaleras, me llegó su respuesta por WhatsApp.
  en: On Thursday, while the technician was climbing the stairs, her reply came through on WhatsApp.
- Lo leí de pie, en el rellano, y tardé un momento en entenderlo.
  en: I read it standing on the landing, and it took me a moment to understand it.
- Fue muy corta: «Tía, la del cuarto soy yo.»
  en: It was very short: «Tía, I'm the one on the fourth floor.»

notes.en:
1. tía (sentence 6): a woman's address to a female friend, common and warm in Spain, and it can carry surprise or complaint. It is not an aunt here. English has no close match, so the Spanish word stays in the message.
2. la del cuarto (sentence 6): la with a floor, a place or a feature names a neighbour without a name. The same pattern gives la del quinto or la del tercero. It is said in buildings, and it is informal.
3. Es que flipa, en serio (sentence 6): es que opens the complaint, and flipar, in this use, means that something is outrageous. En serio adds that the speaker means it. Both are colloquial, so they stay in the chat, not in the letter.
4. de esas que se hacen entre amigas (sentence 7): de esas que followed by a verb means the kind of thing that is done; the relative clause describes a type, not one act. The sentence is in the register of the chat.
5. administrador de fincas (sentence 10): the professional who manages a building for its owners, and who handles the zonas comunes, the common areas. English has no exact equivalent; managing agent is the nearest, but Spanish keeps the title.
6. como quien cambia de zapatos (sentence 11): the comparison makes the change of register physical, as a change of clothes. Quien followed by a present tense means the one who, a generic person.
7. Estimado señor Medina (sentence 14): the standard opening of a formal letter to a man, with his surname. For a woman it is Estimada señora, and for a company Estimados señores. The close must match the opening.
8. le escribo para comunicarle que (sentence 14): le escribo para plus an infinitive opens a formal letter. Comunicar is more formal than decir and means to inform someone of a fact. Example: Le escribo para pedirle una cita, "I am writing to ask you for an appointment".
9. le ruego que lo revise (sentence 15): rogar que with the subjunctive is a formal request, more formal than pedir. Example: Le ruego que me envíe el justificante, "I would be grateful if you would send me the receipt".
10. quedo a la espera de sus noticias (sentence 15): quedar a la espera means to remain waiting, and it is the formal close of a letter. English "I look forward to hearing from you" is looser and friendlier.
11. sus «le ruego» y sus «quedo» (sentence 16): the two phrases are the formulas of the letter, and they belong to writing, not to speech. Using them in a message would sound like a joke.
12. sirve para que alguien actúe (sentence 17): servir para followed by que and the subjunctive means that something is there so that someone does something. The purpose clause is why the email and the message differ.
13. nada que pudiera ofenderla (sentence 23): a relative clause after a negative word such as nada takes the imperfect subjunctive, because the thing is not known to exist. Pudiera ofenderla means it could hurt her.
14. soy yo (sentence 26): soy yo is the way to say it is me, with the stress on yo. Spanish usually drops the subject pronoun, so yo is added only for emphasis or identification.

ex1:
- El vecino de abajo me habló de usted, aunque llevábamos años saludándonos de tú.
  en: The neighbour downstairs addressed me as usted, although we had been greeting each other with tú for years.
- No te preocupes si tardas en contestarme: ya sé que el trabajo te come el tiempo.
  en: Do not worry if you take a while to answer me: I know work eats up your time.
- Ese correo podía haber sido un mensaje de cuatro palabras, pero lo escribió como si fuera una carta notarial.
  en: That email could have been a four-word message, but he wrote it as if it were a notarised letter.
- Aunque el administrador contestó con tres líneas, su tono fue más amable de lo que esperaba.
  en: Although the administrator replied in three lines, his tone was kinder than I expected.
- «Le escribo para pedirle una copia del acta», me dijo mi hermana, que había preparado la carta en el móvil.
  en: "I am writing to ask you for a copy of the minutes," my sister said; she had drafted the letter on her phone.

ex2:
- Vivo en el tercero, y la [del cuarto] pone la música a todas horas.
  en: I live on the third floor, and the woman on the fourth floor plays music at all hours.
- Me pidió disculpas por el ruido, pero [tampoco] cambió nada.
  en: She apologised for the noise, but she did not change anything either.
- Ya sé que es una tontería, pero [es que] me saca de quicio.
  en: I know it is silly, but it really does drive me up the wall.
- Le escribí dos veces, y [quedo] a la espera de su respuesta.
  en: I wrote to him twice, and I am awaiting his reply.
- No me hizo ninguna gracia, pero [me callé].
  en: I did not find it funny at all, but I kept quiet.

tasks:
- Señor director: le escribo para dar a conocer una situación que afecta a mi balcón desde hace una semana. El agua de la lavadora de un piso superior gotea sobre él, y la máquina se pone en marcha a las seis de la mañana. No pido nada extraordinario: solo que los vecinos revisen sus electrodomésticos antes de que el problema vaya a más. Le agradezco la publicación de esta carta. Atentamente.
  task.en: Rewrite the WhatsApp message from the text as a letter to the editor of a newspaper, in Spanish, about 80 words. Keep the complaint, change the register throughout, and close as a formal letter does.
  rubric.en: meaning: the balcony, the water, the washing machine at six in the morning and the request are all there; register: formal and consistent, with no tú, no tía and no exclamation; structure: an opening to the editor, the complaint, a request and a closing; idiom: le escribo, le agradezco and Atentamente are used correctly; effect: the complaint is serious and public, without insult.

== 3 C1
numeral: Lección tercera
title: La tutoría
title.en: Parents' evening

dialogue:
- Señora Ortega nos recibe en el aula, entre las sillas pequeñas y los dibujos de los niños, con la carpeta de Dani abierta sobre la mesa, y empieza con una sonrisa: «Dani es un niño muy sociable».
  en: Ms Ortega receives us in the classroom, among the small chairs and the children's drawings, with Dani's file open on the desk, and begins with a smile: «Dani is a very sociable boy».
- Sonia responde: «Eso ya lo sabíamos, pero nos gustaría saber cómo va en general, y no solo en el recreo».
  en: Sonia replies: «We knew that already, but we would like to know how he is getting on overall, and not only at break».
- Ortega: «Hay que decir que tiene mucha personalidad. Se hace oír en clase, y eso se agradece. Lo digo con cariño».
  en: «We should say that he has a great deal of personality. He makes himself heard in class, and that is appreciated. I say that with affection».
- Jaime pregunta: «¿Y se hace oír en qué sentido?».
  en: Jaime asks: «In what sense does he make himself heard?».
- Ortega: «En el buen sentido. Participa mucho, y siempre tiene algo que decir, aunque a veces no sea lo que toca».
  en: «In the good sense. He takes part a great deal, and he always has something to say, although it is sometimes not what is wanted».
- Sonia: «Entonces, ¿va bien en los trabajos de grupo?».
  en: Sonia: «So he does well in group work?».
- Ortega: «Podría dar más de sí, si se concentrara un poco. Tiene todo para hacerlo. Yo lo veo cada día en el patio, y sé lo que digo».
  en: «He could give more of himself, if he concentrated a little. He has everything it takes to do it. I see him every day in the playground, and I know what I am saying».
- Jaime: «¿Más de sí? ¿En qué?».
  en: Jaime: «More of himself? In what?».
- Ortega: «En todo, en general. Tiene un gran potencial, como todos los chicos de su edad. A esa edad todo es nuevo».

10. no viene a cuento (sentence 23): venir a cuento means to be relevant. The question is not called for, and the tutora says so with the phrase.

notes.en:
1. Vamos a ver (sentence 1): an opening that calls the reader to order, as if a debate were starting. It is the speech of someone who wants to make a point. Example: Vamos a ver, ¿quién ha roto el jarrón?, "let's see, who broke the vase?".
2. la verdad es que (sentence 2): a set phrase that introduces a candid admission. It is the spoken way to say to be honest, and it sounds natural only with a personal statement. Example: La verdad es que no me apetece salir, "to be honest, I do not feel like going out".
3. tres noticias (sentence 2): noticia is countable in Spanish, so the plural is natural. English "news" is uncountable, so the translation needs "pieces of news".
4. Te cuentan (sentence 3): the tú form used in a general sense means anyone or people in general. Example: Te dan el cambio sin mirarte, "they give you your change without looking at you".
5. de la buena (sentence 4): the noun is left out and de la buena means the good kind of something, said with humour. It is spoken, and it ends the sentence with a verdict.
6. Ahora nos venden (sentence 5): the verb in the third person plural without a stated subject is the impersonal use: the market, the shops, the companies. English prefers a passive, "is sold to us", for the same effect. The como si clause takes the imperfect subjunctive, as the comparison is not real.
7. la caja automática, oye (sentence 7): oye is a spoken call for attention. It is informal and it gives the next remark the tone of an aside, "mind you".
8. El frutero sí (sentence 8): sí in this place is contrastive. It answers the machine of the previous sentence with the greengrocer, and it points to the difference between them.
9. el tiempo (sentence 10): in Spanish el tiempo means both the time and the weather. Here it means the weather, and the English keeps that meaning in the word "weather".
10. si no hablas, no llegarás (sentence 10): si with the present, then the future, is the correct pattern. Example: Si llueve, no iré, "if it rains, I will not come".
11. ¿Cuántas veces...? (sentence 11): a rhetorical question that expects the answer "many". It is an argument, not a request for information.
12. Pues a mí me parece (sentence 13): pues here answers the claim before it. A mí me parece gives the writer's own opinion after a general statement, and the two together set up a contrast.
13. Vamos, que (sentence 15): vamos, que introduces the conclusion of the sentence before, in plainer words. Example: Vamos, que no hay sitio, "in other words, there is no room".
14. a propósito (sentence 17): here it means on purpose. It is a false friend for English speakers, who may read it as "by the way".
15. O sea (sentence 18): o sea introduces a restatement of the previous idea, usually as a conclusion the speaker draws. It is spoken, and it is more informal than en otras palabras.
16. la forma más discreta de enfadarse (sentence 26): an ironic phrase. Tapping a bag is a quiet sign of annoyance, and the columnist uses the phrase with a wink.
17. con sorna (sentence 29): sorna is a mocking tone, a sly smile that is not friendly. It describes how the greengrocer asks the question, and it prepares the reply.

notes.en:
1. Queridos vecinos (sentence 1): the opening of a public address to the people of a place. Vecinos are the neighbours, the people who live in the village, and the plural goes with ustedes. The whole text is a saluda: the word, a noun here (el saluda), names the formal greeting from the mayor or the committee printed at the front of a fiestas programme.
2. me dirijo a todos ustedes (sentence 1): a set phrase of formal writing and speech. Me dirijo a means I address myself to, and it is the usual way to begin a notice to a group.
3. Es para mí un honor (sentence 2): a stock phrase of public speeches, used when a speaker takes up a post. It is the Spanish equivalent of the English "It is an honour for me".
4. presidir (sentence 2): to preside over, the verb for chairing a board or committee. Dar and presidir both sound formal, and a committee would use presidir.
5. de primera magnitud (sentence 2): literally of the first magnitude, a phrase for something very important. It is a grand phrase, and it is more formal than English "of the first order".
6. no puedo por menos que lamentar (sentence 4): no poder por menos de with the infinitive means one cannot but do something. It is a formal turn; the English "I cannot help regretting" is a plainer version.
7. se imponga a la comodidad de cada cual (sentence 7): imponerse a means to prevail over, and the subjunctive follows confiar en que. Cada cual is a formal "each person".
8. Recordemos (sentence 8): the first-person plural imperative, as the committee speaks for itself and the village together. It has the tone of a sermon, and it is common in public notices.
9. hacer un llamamiento (sentence 9): the collocation for making an appeal. Llamamiento is formal and public, and it is used for appeals to a community.
10. Ni la edad ni el cargo son excusa (sentence 11): ni... ni joins two things that both fail to be an excuse. The noun excusa has no article here, as in the English "is no excuse".
11. tablón del ayuntamiento (sentence 14): the notice board of the ayuntamiento, the town council. Tablón de anuncios is the general term, and it is what the public reads.
12. dejar constancia (sentence 15): to put on record, a formal phrase from minutes and official notices. It is less direct than decir.
13. Aprovecho la ocasión (sentence 16): the stock phrase that opens a list of announcements. It is always followed by a "para" clause, here recordar que.
14. humilde presidente (sentence 17): humilde is a self-deprecating adjective that goes before a title in formal speech. It is a formula, and the speaker is not being humble by saying it.
15. Viva nuestro pueblo (sentence 23): a cry of praise, with the subjunctive viva used as an exclamation. It is a formula of public speeches and fiestas.
16. Posdata (sentence 25): the postscript, placed after the signature, as in a letter. Written in full, it is how a letter adds a last point, and the abbreviation P.D. is the same thing.

notes.en:
1. que me ponéis nervioso (sentence 1): ponéis is the vosotros form, used among family in Spain. Bueno, bueno is a spoken warm-up, a sign that the speaker is still finding his rhythm.
2. Hace un rato, mi tía Sonia me ha pedido (sentence 2): in Spain the perfect is used for a recent event, when the time is not over, so me ha pedido, not me pidió. Hace un rato is a little while ago.
3. no me está resultando nada fácil (sentence 3): resultar with an adjective means to turn out to be, and it is the spoken way to say that something is hard. Nada with fácil is an intensifier: not at all easy.
4. dan para muchas historias (sentence 5): dar para means to be enough for, to stretch to. Example: Con ese presupuesto no da para más, "that budget does not stretch to anything more".
5. Ella ha criado a tres hijos (sentence 6): criar means to raise a child, the verb for bringing up children, and aguantar means to put up with. Both are everyday verbs, and aguantar is colloquial.
6. con la voz de cuando eran pequeños (sentence 7): the phrase is a detail of memory. The imperfect sets the background of the children's childhood, and the voice is the detail that makes it personal.
7. Dicen que la paciencia se hereda (sentence 8): dicen que is the generic way to report a common saying, and se hereda is the passive reflexive: patience is inherited. Example: Dicen que el talento se hereda, "they say talent is inherited".
8. cuando el novio llegó a la iglesia (sentence 9): the preterite marks the event of the story, the arrival. The imperfect would be used for what was going on at the time. The story moves on by preterites, one after another.
9. Y no tengo claro si el abuelo llegó tarde (sentence 11): no tener claro si takes the indicative: the speaker is not sure what happened. Both the bicycle and the two hours are offered as possible causes.
10. Abuela, perdona, me he liado (sentence 12): liarse means to get tangled or muddled, and it is colloquial. Example: Me he liado con las fechas, "I have got the dates mixed up".
11. la esperaba desde hacía toda la vida (sentence 13): desde hacía plus a period describes something that had been going on for that long in the past. Example: Le dijo que la esperaba desde hacía años, "he told her he had been waiting for her for years".
12. A los que llegan tarde (sentence 15): the preposition a plus the article and a relative clause, "those who arrive late".
13. Que cumplas muchos más (sentence 24): que with the present subjunctive is the form of a wish, here addressed to the grandmother with tú. Ojalá cumplas is also possible, but que is the usual form in a toast, as in (Foundation, lesson 90).
14. ni uno más (sentence 25): the last line says the speeches must stop. Con discursos como este, ni uno más is her verdict, and it closes the toast by taking it back. Ni uno más means not one more.
15. hacerle la pelota (sentence 19): hacer la pelota is to flatter someone, and it is informal. The literal ball is no part of the meaning; the English "to butter up" has the same sense.
16. lleva años intentando copiar (sentence 20): llevar años with the gerund means to have been doing something for years. The aunt's failed attempt sets up the family joke without saying more.

  en: The second is «a ver», which asks for time before saying something that is not yet known: «Let's see, I'll look and tell you».
- Después viene «bueno», que puede ser un sí, una resignación o una forma de cerrar la conversación sin cerrarla.
  en: Then comes «bueno», which can be a yes, a resignation, or a way of closing a conversation without closing it.
- Ninguna de estas palabras dice gran cosa por sí sola; su fuerza está en el tono y en lo que sigue.
  en: None of these words says much on its own; their force lies in the tone and in what follows them.
- Luego están las palabras de cantidad, que bajan el tono de una petición sin cambiar la frase: un poco, un momentito.
  en: Then there are the words of quantity, which lower the tone of a request without changing the sentence: un poco, un momentito.
- «¿Podrías bajar un poco la música?» pide lo mismo que «Baja la música», pero deja al otro la puerta abierta para decir que no.
  en: «¿Podrías bajar un poco la música?» asks for the same thing as «Baja la música», but leaves the other person a way to say no.
- El condicional hace lo mismo con más elegancia: «¿Te importaría cerrar la ventana?» pregunta por la voluntad del otro y no da una orden.
  en: The conditional does the same more elegantly: «¿Te importaría cerrar la ventana?» asks about the other person's willingness and gives no order.

  en: The present can also make a request, and it sounds more natural than the future: «¿Me dejas el coche el sábado?»
- Un momentito es uno de los casos más claros: rara vez dura un momento, y todos entienden que es una espera educada de duración indefinida.
  en: Un momentito is one of the clearest cases: it rarely lasts a moment, and everyone understands it as a polite wait of uncertain length.
- Los ingleses, que suelen decir «just a second» y cumplirlo, se sorprenden cuando el momentito de Madrid dura veinte minutos.
  en: English speakers, who usually say «just a second» and keep to it, are surprised when a Madrid momentito lasts twenty minutes.

  en: «Igual» works in a similar way: «Igual viene más tarde» means that he may perhaps come later, not that it does not matter when he arrives.
- Un poco es otra palabra de cortesía, a veces sincera y a veces una manera de decir mucho sin decirlo: «Estoy un poco cansada» puede significar que no quiere salir.
  en: Un poco is another polite word, sometimes sincere and sometimes a way of saying much without saying it: «Estoy un poco cansada» can mean that she would rather not go out.
- El oyente entiende lo que no se ha dicho, porque el español confía en que el otro sepa leer entre líneas: eso no es falta de franqueza, sino consideración.
  en: The listener understands what has not been said, because Spanish relies on the other person's ability to read between the lines: that is not a lack of frankness, but consideration.
- El inglés suele pedir que se diga todo; cuando algo queda sin decir, puede parecer sospechoso.
  en: English tends to ask for everything to be said outright; when something is left unsaid, it can seem suspicious.
- Ninguno de los dos sistemas es más sincero que el otro; cada uno tiene su modo de expresar el mismo cuidado.
  en: Neither system is more sincere than the other; each has its own way of expressing the same care.
- Un pequeño glosario, para acabar.
  en: A small glossary, to finish.
- A ver: todavía no hay respuesta.
  en: A ver: there is no answer yet.
- Bueno: la conversación sigue, aunque no se haya decidido nada.
  en: Bueno: the conversation goes on, although nothing has been decided.
- Un momentito: una espera de duración indefinida.
  en: Un momentito: a wait of indefinite length.
- Es que...: la excusa viene después.
  en: Es que...: the excuse comes next.

notes.en:
1. suena más seco (sentence 1): seco describes a manner here, a curt tone, and not weather or wine. Example: Me contestó con un tono muy seco, "he answered me in a very curt tone".
2. Ponme una caña (sentence 2): the tú imperative of poner, the ordinary way to order at a Madrid bar. The usted form, póngame una caña, is heard in more formal places. Example: Ponme un café, "get me a coffee".
3. sin más (sentence 3): with nothing added, and no more words. Example: Me lo dijo sin más, "he told me, and that was all".
4. El oído inglés (sentence 4): oído here is the ear as a way of hearing, a sense for what is said. Example: Tiene buen oído para los idiomas, "she has a good ear for languages".
5. ¿Me pasas la sal? (sentence 5): pasar something to someone; the tú form is the everyday one among friends and family. Example: ¿Me pasas el pan?, "can you pass me the bread?".
6. Con desconocidos (sentence 6): the choice between tú and usted is set out in (lesson 1), and an older stranger is addressed as usted. Example: Perdone, ¿me pasa la sal?, "excuse me, could you pass me the salt?".
7. cuanto más lejana es la relación, más fórmulas usa (sentence 7): the correlative cuanto más ... más, "the more ... the more". Example: Cuanto más tarde llegues, más difícil será aparcar, "the later you arrive, the harder it will be to park".
8. Le ruego que me pase la sal (sentence 8): the formula le ruego que comes from the complaint letter of (lesson 2), where it is used in writing. At a table it is too stiff, which is the point of the sentence. Example: Su explicación fue muy rebuscada, "his explanation was very over-elaborate", with the same sense of rebuscado.
9. es que (sentence 11): the phrase that opens an excuse, and the reason follows it. Example: Es que llegué tarde, "it's just that I was late".
10. a ver (sentence 12): a hedge that means "let's see", said before the speaker commits to an answer. It is not the verb ver. Example: A ver si me acuerdo, "let's see if I remember".
11. bueno (sentence 13): the same word can agree, give in or close a talk. The tone decides. Example: Bueno, como quieras, "well, as you like".
12. un poco and un momentito (sentence 15): poco lowers the request, and the diminutive -ito does the same for time, as in Foundation, lesson 86, where the -ito ending is taught.
13. ¿Podrías bajar un poco la música? (sentence 16): the conditional of poder with the tú form. Example: ¿Podrías ayudarme?, "could you help me?".
14. ¿Te importaría cerrar...? (sentence 17): importar means "to matter", so the question asks whether closing the window would matter to the other person. Example: ¿Te importaría esperarme?, "would you mind waiting for me?".
15. ¿Me dejas el coche el sábado? (sentence 18): the present used for asking permission, one of the everyday softeners. Example: ¿Me dejas tu bolígrafo?, "can I borrow your pen?".
16. igual (sentence 21): in Spain igual can mean "perhaps", as in the sentence, and it can also mean "the same". Example: Da igual, "it doesn't matter".
17. Estoy un poco cansada (sentence 22): un poco here is a hedge. The speaker says less than she means, and the listener is expected to understand. Example: Hablo un poco de francés, "I speak a little French".
18. leer entre líneas (sentence 23): the idiom "to read between the lines" is the same image in English, so the sense carries over. Example: Sabe leer entre líneas, "she reads between the lines".

  en: Listen, Sara, get me a white coffee, I've had nothing for two hours.

notes.en:
1. que ya es decir (sentence 1): a fixed phrase that means "which tells you something", said when a fact is surprising in itself. Example: Acabó la carrera en cuatro años, que ya es decir, "he finished the degree in four years, which tells you something".
2. un horno con ruedas (sentence 3): the metaphor puts the image first: a car in July is an oven. English tends to understate here, with "it was a bit warm", and Spanish piles the image on. Example: Esta cocina es un horno, "this kitchen is an oven".
3. mil años (sentence 4): tardar mil años means "to take ages". Spanish takes the round number and makes it bigger, and the same is done with numbers that are exact in form (sentence 10, diez años). Example: Tardó mil años en contestarme, "it took him ages to answer me".
4. hasta en sueños (sentence 5): even in my sleep, a way of saying that the pain never stopped. The phrase follows the verb directly. Example: Lo recuerdo hasta en sueños, "I remember it even in my dreams".
5. nos comieron vivos (sentence 7): to be eaten alive, the same image as in English. Example: Los del jurado nos comieron vivos, "the panel ate us alive".
6. mil veces (sentence 8): mil, a thousand, means "many times" and not an exact count. Example: Te lo he dicho mil veces, "I've told you a thousand times".
7. se moría de calor (sentence 9): morirse de means to be dying of a feeling or a sensation, and it is said often and without alarm. Example: Me muero de sueño, "I'm dying for sleep".
8. tardó tanto que (sentence 10): tanto ... que is the intensive construction, here with envejecer diez años, "to age ten years". Example: Llovió tanto que se inundó el sótano, "it rained so much that the cellar flooded".
9. una eternidad (sentence 11): the set phrase for a very long time, as in English "forever". Example: Tardó una eternidad en llegar, "it took forever to arrive".
10. no cabía ni una más (sentence 12): ni una más, "not one more", is the emphatic form of "there was no room". Example: En el autobús no cabía ni una persona más, "there was no room for one more person on the bus".
11. ¡Está hirviendo! (sentence 13): hervir is used of water as boiling, and it can be said of anything much too hot, a sea included. Example: La sopa está hirviendo, "the soup is boiling hot".
12. como si le hubiera mordido un tiburón (sentence 13): como si with the pluperfect subjunctive, as in (lesson 1, note 9), for a comparison that is not real. Example: Me miró como si no me conociera, "he looked at me as if he did not know me".
13. Nacho se pasó la tormenta gritando (sentence 16): pasarse + a time + gerund means "to spend the time doing something". Example: Me pasé la tarde leyendo, "I spent the afternoon reading".
14. colorados como cangrejos (sentence 18): colorado is the red of sunburn and of blushing, and the simile is the usual one in Spain. Example: Se puso colorada de vergüenza, "she went red with embarrassment".
15. daba tiempo a leer una novela (sentence 25): dar tiempo a plus an infinitive means "there is time to". Example: Da tiempo a ducharse antes de cenar, "there is time to shower before dinner".
16. ni hablar (sentence 24): a fixed refusal, "not a chance". Example: ¿Me prestas el coche? Ni hablar, "can I borrow the car? No way".
17. repetimos (sentence 30): the present for a plan in the near future, as in English "we're going again". Example: El lunes volvemos a Madrid, "on Monday we are going back to Madrid".

  en: But let us come to what matters, which is the bread.

notes.en:
1. «croquetas deconstruidas» (sentence 6): the quotation marks show that the phrase is the restaurant's own, as on a menu. Deconstruir is a cooking term borrowed for a dish whose parts are served apart. Example: Un plato deconstruido, "a deconstructed dish".
2. una lámina de jamón y unas migas (sentence 6): a lámina is a thin sheet, and migas, in Spain, are also a dish of fried breadcrumbs. Example: Unas migas con chorizo, "some fried breadcrumbs with chorizo".
3. «la textura» (sentence 6): the waiter gives the crumbs a technical name, and the critic puts the word in quotation marks to keep her distance from it. The same register appears in the menu's other terms.
4. de bellota (sentence 8): jamón de bellota is ham from acorn-fed Iberian pigs. The word bellota is the acorn, and the phrase is said without an article. Example: Compré jamón de bellota para la cena, "I bought acorn-fed ham for dinner".
5. no volvemos a hablar del jamón (sentence 8): volver a plus an infinitive means "to do again", and here the negative means the topic is dropped for good. Example: No volvió a llamarme, "he did not call me again".
6. o eso dice la carta (sentence 9): the critic does not vouch for the menu's claim. Example: Es de Valencia, o eso dice, "he is from Valencia, or so he says".
7. La idea es ingeniosa, aunque (sentence 10): the concession gives the praise first and the limit after it, which is the usual shape of a critic's judgement. Example: La idea es buena, aunque el resultado no lo sea, "the idea is good, though the result is not".
8. para que el comensal decida (sentence 11): para que takes the subjunctive when the purpose is someone else's action. The comensal is the diner, a formal word that suits the menu's tone. Example: Lo dejo aquí para que lo veas, "I'll leave it here so that you can see it".
9. Confieso que tardo en entender (sentence 13): confesar que introduces a candid admission, and the critic uses it for a small fault of her own. Example: Confieso que no lo he leído, "I confess I have not read it".
10. correcto, sin más (sentence 14): correcto is faint praise in Spanish. It means decent and no more, and sin más closes the judgement. Example: El hotel es correcto, sin más, "the hotel is decent, nothing more".
11. tarda más en desaparecer que (sentence 15): tardar más en plus an infinitive plus que compares two times. Example: Tarda más en llegar el autobús que el metro, "the bus takes longer to arrive than the metro".
12. vayamos a lo esencial (sentence 17): ir a lo esencial means to come to what matters. Example: Vayamos al grano, "let's get to the point".
13. horno de leña (sentence 18): a wood-fired oven, the phrase bakers and menus use to describe bread. Example: Pan de horno de leña, "bread from a wood-fired oven".
14. por eso mismo (sentence 19): por eso mismo is stronger than por eso, and it points at one reason as the only one. Example: Por eso mismo no lo dije, "for exactly that reason I did not say it".
15. Repito dos veces (sentence 20): repetir means to have a second helping, a use common in Spain. Example: ¿Repites?, "are you having seconds?".
16. dejo al gusto de (sentence 24): dejar algo al gusto de alguien means to leave a choice to someone's taste. Example: Lo dejo a tu gusto, "I leave it to your taste".
17. aunque sea solo por el pan (sentence 25): aunque with the subjunctive concedes a condition, and the critic's reason for going back is the smallest one. Example: Iré aunque sea un momento, "I'll go even if only for a moment".
18. Y el pan, que no aparece en la carta (sentence 26): the last line gives the warmest praise to the one item the menu leaves out, and it repeats the bread from sentence 19 in a plain sentence.

notes.en:
1. ¡Enhorabuena! (sentence 1): the formula for good news, a birth included. It is said to the parents, and it is usually the first thing a visitor says. Example: Enhorabuena por el premio, "congratulations on the prize".
2. Estoy hecha polvo (sentence 2): hecho polvo means worn out, and the adjective agrees with the speaker, so Laura says hecha. Example: Llegué hecho polvo, "I arrived worn out", said by a man.
3. ¡Qué rico! (sentence 3): in Spain rico is said of anything that pleases, a baby included, and it is a warm exclamation rather than a comment on food. Example: ¡Qué rico, un día libre!, "how lovely, a free day!".
4. me muero de ganas (sentence 3): morirse de ganas means to be dying to do something, which is stronger than "to want". Example: Me muero de ganas de verte, "I'm dying to see you".
5. que si entras se despierta (sentence 4): que introduces the reason, and the si clause uses the present for a condition that could happen. Example: Vete ya, que llegas tarde, "go now, you're late".
6. clavadita a su padre (sentence 5): clavado a means the spitting image of, and the diminutive -ita makes the compliment warm. Example: Es clavado a su abuelo, "he is the spitting image of his grandfather".
7. la tuya (sentence 7): la tuya stands for your mother, with the noun left out because it has already been named. Example: Mi casa es más grande que la tuya, "my house is bigger than yours".
8. Martina Gil Herrero (sentence 10): a Spanish child usually takes the first surname of each parent, and by custom the father's comes first. Here Gil is the father's and Herrero the mother's.
9. Qué nombre tan bonito (sentence 11): qué plus noun plus tan plus adjective is the standard form of praise. Example: Qué casa tan bonita, "what a lovely house".
10. el de mi abuela (sentence 12): el de followed by a noun means the one of, and it replaces a noun already named. Example: Este paraguas es el de mi hermano, "this umbrella is my brother's".
11. Está hecha un bombón (sentence 13): un bombón, a chocolate, is said of a person who looks delightful, and it is used of babies too. Example: Estás hecho un bombón, "you look gorgeous".
12. ¡Qué bueno es! (sentence 17): the stock praise for a baby's nature. Bueno here means good-natured, not good in quality. Example: Qué bueno es tu perro, "your dog is so good-natured".
13. Duerme que da gusto (sentence 18): dar gusto means to be a pleasure to watch, and que introduces the result. Example: Cocina que da gusto, "she cooks a pleasure to watch".
14. Es lo que toca (sentence 20): tocar in this set phrase means that something is due or to be expected. Example: Hay que madrugar, es lo que toca, "one has to get up early, that is the way it is".
15. igual con Javier (sentence 23): igual here means the same, not perhaps, as in (lesson 8, note 16). Example: Tengo el mismo problema, igual que tú, "I have the same problem, just as you do".
16. Ojalá todas fueran así (sentence 25): ojalá with the imperfect subjunctive wishes for something not true now. Example: Ojalá tuviera más tiempo, "I wish I had more time".
17. ¡No ha llorado nada! (sentence 31): no plus a verb plus nada is the emphatic negative, "not at all". Example: No ha dicho nada, "he has said nothing".
18. Es que de día duerme (sentence 32): es que introduces the reason, as in (lesson 8, note 9). The reason is given plainly and needs no further gloss.

  en: During that time, the opportunity cost of the siesta rises in a way that is not recorded.

  en: Justification. The Observatory has found that the sobremesa generates conversation income that is not taxed.

  en: No other productive sector has so much time at its disposal without contributing to the public purse.

notes.en:
1. Documento de trabajo (sentence 1): the paper's title is the Spanish one of Jonathan Swift's A Modest Proposal (1729), whose calm tone this paper borrows. The paper keeps the tone of a policy file and lets the absurdity come from the content. Example: Documento de trabajo, "working paper".
2. Resumen (sentence 2): the headings of a Spanish policy paper, resumen, justificación, ámbito de aplicación, are its register. Resumen ejecutivo is the phrase for an executive summary. Example: Un resumen ejecutivo de dos páginas, "a two-page executive summary".
3. coste de oportunidad (sentence 5): a term from economics, the value of what is given up by a choice, here used for a nap. Example: El coste de oportunidad de quedarse en casa, "the opportunity cost of staying at home".
4. rentas de conversación (sentence 6): renta means income, and the economic word is applied to talk. The effect of the term is its seriousness. Example: Las rentas del alquiler, "rental income".
5. por hora o fracción (sentence 8): a formula from parking charges and tariffs, where any part of an hour is charged as a whole. Example: Aparcamiento por hora o fracción, "parking by the hour or part of an hour".
6. se devengará (sentence 9): devengar is the legal verb for a tax falling due, and the future makes the rule sound settled. Example: El impuesto se devenga el día de la compra, "the tax accrues on the day of purchase".
7. quedan exentas (sentence 11): exento means exempt, and the list of exemptions is the usual layout of a policy paper. Example: Quedan exentos los menores de edad, "minors are exempt".
8. materias de interés público (sentence 12): the phrase is the official way of saying that a subject concerns the public, and the list after it shows how wide the term is stretched. Example: Es una cuestión de interés público, "it is a matter of public interest".
9. tipo reducido (sentence 14): tipo is the rate of a tax, reducido its reduced form, and dado que gives the reason in policy style. Example: Un tipo reducido para los alimentos básicos, "a reduced rate for basic foods".
10. carajillo (sentence 15): coffee with a measure of brandy or a liqueur, a drink of the Spanish café. Example: Pidió un carajillo tras la comida, "he ordered a coffee with brandy after lunch".
11. no pretende castigar la conversación, sino ordenarla (sentence 16): the construction no ... sino contrasts an aim with the one it is not. Example: No quiero discutir, sino hablar, "I do not want to argue, but to talk".
12. quitamanchas (sentence 17): the noun for a stain remover, and a word for a service as well as a product. Example: Un quitamanchas para la tapicería, "an upholstery stain remover".

15. acogerse a (sentence 22): acogerse a means to shelter under a rule or a custom, and it takes a. Example: Se acogió a la jubilación anticipada, "he took early retirement".
16. ya abonados (sentence 26): the participle agrees with the amount, which is masculine and plural, and abonar means to pay a sum that is due. Example: Los gastos, ya abonados, "the costs, already paid".

notes.en:
1. Señor director (sentence 1): the opening of a letter to the editor of a paper, with the title written in full. In English the same letter begins "Dear Editor". Compare Estimado señor in (lesson 1, note 3): the form of the greeting follows the addressee.
2. ni pío (sentence 2): a fixed phrase, "not a word", said of silence. Example: No dijo ni pío en toda la reunión, "he did not say a word in the whole meeting".
3. cobra por las aceitunas (sentence 3): cobrar por means to charge for something, and the present with siempre and the perfect share a habit that has lasted up to now. Example: Me cobran por el aparcamiento, "they charge me for parking".
4. como si les diera vergüenza (sentence 4): como si with the imperfect subjunctive for a comparison that is not real, and vergüenza is what the writer thinks the bar feels. Example: Me miró como si le debiera dinero, "he looked at me as if I owed him money".
5. cliente fiel (sentence 5): a regular customer. The adjective fiel goes after the noun, and the phrase has a warm tone. Example: Es un cliente fiel de la panadería, "he is a loyal customer of the bakery".
6. Era un detalle de los que ya no se estilan (sentence 8): estilarse means to be done or fashionable, and ya no shows that the custom has ended. Example: Ya no se estila mandar cartas, "nobody writes letters any more".
7. aperitivo de cortesía (sentence 9): cortesía is a gift or a courtesy, and the bar uses it to name something it now charges for. Example: Una copa de cortesía, "a complimentary drink".
8. ¿Se puede cobrar una cortesía? (sentence 11): the writer asks a question and gives the answer in the next sentence, a common pattern of the letter. Example: ¿Quién va a pagar? Yo, "who is going to pay? I am".
9. no admitía réplica (sentence 11): no admitir réplica means to leave no room for an answer, with the smile as the sign of it. Example: Su tono no admitía réplica, "his tone left no room for reply".
10. Nunca me he quejado (sentence 13): the present perfect for a life that is not over, so the claim reaches to today. Example: Nunca he visto una ballena, "I have never seen a whale".
11. He dejado de pedir la caña (sentence 14): dejar de plus an infinitive means to stop doing something. Example: He dejado de fumar, "I have stopped smoking".
12. Ya sé que no es un asunto de Estado (sentence 15): asunto de Estado, a matter of state, is the phrase for something of the greatest weight. Example: No es un asunto de Estado, "it is not a matter of state".
13. Pido al Ayuntamiento que estudie el caso (sentence 16): pedir que takes the subjunctive, and el Ayuntamiento is the town hall of the municipality. Example: Pido que me expliquen el precio, "I ask for the price to be explained to me".
14. no pienso ceder ni un milímetro (sentence 17): ni un milímetro, not one millimetre, is the emphatic refusal to give way. Example: No pienso ceder ni un milímetro, "I'm not giving an inch".
15. Me consta que (sentence 18): me consta is a formal way of saying "I know for a fact". Example: Me consta que el tren sale a las ocho, "I know for a fact that the train leaves at eight".
16. Le ruego que publique esta carta (sentence 20): the formula le ruego que comes from the complaint letter of (lesson 2), and it asks the editor for the favour in the same register.
17. Gracias de antemano por su atención (sentence 21): the closing formula of a letter, thanking the reader before the reply. Example: Gracias de antemano por su ayuda, "thank you in advance for your help".
18. Quedo a la espera (sentence 22): quedar a la espera means to wait for something, here the reply. Example: Quedo a la espera de sus noticias, "I look forward to hearing from you".

notes.en:
1. Un pregón is the speech with which many Spanish towns open their fiestas. It is given from the town hall balcony by a person chosen for the occasion, and it is spoken, not read: the sentences are short, the rhythm is the point, and the crowd is part of the speech.
2. Vosotros (sentence 2): the plural of tú used in Spain to address a crowd of friends and neighbours. Each group in the list gets its own clause, and the speaker moves from one to the next. Example: Vosotros, que me conocéis, "you who know me".
3. Tense in the list (sentence 2): nacisteis and llegasteis are preterites for events that are over, and volvéis is the present for something that happens each August. The list therefore tells three different kinds of time. Example: Nací aquí, "I was born here".
4. Vecinos de Cerrollano (sentence 2): the apostrophe, a direct address to the people as if the place could hear it. Example: ¡Amigos, escuchadme!, "friends, listen to me!".
5. de piedra y de cerezo (sentence 5): the preposition de is repeated at the start of each item, so the list builds from stone to people. Example: Una casa de piedra, de madera y de cal, "a house of stone, wood and lime".
6. Aquí se aprendió (sentences 6 to 8): anaphora, the same words three times. The third repetition changes its second half, from learning to stay and to come back to learning never to let anyone leave without supper. Example: Aquí se trabaja, aquí se descansa, "here people work, here people rest".
7. ¿Y quién te ha querido más que nosotros? (sentence 9): a rhetorical question, asked for the effect and not for an answer. Example: ¿Y quién no ha querido volver alguna vez?, "and who has not wanted to come back at some point?".
8. Según cuentan los mayores (sentence 12): the story is told as the older people tell it, so it is marked as something heard and not checked. Example: Según cuentan, el río se secó una vez, "so they say, the river once dried up".
9. un poco (sentence 13): the phrase comes three times, and its small quantity is the point: the progress is real but modest. See (lesson 8, note 17), where un poco softens a sentence. Example: Estoy un poco cansada, "I am a little tired".
10. wifi (sentence 14): the standard Spanish word for wireless internet, used without translation in Spain. Example: La contraseña del wifi está en la nevera, "the wifi password is on the fridge".
11. aun así (sentence 18): even so, written without an accent. It concedes the point of the sentence before, here the clock that does not work. Example: Llueve, aun así salimos, "it is raining, even so we go out".
12. Llevo veinte minutos (sentence 22): llevar plus a period of time and the present is used for an action that began earlier and is still going on. Example: Llevo dos horas esperando, "I have been waiting for two hours".
13. que empiecen las fiestas (sentence 23): que plus the present subjunctive expresses a wish or an order to others, as it does in Foundation, lesson 90. Example: ¡Que aproveche!, "enjoy your meal!".
14. quedarse sin algo (sentence 19): quedarse sin means to be left without a thing. Example: Me quedé sin dinero, "I was left without money".
15. aunque toque mal (sentence 21): aunque with the subjunctive concedes a case not yet settled, and the list of toasts depends on it. See (lesson 10, note 17), where the same construction is used for the critic's reason. Example: Iré aunque llueva, "I shall go even if it rains".
16. no sabe dónde esconderse (sentence 21): the idiom for being embarrassed to the point of wanting to hide, with the word order fixed. Example: Cuando me vio, no sabía dónde esconderme, "when he saw me, I did not know where to hide".
17. Ya termino (sentence 26): ya with the present means "I am finishing now", a phrase said often at the end of a speech. Its meaning depends on who says it and how long they have been speaking. Example: Ya voy, "I'm coming".

notes.en:
1. The advert is one long act of persuasion in the register of Spanish advertising: the reader is addressed with tú, the verbs are imperatives, and the claims come in superlatives and round numbers. The sceptic's notes quote the advert word for word and say what each form does.
2. ¿Cansado de cocinar? (sentence 1): cansado de plus an infinitive means tired of doing something. The adjective is masculine because adverts address a general reader in that form. Example: Cansado de esperar, se fue, "tired of waiting, he left".
3. Olvídate de los fogones (sentence 1): the imperative of olvidarse, with the pronoun attached to the end. Fogones are the rings of a cooker; in England they are called hobs. Example: Olvídate del tema, "forget the subject".
4. el robot de cocina más silencioso del mercado (sentence 1): the superlative is el más plus an adjective, followed by de and the place where the comparison holds. Example: Es el hotel más céntrico de la ciudad, "it is the most central hotel in the town".
5. Te pide que sepas pulsar un botón (sentence 2): pedir que takes the subjunctive, and the advert makes the product the one asking. Example: Te pido que me escuches, "I ask you to listen to me".
6. por fin (sentence 2): por fin means at last, and it is placed so that the reader feels a long wait is over. Example: Por fin llegó el verano, "summer has come at last".
7. para quien no cocina (sentence 3): para quien means for whoever, with the indefinite quien and no antecedent. Example: Es para quien lo necesite, "it is for whoever needs it".
8. Sin fuego, sin humo (sentence 4): sin plus a noun names what is absent. The advert sells the machine by taking things away. Example: Café sin azúcar, "coffee without sugar".
9. Mira qué sencillo (sentence 6): mira used to draw attention, and qué plus an adjective makes an exclamation. Example: Mira qué bonito, "look how lovely".
10. Hasta el arroz sale perfecto (sentence 8): hasta means even, and marks the extreme case in a list. Example: Hasta mi madre lo sabe, "even my mother knows it".
11. se limpia solo (sentence 12): the reflexive se with a verb that has no object means the thing does the action on itself. Example: La puerta se cierra sola, "the door shuts by itself".
12. Te lo creerás (sentence 14): the pronouns te and lo come before the verb. Example: Te lo creerás cuando lo veas, "you will believe it when you see it".
13. creí hasta esta mañana (sentence 24): creer hasta with a time means believe until. The preterite creí is a belief that has now ended. Example: Lo creí hasta ayer, "I believed it until yesterday".
14. el que friega a Sabrosín soy yo (sentence 26): el que plus a verb puts the person doing the action in focus, and the pronoun yo closes the sentence. Example: El que llega tarde paga la cena, "whoever arrives late pays for dinner".

  en: Óscar: Since you ask, Irene: it is my father's, who has brought me today because it is raining.

notes.en:
1. The debating session follows fixed formulas: se abre la sesión, tienes la palabra, cierra el turno. Each speaker is named with the side they take, and the speeches are timed.
2. Es cierto que el coche nos da comodidad, pero (sentence 4): es cierto que concedes the point first, and pero then turns the sentence. The same pattern returns in Óscar's sentence 25. Example: Es cierto que llueve, pero salimos igualmente, "it is true it is raining, but we are going out anyway".
3. Si bien hay vecinos (sentence 5): si bien concedes a point in a formal register; the argument stands in the main clause after the comma. Example: Si bien el plan es bueno, el presupuesto no alcanza, "although the plan is good, the budget is not enough".
4. Aun reconociendo que el comercio (sentence 6): aun with the gerund concedes a point and then goes on. Aun is written without an accent here, as in aun así (lesson 15, note 11). Example: Aun reconociendo su error, nunca pidió perdón, "even admitting his mistake, he never apologised".
5. Por mucho que nos guste el coche (sentence 7): por mucho que takes the subjunctive, because the amount is not yet fixed. Compare aunque with the subjunctive (lesson 10, note 17). Example: Por mucho que insistas, no cambio de opinión, "however much you insist, I do not change my mind".
6. Lo de la furgoneta del pan (sentence 20): lo de plus a noun introduces a point from the other side, as "as for". Example: Lo del presupuesto lo hablamos mañana, "as for the budget, we'll talk about it tomorrow".
7. tiene razón en que (sentence 19): the phrase for granting that someone is right on one point before answering the rest. Example: Tienes razón en que hace frío, "you are right that it is cold".
8. no es un muro, sino una puerta (sentence 21): no plus es plus a noun, sino plus a noun, contrasts two images, and the rebuttal takes the image of the other side and turns it round. Example: No es un error, sino una decisión, "it is not a mistake but a decision".
9. Cuando Daniel dice que el autobús pasa a las ocho (sentence 22): the rebuttal quotes the opponent's words in the present (dice) and answers them. Example: Cuando dice que no tiene tiempo, le digo que sí, "when he says he has no time, I tell him he has".
10. cargan con la compra (sentence 14): cargar con means to carry something heavy or unpleasant, and here the shopping. Example: Cargó con las maletas hasta el tren, "he carried the cases to the train".
11. doble fila (sentence 2): a car parked alongside another that is already at the kerb, so that it blocks the lane. Example: Dejó el coche en doble fila un momento, "he left the car double-parked for a moment".
12. el de mi padre (sentence 30): el de followed by a noun for the one that belongs to someone. See (lesson 11, note 10). Example: Esta bici es la de mi hermana, "this bike is my sister's".
13. Lleva ya cuarenta minutos (sentence 29): llevar with a period and ya, for an action that began earlier and is still going on. See (lesson 15, note 12). Example: Lleva ya dos horas lloviendo, "it has been raining for two hours".
14. Ya que lo preguntas (sentence 30): ya que gives the reason, "since"; here the reason is the question itself. Example: Ya que estás aquí, ayúdame, "since you are here, help me".

  en: However much we may like a street without cars, the person who has to take their mother to hospital needs an open street.

  en: Even granting that the argument is good, I am not convinced that the solution is to close the whole neighbourhood.

  en: As for the bus, let us leave it for another day, since now it is our turn to talk about those who have no car.
- Tienes razón en que la ciudad está llena de coches, aunque no sé si eso basta para prohibirlos.
  en: You are right that the city is full of cars, though I do not know whether that is enough to ban them.

- El 19 de marzo de 1812, día de San José, las Cortes reunidas en Cádiz proclamaron una Constitución en plena Guerra de la Independencia, con buena parte del país ocupada por las tropas napoleónicas.
  en: On 19 March 1812, the feast of Saint Joseph, the Cortes gathered in Cádiz proclaimed a Constitution in the middle of the War of Independence, with much of the country occupied by Napoleon's troops.
- Las Cortes habían empezado en la Isla de León y se trasladaron a Cádiz, la ciudad que resistía el asedio, con diputados de varias partes de América entre sus miembros.
  en: The Cortes had begun on the Isla de León and moved to Cádiz, the city that was holding out against the siege, with deputies from several parts of America among their members.

  en: The deputies worked for months with the French army around the city, and with the heat and the hurry of any assembly.
- El texto recibió enseguida el nombre de la Pepa, por el santo del día, y ese nombre familiar acabó siendo más conocido que el oficial.
  en: The text was soon given the name la Pepa, after the saint of the day, and that familiar name ended up better known than the official one.
- Al principio se llamó simplemente Constitución, y el apodo tardó poco en ganarle.
  en: At first it was simply called the Constitution, and the nickname did not take long to win out.

  en: It was one of the first constitutions in Spanish history, and its influence reached other countries in Europe and America.

  en: The liberals of the day saw it as a promise, and the absolutists as a threat.

  en: Its validity was interrupted and restored more than once in the century that followed, and many regarded it as a promise that was not always kept.

  en: More than a century and a half later, another Constitution brought the country together again: that of 1978.

  en: Politicians who had been on opposite sides during the civil war sat at the same table, and the text was agreed more than it was imposed.

  en: There were long arguments about how power was to be shared between the state and the regions, and about almost everything else.

  en: In the end the text went through with broad support in the Cortes.

  en: Democracy was then barely three years old, and the country had voted in general elections in 1977 and again in 1979.

  en: The attempt failed that same night, and the next morning the country went back to its everyday business.

  en: Since then, 23-F has been remembered as the day democracy held firm, and the words of that night are part of the country's memory.

  en: The Constitution of 1812 and that of 1978 were born in times of crisis, and in both cases the words were chosen with care.
- El himno nacional, la Marcha Real, suena en los actos oficiales y en los partidos de la selección.
  en: The national anthem, the Marcha Real, is played at official ceremonies and at the matches of the national team.
- Suena sin palabras, como una marcha de desfile.
  en: It is played without words, like a march for a parade.
- Y, sin embargo, sigue sin letra oficial.
  en: And yet it still has no official lyrics.

notes.en:
1. This is the solemn register of Spanish history writing: the past for events that are over, the present only for what is still true, the names of wars and institutions with capitals, and dates written out in full. Most of the sentences are in the past, and the last three say what holds today.
2. en plena Guerra de la Independencia (sentence 1): en plena with a noun means in the middle of it, and the war's name is written with capitals as a proper name. Example: en plena crisis, "in the middle of the crisis".
3. habían empezado ... se trasladaron (sentence 2): the pluperfect habían empezado is an earlier event, and the preterite se trasladaron is the move that followed. Example: Ya habían salido cuando empezó la lluvia, "they had already left when the rain began".
4. la Pepa (sentence 4): the nickname takes the article la. Pepa is a familiar form of Josefa and of José, so the name recalls the saint's day. Example: Mi tía Pepa, "my aunt Pepa".
5. se llamó simplemente Constitución (sentence 5): the reflexive se with llamar means was called. Example: Se llamaba Pepa, "she was called Pepa".
6. una de las primeras constituciones (sentence 6): una de las plus a superlative-like plural noun. Example: Es una de las mejores películas del año, "it is one of the best films of the year".
7. la vieron como una promesa (sentence 7): como gives the way something is seen, and la refers back to the Constitution. Example: Lo tomó como una broma, "he took it as a joke".
8. se interrumpió y se recuperó (sentence 8): the reflexive passive, with no agent named, for something that stopped and started again. Example: La línea se interrumpió, "the line was cut off".
9. Más de siglo y medio (sentence 9): más de with a figure means more than, and siglo y medio is one and a half centuries. Example: Más de cien personas, "more than a hundred people".
10. en bandos opuestos (sentence 10): bando is a side in a conflict. Example: Estaban en bandos opuestos, "they were on opposite sides".
11. aprobada en referéndum (sentence 13): referéndum takes an accent in Spanish. Example: Se votó en referéndum, "it was put to a referendum".
12. sigue siendo festivo (sentence 14): seguir siendo means still to be, the present of a thing that has not changed. Example: Sigue siendo el mismo, "he is still the same".
13. en la madrugada del 24 de febrero de 1981 (sentence 15): dates in full, lower case for the months, and no point in the year. See the phrase de madrugada for the early hours. Example: Llegó de madrugada, "he arrived in the early hours".
14. el 23-F (sentence 21): Spaniards name the 1981 coup by its date, with the day, a hyphen and the letter F. Example: el 23-F, "the 23rd of February attempt".
15. sin embargo, y sin embargo (sentence 25): sin embargo joins a contrast and means however, not without. Example: Llovía; sin embargo, salimos, "it was raining; however, we went out".
16. sigue sin letra oficial (sentence 25): seguir sin with a noun means still without it. Example: Sigue sin respuesta, "it is still unanswered".

notes.en:
1. The visit runs on set phrases on both sides: the seller's sales lines and the grandmother's answers to them. Each line answers the line before it, and the grandmother keeps the upper hand from the first reply to the last.
2. Es solo un momento, señora (sentence 3): the seller's opening evasion, a short wait promised as if it were nothing. The grandmother takes it up in line 8 and again in line 23. Compare the momentito of (lesson 8, note 12), where the diminutive makes the time sound small.
3. Pasa, que la factura (sentence 8): tú is the grandmother's form to the young man, while he uses usted to her. The two forms show the difference of age and the distance they have, and the switch is taught in (lesson 1).
4. Mire: con esta tarifa paga usted (sentence 9): mire is the usted form of the command mirar, used to draw attention. The seller keeps usted to the end of the visit.
5. ¿Más de lo que me corresponde? (sentence 10): corresponder means to be due or right, and lo que me corresponde is what I ought to pay. The grandmother turns the word back on him.
6. Firmo cuando me enseñes (sentence 22): after cuando with a future sense, Spanish uses the subjunctive, so the verb is enseñes and not enseñas. The grandmother's whole reply is set in that future.
7. El Gordo (sentence 24): the Christmas lottery, drawn on 22 December, when a whole ticket is divided into ten décimos (Foundation, lesson 51). A participación is a share of one décimo.
8. a ver (sentence 29): a hedge meaning let's see, said before committing to an answer (lesson 8, note 10).
9. hasta que le toca (sentence 28): tocar here means that it is someone's turn, and the phrase is said of waiting for luck. Compare es lo que toca (lesson 11, note 14).
10. el de hace un año (sentence 20): el de with a noun names the one of the year before, as in (lesson 11, note 10).
11. Mi marido, que en paz descanse (sentence 6): the standard formula when a dead person is named, said without any sadness in the tone of the grandmother's speech.
12. que el asesor ya me conoce (sentence 20): que introduces the reason, here the grandmother's knowledge of the adviser's voice, which she gives as her reason.
13. Pues ya te vas (sentence 34): pues opens the last line and closes the scene; the reader sees the trade at once because the share was offered in sentence 24 and taken in sentence 29.

dialogue:
- Hay una frase que casi todo el mundo en España ha dicho alguna vez y casi nadie ha cumplido: «A ver si quedamos».
  en: There is a phrase almost everyone in Spain has said at some point and almost nobody has kept: «Let's see if we can meet up».
- Es la forma más corriente de decir que no sin decirlo, y sirve para casi todo: para una boda a la que no quieres ir, para un café con un antiguo compañero o para una cena con la familia política.
  en: It is the most common way of saying no without saying it, and it serves for almost anything: for a wedding you do not want to attend, for a coffee with a former colleague, or for a dinner with the in-laws.
- Lo curioso es que la frase no es una mentira del todo, sino una cortesía con la fecha en blanco.
  en: The curious thing is that the phrase is not quite a lie, but a courtesy with the date left blank.
- Quien la dice tiene intención de quedar algún día, o al menos de no volver a pensar en ello, y quien la recibe sabe que el día no llegará.
  en: The one who says it intends to meet up some day, or at least not to think about it again, and the one who hears it knows the day will not come.
- La frase tiene parientes cercanos, y cada uno tiene su carácter.
  en: The phrase has close relatives, and each has its own character.
- «Ya te diré» es el primo más educado: promete una respuesta que casi nunca llega.
  en: «I'll let you know» is the most polite cousin: it promises an answer that almost never comes.
- «Lo miro y te digo» es el hermano que trabaja: lo mira, claro, con la tranquilidad de quien sabe que no hay nada que mirar.
  en: «I'll check and tell you» is the working brother: he does look, of course, with the calm of someone who knows there is nothing to look at.
- «Me pilla fatal» es la excusa de quien no tiene otra mejor: la fecha no le viene bien, y lo dice sin entrar en el motivo.
  en: «It's a bad time for me» is the excuse of someone who has no better one: the date does not suit, and they say so without going into the reason.
- Ninguna de estas frases dice que no, y todas lo dicen perfectamente.
  en: None of these phrases says no, and all of them say it perfectly.
- Yo las uso con cariño, y con los años he aprendido a usarlas en orden, de la más suave a la más directa.
  en: I use them fondly, and over the years I have learnt to use them in order, from the gentlest to the most direct.
- Para los amigos de siempre sirve «ya te diré», que deja la puerta entreabierta sin prometer nada.
  en: For old friends «I'll let you know» will do, leaving the door ajar without promising anything.
- Para el jefe, o para la suegra, es mejor «lo miro y te digo», que suena a trámite y da tiempo para pensar.
  en: For the boss, or the mother-in-law, «I'll check and tell you» is better, since it sounds like a formality and gives time to think.
- Para cuando no queda otra, queda «me pilla fatal», dicho con pena, como si la fecha fuera la culpable.
  en: When there is no alternative, there is «it's a bad time for me», said with regret, as if the date were to blame.
- Cada una pide su registro. Con tú basta «ya te diré»; con usted conviene decir «lo miro y le digo», que es la forma de las oficinas y de los trámites.
  en: Each one needs its own register. With tú, «ya te diré» is enough; with usted it is better to say «lo miro y le digo», the form used in offices and formalities.
- El tono lo decide todo: si la frase suena alegre, parece una promesa; si suena seria, parece un rechazo, y el otro lo entiende a la primera.
  en: Tone decides everything: if the phrase sounds cheerful, it sounds like a promise; if it sounds serious, it sounds like a refusal, and the other person understands at once.
- Muchos angloparlantes prefieren un no claro, y lo consideran más honesto, aunque a veces sea más brusco.
  en: Many English speakers prefer a clear no, and consider it more honest, even though it can sometimes be blunter.
- Yo confieso que, durante años, creí que «a ver si quedamos» era una forma de mentir. Hasta que la dije yo y entendí para qué sirve.
  en: I confess that for years I thought «let's see if we can meet up» was a way of lying. Until I said it myself and understood what it is for.
- Sirve para que los dos salgan sin cara perdida, y para que el que no quiere ir no tenga que explicar por qué.
  en: It lets both sides come out without losing face, and it spares the one who does not want to go from having to explain why.
- Eso sí, tiene un límite: si la repites tres veces con la misma persona, ya no es cortesía, es un compromiso que nadie cumple.
  en: Still, it has its limits: if you repeat it three times to the same person, it is no longer a courtesy, but a promise that nobody keeps.
- El que la oye tiene también su parte: no debe contestar «sí, claro, pongamos el jueves», porque entonces el jueves llega y hay que cumplir.
  en: The one who hears it has a part to play too: they should not answer «yes, of course, let's say Thursday», because then Thursday comes and somebody has to turn up.
- Por eso la mejor respuesta, cuando alguien te propone algo que no quieres, es esta: «Pues ya nos vemos», que suena a cita y no lo es.
  en: That is why, when someone proposes something you do not want, the best answer is this: «Well, we'll see each other», which sounds like an arrangement and is not one.

  en: A few weeks ago I told my friend Nuria my plan: to stop using these phrases and say no clearly, just once in my life.
- Le pareció una buena idea, y dijo que lo intentaría esa misma semana.
  en: She thought it was a good idea, and said she would try that same week.
- Después de leer este artículo, Nuria me escribió por WhatsApp, en el tono de siempre:
  en: After reading this article, Nuria wrote to me on WhatsApp, in her usual tone:
- Muy buena. A ver si quedamos.
  en: Very good. Let's see if we can meet up.

notes.en:
1. A ver si quedamos (sentence 1): a ver here means let's see, as in (lesson 8, note 10), and quedar means to arrange a meeting. The phrase is said without a date. Example: A ver si quedamos un día, "let's see if we can meet up one day".
2. la familia política (sentence 2): the in-laws, the family of one's husband or wife. Example: Cenamos con la familia política, "we had dinner with the in-laws".
3. Ya te diré (sentence 6): ya plus the future of decir means I will tell you later. The future promises an answer without giving a date. Example: Ya te diré algo, "I'll tell you something later".
4. Lo miro y te digo (sentence 7): both verbs are in the present, and the pair is a set formula of offices and formalities, meaning I'll look into it and let you know. Example: Lo miro y te digo mañana, "I'll look into it and let you know tomorrow".
5. Me pilla fatal (sentence 8): pillar, in the colloquial sense of coming at a bad moment; the speaker says the moment is awkward, not the person. Example: Me pilla fatal el martes, "Tuesday is a bad day for me".
6. Cada una pide su registro (sentence 14): with tú, ya te diré is enough; with usted, lo miro y le digo is the form for usted, with le and not te. Tú and usted are set out in (lesson 1).
7. de la más suave a la más directa (sentence 10): a graded list, with the gentlest first. Example: Desde el más sencillo al más difícil, "from the simplest to the hardest".
8. cuando no queda otra (sentence 13): queda otra with no before it means there is no other choice. Example: No queda otra, "there is no other way".
9. la dije yo (sentence 17): the feminine pronoun la refers back to the phrase, which is feminine. Example: La dije sin pensar, "I said it without thinking".
10. pongamos el jueves (sentence 20): poner in the first-person plural subjunctive proposes a time, let's say Thursday. Example: Pongamos a las seis, "let's say at six".
11. Pues ya nos vemos (sentence 21): pues opens a closing remark, and ya nos vemos means see you, said without a date. Example: Pues ya nos vemos, "well, see you then".
12. le conté (sentence 22): the preterite, because the telling happened and is over. Example: Le conté lo que pasó, "I told him what happened".
13. Nuria me escribió (sentence 24): the preterite for a message sent after the reading, which ends the sequence. Example: Me escribió ayer, "she wrote to me yesterday".

notes.en:
1. Son casi las tres de la madrugada (sentence 1): madrugada is the hours between midnight and dawn, so three in the morning is las tres de la madrugada. The whole story is told in the present (sale, se ven, se sienta, se abre) and stays there to the last line. The past comes only in a background clause, durante treinta años (sentence 11), or in a speaker's words, as yo trabajé (sentence 17). Example: Son casi las cinco de la madrugada, "it is nearly five in the morning".
2. el búho, el último autobús de la noche (sentence 1): búho is an owl; in Madrid the night buses are called búhos, and the title comes from them.
3. Por la ventanilla se ven la diosa de la fuente (sentence 2): the impersonal se with a plural subject. The things seen (the goddess and the palace) are the subject, so the verb is plural. Example: Desde la terraza se ven las estrellas, "from the terrace the stars can be seen".
4. la diosa de la fuente, con su carro tirado por leones (sentence 2): the fountain of Cibeles, at the head of the Paseo del Prado, shows the goddess Cybele on a chariot drawn by two lions. Madrileños say simply Cibeles.
5. el palacio de Correos (sentence 2): the building was first the central post office. Its official name is Palacio de Comunicaciones, but many people still say Correos, and the name is kept here as the speakers would use it.
6. Hay dos pasajeros (sentence 3): haber stays in the singular whatever number follows, so hay goes with two passengers. Example: Había muchas personas en la parada, "there were many people at the stop".
7. ni sube ni baja nadie (sentence 8): ni ... ni wraps the two verbs, and nadie follows them as the subject. Example: Ni llama ni escribe nadie, "nobody calls or writes".
8. Perdone, ¿ha dibujado usted la fuente de memoria? (sentence 14): between two strangers Spanish uses usted, and perdone opens the question; English has only one word for you. The perfect ha dibujado is used for a drawing that is still there to be seen. Example: ¿Ha visto usted mi paraguas?, "have you seen my umbrella?"
9. De memoria, no (sentence 15): de memoria means from memory, and the answer rejects the idea with a plain no. Lo hice desde la ventana de mi padre uses the preterite for an act that is finished. Example: Lo hice ayer, "I did it yesterday".
10. Yo trabajé muchos años enfrente (sentence 17): the preterite for a period that is over, set against Ahora no trabajo (sentence 20), where the present says what is true now. A question about work with dedicarse a takes a noun. Example: Me dedico a la enseñanza, "I work in teaching".
11. se me olvidó comerlo (sentence 22): with olvidar and se me, the person is the indirect object and the thing forgotten is the subject. Here the subject is the infinitive comerlo, so the English is "I forgot to eat it". Example: Se me olvidó la llave, "I forgot the key".
12. ayer por la tarde (sentence 22): the day is over, so the preterite lo compré is used, not the perfect. The perfect needs a time that is still running. Example: Hoy lo he comprado, "I have bought it today".
13. un silbido (sentence 24): silbido is a hiss or whistle, the sound a bus door makes as it opens; a pitido is a beep. Example: un pitido de la máquina, "a beep from the machine".
14. ya empieza a frenar (sentence 29): ya with the present means already, and empezar a with an infinitive means to begin to. Example: Ya empieza a llover, "it is already starting to rain".
15. como si también él estuviera esperando (sentence 28): como si takes the imperfect subjunctive when the comparison is not true. Example: Habla como si lo supiera todo, "he talks as if he knew everything".

notes.en:
1. Don Procopio Ruiz de Tejada (sentence 1): the sketch imitates the costumbrista article, a short prose piece on manners that Mariano José de Larra (1809–1837) and other writers printed in the Madrid press in the 1830s. Larra's "Vuelva usted mañana" (1833) is named here for its place in the genre; this sketch takes none of its story.
2. propietario de tres viñas, dos mulas y una opinión sobre todas las cosas (sentence 1): the appositive counts the uncle's property in one breath, and his opinion is counted with the vines and the mules.
3. el tiempo que tuviera a bien (sentence 3): tener a bien is the formal phrase of a letter, "for as long as you think fit". The uncle reads it as an invitation to stay for weeks, and the sentence shows what he took from it.
4. oficial segundo en una oficina de Hacienda (sentence 4): oficial segundo is a rank in the civil service of the period, and Hacienda is the Treasury. The phrase places Eusebio in an office, not in the upper ranks of a ministry.
5. la levita abotonada hasta el cuello (sentence 6): the levita is a frock coat, the formal dress of the period. Abotonada is a past participle used as an adjective: buttoned.
6. como quien mira una estatua que de pronto empieza a hablar (sentence 7): como quien with the present describes a general way of looking, not one particular look. The verb is in the present because the comparison holds for anyone.
7. lo malos que estaban los caminos reales (sentence 8): lo with an adjective and que is the old way of saying how bad; modern Spanish would say qué malos. The sentence keeps adding its topics one after another, as a speech does; modern prose would break it into three or four.
8. de par en par (sentence 12): the phrase means wide open, and it is used with windows and doors. Par en par is fixed, so it does not change with the noun.
9. El sí de las niñas (sentence 15): a comedy by Leandro Fernández de Moratín, written at the start of the nineteenth century and still named in Spanish theatre histories. It is quoted by its title only.
10. la paciencia de quien espera a que escampe (sentence 19): escampar is said of rain that stops, and esperar a que takes the subjunctive, so the phrase means waiting for the weather to clear.
11. dos reales de más (sentence 21): the real is an old silver coin, used before the peseta. Dos reales is a sum of two of them, and de más means too much.
12. quince días (sentence 22): quince días is the usual Spanish way to say a fortnight, fifteen days. Dos semanas is also said, but quince días is the usual phrase for a stay.
13. no fía nadie (sentence 27): fiar means to give credit, to lend on trust. The note explains the word; the reader takes the rest from the line.

- Mi abuelo se lavaba las manos en la pila del patio antes de sentarse a comer, sin prisa, como si tuviera toda la tarde por delante.
  en: My grandfather washed his hands at the courtyard basin before sitting down to eat, unhurriedly, as if he had the whole afternoon ahead of him.
- Después de comer llegaba la siesta, y la casa entera se quedaba quieta detrás de los postigos cerrados.
  en: After lunch came the siesta, and the whole house stayed still behind the closed shutters.
- En la penumbra solo se oía el zumbido de una mosca contra el cristal de la alcoba.
  en: In the half-light the only sound was the buzz of a fly against the bedroom window.
- Yo no dormía nunca la siesta, pero fingía hacerlo, porque era la única manera de que me dejaran tumbarme en la cama grande.
  en: I never slept the siesta, but I pretended to, because it was the only way they would let me lie down on the big bed.
- Cuando el calor aflojaba, salíamos a la puerta, y cada cual sacaba su silla de enea y la ponía en la acera, a la sombra de la pared.
  en: When the heat let up, we went out to the door, and each of us took out a rush chair and set it on the pavement, in the shade of the wall.
- Allí se tomaba el fresco, se comentaba quién había pasado en coche y quién volvía de la viña con las mulas.
  en: There people sat out in the cool of the evening, discussing who had driven past and who was coming back from the vineyard with the mules.
- Las vecinas hablaban de sus hijos, que vivían en Madrid y en Barcelona, y de lo que costaba el pan, y mi abuelo les contestaba con monosílabos y con el sombrero sobre los ojos.
  en: The neighbours talked about their children, who lived in Madrid and in Barcelona, and about what bread cost, and my grandfather answered them in single words, with his hat over his eyes.
- Yo me sentaba en el suelo, con la espalda contra la pared encalada, y escuchaba sin entender la mitad.
  en: I sat on the ground, my back against the whitewashed wall, and listened without understanding half of it.
- Al atardecer, las golondrinas cruzaban la calle tan bajas que casi rozaban los tejados, y la luz se ponía dorada en la pared de la iglesia.
  en: At dusk the swallows crossed the street so low that they almost brushed the roofs, and the light turned gold on the church wall.
- Entonces la campana daba las horas, y cada golpe tardaba en apagarse sobre los tejados, como si el pueblo entero lo estuviera contando.
  en: Then the bell struck the hours, and each stroke took its time to die away over the roofs, as if the whole village were counting it.
- Nadie en el pueblo miraba el reloj de la muñeca; todos se fiaban del de la iglesia, aunque a veces se equivocaba.
  en: Nobody in the village looked at their wristwatch; everyone trusted the church clock, though it was sometimes wrong.
- Cuando anochecía, mi abuelo encendía una bombilla de cuarenta vatios que alumbraba la mesa del patio y nada más.
  en: When night fell, my grandfather switched on a forty-watt bulb that lit the courtyard table and nothing else.
- Cenábamos tortilla y pan, que siempre se partía con la mano.
  en: We had omelette and bread for supper, and the bread was always broken by hand.
- Después volvíamos a la puerta, a tomar el fresco, y la calle se llenaba de niños que jugaban a la comba hasta que una madre los llamaba por su nombre completo.
  en: Afterwards we went back to the door to sit out, and the street filled with children skipping rope until a mother called them by their full names.
- Una tarde de agosto cayó una tormenta breve; nos metimos todos en el zaguán, y el agua olía a polvo y a tierra mojada.
  en: One August afternoon a brief storm fell; we all went into the entrance hall, and the rain smelt of dust and wet earth.
- Tardé años en entender que aquellas tardes eran lo que más echaba de menos; entonces solo quería que se acabaran para irme a la cama.
  en: It took me years to understand that those afternoons were what I missed most; back then I only wanted them to end so that I could go to bed.
- Hoy, cuando alguien me pregunta por mi infancia, lo primero que me viene a la cabeza es el agua del botijo cayendo por mi barbilla.
  en: Today, when someone asks me about my childhood, the first thing that comes to mind is the water from the botijo running down my chin.
- La casa se vendió hace años, y el patio ya tiene otra puerta, otro color y otra familia.
  en: The house was sold years ago, and the courtyard now has another door, another colour and another family.
- Pero el tañido de la campana, al atardecer, sigue sonando en mi cabeza con la misma calma de entonces.
  en: But the toll of the bell at dusk still rings in my head with the same calm as then.

notes.en:
1. la calle del Pozo (sentence 1): the name means the street of the well, and many Spanish villages have a street with that name.
2. de barro sin esmaltar (sentence 4): the botijo is a jar of unglazed clay. Because the clay is porous, water seeps through the walls and evaporates, and the water inside stays cool.
3. el barro suda por fuera (sentence 5): the grandfather's words are folk talk, but the clay does sweat: the water seeps through and evaporates, and that evaporation is what cools the jar.
4. a morro (sentence 6): drinking straight from the spout of a jar, without a glass. Example: Bebió a morro de la botella, "he drank straight from the bottle".
5. la siesta (sentence 9): the custom and its myths are taught in (Foundation, lesson 82). Here it is a memory of the shutters and the stillness, not a lesson about it.
6. fingía hacerlo (sentence 11): hacerlo stands for dormir la siesta, so the pronoun points back to the sentence before. The imperfect fingía says it was a habit, not one afternoon.
7. de enea (sentence 12): enea is the bulrush or reed mace, a plant whose stalks were woven into rush seats for chairs.
8. tomar el fresco (sentence 13): to sit outside in the cool of the evening, a custom of Spanish summer evenings. Example: Salimos a tomar el fresco, "we went out to sit in the cool of the evening".
9. tardaba en apagarse (sentence 17): tardar en with an infinitive means to take time to do something, here to die away. The subject is cada golpe, so the verb is singular.
10. se fiaban del de la iglesia (sentence 18): fiarse de means to trust, and del de la iglesia is the clock of the church, the word reloj left out after de.
11. Tardé años en entender (sentence 23): tardar en with a period of time, años, says how long it took. Entender is the infinitive that follows.
12. La casa se vendió hace años (sentence 25): the preterite after hace años, because the sale is over. A perfect would need a time that is still running.

ex1:
- Cuando el abuelo hablaba de la guerra, cambiaba de tema antes de llegar al final, como quien cierra una puerta sin hacer ruido.
  en: When grandfather spoke of the war, he changed the subject before he reached the end, like someone closing a door without making a sound.
- Recuerdo que la casa olía a cal y a leña, aunque nunca supe de dónde venía el olor.
  en: I remember that the house smelt of lime and firewood, though I never found out where the smell came from.
- Si hubiéramos vuelto al pueblo en agosto, habríamos encontrado a los abuelos sentados en la puerta.
  en: If we had gone back to the village in August, we would have found the grandparents sitting at the door.
- Ojalá pudiera volver una tarde de julio y sentarme otra vez en el suelo, contra la pared encalada.
  en: I wish I could go back to one July afternoon and sit on the floor again, against the whitewashed wall.
- Todavía hoy, cuando el calor aprieta, me acuerdo de la sombra de aquel patio y me quedo quieto un rato.
  en: Even today, when the heat is intense, I remember the shade of that courtyard and stay still for a while.

  en: He pretended to be asleep so that they would not scold him.

tasks:
- La cocina de mi madre era pequeña, y tenía una ventana que daba a un patio con una parra. Por la mañana entraba una luz blanca, y el aire olía a café recién hecho y a aceite caliente. Mi madre cantaba mientras freía, siempre la misma canción, y nunca supe si se acordaba de la letra. Los domingos, la mesa se alargaba con una tabla que guardábamos detrás de la despensa, y entonces la cocina parecía más grande de lo que era. Cuando vendimos la casa me llevé la cuchara de madera con la que removía el arroz, y todavía la guardo en el cajón de la mía. Cuando la saco, el olor vuelve, aunque sea muy débil.
  task.en: Write in Spanish, about 150 words, a memoir of a place you remember well: a house, a street or a room. Use the imperfect for the background and the preterite for the events, and end on an image, not on a conclusion.
  rubric.en: meaning: the text describes one place from childhood with its sounds, smells and light; register: first person, warm and plain; structure: a general picture first, then one or two events, then an image; idiom: tomar el fresco, a morro or hace años are used only where they fit; effect: the ending is an image, so the reader feels the place without being told what it meant.

== 25 C1
numeral: Lección veinticinco
title: Seis cuentos de una frase
title.en: Six stories of one sentence

dialogue:
- Un microrrelato cabe en una frase, y a veces en una sola palabra que el lector tiene que terminar.
  en: A microrrelato fits into one sentence, and sometimes into a single word that the reader has to finish.
- Unos lo llaman minicuento y otros prefieren microrrelato, y los dos nombres sirven para lo mismo.
  en: Some call it a minicuento and others prefer microrrelato, and both names serve for the same thing.
- No es un cuento corto, sino un género con sus propias reglas, y en él lo que no se cuenta pesa más que lo que se cuenta.
  en: It is not a short story, but a genre with its own rules, and in it what is not told weighs more than what is told.
- En español la tradición es larga, y tiene una referencia que casi todos conocen: Augusto Monterroso (1921–2003), cuyo «El dinosaurio» es probablemente la más famosa de las historias de una sola frase en la lengua.
  en: In Spanish the tradition is long, and it has a reference that almost everyone knows: Augusto Monterroso (1921–2003), whose «El dinosaurio» is probably the most famous one-sentence story in the language.
- Lo que hace el género es sugerir: el personaje, el tiempo y la causa se quedan fuera, y el lector los pone.
  en: What the genre does is suggest: the character, the time and the cause are left out, and the reader supplies them.
- Una buena última frase no explica nada; deja una puerta abierta que el lector cierra cuando quiere.
  en: A good last sentence explains nothing; it leaves a door open which the reader closes when they like.
- A continuación hay seis, cada una en una sola frase y sin título, porque aquí la frase es el título.
  en: Below there are six, each in a single sentence and without a title, because here the sentence is the title.
- Durante dieciocho años esperó una carta de su hijo, y cuando por fin llegó la leyó dos veces en la cocina y la guardó en el cajón, sin contestarla.
  en: For eighteen years she waited for a letter from her son, and when at last it came she read it twice in the kitchen and put it in the drawer, without answering it.
- Mi madre ya no sabe cocinar, pero cada domingo me pregunta si he comido, y yo le contesto que sí, para que ella sienta que todavía cuida de alguien.
  en: My mother no longer knows how to cook, but every Sunday she asks me whether I have eaten, and I tell her yes, so that she feels she is still looking after someone.
- Desde que murió su marido, dejaba la luz del pasillo encendida para que él encontrara el camino de vuelta, y cada mañana la apagaba sin decírselo a nadie.
  en: Since her husband died, she left the hall light on so that he could find his way back, and every morning she switched it off without telling anyone.
- El viajero preguntó en la estación cuánto tardaba el tren hasta su pueblo, y el empleado, con una sonrisa, le contestó que ya había llegado.
  en: At the station the traveller asked how long the train took to his village, and the clerk, with a smile, told him that he had already arrived.
- Ella no volvió a llamarle, pero cada año, el día de su cumpleaños, le llegaba una postal sin firma con una sola frase: «Todavía no es tarde».
  en: She never called him again, but every year, on his birthday, a postcard arrived without a signature, with a single sentence: «It is not too late yet».
- En su última carta le pidió perdón por tres cosas, y solo escribió dos, porque la tercera era la única que ella no le había perdonado.
  en: In his last letter he asked her to forgive him for three things, and wrote only two, because the third was the only one she had not forgiven him for.
- Ninguna de estas frases dice por qué, ni cuándo, ni quién habla, y sin embargo cada una deja al lector con una pregunta concreta.
  en: None of these sentences says why, when, or who is speaking, and yet each one leaves the reader with a precise question.
- Cada una tiene su propio clima: la primera es lenta, la tercera es casi tierna y la cuarta es seca.
  en: Each one has its own mood: the first is slow, the third is almost tender and the fourth is dry.
- En la primera, los dieciocho años se entienden en un solo gesto: leer la carta dos veces, y guardarla sin contestar.
  en: In the first, the eighteen years are understood through a single gesture: reading the letter twice, and putting it away without an answer.
- En la segunda, el verbo cuidar dice lo que el texto calla, porque la madre ya no cocina, pero no ha dejado de cuidar.
  en: In the second, the verb cuidar says what the text leaves unsaid, because the mother no longer cooks, but she has not stopped looking after people.
- En la tercera, la luz encendida hace el trabajo que una descripción haría peor, y basta con la palabra apagaba.
  en: In the third, the light left on does the work that a description would do worse, and the word apagaba is enough.
- En la cuarta, una sola palabra cambia el sentido de todo, y el empleado la pronuncia una vez, sin subrayarla.
  en: In the fourth, a single word changes the meaning of everything, and the clerk says it once, without emphasis.
- En la quinta hay una postal, una frase y una firma que falta, y eso basta para que el lector sepa algo de los dos.
  en: In the fifth there is a postcard, a sentence and a missing signature, and that is enough for the reader to know something about both of them.
- La sexta, la del perdón, es la más difícil de escribir, porque hay que contar la tercera cosa sin nombrarla.
  en: The sixth, the one about forgiveness, is the hardest to write, because the third thing has to be told without being named.
- Escribir uno de estos textos cuesta más de lo que parece, porque hay que decidir qué se calla y confiar en que el lector lo encuentre solo.
  en: Writing one of these texts costs more than it seems, because you have to decide what to leave out and trust the reader to find it alone.
- Conviene leerla en voz alta antes de darla por buena, porque una frase que no suena bien tampoco se entiende en silencio.
  en: It is worth reading it aloud before calling it finished, because a sentence that does not sound right does not make sense in silence either.
- Nadie se queda con el número de palabras; el lector recuerda la impresión que le deja el final.
  en: Nobody remembers the number of words; the reader remembers the impression the ending leaves.
- Por eso el género se aprende leyendo mucho y corrigiendo más, con una regla sencilla: cada palabra tiene que trabajar.
  en: That is why the genre is learnt by reading a great deal and correcting even more, with one simple rule: every word has to work.

notes.en:
1. microrrelato (sentence 1): the Spanish word for the very short story, which the next sentence compares with minicuento. Both are current; neither is more correct.
2. «El dinosaurio» (sentence 4): a story by Augusto Monterroso (1921–2003), named here by its title only. It is one of the best-known stories in Spanish.
3. cabe en una frase (sentence 1): caber is irregular in the first person (quepo), and cabe is the third-person form: it fits. The sentence says the story fits into a single sentence, and sometimes a single word.
4. pesa más (sentence 3): pesar used figuratively means to matter, to count for more. Here it is the unstated part of a story that counts most.
5. Durante dieciocho años esperó (sentence 8): the preterite for a period that is over, and for the events that follow it. Esperaba would describe a habit; esperó says the waiting ended.
6. sin contestarla (sentence 8): sin with an infinitive means without doing something. The pronoun la points back to the letter, which is feminine.
7. le contesto que sí (sentence 9): contestar takes the person as indirect object (le) and a clause after que. Sí answers the question with a plain yes.
8. dejaba la luz del pasillo encendida (sentence 10): the imperfect for a habit, dejaba, and the adjective encendida, which agrees with la luz. Desde que murió uses the preterite for a finished event.
9. para que él encontrara (sentence 10): para que takes the subjunctive, here the imperfect, because the purpose is in the past.
10. ya había llegado (sentence 11): the pluperfect, because the arrival comes before the question. Ya here means already.
11. le llegaba una postal sin firma (sentence 12): llegar with the person as indirect object (le) means a letter or card reached someone. Sin firma is without a signature.
12. «Todavía no es tarde» (sentence 12): the phrase is said as a sign of hope; it stays in Spanish in the quotation, with its punctuation.
13. le había perdonado (sentence 13): the pluperfect for an earlier fact: she had not forgiven him before the letter. Pedir perdón takes por before the thing forgiven.
14. cuidar dice lo que el texto calla (sentence 17): cuidar means to look after or to take care of; the sentence sets it against callar, to leave unsaid.
15. basta con la palabra apagaba (sentence 18): bastar con means to be enough with. The verb apagaba, in the imperfect, is the habit of switching the light off.

ex1:
- No te preocupes por el tren: si pierdes este, siempre queda el de las nueve, aunque tardes más en llegar.
  en: Do not worry about the train: if you miss this one, there is always the nine o'clock, even if you take longer to arrive.
- Le escribí tres veces, y las tres cartas siguen en el cajón, sin abrir.
  en: I wrote to him three times, and the three letters are still in the drawer, unopened.
- Hija, si me hubieras preguntado antes, te habría dicho que sí, pero ya era tarde para contestar.
  en: Daughter, if you had asked me earlier, I would have told you yes, but it was already too late to answer.
- Nadie sabe por qué el abuelo guardaba las llaves de todas las casas del pueblo, pero nunca se las pidió nadie.
  en: Nobody knows why grandfather kept the keys to every house in the village, but nobody ever asked him for them.
- Por mucho que lo intentara, la frase no le salía: siempre le sobraba una palabra.
  en: However hard she tried, the sentence would not come out right: there was always one word too many.

  en: The telegram arrived too late.

  en: Now I no longer expect anything from him.
