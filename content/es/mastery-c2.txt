numeral: Lección veintiséis
title: Domingo por la tarde
title.en: A Sunday afternoon

dialogue:
- Las campanas de la catedral llamaban a misa mayor, y doña Matilde, que las oía desde la cama como se oye la lluvia, decidió que aquel domingo sería igual que los demás.
  en: The cathedral bells were calling to the High Mass, and Doña Matilde, who heard them from her bed the way one hears rain, decided that this Sunday would be like the others.
- Igual que los demás: la misa de once, el sermón del chantre sobre la paciencia, el paseo hasta el río y la tarde larga que empezaba después de comer.
  en: Like the others: the eleven o'clock Mass, the canon's sermon on patience, the walk down to the river and the long afternoon that began after lunch.
- Don Ernesto ya se había ido al casino con el periódico de Madrid bajo el brazo, y la casa quedaba para ella, con sus cortinas de damasco y el ruido de la criada en la cocina.
  en: Don Ernesto had already gone to the club with the Madrid newspaper under his arm, and the house was left to her, with its damask curtains, its clocks and the noise of the maid in the kitchen.
- ¡Qué aburrimiento, Dios mío! No era que no quisiera a su marido; lo quería, a su manera, como se quiere un mueble bien conservado.
  en: What boredom, good God! It was not that she did not love her husband; she loved him, in her way, as one loves a well-kept piece of furniture.
- Se sentó junto a la ventana del salón y miró la calle, donde no pasaba nada, salvo un perro que buscaba sombra y un muchacho que vendía periódicos atrasados.
  en: She sat down by the sitting-room window and looked at the street, where nothing was happening, except a dog looking for shade and a boy selling old newspapers.
- Pensó en las cartas de su prima Adela, que vivía en Madrid y escribía cada mes sobre teatros, modas y bailes de la Corte, con una alegría que a Matilde le parecía casi una falta de respeto.
  en: She thought of the letters from her cousin Adela, who lived in Madrid and wrote every month about theatres, fashions and balls at Court, with a gaiety that struck Matilde as almost a lack of respect.
- Adela decía que en Madrid se podía hablar sin que lo oyera toda la ciudad, y eso, para doña Matilde, era lo más parecido a la libertad que había imaginado nunca.
  en: Adela said that in Madrid one could speak without the whole city hearing, and for Doña Matilde that was the closest thing to freedom she had ever imagined.
- Hacía tres meses que no llegaba carta. Quizá el correo se había retrasado con las lluvias, o quizá Adela se había cansado de escribirle a una mujer que nunca contestaba nada interesante.
  en: It had been three months since a letter came. Perhaps the post had been held up by the rains, or perhaps Adela had tired of writing to a woman who never answered with anything interesting.
- Se levantó, fue al escritorio y sacó el papel de carta azul pálido, el que reservaba para las ocasiones, y lo dejó sobre la mesa sin escribir nada.
  en: She got up, went to the desk and took out the pale blue writing paper she kept for special occasions, and left it on the table without writing anything.
- ¿Qué contar? ¿Que había ido a misa, que el chantre había predicado sobre la paciencia, que la criada había quemado el puchero?
  en: What was there to tell? That she had been to Mass, that the canon had preached on patience, that the maid had burnt the stew pot?
- Lo del puchero era, al menos, una novedad, y doña Matilde se permitió sonreír ante la idea de que aquello fuera el acontecimiento de la semana.
  en: The stew pot was at least something new, and Doña Matilde allowed herself a smile at the thought that it might be the event of the week.
- Afuera, el sol se había escondido detrás de una nube gris que venía del lado de la sierra, y el aire olía a tierra mojada desde la lluvia de la mañana.
  en: Outside, the sun had hidden behind a grey cloud coming from the direction of the mountains, and the air smelt of wet earth since the morning's rain.
- Pensó que tal vez el paseo fuera imposible, y sintió una pequeña decepción que no supo explicarse.
  en: She thought that perhaps the walk would be impossible, and felt a small disappointment she could not account for.
- En la calle, un coche de caballos pasó despacio, con las cortinillas echadas, y doña Matilde se preguntó quién iría dentro, y adónde, y si alguien pensaría en ella con la misma curiosidad.
  en: In the street a carriage went slowly past, with its blinds drawn, and Doña Matilde wondered who was inside, and where to, and whether anyone thought about her with the same curiosity.
- Nunca sabía nada de nadie, y eso era lo que más le pesaba.
  en: She never knew anything about anyone, and that was what weighed on her most.
- Las campanas volvieron a sonar, esta vez para las vísperas, y su eco se quedó un rato colgado sobre los tejados como una pregunta sin respuesta.
  en: The bells rang again, this time for vespers, and their echo hung for a while over the roofs like a question without an answer.
- Doña Matilde contó las campanadas sin proponérselo, y al llegar a seis se dio cuenta de que el día había pasado sin que ella hiciera nada, como pasaban los demás.
  en: Doña Matilde counted the strokes without meaning to, and on reaching six she realised that the day had gone by without her doing anything, as the other days did.
- Se acordó de un baile del casino, de un joven ingeniero que hablaba de los ferrocarriles como otros hablan de Dios, y que la miró una sola vez, durante una polca.
  en: She remembered a ball at the club, a young engineer who talked about railways the way others talk about God, and who looked at her only once, during a polka.
- Se llamaba Ángel, o eso creía recordar, y se había marchado a Bilbao, o a Cuba, o a ninguna parte.
  en: His name was Ángel, or so she believed she remembered, and he had gone to Bilbao, or to Cuba, or to nowhere in particular.
- Ya nunca sabría si la había querido de verdad, y esa duda era lo único bonito que le quedaba de aquel verano.
  en: She would never know whether he had really loved her, and this doubt was the only pleasant thing left to her from that summer.
- «Doña Matilde, ¿no sale usted?», le preguntó la criada desde la puerta, con el delantal todavía manchado de harina.
  en: «Doña Matilde, aren't you going out?» the maid asked from the door, her apron still floury.
- «No, Pascuala. Hoy no.»
  en: «No, Pascuala. Not today.»
- «Pues el señor ha dejado recado de que vendrá tarde.»
  en: «Well, the master left word that he will be late.»
- Doña Matilde asintió sin preguntar más, porque la noticia le daba igual: don Ernesto siempre venía tarde los domingos, y ella siempre fingía que no lo esperaba.
  en: Doña Matilde nodded without asking anything more, since the news made no difference to her: Don Ernesto always came home late on Sundays, and she always pretended not to be waiting for him.
- Se levantó, se acercó al espejo del pasillo y se quitó el prendedor de azabache, que le apretaba como una promesa que nadie le había hecho.
  en: She got up, went to the hall mirror and took off the jet brooch, which pressed on her like a promise nobody had made.
- Tal vez mañana llegara la carta de Madrid, con su letra apretada y sus noticias de teatros; tal vez Adela contara algo que valiera la pena contestar.
  en: Perhaps tomorrow the letter from Madrid would come, with its cramped handwriting and its news of the theatres; perhaps Adela would say something worth answering.
- Mientras tanto, el sol volvió a salir un instante por detrás de la nube, y la luz se posó sobre el papel azul de la mesa, como si alguien lo hubiera escrito ya.
  en: Meanwhile, the sun came out for a moment from behind the cloud, and the light settled on the blue paper on the table, as if someone had already written on it.

notes.en:
1. Las campanas de la catedral (sentence 1): the city is not named, as the narrator does not need it. The bells call to the misa mayor, the main Mass of the Sunday, and they ring again for vespers later in the afternoon.
2. la misa de once (sentence 2): the eleven o'clock Mass, a regular Sunday time in a Spanish town; the list gives the routine she wishes were different.
3. el casino (sentence 3): the club where the men of a provincial town spent their afternoons, reading the papers and playing cards; it was for men. Don Ernesto goes there while his wife stays at home.
4. ¡Qué aburrimiento, Dios mío! (sentence 4): the exclamation belongs to Doña Matilde, but it is not in quotation marks; this is free indirect style, the narrator's sentence taking on the woman's own voice. Clarín's La Regenta (1884–1885) is the best-known example in Spanish; this text borrows its manner only.
5. como se quiere un mueble bien conservado (sentence 4): se with the third person is impersonal here: one loves a piece of furniture the way one looks after it. The comparison is her joke at her own marriage, and the narrator lets it stand.
6. Hacía tres meses que no llegaba carta (sentence 8): hacía with the imperfect says how long a state had lasted up to the moment described. The sentence is about the past, so the imperfect is right.
7. ¿Qué contar? (sentence 10): the questions are her own thoughts, with no speaker tag, and they are answered by her own list of trivia.
8. Tal vez el paseo fuera imposible (sentence 13): tal vez takes the subjunctive when the possibility is in doubt; fuera is the imperfect subjunctive because the sentence is about the past.
9. sin proponérselo (sentence 17): without meaning to. The pronoun se is attached to the infinitive proponer and points back to Doña Matilde, the one who counts.
10. como una promesa que nadie le había hecho (sentence 25): the pluperfect for an earlier event, the promise that nobody had made; the simile sits in her own perception of the brooch.
11. fingía que no lo esperaba (sentence 24): fingir with que and the indicative; the imperfect says it was a habit of every Sunday.
12. como si alguien lo hubiera escrito ya (sentence 27): como si takes the pluperfect subjunctive for a past that did not happen; the image closes on the blue paper that is still blank.

title: El abuelo y la final del 64
title.en: Grandad and the 1964 final

dialogue:
- Elena: Abuelo, cuéntanos otra vez lo de la final del 64, que hoy se ha acordado papá.
  en: Grandad, tell us again about the 1964 final, since Dad brought it up today.
- Julián: ¡Que me acuerdo perfectamente! Fue en junio, en el Bernabéu, con el estadio lleno hasta la última grada.
  en: I remember it perfectly! It was in June, at the Bernabéu, with the stadium full up to the top tier.
- Javier: Ya empieza.
  en: Here we go.
- Julián: Enfrente estaba la Unión Soviética, que venía con un equipo de acero, y ganamos tres a uno.
  en: Facing us was the Soviet Union, which had come with a team of steel, and we won three–one.
- Pilar: (Dos a uno, Julián.)
  en: (Two–one, Julián.)
- Julián: Tres a uno, Pilar, que lo vi con mis ojos.
  en: Three–one, Pilar, I saw it with my own eyes.
- Javier: (Dos a uno, papá. Lo pone en todas las crónicas.)
  en: (Two–one, Dad. It is in every report.)
- Julián: Vale, dos. Y el gol de la victoria lo metió un extremo con la pierna izquierda, desde fuera del área.
  en: Fine, two. And the winning goal was scored by a winger with his left foot, from outside the box.
- Elena: (De cabeza, abuelo. Fue Marcelino.)
  en: (A header, Grandad. It was Marcelino.)
- Julián: Hija, que yo estaba allí y tú no habías nacido.
  en: Girl, I was there and you had not been born.
- Elena: (Ni yo ni papá habíamos nacido, abuelo, y lo sabemos todos.)
  en: (Neither I nor Dad had been born, Grandad, and we all know it.)
- Julián: Pues por eso lo cuento yo.
  en: Then that is why I am the one telling it.
- Pilar: (Tú no estabas en la grada, Julián.)
  en: (You were not in the stand, Julián.)
- Julián: ¿Cómo que no? Si tengo la entrada guardada en el cajón.
  en: What do you mean, not? I have the ticket kept in the drawer.
- Pilar: (Esa entrada es la de tu cuñado. Y tú estabas en el bar de enfrente, con un vaso de cerveza, que lo sabe todo el barrio.)
  en: (That ticket is your brother-in-law's. And you were in the bar opposite, with a glass of beer, as the whole neighbourhood knows.)
- Julián: Entré un rato al bar, eso sí, pero solo para hacer tiempo.
  en: I did go into the bar for a while, I admit it, but only to pass the time.
- Javier: (Un rato de hora y media, papá, que el partido dura lo que dura.)
  en: (An hour and a half, Dad, and the match lasts as long as it lasts.)
- Pilar: (Y luego te dieron una tapa de jamón para que te callaras.)
  en: (And then they gave you a slice of ham to keep you quiet.)
- Julián: Eso fue después del gol, Pilar, no cuenta.
  en: That was after the goal, Pilar, it does not count.
- Elena: Abuelo, ¿y dónde estabas cuando marcó Marcelino?
  en: Grandad, where were you when Marcelino scored?
- Julián: Arriba, en la grada, te lo he dicho. Lo vi todo desde allí.
  en: Up in the stand, I told you. I saw all of it from up there.
- Elena: Pues cuéntanos qué vio, abuelo, que yo no he visto ni un partido entero.
  en: Then tell us what you saw, Grandad, because I have never watched a whole match.
- Pilar: (Desde la barra, con la ventana llena de anuncios de cerveza.)
  en: (From the bar counter, with the window full of beer adverts.)
- Julián: Eso no es verdad, Pilar. Los anuncios los pusieron después.
  en: That is not true, Pilar. They put the adverts up later.
- Javier: Mamá, déjalo, que ahora sale lo de la radio.
  en: Mum, leave him alone, now he'll bring up the radio.
- Julián: La radio dijo el gol cuando la gente ya lo había gritado, pero el estadio gritó antes que la radio.
  en: The radio announced the goal after the crowd had already shouted it, but the stadium roared before the radio did.
- Pilar: (El estadio no grita, Julián. Grita la gente.)
  en: (The stadium does not shout, Julián. The people do.)
- Elena: Entonces, ¿estabas en el bar o en el estadio?
  en: So were you in the bar or in the stadium?
- Julián: Estaba en el Bernabéu, en el bar de enfrente, y oí el gol antes que nadie del bar.
  en: I was at the Bernabéu, in the bar opposite, and I heard the goal before anyone in the bar did.

notes.en:
1. Julián (sentence 2): the grandfather is the one telling the story at the table, and his family answers him in round brackets; the brackets are the family's interruptions, not the narration.
2. el Bernabéu (sentence 2): the Santiago Bernabéu, the stadium of Real Madrid, named after the club's former president. The final of the European Nations' Cup was played there in June 1964.
3. Unión Soviética (sentence 4): the country is named with its article, la Unión Soviética, as Spanish does; it is the name for the opposing team in the story.
4. tres a uno (sentence 4): a score is read with a: dos a uno, "two–one". Julián says three; the family's corrections give the true score, two goals to one in Spain's favour.
5. Vale, dos (sentence 8): vale is the common way of giving in; dos is the correction he accepts, and the reader sees him change his figures in front of his family.
6. De cabeza (sentence 9): a header, with the head. The phrase is said of a goal scored with the head, and it is set against the winger's left foot that Julián gave before.
7. no habías nacido (sentence 10): the pluperfect for a state before 1964. Habías is the tú form, because the grandfather speaks to his granddaughter.
8. Pues por eso lo cuento yo (sentence 12): por eso points to the line before it; the grandfather's claim is that the fact of being older gives the right to tell the story.
9. Entré un rato al bar (sentence 16): entrar al bar means going in for a drink; the grandfather admits the bar, but calls it a short visit.
10. hacer tiempo (sentence 16): to pass the time while waiting, a fixed phrase. The phrase is the one the grandfather uses for his own excuse.
11. lo que dura (sentence 17): lo que dura means for as long as it lasts, a fixed phrase that Javier turns against his father.
12. tapa de jamón (sentence 18): a small plate of food served with a drink in a bar; here it is the reason the grandfather stayed.
13. antes que la radio (sentence 26): antes que takes the indicative when it compares two events that have happened, here the roar of the stadium and the radio.
14. antes que nadie del bar (sentence 29): antes que nadie with the indicative, the claim that closes the story. Nadie is the subject of the comparison, and del bar says whose ears are meant.

|---|---|---|
| present, for a scene as it is seen | the reader watches each move | Ella se sienta junto a la puerta, con una carpeta grande de dibujos |
| preterite, for a period that is over | a life that has ended | Yo trabajé muchos años enfrente |
| como si with the imperfect subjunctive | a comparison that is not true | como si también él estuviera esperando |
2. Habit, finished time and sound (lesson 24): the imperfect keeps a routine going, the preterite with hace años closes a past event, and solo se oía keeps a quiet scene quiet.
| Form | What it does | Line from lesson 24 |
|---|---|---|
| imperfect, for a habit | the same thing every day | Yo no dormía nunca la siesta |
| preterite with hace años | the event is over | La casa se vendió hace años |
| solo se oía | the single sound in a still room | En la penumbra solo se oía el zumbido de una mosca |
3. What the text leaves unsaid (lesson 25): a sentence can hand the reader a question instead of an answer, and one verb can carry what the text does not say.
| Device | What it does | Line from lesson 25 |
|---|---|---|
| a question left open | the reader supplies the reason | Ninguna de estas frases dice por qué, ni cuándo, ni quién habla |
| a verb with more in it than its meaning | the story is told by the verb | pero no ha dejado de cuidar |
4. Free indirect style (lesson 26): the narrator's sentence takes on the voice of the person, with no quotation marks.
| Device | What it does | Line from lesson 26 |
|---|---|---|
| a question in the narration | her own thought, with no speaker | ¿Qué contar? |
| an exclamation without quotation marks | her own voice in the narrator's sentence | ¡Qué aburrimiento, Dios mío! |
| tal vez with the subjunctive | a doubt she does not settle | tal vez el paseo fuera imposible |
5. The narrator who cannot be trusted (lesson 27): the family's corrections in round brackets work against the speaker, and a claim that shrinks is the best sign of it.
| Device | What it does | Line from lesson 27 |
|---|---|---|
| a correction in round brackets | the listener's record against the speaker | (Dos a uno, Julián.) |
| an admission that shrinks the claim | the claim gives way on one point | Entré un rato al bar, eso sí, pero solo para hacer tiempo. |

== 29 C2
numeral: Lección veintinueve
title: Morir, fallecer, palmarla
title.en: Dying, passing away, kicking the bucket

dialogue:
- Pocos verbos del español tienen tantos sinónimos como el que significa dejar de vivir, y casi ninguno quiere decir exactamente lo mismo.
  en: Few Spanish verbs have as many synonyms as the one that means to stop living, and almost none of them means exactly the same thing.
- El más sencillo es morir, que sirve para todo: para las personas, los animales, las plantas y hasta las estrellas.
  en: The plainest is morir, which serves for everything: people, animals, plants and even stars.
- Es el verbo de los diccionarios, de los hospitales y de las noticias, y no necesita nada más.
  en: It is the word of dictionaries, hospitals and the news, and it needs nothing more.
- Fallecer es más formal. Aparece sobre todo en las esquelas, en los comunicados del ayuntamiento y en los textos oficiales: «Doña Teresa Lorenzo Vega ha fallecido esta madrugada, a los ochenta y dos años».
  en: Fallecer is more formal. It appears above all in death notices, in town council announcements and in official texts: «Doña Teresa Lorenzo Vega passed away early this morning, aged eighty-two».
- Su tono es sobrio, casi administrativo, y por eso el lector lo acepta sin pensar en el dolor que hay detrás.
  en: Its tone is sober, almost administrative, and that is why the reader accepts it without thinking of the grief behind it.
- Perecer tiene otra historia. Se usa sobre todo para las muertes colectivas o violentas: en un accidente, en un incendio, en una catástrofe.
  en: Perecer has a different history. It is used above all for deaths in groups or by violence: in an accident, in a fire, in a disaster.
- «Perecieron en el incendio» suena a crónica de periódico; «Perecí de un catarro» sería una broma, y una broma muy mala.
  en: «They perished in the fire» sounds like a newspaper report; «I perished of a cold» would be a joke, and a very bad one.
- Fenecer es todavía más raro. Es un verbo antiguo de la lengua escrita, que aparece en la literatura y en algunos textos formales.
  en: Fenecer is rarer still. It is an old verb of the written language, found in literature and in some formal texts.
- En la conversación casi nunca se oye, y en los textos jurídicos se dice también de un plazo que se acaba.
  en: In conversation it is almost never heard, and in legal texts it is also said of a period of time that comes to an end.
- Irse es el más neutro de los verbos que hablan de la marcha y no de la muerte: «Se fue ayer por la tarde», dicen las vecinas, y todos entienden.
  en: Irse is the most neutral of the verbs that speak of leaving rather than of death: «She went yesterday afternoon», the neighbours say, and everyone understands.
- Dejarnos pone el dolor en quien se queda: el que se ha ido nos ha dejado a nosotros, y no al revés.
  en: Dejarnos puts the grief on the one who stays: the one who has gone has left us, and not the other way round.
- Pasar a mejor vida es una fórmula de consuelo, la que se dice en el velatorio, con un tono casi de alivio.
  en: Pasar a mejor vida is a formula of comfort, the one said at the wake, in a tone that is almost relieved.
- Su problema es que a veces suena a chiste, y en un entierro hay que cuidar el tono.
  en: Its problem is that it sometimes sounds like a joke, and at a burial the tone has to be watched.
- Estirar la pata es coloquial y familiar. Se usa sin dramatismo, a veces con cariño y a veces con una pizca de sorna.
  en: Estirar la pata is colloquial and familiar. It is used without drama, sometimes affectionately and sometimes with a touch of mockery.
- Irse al otro barrio pertenece al mismo registro, y convierte la muerte en una mudanza a un barrio lejano.
  en: Irse al otro barrio belongs to the same register, and turns death into a move to a distant district.
- Palmarla es más áspero. Viene de palmar, que en la lengua coloquial significa morir, y se dice entre amigos, casi siempre en broma: «Como sigas así, la palmamos los dos».
  en: Palmarla is harsher. It comes from palmar, which in colloquial speech means to die, and it is said between friends, nearly always as a joke: «If you carry on like that, we'll both kick the bucket».
- Criar malvas es el más irónico de la lista: la imagen es la de malvas creciendo sobre una tumba, y el verbo se dice de quien lleva ya mucho tiempo enterrado.
  en: Criar malvas is the driest of the list: the image is of mallows growing over a grave, and it is said of someone who has been buried for a long time.
- Con tantas palabras hay que saber cuál usar. Un discurso fúnebre pide solemnidad; una charla entre amigos admite humor; un titular de periódico pide frialdad.
  en: With so many words one has to know which to use. A funeral speech asks for solemnity, a chat between friends allows humour, and a newspaper headline asks for coldness.
- Lo que no funciona es mezclarlas: un «ha palmado» en una esquela sería una falta de respeto, y un «ha fallecido» en un brindis de amigos sonaría a discurso del alcalde.
  en: What does not work is mixing them: «he has kicked the bucket» in a death notice would be disrespectful, and «he has passed away» in a friends' toast would sound like a mayor's speech.
- Muchos hablantes usan morir sin más, y no por pobreza de vocabulario: es el verbo que se entiende a la primera.
  en: Many speakers use morir without more, and not because their vocabulary is poor: it is the verb that is understood at first hearing.
- Un buen ejercicio, si quieres practicar, es leer las esquelas de un periódico y después la crónica de una catástrofe, y fijarte en el verbo de cada una.
  en: A good exercise, if you want to practise, is to read the death notices in a newspaper and then the report of a disaster, and to notice the verb in each.
- En las esquelas, el que muere suele ser una persona con nombre y apellidos; en la crónica, a menudo, son cifras.
  en: In the notices the one who died is usually a person with a name and two surnames; in the report, often, they are figures.
- Con morir, fallecer, perecer y los demás se puede contar casi todo: quién murió, cómo, dónde y con qué tono.
  en: With morir, fallecer, perecer and the rest, you can tell almost everything: who died, how, where, and in what tone.
- Los diccionarios dan el significado; el uso, la intención y el oyente deciden el resto.
  en: Dictionaries give the meaning; use, intention and the listener decide the rest.
- Elegir bien un verbo es, al fin y al cabo, elegir con cuidado a quién se le habla.
  en: Choosing a verb well is, after all, choosing carefully whom one is speaking to.

notes.en:
1. morir (sentence 2): the plainest verb of dying, and the one that covers every kind of living thing. Example: Los árboles murieron con la sequía, "the trees died in the drought". English die has the same range, so the choice in this lesson lies in the other verbs.
2. Fallecer (sentence 4): formal, and at home in death notices and official texts. Example: Su marido falleció hace dos años, "her husband died two years ago": the preterite with hace años, because the death is over. "Passed away" is the nearest English, though it sounds a little softer.
3. perecer (sentences 6 and 7): used for deaths in groups, and for deaths by accident or violence, as in a newspaper report. Example: Perecieron los tres ocupantes del coche, "the three people in the car died". Perecí de un catarro shows what happens when the verb is too grand for the event.
4. fenecer (sentences 8 and 9): an old verb of the written language, rare in speech. In legal texts a period of time can fenecer, as in El plazo fenece el lunes, "the deadline ends on Monday".
5. irse (sentence 10): the most neutral of the verbs that speak of leaving rather than dying. The neighbours use it, and the context does the rest. Example: Se fue sin decir adiós, "she left without saying goodbye": the same verb can be literal, and the sense of death depends on the situation.
6. dejarnos (sentence 11): the dead person is the subject, but the speakers are the ones left behind. Example: Nos ha dejado esta madrugada, "he has left us early this morning": the perfect, because today is not over.
7. pasar a mejor vida (sentence 12): a formula of consolation said at a wake, and the nearest English is "passed on to a better life". Example: Mi tía pasó a mejor vida hace años, "my aunt passed on to a better life years ago".
8. estirar la pata (sentence 14): literally "to stretch the leg", and the sense is to die. It is colloquial and belongs to speech; in writing it turns up mainly in dialogue or in a joking tone.
9. irse al otro barrio (sentence 15): literally "to go to the other neighbourhood". The image turns death into a move. Example: Se fue al otro barrio en primavera, "he went to the other neighbourhood in spring".
10. palmarla (sentences 16 and 19): colloquial, from palmar, which means to die in familiar speech. It is for jokes between friends and out of place in a notice. Example: Casi la palmo en la carretera, "I nearly kicked the bucket on the road".
11. criar malvas (sentence 17): literally "to grow mallows", and the sense is to have been dead and buried for some time. The image is of mallows over a grave, and the phrase is heard in Spain as a joke. Example: Mi bisabuelo lleva años criando malvas, "my great-grandfather has been pushing up daisies for years".
12. solemnidad (sentences 18 and 19): the choice follows the occasion. English has the same range: "pass away" for a notice, "kick the bucket" for a joke, but English seldom puts "pass away" into a joke.
13. Muchos hablantes (sentences 20 and 21): a plain verb is not a poor one. It is the verb understood at first hearing, and a news report can use it for the same reason.
14. al fin y al cabo (sentence 25): a fixed phrase meaning in the end, when all is said and done. Here it introduces the lesson's last point, and it is set off by commas.

== 30 C2
numeral: Lección treinta
title: ¿Porque o por qué?
title.en: Porque or por qué?

dialogue:
- Hay palabras que suenan igual y se escriben de cuatro maneras, y esta semana una lectora me ha escrito para preguntarme cuál es cuál.
  en: There are words that sound the same and are written in four ways, and this week a reader wrote to ask me which is which.
- Su carta tiene tres errores, y los tres son muy comunes: «No sé porqué se enfada», «Lo hizo por que no sabía» y «Vino por qué estaba cansado».
  en: Her letter has three mistakes, all of them very common: «I don't know porqué she is cross», «He did it por que he did not know» and «He came por qué he was tired».
- Empecemos por la primera: porque, en una sola palabra, es la conjunción que da la causa. «Lo hizo porque no sabía» responde a la pregunta «¿por qué lo hizo?».
  en: Let us start with the first: porque, in one word, is the conjunction that gives the cause. «He did it because he did not know» answers the question «why did he do it?».
- Por qué, en dos palabras, sirve para preguntar, con tilde en la pregunta directa, o para introducir una oración indirecta, sin tilde: «¿Por qué lo hizo?» y «No sé por qué se enfada».
  en: Por qué, in two words, asks a question, with an accent in a direct question, or introduces an indirect clause, without one: «Why did he do it?» and «I do not know why she is cross».
- El porqué es un sustantivo: lleva artículo, significa la razón, y su plural es los porqués. «Quiero saber el porqué de tanto silencio».
  en: El porqué is a noun: it takes an article, means the reason, and its plural is los porqués. «I want to know the reason for all this silence».
- Por que, separado, va casi siempre con subjuntivo y quiere decir para que: «Luchamos por que nos paguen», o detrás de un nombre, como en «el motivo por que se fue».
  en: Por que, written apart, almost always takes the subjunctive and means so that: «We are fighting for them to pay us», or follows a noun, as in «the reason why he left».
- Porque, ya que, puesto que y dado que introducen una causa, y lo normal es que la causa vaya en indicativo, porque se presenta como un hecho.
  en: Porque, ya que, puesto que and dado que all introduce a cause, and the cause usually takes the indicative, because it is presented as a fact.
- Ya que suena natural en la conversación: «Ya que vienes, ayúdame con las bolsas».
  en: Ya que sounds natural in conversation: «Since you are coming, help me with the bags».
- Puesto que aparece sobre todo en la lengua escrita: «Puesto que la reunión se ha aplazado, no hace falta que vengan todos».
  en: Puesto que is found mostly in writing: «Since the meeting has been put back, not everyone needs to come».
- Dado que es todavía más formal y se usa en informes y documentos: «Dado que el plazo vence el viernes, pedimos respuesta antes».
  en: Dado que is more formal still and is used in reports and documents: «Given that the deadline ends on Friday, we ask for a reply before then».
- Como da una causa sobre todo cuando va al principio de la oración: «Como no contestabas, me fui».
  en: Como gives a cause mostly when it comes at the start of its sentence: «As you did not answer, I left».
- La causa también puede ser un nombre. Debido a, a causa de, gracias a y por culpa de van normalmente seguidas de un sustantivo, no de un verbo conjugado.
  en: The cause can also be a noun. Debido a, a causa de, gracias a and por culpa de are normally followed by a noun, not by a conjugated verb.
- Debido a y a causa de son neutras: «Debido a la lluvia, se suspendió la excursión».
  en: Debido a and a causa de are neutral: «Owing to the rain, the outing was cancelled».
- Gracias a da una causa buena: «Gracias a tu ayuda, llegamos a tiempo».
  en: Gracias a gives a good cause: «Thanks to your help, we arrived on time».
- Por culpa de da una causa mala: «Por culpa de la obra, llegamos tarde al concierto».
  en: Por culpa de gives a bad cause: «Because of the roadworks, we were late for the concert».
- «No sé porqué se enfada» es un error muy frecuente, y se corrige con un solo cambio: no sé por qué se enfada.
  en: «I don't know porqué she is cross» is a very common mistake, and it is put right with one change: I do not know why she is cross.
- Para no equivocarse, pregúntese si puede poner un artículo delante: si puede, es el porqué; si introduce una pregunta, es por qué; si da la causa, es porque.
  en: To avoid mistakes, ask yourself whether an article can go in front: if it can, it is el porqué; if it introduces a question, it is por qué; if it gives the cause, it is porque.
- En «Lo hizo por que no sabía», la palabra da una causa, así que va en una sola palabra: porque.
  en: In «He did it por que he did not know», the word gives a cause, so it is written as one word: porque.
- «Vino por qué estaba cansado» no lleva ninguna pregunta, y también debe ser porque.
  en: «He came por qué he was tired» has no question in it, and it must also be porque.
- Esta semana ha llegado la respuesta de la lectora, y la publico tal cual, porque no tiene ni un solo error.
  en: This week the reader's reply has arrived, and I print it as it is, because it does not have a single mistake.
- «Estimada señora Aguirre: ya que me ha corregido, le contesto con las palabras que me ha enseñado.»
  en: «Dear Mrs Aguirre: since you have corrected me, I reply with the words you have taught me.»
- «No necesitaba el porqué de sus reglas, porque ya las conocía, pero me han servido de algo.»
  en: «I did not need the reason behind your rules, because I already knew them, but they have been of some use.»
- «Me pregunto por qué sus respuestas tienen que ser tan largas.»
  en: «I wonder why your answers have to be so long.»

  en: «Since you write so much, each column takes me a whole Sunday.»

  en: «Given that the column takes up half a page, I read it over two Sundays.»

  en: «As you have corrected me so carefully, I return the favour: your answer is far too long.»

  en: «Owing to the length of your columns, my grandchildren no longer pass me the newspaper.»

  en: «Because of your rules, I have started correcting even the council's signs.»

  en: «Thanks to you, I can now tell porque, por qué and el porqué apart, and the reason why I am writing to you is this.»
- «Por culpa de tanta regla, ya no me atrevo a escribir ni una carta.»
  en: «Because of all these rules, I no longer dare to write so much as a letter.»
- «Como usted enseña que cada palabra cuenta, le pido que la próxima vez sea breve.»
  en: «As you teach that every word counts, I ask you to be brief next time.»
- «Con tres líneas me habría bastado, y lo demás, señora Aguirre, se lo ahorra usted a sus lectores.»
  en: «Three lines would have been enough for me, and the rest, Mrs Aguirre, you can spare your readers.»

notes.en:
1. porque (sentence 3): one word, the conjunction that gives the cause, and the answer to a question with por qué. Example: Llegué tarde porque llovía, "I arrived late because it was raining".
2. Por qué (sentence 4): two words. The qué takes an accent in a direct question (¿Por qué lo hizo?) and none in an indirect clause (no sé por qué). In English the word is always "why".
3. el porqué (sentence 5): a noun, so it takes an article and means the reason; its plural is los porqués. Example: Nadie conoce los porqués de su silencio, "nobody knows the reasons for his silence".
4. Por que (sentence 6): two words, usually with the subjunctive, meaning so that, or after a noun such as motivo. Example: Se esfuerza por que lo escuchen, "he strives to be listened to".
5. Porque, ya que, puesto que, dado que (sentence 7): all four give a cause, and English "because" or "since" covers most of them. The cause usually takes the indicative, because it is given as a fact, not as a wish.
6. ya que (sentence 8): the most natural of the causal group in speech. Example: Ya que lo sabes, dímelo, "since you know it, tell me".
7. puesto que (sentence 9): formal and mostly written. The sentence goes on with no hace falta que and the subjunctive, which is what the rest of the sentence needs.
8. dado que (sentence 10): the most formal of the four, found in reports, rules and documents. English "given that" is the nearest sense.
9. como (sentence 11): as a cause, como usually comes at the start of its sentence, before the main clause. Inside a sentence it means as or like, a comparison or a manner: como un niño, "like a child".
10. debido a and a causa de (sentence 13): neutral causes, followed by a noun. Example: Debido a la obra, el tráfico está cortado, "owing to the roadworks, the traffic is blocked".
11. gracias a (sentence 14) and por culpa de (sentence 15): a good cause and a bad one. English "thanks to" and "because of" can both be used, but por culpa de says that someone or something is to blame.
12. porqué (sentence 16): a very common mistake. After no sé the word is por qué, and the English "why" makes it plain.
13. The test (sentence 17): ask whether an article can go in front. El porqué takes one; por qué introduces a question; porque gives a cause.
14. The letter (sentences 18 and 19): both mistakes are about a cause. The reader wrote por que and por qué where Spanish needs porque, once as a cause and once without any question.
15. The reply (sentence 32): habría bastado is the conditional perfect for what did not happen: three lines would have been enough, and the column ran to much more.

  en: (to a friend) I don't want to know why you left, but the reason you did not ring me.

  en: We work hard so that it turns out well, and not because they ask us to.

  en: (to a friend) Since you are here, stay for supper; given that it is raining, it is not worth going back.

  en: As she did not answer, and because of the fog, we missed the last train.

  en: Thanks to her patience, the project came out well, although it took so long because of the deadlines.

notes.en:
1. realizar (line 1): a formal verb that makes a plain action sound like an event. "Carried out a visit" makes the reader ask what kind of event it was, while "visited" says what happened. Example: Realizaron un estudio del puerto, "they carried out a study of the port".
2. hacer (line 5): the everyday verb is often the precise one. Hacer names the act and needs no help, so it is the better choice when the act is simple. Example: Hicieron una visita al colegio, "they paid a visit to the school".
3. en base a (lines 7 and 8): a phrase from office language, and a calque of the English "on the basis of". Many style guides frown on it, and it is widely heard in ministries. The usual Spanish forms are a partir de and basándose en. Example: A partir de los datos, la cifra baja, "on the basis of the data, the figure falls".
4. a nivel de (lines 10 and 11): vague. It means in the matter of or as far as something goes, and it adds words without adding facts. Example: a nivel de precio, "as far as price goes"; in mental health, en salud mental, is shorter and says more.
5. de cara a (lines 12 to 14): with a view to, as in de cara al verano, "for the summer". It is correct, but in a press text it turns into a formula, de cara a la mejora del servicio, and the sentence says the purpose without saying the act.
6. poner en valor (lines 15 to 17): to highlight or to give value to something. In administrative Spanish it is a stock phrase, and the simple verbs valorar, reconocer or destacar say the same thing in fewer words. Example: Hay que poner en valor el patrimonio del pueblo, "the village heritage must be highlighted".
7. el mismo (lines 18 to 21): as a pronoun, el mismo has to point to a noun the reader can see. With two people in the sentence it points nowhere, so the noun is repeated or the pronoun él is used. As an adjective it is clear: El mismo técnico dijo lo contrario, "the same technician said the opposite".
8. Adjectives in press texts (lines 23 to 25): excelente, magnífico and histórico add a judgement and no information. The fact (more than a hundred patients) and a quotation from a patient or a nurse do the praising instead.
9. Gracias a + noun (line 30): thanks given as a noun phrase is a cause with no fact in it. Nothing in gracias a la paciencia can be checked, and that is the reason it is cut.
10. tachar (line 33): to cross out, to delete in a text; la tacho means I cross it out. The verb is used in editing and in ordinary speech alike.

numeral: Lección treinta y dos
title: Decir, afirmar, reconocer
title.en: Saying, stating, acknowledging

dialogue:
- Cuando se cita a alguien, el verbo nunca es neutro: cada uno dice algo de quien habla y de lo que se dijo.
  en: When someone is quoted, the verb is never neutral: each one tells us something about the speaker and about what was said.
- El verbo más sencillo es decir, y por eso se usa sin pensar: «Dijo que llegaría tarde» no toma partido por nadie.
  en: The plainest verb is decir, and that is why it is used without thought: «She said she would be late» takes no side.
- Pero en un texto cuidado decir se queda corto, y el lector quiere saber cómo lo dijo alguien: con seguridad, con duda, con rabia o con miedo.
  en: But in careful writing decir falls short, and the reader wants to know how someone said it: with certainty, with doubt, in anger or in fear.
- Afirmar es decir algo con firmeza, como si se firmara debajo: «El director afirmó que la obra estará lista en mayo».
  en: Afirmar is to say something firmly, as if signing it underneath: «The director stated that the works will be ready in May».
- Asegurar va un paso más allá: quien asegura quiere que el otro le crea, y a veces suena a promesa: «Te aseguro que no lo sabía».
  en: Asegurar goes a step further: whoever assures wants the other to believe them, and it sometimes sounds like a promise: «I assure you I did not know».
- Sostener es mantener una opinión frente a los demás, y aparece cuando hay objeciones en el aire: «Ella sostiene que el tren llega siempre a tiempo».
  en: Sostener is to maintain an opinion against others, and it appears when there are objections in the air: «She maintains that the train always arrives on time».
- Alegar pertenece a la discusión y al derecho: se alega una razón, a menudo como excusa, y quien la alega puede tener razón o no: «Alegó que tenía fiebre».
  en: Alegar belongs to argument and to the law: one puts forward a reason, often as an excuse, and whoever puts it forward may or may not be right: «He claimed he had a fever».
- Admitir es ceder algo a regañadientes: se admite lo que no se quería reconocer, y el verbo lleva dentro un «sí, pero».
  en: Admitir is to give way on something reluctantly: one admits what one did not want to acknowledge, and the verb carries a «yes, but» inside it.
- Reconocer es aceptar un hecho de manera más abierta, sin la reticencia de admitir: «Reconoció que se había equivocado».
  en: Reconocer is to accept a fact more openly, without the reluctance of admitir: «He acknowledged that he had been wrong».
- Confesar tiene la carga de algo escondido: se confiesa lo que se ocultaba, y casi siempre tiene que ver con una culpa o con un secreto: «Confesó que había leído sus cartas».
  en: Confesar carries the weight of something hidden: one confesses what was being concealed, and it nearly always has to do with a fault or a secret: «She confessed that she had read his letters».
- Señalar es hablar con tono de informe, sin emoción: se señala un dato, un error o una contradicción: «El informe señala que faltan dos firmas».
  en: Señalar is to speak in the tone of a report, without emotion: one points out a fact, an error or a contradiction: «The report notes that two signatures are missing».
- Comentar es la conversación de cada día, y sirve para contar lo que se ha oído sin tomar partido: «Comentó que el vecino había vuelto a poner la música alta».
  en: Comentar is the talk of every day, used to pass on what has been heard without taking sides: «She mentioned that the neighbour had turned the music up again».
- Murmurar es hablar en voz baja, y casi siempre con mala intención: murmurar de alguien es hablar mal de él a sus espaldas.
  en: Murmurar is to speak in a low voice, and nearly always with bad intent: murmurar de alguien is to speak badly of someone behind their back.
- Espetar y soltar son verbos de golpe: lo dicho sale de repente, sin preparación. Espetar suele ser brusco y va dirigido a alguien a la cara; soltar es más coloquial, y se usa para lo que alguien suelta sin pensar.
  en: Espetar and soltar are verbs of sudden speech: what is said comes out at once, unprepared. Espetar is usually curt and aimed at someone's face; soltar is more colloquial, and it is used for what someone lets out without thinking.
- «Le espetó que no le debía nada» dice mucho más que una simple cita.
  en: «He snapped at her that he owed her nothing» says much more than a plain quotation.
- «Soltó una barbaridad» dice que el hablante no se detuvo a pensar.
  en: «He came out with something outrageous» says that the speaker did not stop to think.
- Elegir el verbo es, por tanto, elegir lo que el lector va a creer de quien habla.
  en: Choosing the verb is, therefore, choosing what the reader is going to believe about the speaker.
- Un mismo hecho cambia de color según el verbo: si el alcalde «admitió» el error, lo hizo a regañadientes; si lo «reconoció», lo asumió; si lo «confesó», lo estaba ocultando.
  en: One and the same fact changes colour with the verb: if the mayor «admitted» the mistake, he did it grudgingly; if he «acknowledged» it, he took it on; if he «confessed» it, he had been hiding it.

  en: The verbs on this list are not perfect synonyms, but they share one thing: they all serve to quote, and none of them is innocent.

  en: Before quoting, it is worth asking what the verb means, as well as what was said.

  en: When a conversation is put into writing, the verb is chosen as a poet chooses a word: with care and for a reason.

  en: A verb with no reason, put in out of habit, is noise, and a good text takes it out.

  en: When a quarrel is written down, the verb sets the rhythm of the fight.

  en: Verbs do not only tell us what was said: they also tell us what the speaker wanted it to seem.

notes.en:
1. decir (sentence 2): the plainest of the reporting verbs, and the one that takes no side. Example: Dijo que llegaría tarde, "she said she would be late". Its weakness is that it says nothing about how the words were meant.
2. afirmar (sentence 4): to state firmly, with the authority of the speaker behind it. Example: Afirmó que no había firmado nada, "he stated that he had signed nothing". English "state" is the nearest, though it is a little more formal.
3. asegurar and sostener (sentences 5 and 6): asegurar is to assure, and the speaker wants to be believed; te aseguro is said to persuade. Sostener is to maintain a view against objections. Example: Sostiene que la pared es de carga, "she maintains that the wall is load-bearing".
4. alegar (sentence 7): to put forward a reason, often as an excuse; in law it is to plead. Example: Alegó problemas de salud para no asistir, "he cited health problems as a reason for not attending".
5. admitir and reconocer (sentences 8 and 9): both mean to accept a fact. Admitir carries the reluctance, the "yes, but"; reconocer is more open and often means owning a mistake. Example: Reconoció el retraso sin excusas, "he acknowledged the delay without excuses".
6. confesar (sentence 10): to confess, and the confession is of something hidden. Example: Confesó su error al jefe, "he confessed his mistake to the boss".
7. señalar and comentar (sentences 11 and 12): señalar is a neutral, report-like verb, and comentar is a casual way of passing on what was heard. Example: Comentó que el tren llegaba tarde, "she mentioned that the train was late".
8. murmurar (sentence 13): in its sense of gossip, murmurar de alguien is to speak ill of someone behind their back. Murmurar can also mean to murmur in a plain sense, but in a quote it nearly always means gossip.
9. espetar and soltar (sentences 14 to 16): both are sudden, and both show the speaker losing control of the moment. Espetar is curt and aimed at someone; soltar is colloquial and blunt. English "snap" and "come out with" are the nearest.
10. The mayor (sentence 18): the same fact, three verbs, three readings: admitir is reluctant, reconocer is open, confesar means the fact was hidden. The English "admitted", "acknowledged" and "confessed" follow the same distinction.
11. The press and the street (sentence 21): the registers choose the verbs. Afirmar and señalar suit reports, decir and comentar suit talk, and soltar suits the moment of anger or surprise.
12. Report and chronicle (sentence 27): the report summarises what was stated, the chronicle lets the reader hear the speech itself. The verb is the difference between them.

14. Reading the whole sentence (sentences 16 to 18 and 22): the method is the same for every false friend. Read the whole sentence, doubt the word that looks like its English, and check it in context.

notes.en:
1. The English line (sentence 3): the actress's line is "That is so awkward", and the English word that stays here is awkward. Spanish has no single match: a Spanish dictionary gives incómodo (uncomfortable), torpe (clumsy), embarazoso (embarrassing) and, for a situation, violento, which is the awkward sense.
2. violenta (sentences 5 and 6): the adjective for a situation that makes people feel embarrassed, as in Fue una situación violenta, "it was an awkward situation". The first sense of the same word is force or blows, and that sense is the danger.
3. incómodo (sentence 7): uncomfortable, physically or in company. It names the discomfort, not the shame. Example: Esta silla es muy incómoda, "this chair is very uncomfortable".
4. torpe (sentence 8): clumsy, slow in the hands or the mind. It is the wrong word for a social moment. Example: Es muy torpe con los cubiertos, "he is very clumsy with the cutlery".
5. embarazoso (sentence 9): embarrassing, and five syllables long. It is not the same as embarazada, which means pregnant; that false friend is in (Foundation, lesson 96). Example: Fue un momento embarazoso, "it was an embarrassing moment".
6. The one who feels it (sentence 16): sentirse is the verb for the feeling, and each adjective gives the feeling a different shape: torpe when one trips, incómodo when one wants to go, embarazoso when one goes red, violento when one wants the ground to open. Example: Me siento violento pidiéndote dinero, "it is awkward for me to ask you for money".
7. que le trague la tierra (sentence 16 and 17): an idiom, a wish that the ground would swallow one up. It is said of a moment of shame, and it keeps its subjunctive after quería que. Example: Quería que se lo tragara la tierra, "he wished the ground would swallow him up".
8. The word in the scene (sentences 22 and 23): the viewer takes the sense from the scene and not from the dictionary, so a word with two senses is safe when the scene settles which one it means.
9. Choosing the first word (sentence 28): the first draft is not always the worst one, and a translator who doubts it should check the scene before changing it.
10. Agreement (sentences 5, 7 and 9): incómoda, embarazosa and violenta agree with the feminine situación, as they do in Qué situación más violenta, "what an awkward situation". An adjective with no noun in front of it still takes the same ending.

notes.en:
1. Artículo 1, "Denominación y fines": statutes number each article and head it with a short noun phrase, not a sentence. Article 4 has two apartados, numbered 1 and 2 inside it, so that a reader can cite "apartado 2 del artículo 4". English does the same with "Name and purposes", but the Spanish heading is never a full clause.
2. "Estatutos" and "reglamento" (the title): the estatutos are the basic rulebook of an association; a reglamento is usually a more detailed set of rules that develops them. The learner should keep the two apart when reading an association's documents.
3. "quedarán obligados a cultivar": in a statute the future is not a forecast. It says what the rule establishes from now on. "Quedar obligado a" means to be bound to do something, and it stresses that the obligation comes into being through the statutes; "estar obligado a" is also correct.
4. "Quedan prohibidos el uso de productos no autorizados": there are two subjects, so the verb is plural, quedan. Salvo before a noun means "except"; before a clause it becomes salvo que. The "las" of "las de carácter provisional" refers back to "estructuras", the feminine noun of the same sentence.
5. "sin perjuicio de pagar las cuotas vencidas": the phrase means that the right to withdraw is not affected by the duty to pay what is already due. Translate it by its effect: "without prejudice" has a narrower legal meaning in English law.
6. "a tal efecto": "for that purpose". It links a second duty to the first, here the agenda to the calling of the meeting, and it is the formula statutes use when one act is made for another.
7. "en su caso": "where applicable". The measure depends on the case, and here the case is named in the same sentence: a member who seriously breaks article 4. The phrase can sit after the verb, as it does here.
8. "socio" and "miembro": socio is the usual word for a member of an association in Spain; miembro is more used for a member of a body, as in "miembro de la Junta". The text keeps socio for the people and names the bodies separately.
9. "la Junta" and "la asamblea": in this text la Junta always means the board, the Junta Directiva of article 7, and the general meeting is always la asamblea. Elsewhere junta can also mean a general meeting, so the learner should check which body is meant.
10. The commentary is written in the plain present ("dicen", "explican"), so it drops the future and the formulas of the articles. The contrast between the statute and its commentary is the register of the whole text.
11. "el único que de verdad importa es el último": the commentary sets the most routine article against the rest. The last one gives the board everything the others leave open, so it governs the cases that nobody wrote down. The ending says this plainly and adds no second point.

notes.en:
1. "Señor Beltrán:" (the first version): the greeting of a formal letter is normally "Estimado señor Beltrán:", with the adjective agreeing with the addressee. Dropping it, as the furious letter does, makes the opening curt. The colon after the greeting is the usual punctuation of a Spanish letter.
2. "le comunico que me doy de baja": the furious letter keeps the formal pronoun le, because the director is addressed as usted. The anger is in the verbs and the nouns, not in the forms of address, and the reader sees the same respect in every version.
3. "no pienso fingir que me da pena": fingir is "to pretend", and dar pena means to make someone feel sorry or sad. The sentence says the opposite of what a polite letter would say, which is why it reads as rage.
4. "no me pida que sea amable": for usted the negative command takes the subjunctive, here pida, and the pronoun comes before the verb, as in no me pida. The writer addresses the director directly, and the subjunctive keeps the command polite in form while it is rude in content.
5. "surtirá efecto al término del preaviso": surtir efecto is the formal phrase for "to take effect", and it belongs to letters and notices. English "take effect" is the same register.
6. "Agradezco la oportunidad de haber trabajado": the perfect infinitive haber trabajado names an action finished before the verb of thanking. It is the form a formal letter uses when it thanks someone for what has already happened.
7. "Quedo a su disposición": quedar a disposición de alguien means to remain at someone's disposal. Quedo is the first person of quedar, and the letter uses it as a formula, as in quedo a la espera.
8. The closing matches the opening: "Estimado señor Beltrán:" goes with "Un cordial saludo" or with "Atentamente". The polite letter closes warmly, and the perfect one closes with the most neutral form. In English the pair is "Dear Mr Beltrán" and "Yours sincerely".
9. "la confianza depositada en mí": depositar confianza en alguien is a figure of speech, the trust placed like money in a bank. English "the trust placed in me" keeps the same image, and the perfect letter uses it once, in the place where a reader expects it.
10. "causar baja voluntaria": causar baja is the formal phrase used in companies and in official forms. Darse de baja is the more common phrase of everyday speech, and both are correct in a letter of resignation.
11. "con el preaviso establecido en mi contrato": the notice period is fixed by the law, the collective agreement or the contract. The letter cites the contract and gives no number, because the writer does not need one and a wrong number would be worse than none.
12. "pondré a disposición de quien me sustituya los expedientes abiertos": the future states a promise, and the concrete detail is what makes the third letter perfect. It says what the writer will do, where the others only say what they feel.
13. "lo que llegó al despacho del señor Beltrán fue la primera": the sentence puts the outcome at the end of the line, and the preterite fue marks an act that is finished. Nothing after it explains the mistake, and the reader sees the choice at once.

notes.en:
1. "Hacemos la evaluación anual, que es un buen momento para los dos": this is the formula of a calendar invitation, and it presents the meeting as shared and useful. The learner will hear it in many Spanish offices, and the phrase tells the employee very little about what is coming.
2. "eso lo tengo apuntado": apuntar means to write something down, and tener apuntado says that a point has been taken. In the office the phrase reports a note, not an action, and the review uses it before any decision is named.
3. "algunas áreas de mejora": the set phrase of reviews. It means faults or weak points, and the word that would say so, errores or fallos, is never used. The euphemism is the register of the whole scene.
4. "salir un poco de tu zona de confort": the idiom means to leave the familiar place where one works without effort. It comes from management language and is now common in Spanish offices; the English "step out of your comfort zone" is the same idiom.
5. "¿Tú cómo lo ves?": the verb ver is used here for an opinion, and the question asks for a view without giving any power to the one who answers. English "What do you think?" is the nearest plain equivalent.
6. "Te escucho, de verdad": te escucho means "I am listening" and is the usual formula of a listener. The adverb de verdad is added when the listening is in doubt, and the employee hears it that way.
7. "No es que no los haga": no es que takes the subjunctive, and the phrase denies a cause that someone has suggested. Los refers back to the reports of the line before, and the English "It is not that I do not do them" keeps the same structure.
8. "Lo anoto" and "Me lo apunto": anotar and apuntar both mean to write down. In Spain apuntar is the everyday verb, and anotar is more written and formal. The manager has used both verbs for her notes earlier in the review, so the verb is already familiar when it comes back at the end.
9. "Lo tengo en cuenta": tener en cuenta means to take into account. It is a commitment with no date, and the employee asks at once how it will show in the review.
10. "el plan de desarrollo": a plan of future goals and training, which in Spanish companies is usually written in the review. The English "development plan" is the same term.
11. "Tú" throughout: manager and employee are colleagues who address each other as tú, as they do in most Spanish offices today. Usted would make the meeting formal, and the scene is about the limits of that informal tone.

1. "Asunto: Acuerdos de la reunión del lunes": asunto is the subject line of an email in Spanish. English "Subject" is the same field, and the Spanish word is the one the learner will see in every office inbox.

7. "¿Alguien se acuerda de lo que se acordó el lunes, o solo hablamos del café?": acordar means to agree, and acordarse de means to remember. Both verbs are in the same question, and the learner should note the difference between them.
8. "Lo de «quedamos a la espera» me ha dado risa": dar risa is the idiom for "to make someone laugh", and the perfect ha dado is the right tense for a reaction that has just happened. The quotes mark that the phrase is the email's, not Marta's.
9. "Tomás, que te va a preguntar": the colloquial que opens a warning or a comment on what someone will do. It is typical of chat and of speech, and it has no equivalent in the formal email.
10. "Lo que le diría de su corbata de tres colores no se puede escribir en un correo": the conditional diría describes what he would say, and the sentence stops before it names the thing. The reader is left to notice the register, not the content.
11. "Tranquila, que lo escribo en el grupo y punto": y punto closes a statement as final, and it is chat and speech only. The English "and that's that" is its equivalent.
12. "responder a todos": the Spanish for "reply all". The action is the same in English, and the reply uses the chat's tone in a message that goes to the whole list, including the department head.
13. "que era para el grupo, no para aquí": era says what the sentence was meant to be, and the preposition para gives its destination.

notes.en:
1. "Siéntese, doña Pilar": usted in the imperative, the notary's form for an older client. Doña and the first name is the courteous form of address for a woman of her age in a formal setting.
2. "la letra pequeña": the small print of a contract or a document. The phrase is used literally here, and the English "small print" carries the same figure.
3. "Viuda desde hace once años": desde hace takes the present for a state that still lasts. The sentence is elliptical, with soy left out, and it stays natural in speech.
4. "Sus hijos tienen derecho a dos tercios de la herencia": in the Código Civil that applies in most of Spain, the children's legítima is two thirds of the estate. The term names the share the law keeps for them, which the testator cannot take away without a legal cause.
5. "Galicia, que tiene su propio derecho civil": several regions, Galicia and Cataluña among them, have their own civil law, with rules that differ from the Código Civil. The notary says so once, and the scene applies the common rules because the flat is in Madrid.
6. "el tercio de libre disposición": the third that the testator can leave to anyone. It is called free because the law does not reserve it for the family; the notary says that the choice is hers.
7. "heredero" and "legado": an heir receives a share of the estate as a whole, while a legacy is a specific item, a flat or a sum or a painting. The difference is what makes the notary's question necessary.
8. "el usufructo": the right to use and enjoy property that belongs to someone else, here the flat. The children become owners, and the mother keeps the right to live there for as long as she lives.
9. "el albacea": the person named in the will to carry it out, paying debts and dividing what is left. In Spanish the word names a role, not a family relation.
10. "siempre que no sea imposible ni contraria a la ley o a las buenas costumbres": the subjunctive sea after siempre que states the condition the notary sets. The conditions the law does not accept are described by their kind, not by a list.
11. "y así vendrán a verme los domingos": así introduces the result of what has just been said, and vendrán is the future of an expectation. The English "so they will come" keeps the same logic.

notes.en:
1. "Buenas tardes, señoras y señores": the opening of a talk in Spain. The speaker addresses a room of strangers with ustedes, the formal plural, and the English "you" hides that choice; the formula is the same whatever the lecture is about.
2. "Perdonen, que ahora pongo la lámina": perdonen is the ustedes form of the imperative, and the que clause gives the reason for the request. Lámina is the illustration itself; in a slide show the speaker might say diapositiva, which is also used in Spain.
3. "con una precisión que hoy se consigue con cámaras": se consigue is the passive with se. The verb agrees with precisión, the singular subject, so the sentence says the precision is achieved, not that someone achieves it.
4. "Hasta entonces, muchos científicos creían": the imperfect describes the belief as the state of things before Cajal's work, and it is the background for the claim that follows. The English "believed" carries the same background sense.
5. "Cajal sostuvo lo contrario": sostener means to hold a view as true, a more exact verb than decir in a lecture. Lo contrario is the neuter article with an adjective used as a noun, and it refers to the whole idea just stated.
6. "se tocan pero no se funden": tocarse and fundirse are the two verbs that describe contact without fusion. The English "touch but do not merge" is literal, and the difference between them is the whole argument.
7. "Dicho en términos sencillos": the marker the speaker uses to move from the technical account to the popular one. Put simply is the English equivalent, and the lecture uses it more than once.
8. "la tinción con plata que había ideado Camillo Golgi": había ideado is the pluperfect for an invention that came before Cajal used it. The stain is Golgi's, and the sentence keeps the credit where it belongs.
9. "Ustedes me dirán qué tiene que ver esto": the future of anticipation, used by a speaker who expects the question from the room. It is a lecturer's device, and the English "You will be asking" keeps the same sense.
10. "compartieron el Premio Nobel de Fisiología o Medicina": the official name of the prize, with the capital on the first word only. The next line repeats the verb on purpose: they shared the prize, and the same verb is then denied for the idea.
11. "Golgi seguía defendiendo su red continua": seguir with the gerund says the activity went on after the moment described. Defender is the verb of argument, a view defended against another, and it is stronger than the sostener of the earlier line.
12. "Sin el método de Golgi no habría habido aquellos dibujos": the conditional habría habido gives the result of a condition that is not met. Here sin el método de Golgi is the condition, and the English "there would have been no" is the same construction.
13. "Nadie lo sabe del todo": lo stands for what happens in the brain, the question of the line before, and the claim is hedged by del todo. The lecturer states what is not known, which is the honest form of an expert's claim.
14. "Según cuentan sus biógrafos": según with a plural verb names the source of a statement that the speaker does not vouch for. It is how a lecture hedges a detail it cannot check, and the next claim, about his studies, is stated plainly.
15. "que es mío y no de Cajal": the speaker separates his own title from the scientist's words, so the audience knows who is responsible for the image. The English "which is mine and not Cajal's" is a plain statement of that difference.
16. "en cierto modo lo fue": en cierto modo is the hedge that keeps the last claim modest. The speaker does not say that Cajal was a painter; he says that the drawings did a painter's work, and the English "in a way" keeps that limit.

notes.en:
1. "Resumen": in a Spanish article this is the abstract, the short account of the study placed at the top. The word also means a summary in general, but in a journal it names that section.
2. "se ha sugerido que mejora la memoria": the impersonal se with haber leaves the source unnamed. Nobody is said to have suggested it, and the claim is kept at the level of a suggestion. The siesta as a myth and a habit is the subject of (Foundation, lesson 82), and this article does not retell it.
3. "Se reclutaron sesenta y dos voluntarios": the passive with se, where the verb agrees with its plural subject, voluntarios. The English "were recruited" has no agent for the same reason.
4. "Los resultados sugieren que": the noun results is the subject, so the article says what the data point to without saying "we show". Sugerir takes que and the indicative, and it is weaker than demostrar, a verb the article never uses.
5. "parece indicar una tendencia más que un efecto claro": parecer with an infinitive gives the impression of the data, not the fact. Indicar is a more exact verb than sugerir here, and the English "it seems to indicate" keeps the hedge.
6. "Conviene señalar que": convenir is used impersonally for what is advisable. The formula introduces a caveat the reader must not skip, and the English "It should be pointed out" is the same move.
7. "que se exigiría para afirmar el efecto": the conditional exigiría gives the standard that a rule would set, and the passive se leaves the agent out. Afirmar is stronger than sugerir, and the article keeps the two apart.
8. "cabe pensar que": caber here means it is reasonable or possible. The subject is the clause that follows, so no one has to say "I think", which is the noun style of the article.
9. "No puede descartarse que": the impersonal passive with se and poder. The subject is the clause, and the English "cannot be ruled out" is the same passive.
10. "dado que muchos llegaron tras una comida copiosa": dado que is a formal causal connector, with the indicative after it. It states the reason as a fact, which is why the sentence can say so without hedging.
11. "no permiten concluir que la siesta mejora la memoria": permitir takes an infinitive, and the data are the subject. The article does not reject the claim outright; it says that the data do not allow the conclusion.
12. "de existir": a conditional phrase with de and an infinitive, common in formal writing. The English "if there is one" keeps the same condition, and the sentence allows for the effect not being there at all.
13. "Se requieren más estudios": requerir with se is passive, so the verb is plural to agree with estudios. A singular "se requiere más estudio" would be wrong.
14. "Sería prematuro recomendar": the conditional states a judgement about the present, and the adjective prematuro is a standard way to close a discussion without a flat "no".
15. "Declaración de intereses": the heading for a statement of conflicts of interest, placed at the end of a Spanish article. The English "Declaration of interests" is the same heading, with the same position.
16. "S. L.": the abbreviation of sociedad limitada, the usual form for a Spanish private limited company. The English "Ltd" is its equivalent, and the full form is written out in the company's name.
17. "fabricante de las camas en las que se hizo la siesta": the relative with a preposition, en las que, is the form the English "on which" needs. The last line keeps the register of the article to the end: a formal disclosure in the same hedged noun style.

notes.en:
1. "En inglés, el autor suele escribir I": soler with an infinitive means that something is usual, not always the case. The essay says suele where it could say siempre, which keeps the claim about English true.
2. "«creemos que el argumento es sólido»": creemos is the nosotros form of creer. The quoted sentence is what one writer says in the voice of a group, and the verb agrees with the plural, so the writer stays unnamed.
3. "el de la familia": the family we includes the speaker and somebody else. In «¿Nos vamos ya?» the listener is part of the action, which the academic we does not do.
4. "«hemos comprobado» suena más serio que «comprobé»": the perfect hemos comprobado keeps the result present in the text, while the preterite comprobé places the act in the past and names the person who did it.
5. "la pasiva refleja": se with a transitive verb and a plural subject. The verb agrees with the subject, not with se, and the English "were collected" has the same structure with a passive verb.
6. "se recogieron las muestras": the plural verb recogieron agrees with muestras, which is the subject. If the sentence had a singular noun, the verb would be singular too.

10. "el estilo nominal": verbs turned into nouns, as in la recogida de muestras. The English "the collection of samples" makes the same move, and both languages use it in scientific prose.

12. "Hay quien cree que": hay quien means some people, and the phrase takes the indicative after cree. The writer answers an opinion that nobody is named as holding.

notes.en:
1. "Reseña": the name of the genre, a critical notice of a book. The title of the book keeps its Spanish form, and "pretexto" is a pretext, a reason given for something that is really for another purpose.
2. "un libro que se lee con gusto": leer con gusto is the evaluative formula for a pleasant reading, and the English "a pleasure to read" is a plain equivalent. Sin embargo introduces the reservation the review is building towards.
3. "pide más de lo que da": the contrast between two verbs, pedir and dar, is the reviewer's own balance. The English "asks more of the reader than it gives" keeps the same two verbs.
4. "La idea no es nueva, pero": a concession followed by a qualification. The reviewer grants the point before saying what is missing.
5. "esa es la mejor parte del libro": the demonstrative esa refers to the list of sources, and the superlative names the best part. Evaluation in a review works through these short judgements.
6. "se agradece": agradecer in the passive with se is the normal evaluative form for something welcome. The English "that is welcome" is a close match.
7. "según se cuenta": the phrase marks a story told about a thing without a document behind it, as in (lesson 41, note 3). The review asks for the same marker here.
8. "Es un error que el lector reconoce a la primera": a la primera means at the first attempt, or at once, and it is used of something easy to see. The English "spots at once" keeps that sense.
9. "se echa en falta": echar en falta is the formal verb for missing something. Echar de menos is the common alternative, and both are correct, but the review keeps the more formal one.
10. "Pero el tono se vuelve a veces lacrimoso": volverse with an adjective means that a state arrives, not that the subject changes its nature. Lacrimoso describes a tone too sentimental, and the English "maudlin" is the exact word.
11. "remiten con precisión a las fuentes": remitir a is to refer the reader to a source. The adverb con precisión says that the reference is exact, which is the praise the reviewer gives.
12. "libros de divulgación": popular books, written for a general reader. Divulgación is a precise term, and the review names it as the category that the bibliography has mixed up.
13. "obliga a comprobar todas las citas": obligar a with an infinitive means that the book forces a specialist to do something. The English "forces them to check" is the same structure.
14. "Lo que el libro propone, en el fondo, es algo sencillo": the sentence starts with lo que, and the verb ser gives the key idea after it. The English "What the book proposes is" is the same cleft.
15. "Esa tesis merece discusión": merecer takes a noun for what is deserved, and the English "deserves discussion" keeps the same noun. The review ends its argument with a judgement the reader can take up.
16. "Lo recomiendo, con una advertencia": the closing judgement in the first person. The warning is set against the recommendation in the same sentence, and it stays in the review's voice.
17. "debe leerse con un lápiz en la mano": deber with a reflexive infinitive gives the sense of "should be read", with the legend as its subject. The last note is about register: this is the close of a review in plain, dry Spanish.
