== 1 B2
numeral: Lección primera
title: Echar
title.en: To throw

dialogue:
- Lunes, siete y cuarto. Lucía se despierta tarde y, con los ojos cerrados, busca el móvil.
  en: Monday, quarter past seven. Lucía wakes up late and, with her eyes shut, looks for her phone.
- Su madre no llama desde el jueves, y Lucía la echa de menos.
  en: Her mother has not rung since Thursday, and Lucía misses her (she throws her of less).
- Se levanta, tropieza con la maleta y le echa la culpa al gato.
  en: She gets up, trips over the suitcase and blames the cat (she throws the fault to the cat).
- La leche del desayuno se ha echado a perder, así que la tira por el fregadero.
  en: The milk from breakfast has gone off (it has thrown itself to lose), so she pours it down the sink.
- En la oficina echa un vistazo al correo: cuarenta mensajes, y tres son del jefe.
  en: At the office she has a quick look at her emails: forty messages, three of them from the boss (she throws a glance).
- Pablo, su compañero, no encuentra el informe, y Lucía le echa una mano.
  en: Pablo, her colleague, cannot find the report, so Lucía lends him a hand (she throws him a hand).
- El informe estaba en el cajón, pero el jefe, que lo ve todo, le echa en cara que llegó tarde.
  en: The report was in the drawer, but the boss, who sees everything, reproaches her for arriving late (he throws it in her face that she arrived late).
- Lucía echa humo por las orejas, pero no dice nada.
  en: Lucía is furious, but says nothing (she throws smoke out of her ears).
- A mediodía, Pablo le cuenta un chiste, y ella se echa a reír por primera vez.
  en: At midday Pablo tells her a joke, and she bursts out laughing for the first time (she throws herself to laugh).
- Después de comer, echa una siesta de diez minutos en el sofá de la sala de reuniones.
  en: After lunch she has a ten-minute nap on the sofa in the meeting room (she throws a siesta).
- Por la tarde, el jefe echa chispas por un correo mal escrito.
  en: In the afternoon the boss is furious about a badly written email (he throws sparks about it).
- Al final del día, Lucía echa cuentas: el sueldo no llega a fin de mes.
  en: At the end of the day Lucía works out the sums: the salary does not stretch to the end of the month (she throws accounts).
- Lleva cinco años en la empresa y ya ha echado raíces: nadie la imagina en otra oficina.
  en: She has been at the firm for five years and has put down roots: nobody can picture her in another office (she has thrown roots).
- Si sigue así, el jefe la echa del trabajo.
  en: If this goes on, the boss will fire her (he throws her out of work).
- Ha quedado con una amiga para cenar, pero a última hora se echa atrás.
  en: She has arranged to have dinner with a friend, but at the last minute she backs out (she throws herself back).
- Camino de casa echa una carta al buzón, la de la renovación del gimnasio.
  en: On the way home she posts a letter, the one about renewing her gym membership (she throws a letter into the postbox).
- En la gasolinera echa gasolina: el depósito estaba casi vacío.
  en: At the petrol station she fills up: the tank was almost empty (she throws petrol in).
- Al arrancar, el coche no se mueve, y ella le echa la bronca.
  en: When she starts it, the car does not move, and she tells it off (she throws it the row).
- Por fin se acuesta. Mañana es martes, y el gato, que ya no tiene la culpa de nada, duerme a sus pies.
  en: At last she goes to bed. Tomorrow is Tuesday, and the cat, no longer to blame for anything, sleeps at her feet.
- Y aquí echamos el cierre.
  en: And that is where we pull the shutter down for today (we throw the closing).

notes.en:
1. Echar: English splits this verb into throw, pour, post, fire, miss and nap; Spanish uses one verb, and the object tells you which sense is meant. Its basic sense is "to throw" or "to put in": echa sal a la sopa, "she adds salt to the soup", and echo el correo, "I post the mail".
2. echa de menos (sentence 2): to miss someone or something; literal "to throw of less", as if something has gone missing from where it should be. The person comes before the verb: la echa de menos, "she misses her", and Te echo de menos, "I miss you". In much of Latin America you will hear extrañar more often: Te extraño.
3. le echa la culpa al gato (sentence 3): to blame; literal "to throw the fault to". The person blamed is named twice, once as le and once as al gato. Example: No me eches la culpa a mí, "don't blame me".
4. se ha echado a perder (sentence 4): to spoil or go off, said of food, plans and the weather; literal "to throw oneself to lose". Example: La fruta se ha echado a perder, "the fruit has gone off". The pronoun se stays in every form.
5. echa un vistazo (sentence 5): to have a quick look; literal "to throw a glance". Echar un ojo means the same and is more familiar. Example: Echa un vistazo a esto, "have a quick look at this".
6. le echa una mano (sentence 6; Foundation, lesson 87): to lend a hand; literal "to throw a hand". Foundation lesson 87 has the question ¿Y no me vas a echar una mano? The person helped takes le. Example: ¿Me echas una mano con las cajas?, "can you give me a hand with the boxes?"
7. le echa en cara (sentence 7): to reproach someone for something; literal "to throw in the face". What is reproached comes after en cara, as a noun or as a que clause. Example: Siempre me echa en cara lo del coche, "he always brings up the car business with me".
8. echa humo por las orejas (sentence 8): to be furious; literal "to throw smoke out of the ears". The shortened form echa humo, without por las orejas, is also used in speech. Example: Mi padre echaba humo por las orejas, "my father was furious".
9. se echa a reír (sentence 9): to burst out laughing. Echarse a followed by an infinitive means to start suddenly, and the English "burst out" has the same sense. Example: Se echó a llorar al oír la noticia, "she burst into tears when she heard the news".
10. echa una siesta (sentence 10): to have a nap. Spanish says echar una siesta or echarse una siesta, and the nap is usually taken after lunch. Example: Después de comer me echo una siesta, "after lunch I have a nap".
11. echa chispas (sentence 11): to be furious, from the image of sparks; it is close in sense to note 8, and both are about anger. Example: Cuando se enteró, echó chispas, "when he found out, he hit the roof".
12. echa cuentas (sentence 12): to work out the sums, to see whether money will stretch; literal "to throw accounts". Cuenta means both "account" and "bill", and it also means "count". Example: Echa cuentas antes de comprar un coche, "she works out the sums before buying a car".
13. ha echado raíces (sentence 13): to put down roots, to settle somewhere. The English uses the same image, so this one is easy to keep. Example: Los abuelos han echado raíces en ese pueblo, "my grandparents have settled in that village".
14. la echa del trabajo (sentence 14): to dismiss someone, to throw them out of a job. The place or group comes after de, as in del trabajo, "from work". Example: Lo echaron del equipo por llegar tarde, "they threw him off the team for being late".
15. se echa atrás (sentence 15): to back out, to change one's mind about something agreed. The reflexive pronoun stays: se echa atrás, me eché atrás. Example: Me eché atrás en el último momento, "I backed out at the last moment".
16. echa una carta al buzón (sentence 16): to post a letter; literal "to throw a letter into the postbox". Buzón is the postbox, and the same verb is used for putting in a card or a leaflet. Example: Echa la carta al buzón antes de las cinco, "post the letter before five".
17. echa gasolina (sentence 17): to put petrol into a car. Figuratively, echar gasolina al fuego means to make an argument worse. Example: No le eches gasolina al fuego, "don't make things worse".
18. le echa la bronca (sentence 18): to scold or tell someone off; bronca is the telling-off itself and also a noisy row. Example: Mi madre me echó la bronca por llegar tarde, "my mother told me off for being late".
19. echamos el cierre (sentence 20): to close a shop or a business for the day; literal "to throw the closing". Example: La panadería echa el cierre a las tres, "the bakery closes at three".
20. Expressions of this lesson, with the sentence and the note:
echar de menos (sentence 2, note 2)
echar la culpa (sentence 3, note 3)
echar a perder (sentence 4, note 4)
echar un vistazo (sentence 5, note 5)
echar una mano (sentence 6, note 6)
echar en cara (sentence 7, note 7)
echar humo por las orejas (sentence 8, note 8)
echarse a reír (sentence 9, note 9)
echar una siesta (sentence 10, note 10)
echar chispas (sentence 11, note 11)
echar cuentas (sentence 12, note 12)
echar raíces (sentence 13, note 13)
echar a alguien del trabajo (sentence 14, note 14)
echarse atrás (sentence 15, note 15)
echar una carta al buzón (sentence 16, note 16)
echar gasolina (sentence 17, note 17)
echar la bronca (sentence 18, note 18)
echar el cierre (sentence 20, note 19)

ex1:
- Te echo mucho de menos cuando viajas.
  en: I really miss you when you travel (to a friend).
- ¿Me echas una mano con las maletas?
  en: Can you give me a hand with the suitcases (to a friend)?
- No me eches la culpa a mí.
  en: Don't blame me (to a friend).
- El jefe me ha echado en cara el retraso.
  en: The boss has reproached me for being late.
- Después de comer, siempre me echo una siesta.
  en: After lunch I always have a nap.

ex2:
- Mi hermana echa [de] menos a sus amigos de Valencia.
  en: My sister misses her friends from Valencia.
- Lo siento, no quería [echar] la culpa a nadie.
  en: I'm sorry, I didn't want to blame anyone.
- Cuando vio al gato, ella [se echó] a reír.
  en: When she saw the cat, she burst out laughing.
- La leche se ha echado [a] perder.
  en: The milk has gone off.
- El director [le] echó la bronca a Pablo.
  en: The manager told Pablo off.

== 2 B2
numeral: Lección segunda
title: Lo que te espera
title.en: What lies ahead

dialogue:
- Este curso es para quien ya habla bien español y quiere hablarlo mejor, sin prisa.
  en: This course is for someone who already speaks Spanish well and wants to speak it better, without hurry.
- Aquí vas a leer textos de todo tipo: un chiste, una carta al ayuntamiento, un cuento, un poema.
  en: Here you will read texts of every kind: a joke, a letter to the town hall, a story, a poem.
- Viajaremos por España y por el mundo hispano, de Sevilla a Buenos Aires.
  en: We will travel around Spain and the Spanish-speaking world, from Seville to Buenos Aires.
- Conocerás a pintores, a escritores y a gente corriente, y a todos les daremos la palabra.
  en: You will meet painters, writers and ordinary people, and we will give each of them the floor (we will give each of them the word).
- Habrá modismos a docenas, de esos que parecen sencillos y no lo son.
  en: There will be idioms by the dozen, the kind that look simple and are not.
- Cada lección es un texto completo, y nadie te pedirá que lo recites de memoria.
  en: Each lesson is a complete text, and nobody will ask you to recite it from memory.
- Mientras tanto, la segunda vuelta del curso anterior sigue en paralelo: cada lección, hasta la cuarenta y nueve, te trae de vuelta una frase de ella.
  en: Meanwhile the second wave of the earlier course goes on alongside: each lesson up to the forty-ninth brings back a sentence from it (brings back: literally brings of return).
- Si empiezas aquí directamente, la segunda vuelta de este curso empieza en la lección cincuenta.
  en: If you start here directly, this course's own wave begins at lesson fifty.
- Hay textos que hacen reír, otros que hacen pensar, y algunos hacen las dos cosas.
  en: Some texts make you laugh, others make you think, and some do both.
- Si lees una lección al día, dentro de un mes habrás leído treinta textos.
  en: If you read one lesson a day, in a month's time you will have read thirty texts.
- Para leer bien no hace falta entender cada palabra: basta con captar la idea.
  en: To read well you do not need to understand every word: it is enough to grasp the idea.
- Lee en voz alta, para que el oído se acostumbre al ritmo de la frase.
  en: Read aloud, so that your ear gets used to the rhythm of the sentence.
- Y cuando dudes, usa las notas para comprobar tu respuesta sin volver al texto.
  en: And when you are unsure, use the notes to check your answer without going back to the text.
- Hace falta paciencia, pero no hace falta correr.
  en: You need patience, but you do not need to rush.
- Aquí nadie te pide la cuenta.
  en: Here nobody asks you for the bill.
- Mañana, dos amigos se pelearán por pagarla.
  en: Tomorrow, two friends will fight over paying it.

notes.en:
1. habrás leído (sentence 10): the future perfect, habrás plus the past participle, for an action that will be finished by a future point. Example: Mañana a esta hora habré terminado el libro, "by this time tomorrow I will have finished the book".
2. dentro de un mes (sentence 10): dentro de plus a time means "in a month's time, from now", while en un mes gives the length of time something takes. Example: Lo termino en un mes, "I will finish it within a month".
3. para comprobar (sentence 13): para plus an infinitive is used when the same person does both actions, here the one who checks. Example: Vino para hablar conmigo, "he came to talk to me".
4. para que el oído se acostumbre (sentence 12; Foundation, lesson 52): para que plus the subjunctive is used when the subject changes, here the ear is the one that gets used to the rhythm. Example: Lo escribo para que lo entiendas, "I write it so that you understand it".
5. te trae de vuelta (sentence 7): traer de vuelta means "to bring back" in the sense of recalling a memory, not carrying anything physically. Example: Esta canción me trae de vuelta mi infancia, "this song brings my childhood back to me".
6. les daremos la palabra (sentence 4): dar la palabra means to give someone the floor so that they can speak; literal "to give the word". Example: El presidente le dio la palabra a la secretaria, "the chairman gave the secretary the floor".
7. a docenas (sentence 5): in large numbers, by the dozen, with no exact count. Example: Hay libros a docenas en esa librería, "there are books by the dozen in that bookshop".
8. no lo son (sentence 5): lo replaces an adjective already said, and it does not change with the noun. Example: ¿Es fácil? No, no lo es, "Is it easy? No, it is not".
9. hacen reír, hacen pensar (sentence 9): hacer plus an infinitive means to make someone do something, here make people laugh or think. Example: Esa película me hizo llorar, "that film made me cry".
10. basta con (sentence 11): to be enough, followed by a noun or an infinitive. Example: Basta con decir que sí, "saying yes is enough".
11. la cuenta (sentence 15): the bill, in a restaurant; cuenta also means "account" and "count", as in lesson 1, note 12. Example: Pedimos la cuenta, "we asked for the bill".
12. se pelearán por pagarla (sentence 16): pelearse por means to fight over something, and por introduces what they fight over. Example: Se pelean por el último trozo de tarta, "they are fighting over the last piece of cake".

ex1:
- Dentro de dos semanas habré acabado el capítulo.
  en: In two weeks' time I will have finished the chapter.
- Para que lo entiendas, te lo explico otra vez.
  en: So that you understand it, I will explain it to you again (to a friend).
- Me trajo de vuelta muchos recuerdos de la infancia.
  en: It brought back a lot of childhood memories to me.
- No hace falta llegar pronto, basta con llegar a tiempo.
  en: You do not need to arrive early, it is enough to arrive on time.
- Hay cuadros a docenas en ese museo de Sevilla.
  en: There are paintings by the dozen in that museum in Seville.

ex2:
- Mañana a estas horas [habré] terminado el informe.
  en: By this time tomorrow I will have finished the report.
- Esa novela me [hizo] reír mucho.
  en: That novel made me laugh a lot.
- Te lo digo para [que] lo sepas a tiempo.
  en: I am telling you so that you know in time.
- Pedimos la [cuenta] después del postre.
  en: We asked for the bill after dessert.
- Mis primos se [pelean] por el último trozo de pastel.
  en: My cousins are fighting over the last piece of cake.

== 3 B2
numeral: Lección tercera
title: ¡Que pago yo!
title.en: No, I'm paying!

dialogue:
- Dos cafés con leche y dos churros, por favor.
  en: Two white coffees and two churros, please.
- Ni hablar, Javier. Esta ronda la pago yo.
  en: No way, Javier. I'm paying for this round.
- ¡Que no, Rubén, que esta ronda la pago yo, que la última vez pagaste tú!
  en: No, Rubén, I'm paying for this round, you paid last time!
- Qué va, hombre. Eso fue en diciembre, y la próxima la pagas tú.
  en: Come off it, mate. That was in December, and you can pay next time.
- De eso nada. Tú a mí me invitarás cuando te toque, y hoy me toca a mí.
  en: No way. You'll treat me when it's your turn, and today it's my turn.
- Anda, déjate de tonterías y cómete el churro antes de que se enfríe.
  en: Come on, stop being silly and eat your churro before it goes cold.
- No es ninguna tontería. El que invita se acuerda, y yo me acuerdo de todo.
  en: It is no silly matter. The one who treats remembers, and I remember everything.
- Pues yo me acuerdo de más cosas, y tu madre, que nos mira desde el fondo, también.
  en: Well, I remember more than you do, and so does your mother, who is watching us from the back.
- Mi madre no se mete en esto, y no la mires, que se va a dar cuenta.
  en: My mother stays out of this, and don't look at her, she will notice.
- Entonces, a medias, que es lo justo.
  en: Then we split it, which is only fair.
- Perdonad, pero la máquina de la tarjeta se está quedando sin paciencia.
  en: Sorry, but the card machine is running out of patience.
- Venga, vale. A medias, pues, y que conste que la próxima la pagas tú.
  en: Fine, then. Half each, and let it be noted that you pay next time.
- Ya lo sé, que lo has dicho tres veces.
  en: I know, you have said it three times.
- Perfecto. Pero hay un problema.
  en: Perfect. But there's a problem.
- ¿Qué problema? ¿No funciona la tarjeta?
  en: What problem? Doesn't the card work?
- Que ya está pagado. Lo ha pagado la señora del fondo, que dice que es la madre de Javier.
  en: It's already paid. The lady at the back has paid for it, and she says she is Javier's mother.

notes.en:
1. ¡Que no!, que esta ronda la pago yo (line 3): a spoken way of saying "no, I tell you", with que before each clause to press the point. Example: ¡Que sí, que lo tengo!, "yes, I tell you, I have it!".
2. Ni hablar (line 2): literally "not to speak", a flat no to a suggestion or a request. Example: ¿Me prestas el coche? Ni hablar, "can I borrow the car? No way".
3. esta ronda la pago yo (line 2): the thing paid comes first and the pronoun la repeats it. English keeps the order, I'm paying for this round, but Spanish puts the round first. Example: El café lo pago yo, "I'm paying for the coffee".
4. Qué va, hombre (line 4): qué va means "not at all", and hombre is said between friends, whatever their sex, the way English says "mate". Example: Qué va, hombre, no te preocupes, "not at all, mate, don't worry".
5. la próxima la pagas tú (line 4): la próxima means "the next time" (la próxima vez), and the pronoun la repeats the round, as in note 3. Example: La próxima la invito yo, "next time I'll treat you".
6. Tú a mí me invitarás (line 5; Foundation, lesson 54): invitar means "to treat someone", to pay for their food or drink, as in Si gana el Madrid, me invitas tú. Te invito is "it's my treat". The a mí me doubles the pronoun to put the stress on the person.
7. Venga, vale (line 12): venga is "come on" or "all right", and vale is "okay". Together they mark a quick agreement. Example: Venga, vamos, que llegamos tarde, "come on, let's go, we're late".
8. Anda, déjate de tonterías (line 6): anda is a push to get on with it, and dejarse de plus a noun means to stop with a nonsense. Example: Déjate de rodeos, "stop beating around the bush". Compare dejar de plus an infinitive, which means to stop doing something.
9. El que invita se acuerda (line 7): el que plus a verb means "the one who", and here it names the friend who pays. Example: El que llega tarde paga el café, "the one who arrives late pays for the coffee".
10. Pues yo me acuerdo (line 8): pues starts the reply, as "well" or "so" does in English, and it links the answer to what was just said. Example: ¿Te acuerdas del taxi? Pues sí, "do you remember the taxi? Well, yes".
11. la señora del fondo (lines 8 and 16): del fondo means "at the back", the far end of the room or the bar. Example: Nos sentamos en la mesa del fondo, "we sat at the table at the back".
12. a medias (lines 10 and 12): half each, split equally between two people. Example: Lo pagamos a medias, "we pay half each".
13. Que ya está pagado (line 16): an answer can start with que when it gives the reason asked for, "the thing is, it's already paid". Example: ¿Qué pasa? Que no tengo llaves, "what's up? I haven't got my keys".
14. dice que es la madre de Javier (line 16): decir que plus the indicative reports what someone says about themselves, here the woman claims to be Javier's mother. Example: Dice que es médico, "she says she is a doctor".

ex1:
- Esta vez lo pago yo, y la próxima lo pagas tú.
  en: This time I'm paying, and next time you pay (to a friend).
- Ni hablar, no te presto el coche.
  en: No way, I won't lend you the car (to a friend).
- Que sí, que te lo devuelvo mañana.
  en: Yes, I tell you, I'll give it back to you tomorrow (to a friend).
- Pagamos a medias y ya está.
  en: We pay half each and that's that.
- Te invito a un café, venga.
  en: Let me treat you to a coffee, come on (to a friend).

ex2:
- Esa cuenta la [pago] yo, no discutas.
  en: I'm paying that bill, don't argue (to a friend).
- Esta [ronda] la pago yo, de verdad.
  en: Honestly, I am paying for this round (to a friend).
- Nos sentamos en la mesa del [fondo].
  en: We sat at the table at the back.
- Ya me [invitarás] cuando quieras.
  en: You will treat me whenever you like (to a friend).
- [Qué] va, no es para tanto.
  en: Not at all, it's not that bad.

== 4 B2
numeral: Lección cuarta
title: Andalucía
title.en: Andalusia

dialogue:
- Andalucía es la comunidad más al sur de la España peninsular, y en ella se encuentran el mar, la montaña y las llanuras.
  en: Andalusia is the region furthest south on the Spanish mainland, and the sea, the mountains and the plains all meet there.
- Tiene ocho provincias: Almería, Cádiz, Córdoba, Granada, Huelva, Jaén, Málaga y Sevilla.
  en: It has eight provinces: Almería, Cádiz, Córdoba, Granada, Huelva, Jaén, Málaga and Seville.
- Su capital es Sevilla, situada a orillas del Guadalquivir, que atraviesa la ciudad.
  en: Its capital is Seville, set on the banks of the Guadalquivir, which runs through the city.
- Su símbolo más conocido es la Giralda, que fue el minarete de una mezquita y hoy es el campanario de la catedral.
  en: Its best-known symbol is the Giralda, once the minaret of a mosque and now the bell tower of the cathedral.
- En Córdoba, la Mezquita conserva un bosque de columnas, y en medio se levanta una catedral construida dentro del antiguo edificio.
  en: In Córdoba, the Mezquita keeps a forest of columns, and in the middle stands a cathedral built inside the old building.
- En Granada, la Alhambra fue el palacio de los reyes nazaríes, y está situada sobre una colina, frente al Albaicín.
  en: In Granada, the Alhambra was the palace of the Nasrid kings, and it stands on a hill facing the Albaicín.
- El dos de enero de mil cuatrocientos noventa y dos, Granada fue entregada a los Reyes Católicos.
  en: On 2 January 1492, Granada was handed over to the Catholic Monarchs.
- Jaén, Córdoba y Sevilla tienen olivares que llegan hasta el horizonte, y el aceite es una de sus grandes riquezas.
  en: Jaén, Córdoba and Seville have olive groves that stretch to the horizon, and olive oil is one of their great riches.
- Al suroeste, Doñana es un parque nacional de marismas, donde descansan muchas aves migratorias.
  en: To the south-west, Doñana is a national park of marshes, where many migratory birds rest.
- En la Sierra Nevada, cerca de Granada, está el Mulhacén, que supera los tres mil metros: es el pico más alto de la península ibérica.
  en: In the Sierra Nevada, near Granada, stands the Mulhacén, which is over three thousand metres high: it is the highest peak in the Iberian Peninsula.
- En Jerez de la Frontera, cerca de Cádiz, se elabora el jerez, que lleva el nombre de la ciudad.
  en: In Jerez de la Frontera, near Cádiz, sherry is made, and it takes its name from the town.
- En la mesa aparecen el gazpacho, el salmorejo cordobés y el jamón de Jabugo, de la provincia de Huelva.
  en: On the table there are gazpacho, salmorejo from Córdoba and the ham from Jabugo, in the province of Huelva.
- La Feria de Abril es en Sevilla, en abril, y en sus casetas se baila hasta la madrugada.
  en: The April Fair is held in Seville, in April, and people dance in its tents until the early hours.
- Pero la verdadera tradición de la región es la sobremesa: se come tarde, se habla mucho y nadie tiene prisa.
  en: But the real tradition of the region is the long talk after lunch: people eat late, talk a lot and nobody is in a hurry.
- Y para terminar, un plato de pescaíto frito, cuyo nombre ya ha perdido la d por el camino.
  en: And to finish, a plate of pescaíto frito, small fried fish, whose name has already lost its d on the way.

notes.en:
1. Andalucía (line 1): the Spanish name of the region, which the English calls Andalusia. The regions of Spain are called comunidades, and this one is in the south.
2. ocho provincias (line 2): a province is a smaller division inside a region, and Andalusia has eight of them. Sevilla is spelled without a c, as Seville is in English.
3. situada a orillas del Guadalquivir (line 3): a participle phrase, which describes a place without a verb of its own. The participle agrees with the noun, so situada is feminine for Sevilla. Example: Una casa situada en el centro, "a house situated in the centre".
4. fue el minarete (line 4): ser with fue says what something was, a role that has ended, while hoy es says what it is now. Example: Antes era una escuela y hoy es un museo, "it used to be a school and today it is a museum".
5. construida dentro del antiguo edificio (line 5): another participle phrase, with the same agreement as note 3. Example: Una carta escrita a mano, "a letter written by hand".
6. está situada sobre una colina (line 6): estar with a participle describes where something is and how it stands now. Example: El pueblo está situado en un valle, "the village is set in a valley".
7. fue entregada (line 7): ser with a participle is the passive, and it reports an action that happened. Example: La carta fue enviada ayer, "the letter was sent yesterday". Compare the state with estar in note 6.
8. olivares (line 8): an olivar is an olive grove, from olivo, "olive tree". The -ar ending names the place where a plant grows, as naranjal is an orange grove. Example: Pasamos por un naranjal, "we went past an orange grove".
9. marismas (line 9): marshes, low flat land that floods when the rivers rise and the sea comes in. Example: Las marismas están inundadas en invierno, "the marshes are flooded in winter".
10. supera los tres mil metros (line 10): supera means "is over" or "goes beyond", and it is used for a height as well as a number. Example: El avión supera los diez mil metros, "the plane flies above ten thousand metres".
11. el jerez (line 11): jerez, with a small letter, is the wine, and Jerez with a capital is the town it comes from. The English word sherry comes from the same name. Example: Me tomo un jerez seco, "I'll have a dry sherry".
12. el salmorejo cordobés (line 12): salmorejo is a thick cold soup of tomato and bread, from Córdoba. Gazpacho is thinner, so the two are not the same soup. Example: El salmorejo es más espeso que el gazpacho, "salmorejo is thicker than gazpacho".
13. La Feria de Abril es en Sevilla (line 13): where an event takes place takes ser, while where a thing is takes estar. So Sevilla está en Andalucía, but la feria es en Sevilla. Example: La reunión es en mi casa, "the meeting is at my house".
14. la sobremesa (line 14): the time spent at the table after a meal, talking, and not hurrying to leave. Example: Después de la comida hubo una buena sobremesa, "after the meal there was a long talk at the table".
15. pescaíto frito (line 15): pescaíto is the diminutive of pescado, "fish", and it is used for small fried fish. The d of -ado is dropped in speech, as in the Andalusian accent of Foundation, lesson 94. Example: Un plato de pescaíto, "a plate of small fried fish".

- Andalucía es la región más al sur de la España peninsular.
  en: Andalusia is the region furthest south on the Spanish mainland.
- La Giralda fue el minarete de una mezquita.
  en: The Giralda was the minaret of a mosque.
- La catedral está construida dentro de la antigua mezquita.
  en: The cathedral is built on the site of the old mosque.
- La boda es en Sevilla, en una iglesia del centro.
  en: The wedding is in Seville, in a church in the centre.
- Las marismas están inundadas en invierno.
  en: The marshes are flooded in winter.

ex2:
- El pueblo [está] situado en un valle.
  en: The village is set in a valley.
- La carta [fue] enviada ayer.
  en: The letter was sent yesterday.
- Una casa [situada] en el centro.
  en: A house situated in the centre.
- La feria [es] en abril.
  en: The fair is in April.

  en: Today the building is a museum.

== 5 B2
numeral: Lección quinta
title: Las doce uvas
title.en: The twelve grapes

dialogue:
- Durante años, Ramón no consiguió comerse las doce uvas de Nochevieja: cada año se atragantaba antes de la última campanada.
  en: For years Ramón failed to eat the twelve grapes on New Year's Eve: every year he choked before the last chime.
- Su mujer lo conoció en una cena de Nochevieja y, desde entonces, se ríe de sus propósitos.
  en: His wife met him at a New Year's Eve dinner, and she has laughed at his resolutions ever since.
- Ese año, por fin, decidió que lo iba a conseguir, y se preparó durante una semana entera.
  en: That year, at last, he decided he would manage it, and he trained for a whole week.
- Compró uvas sin pepitas, las pelaba una a una y las contaba en un cuenco azul.
  en: He bought seedless grapes, peeled them one by one and counted them into a blue bowl.
- Su hija se reía de él, y su mujer le preguntaba si el cuenco era para la ensalada.
  en: His daughter laughed at him, and his wife asked whether the bowl was for the salad.
- La noche del treinta y uno, la familia se sentó delante de la tele, y Ramón puso el cuenco en la mesita.
  en: On the night of the thirty-first the family sat down in front of the telly, and Ramón put the bowl on the little table.
- Su hija miraba el móvil, pero él no le hizo caso.
  en: His daughter was looking at her phone, but he paid her no attention (he did not make any case of her).
- Faltaban cinco minutos. Ramón se acomodó en el sofá con las uvas en la mano.
  en: There were five minutes to go. Ramón settled into the sofa with the grapes in his hand.
- Cuando empezaron las campanadas, se metió la primera uva en la boca.
  en: When the chimes began, he put the first grape in his mouth.
- A la tercera ya tenía la boca llena, y a la sexta empezó a sudar.
  en: By the third he had his mouth full, and by the sixth he had started to sweat.
- Justo cuando se metía la octava, su mujer le ofreció un vaso de agua, y él lo rechazó: no quería perder el ritmo.
  en: Just as he was putting in the eighth, his wife offered him a glass of water, and he turned it down: he did not want to lose the rhythm.
- En ese momento se le cayó una uva debajo del sofá, y tuvo que agacharse a buscarla sin dejar de contar.
  en: At that moment a grape fell under the sofa, and he had to bend down to find it without stopping his count.
- Al terminar la undécima, tenía la cara roja, pero no quiso parar.
  en: After the eleventh he had a red face, but he refused to stop.
- De repente, la tele mostró la plaza llena de gente, y las campanadas sonaron por todo el salón.
  en: Suddenly the telly showed the square full of people, and the chimes rang out through the whole living room.
- Ramón se metió la última uva en la boca, levantó la copa y por fin pudo gritar que lo había conseguido.
  en: Ramón put the last grape in his mouth, raised his glass and at last managed to shout that he had done it.
- Su hija, que había dejado el móvil, supo enseguida lo que pasaba, y lo miró sin decir nada.
  en: His daughter, who had put down her phone, found out at once what was going on, and looked at him without a word.
- Papá, eso es la repetición del año pasado. Las campanadas de verdad ya sonaron hace un minuto.
  en: Dad, that is last year's rerun. The real chimes rang out a minute ago.

notes.en:
1. no consiguió (line 1): the preterite gives the result that never came, so he never managed it. se atragantaba, in the same line, is the imperfect, because it was what happened every year.
2. conoció (line 2): conocer in the preterite means "met", the moment of getting to know someone. In the imperfect it means "knew", as in sabía. Example: Conocí a mi vecino en una boda, "I met my neighbour at a wedding".
3. decidió (line 3): the preterite gives the moment of the decision, while the imperfect iba a conseguir describes what he was going to do. Example: Decidí que iba a cambiar de trabajo, "I decided I was going to change jobs".
4. pelaba, contaba (line 4): the imperfect describes the way he was doing it, one grape after another, as background, while compró is a single event. Example: Compró el pan y lo llevaba en una bolsa, "he bought the bread and was carrying it in a bag".
5. se reía, preguntaba (line 5): the imperfect for the scene, what was going on around the event. Example: Su hermana se reía mientras yo cocinaba, "my sister was laughing while I cooked".
6. no le hizo caso (line 7): hacer caso means to pay attention to someone, and the pronoun le shows who was ignored. Example: No le hagas caso, "don't pay any attention to him".
7. Justo cuando se metía la octava (line 11): justo cuando plus the imperfect sets what was happening at the very moment another event took place. Example: Justo cuando salía de casa, sonó el teléfono, "just as I was leaving the house, the phone rang".
8. En ese momento (line 12): a fixed phrase that points to the moment of an event. Example: En ese momento se apagó la luz, "at that moment the light went out".
9. tuvo que agacharse (line 12): tener que in the preterite means "had to" and it was done. Example: Tuve que esperar una hora, "I had to wait an hour".
10. Al terminar la undécima (line 13): al plus an infinitive means "on finishing", and the subject of both actions is the same person. Example: Al llegar, lo primero fue la catedral, "on arrival, the first thing was the cathedral" (Foundation, lesson 58).
11. no quiso parar (line 13): the preterite of querer with no means "refused", as a decision not taken. The imperfect quería means "wanted". Example: No quise decirle nada, "I refused to tell him anything".
12. De repente (line 14): all of a sudden, an event that comes without warning. Example: De repente empezó a llover, "suddenly it began to rain".
13. por fin pudo gritar (line 15): the preterite of poder means "managed", as the result of an effort. Example: Por fin pude terminar el libro, "at last I managed to finish the book".
14. lo había conseguido (line 15): the pluperfect gives what had already happened before the shout. Example: Cuando llegué, ya habían cenado, "when I arrived, they had already eaten" (Foundation, lesson 53).
15. supo enseguida (line 16): saber in the preterite means "found out" or "learned", a single moment; the imperfect sabía means "knew". Example: Lo supe por la radio, "I heard about it on the radio" (Foundation, lesson 42).

ex1:
- Conocí a mi marido en una fiesta de Nochevieja.
  en: I met my husband at a New Year's Eve party.
- Justo cuando salía, empezó a llover.
  en: Just as I was leaving, it started to rain.
- Por fin pude terminar el libro.
  en: At last I managed to finish the book.
- Lo supe por la radio, esta mañana.
  en: I found out about it on the radio this morning.
- No quise decirle nada.
  en: I refused to tell him anything.

ex2:
- Ayer [supe] que habían llegado los primos.
  en: Yesterday I found out that the cousins had arrived.
- Cuando llegamos, la tele ya [estaba] encendida.
  en: When we arrived, the telly was already on.
- Al [terminar] la cena, nos fuimos a dormir.
  en: After finishing dinner, we went to bed.
- Mi hijo [se echó] a reír cuando vio al gato.
  en: My son burst out laughing when he saw the cat.
- Por fin [pudo] salir de casa a las diez.
  en: At last he managed to leave the house at ten.

numeral: Lección sexta
title: ¡Qué calor!
title.en: So hot!

dialogue:
- Nada más salir del hotel, un turista inglés decide dar un paseo por Sevilla: hace un calor de muerte, son las tres de una tarde de agosto.
  en: As soon as he leaves the hotel, an English tourist decides to go for a walk in Seville: it is roasting, three o'clock on an August afternoon (it is a heat of death).
- Las calles están vacías, las persianas bajadas y hay un bochorno que se pega a la piel.
  en: The streets are empty, the shutters are down and there is a muggy heat that sticks to the skin.
- Nada más doblar la esquina, se da cuenta de que no hay nadie.
  en: As soon as he turns the corner, he realises there is nobody about.
- Por fin ve a otro hombre paseando, y es otro inglés: los dos se miran la mar de sorprendidos.
  en: At last he sees another man out walking, and he is another Englishman: the two of them look at each other, really surprised.
- En Madrid, una mujer no se fía del hombre del tiempo, sino de la cruz verde de la farmacia de la esquina.
  en: In Madrid, a woman does not trust the weatherman, but the green cross of the chemist's on the corner.
- En cuanto sale de casa, mira la cruz, y la cruz decide si lleva abanico o paraguas.
  en: As soon as she leaves home, she looks at the cross, and the cross decides whether she takes a fan or an umbrella.
- Un día, el hombre del tiempo anuncia el fin de la ola de calor: veintidós grados por la tarde.
  en: One day the weatherman announces the end of the heatwave: twenty-two degrees in the afternoon.
- Pero a las cuatro y pico ella mira la cruz y lee un cuarenta y uno.
  en: But at just after four she looks at the cross and reads forty-one.
- Vuelve a casa, baja las persianas y pasa la tarde con el abanico.
  en: She goes back home, pulls down the blinds and spends the afternoon with her fan.
- Por la noche, una vecina le cuenta que la tarde ha sido de lo más agradable, y que la cruz, a esa hora, no daba los grados: daba la hora, las cuatro y cuarenta y uno.
  en: In the evening a neighbour tells her that the afternoon was as pleasant as can be, and that at that moment the cross was not showing the degrees: it was showing the time, forty-one minutes past four.
- Un visitante llega a Burgos en mayo, con una camiseta de manga corta y bastante optimismo.
  en: A visitor arrives in Burgos in May, in a short-sleeved shirt and with quite a lot of optimism.
- Apenas ha dejado la maleta, el encargado del hotel le recita el viejo refrán castellano: nueve meses de invierno y tres de infierno.
  en: He has barely put down his suitcase when the hotel manager recites the old Castilian saying: nine months of winter and three of hell.
- El visitante se pone serio y pregunta en cuál de las dos estaciones ha llegado.
  en: The visitor looks serious and asks which of the two seasons he has arrived in.
- A la mañana siguiente, a las ocho, hay escarcha en las ventanas del comedor, y él desayuna con el abrigo puesto.
  en: The next morning, at eight, there is frost on the dining-room windows, and he has his breakfast with his coat on.
- Vuelve a preguntarlo, y el camarero mira el sol, que ya asoma, y contesta sin dudar.
  en: He asks again, and the waiter looks at the sun, which is already coming out, and answers without a moment's doubt.
- Hoy tiene usted suerte: los dos.
  en: Today you are in luck: both of them.

notes.en:
1. Nada más salir (line 1), nada más doblar (line 3): nada más plus an infinitive means "as soon as", and the same person does both actions. Example: Nada más llegar, me puse a trabajar, "as soon as I arrived, I got down to work".
2. hace un calor de muerte (line 1): literally "it makes a heat of death", the set phrase for extreme heat. The same pattern works for cold: hace un frío de muerte, "it is bitterly cold".
3. bochorno (line 2): a close, muggy heat with no breeze, the kind that sticks to the skin. Example: Hay mucho bochorno antes de la tormenta, "it's very muggy before the storm".
4. chispea, chaparrón, refresca: chispea is a light drizzle, a chaparrón is a sudden heavy shower, and refrescar used of the weather means that it gets cooler. Example: Ha caído un chaparrón y ahora refresca, "a heavy shower came down and now it's cooler".
5. la mar de sorprendidos (line 4): la mar de plus an adjective is familiar and emphatic, "really" or "awfully", and it can be used for good things too. Example: Estoy la mar de contento, "I'm really pleased".
6. En cuanto sale (line 6): en cuanto plus the present indicative describes what happens each time; the future form takes the subjunctive (Foundation, lesson 73). Example: En cuanto llega el verano, se va a la playa, "as soon as summer comes, she goes to the beach".
7. la ola de calor (line 7): a heatwave, where ola is a wave, the same word as in the sea. Example: Hay una ola de calor en toda la península, "there is a heatwave across the whole peninsula".
8. de lo más agradable (line 10): de lo más plus an adjective means "as much as can be", a strong intensifier. Example: Es de lo más amable, "he is as kind as can be".
9. bastante optimismo (line 11): bastante before a noun means "quite a lot". It also means "enough", so the context decides: Tenemos bastante dinero, "we have enough money". Before an adjective, bastante caliente means "rather hot", not "very hot".
10. Apenas ha dejado (line 12): apenas with the perfect means "hardly", and it is followed by the next event at once. Example: Apenas he llegado, ya quieren irse, "I have hardly arrived and they already want to leave".
11. el viejo refrán castellano (line 12): a refrán is a proverb, and castellano refers to Castile, the old kingdom at the heart of the peninsula. Example: Hay un refrán para cada cosa, "there is a saying for everything".
12. en cuál de las dos estaciones (line 13): cuál asks which one of a small set, here two seasons. Example: ¿Cuál de los dos prefieres?, "which of the two do you prefer?"
13. Hoy tiene usted suerte: los dos (line 16): the waiter uses usted with the visitor, the form for a stranger, and the answer turns the saying around, so that both seasons arrive in one day. Example: Tiene usted razón, "you are right" (formal).

  en: As soon as we went in, we noticed the muggy heat.
- Hace un frío de muerte en enero.
  en: It is bitterly cold in January.
- Estoy la mar de contento con el resultado.
  en: I'm really pleased with the result (to a friend).
- En cuanto llega el verano, nos vamos a la playa.
  en: As soon as summer comes, we go to the beach.
- Hay bastante gente en la plaza esta noche.
  en: There are quite a lot of people in the square tonight.

ex2:
- Nada más [llegar], me puse a trabajar.
  en: As soon as I arrived, I got down to work.
- Hay una [ola] de calor en toda la península.
  en: There is a heatwave across the whole peninsula.
- Es de lo más [amable], siempre tiene una sonrisa.
  en: He is as kind as can be; he always has a smile.
- Apenas he [llegado], ya quieren irse.
  en: I have hardly arrived, and they already want to leave.
- ¿[Cuál] de los dos prefieres?
  en: Which of the two do you prefer?

- Los amigos lo querían, aunque algunos decían que era un poco pesado con la puntualidad.

- Él no lo entendía: para él, llegar tarde era una falta de respeto, y no soportaba que alguien se lo tomara a broma.
  en: He did not understand it: for him, arriving late was a lack of respect, and he could not stand anyone taking it as a joke.
- Con los años se había vuelto algo más flexible, o eso decía él.
  en: Over the years he had become a little more flexible, or so he said.
- Pero seguía sin soportar que alguien dijera «hacia las nueve».
  en: But he still could not bear anyone saying "around nine".
- Un viernes de octubre, sus amigos lo invitaron a cenar en casa de Marcos. La invitación decía «a las ocho en punto».
  en: One Friday in October his friends invited him to dinner at Marcos's flat. The invitation said "eight o'clock sharp".
- Julián salió a las siete y media, pero el autobús se averió en la avenida y no llegó a casa de Marcos hasta las nueve.
  en: Julián left at half past seven, but the bus broke down on the avenue and did not reach Marcos's flat until nine.
- Por primera vez en su vida llegaba tarde, y se sentía raro, como si hubiera olvidado algo.
  en: For the first time in his life he was late, and he felt odd, as if he had forgotten something.
- Llamó al timbre, y Marcos le abrió sonriendo, con una copa en la mano.
  en: He rang the bell, and Marcos opened the door smiling, with a glass in his hand.
- Dentro sonaba música, y nadie le preguntó por el retraso: todos estaban sentados a la mesa, con las copas llenas y cara de haber llegado a su hora.
  en: Music was playing inside, and nobody asked him about the delay: everyone was sitting at the table, with full glasses and the look of having arrived on time.
- Julián miró a su alrededor y no supo qué decir, porque nunca había visto a sus amigos tan puntuales.
  en: Julián looked around and did not know what to say, because he had never seen his friends so punctual.
- Marcos le sirvió otra copa y le pidió que se sentara, que todo estaba listo.
  en: Marcos poured him another glass and asked him to sit down, because everything was ready.
- Julián se sentó sin entender nada, y miró el reloj: las nueve y diez.
  en: Julián sat down without understanding anything, and looked at his watch: ten past nine.
- Era su fiesta sorpresa, y el único que había llegado tarde era él.
  en: It was his surprise party, and the only one who had arrived late was him.

notes.en:
1. a las nueve (line 1; Foundation, lesson 9): in everyday Spanish a clock time with a las is often a rough guide, and the text says it means more or less nine. Example: Quedamos a las nueve, "let's meet at nine".
2. solía llegar (line 2): soler plus an infinitive gives a habit, "used to" or "usually"; in the past it takes the imperfect, solía or solíamos. Foundation lesson 37 has soler in the present, suelo. Example: Solíamos cenar en el jardín, "we used to have dinner in the garden".
3. antes de que abriera (line 2): antes de que always takes the subjunctive, here the imperfect one, abriera, because the opening is in the past. Example: Salí antes de que llegaran los invitados, "I left before the guests arrived".
4. ya estaba sentado (line 3): estar with a past participle describes the state that has been reached, here already seated; ya adds that it was before the others were. Example: Cuando llegué, la tienda ya estaba cerrada, "when I got there, the shop was already closed".
5. le molestaba que la gente llegara tarde (line 4): a feeling about a whole clause takes que plus the subjunctive, where English uses an ordinary verb: "it annoyed him that people arrived late". The same pattern goes with le extraña que, no soporta que and le parece mal que. Foundation lesson 55 gave es importante que as the same kind of trigger. Example: Le molesta que la gente llegue tarde, "it annoys him that people arrive late".
6. le extrañaba (line 4): in Spain extrañar means to find something strange or surprising, not to miss someone; that is echar de menos, lesson 1, note 2. In much of Latin America extrañar does mean to miss. Example: Me extraña que no haya llamado, "it surprises me that he hasn't rung".
7. acostumbraba a llegar (line 5): acostumbrar a plus an infinitive means "to be in the habit of", a little more formal than soler. Example: Acostumbra a cenar a las diez, "he is in the habit of having dinner at ten".
8. se encontraba bien (line 5): encontrarse bien means to feel well, and it is the usual polite way to ask after someone's health. Example: ¿Te encuentras bien? "Are you all right?" (to a friend).
9. se lo tomara a broma (line 7): tomarse algo a broma means to take something as a joke. Lo is the lateness, and the verb keeps its reflexive se, so the two words come out as se lo. Example: No te lo tomes a mal, "don't take it badly".
10. no soportaba que (line 7; no soportar que, line 9): soportar means "to bear" or "to stand", and it takes que plus the subjunctive when another person does the thing. Example: No soporto que me interrumpan, "I can't stand being interrupted".
11. hacia las nueve (line 9): hacia with a time means around that time, the looser version of the a las nueve of note 1. Example: Nos vemos hacia las siete, "let's meet around seven".
12. como si hubiera olvidado algo (line 12): como si means "as if", and for something imagined in the past it takes the pluperfect subjunctive, hubiera olvidado. Foundation lesson 72 has como si nada. Example: Habla como si lo supiera todo, "he talks as if he knew everything".
13. a su hora (line 14): at the time that was set, on time. It is said of a thing or a person that keeps the agreed hour. Example: El tren llegó a su hora, "the train arrived on time".

  en: I had tidied the house before the guests arrived.

dialogue:
- Ana y Dani llevan tres meses juntos, y por fin toca presentar a sus familias.
  en: Ana and Dani have been together for three months, and at last it is time to introduce their families.
- Ana propone quedar con las dos familias en casa de Dani, para cenar el sábado.
  en: Ana proposes meeting both families at Dani's flat for dinner on Saturday.
- «¿Y en qué quedamos? ¿El sábado o el domingo?», pregunta Dani, que ya no recuerda nada.
  en: "So what did we agree? Saturday or Sunday?" asks Dani, who no longer remembers anything.
- Ana le recuerda que han quedado en el sábado a las nueve, y que su madre lo ha apuntado.
  en: Ana reminds him that they agreed on Saturday at nine, and that her mother has written it down.
- Dani asiente, pero teme quedar mal con su madre si se le olvida algo.
  en: Dani nods, but he is afraid of letting his mother down if he forgets something.
- Ana le asegura que no va a quedar mal con nadie, y que el traje le queda muy bien.
  en: Ana assures him that he will not let anyone down, and that the suit suits him very well.
- Él se mira en el espejo, y el traje le queda grande por los hombros, así que lo lleva al sastre.
  en: He looks in the mirror, and the suit is too big for him across the shoulders, so he takes it to the tailor.
- El sastre dice que tardará dos días, y Dani se queda con la chaqueta vieja, que ya le queda pequeña.
  en: The tailor says it will take two days, so Dani keeps the old jacket, which is too small for him now.
- Aún queda por hacer lo más difícil: decidir quién lleva el postre.
  en: The hardest part still remains to be done: deciding who brings the dessert.
- Ana habla con su madre, que dice que sí, pero que no hará el flan, porque se ha quedado sin azúcar.
  en: Ana speaks to her mother, who says yes, but who will not make the flan, because she has run out of sugar.
- Dani llama a su padre y le pide ayuda para ensayar: su padre se queda de piedra, porque nunca se la ha pedido.
  en: Dani rings his father and asks for help rehearsing: his father is stunned, because Dani has never asked him for help.
- El padre responde que no queda más remedio que hacerlo bien.
  en: His father says there is no choice but to do it properly.
- Ana se queda con el número de la pastelería, por si acaso.
  en: Ana keeps the number of the cake shop, just in case.
- El jueves por la noche, Dani propone quedar otra vez para repasar, y Ana le contesta: «¿Te estás quedando conmigo?».
  en: On Thursday night Dani suggests meeting again to go over everything, and Ana answers, "Are you pulling my leg?"

  en: He swears he is serious, and that there are two days left until Saturday.
- El sábado por la tarde, Ana se prueba el vestido, y le queda perfecto.
  en: On Saturday afternoon Ana tries on her dress, and it fits her perfectly.
- Dani se abotona la chaqueta nueva y se promete que su mente no se quedará en blanco.
  en: Dani buttons up his new jacket and promises himself that his mind will not go blank.
- A las ocho y media, Ana propone echarse un rato antes de que lleguen, y Dani dice que sí.
  en: At half past eight Ana suggests lying down for a while before the guests arrive, and Dani agrees.
- Se tumban en el sofá, con la idea de cerrar los ojos cinco minutos.
  en: They lie down on the sofa, meaning to close their eyes for five minutes.
- A las nueve en punto suena el timbre: los padres de Dani y los de Ana están en la puerta, con una tarta.
  en: At nine sharp the doorbell rings: Dani's parents and Ana's are at the door, with a cake.
- Nadie contesta al timbre, y los cuatro se miran sin saber qué pensar.
  en: Nobody answers the bell, and the four parents look at one another, not knowing what to think.
- Las dos familias han llegado a las nueve, puntuales, tal como habían quedado. Ana y Dani, no: se han quedado dormidos.
  en: The two families have arrived at nine, on time, just as they had arranged. Ana and Dani have not: they have fallen asleep.

notes.en:
1. quedar con las dos familias (sentence 2): quedar con someone means to arrange to meet them. Without se, quedar means to arrange, agree, fit or be left; with se, quedarse means to stay, to keep or to become. Example: He quedado con Marta a las siete, "I've arranged to meet Marta at seven".
2. ¿En qué quedamos? (sentence 3): a question asked when two people are no longer sure what they agreed. The answer is the plan itself. Example: ¿En qué quedamos, el viernes o el sábado? "so what did we agree, Friday or Saturday?"
3. han quedado en el sábado (sentence 4): quedar en with a day, a time or a place means to agree on it, and it always takes en. Before an infinitive it means to agree to do something. Example: Quedamos en vernos el lunes, "we agreed to see each other on Monday".
4. quedar mal con su madre (sentences 5 and 6): quedar mal con someone means to let them down, to fall short of what they expected, and quedar bien is its opposite, to come across well. Example: Quedaste muy bien en la entrevista, "you came across very well at the interview".
5. le queda muy bien (sentence 6): said of clothes, quedar means to fit or to suit. The thing is the subject, and the person wearing it is le, me or te. English turns it round: "it suits him". Example: Esas botas te quedan muy bien, "those boots suit you very well" (to a friend).
6. le queda grande, le queda pequeña (sentences 7 and 8): too big or too small for someone. The adjective agrees with the thing, so grande and pequeña match el traje and la chaqueta. Example: Este abrigo me queda pequeño, "this coat is too small for me".
7. se queda con la chaqueta (sentence 8; sentence 13): quedarse con means to keep something or to take it. Example: Quédate con el cambio, "keep the change" (to a friend).
8. queda por hacer (sentence 9): quedar por plus an infinitive means that something is still left to be done. Example: Queda mucho por hacer en la cocina, "there is still a lot to do in the kitchen".
9. se ha quedado sin azúcar (sentence 10): quedarse sin means to run out of something, literally "to stay without". The se stays in every form. Example: Nos hemos quedado sin gasolina en la autopista, "we ran out of petrol on the motorway".
10. se queda de piedra (sentence 11): quedarse de piedra means to be stunned, to be dumbfounded; the literal image is "to stay of stone". Example: Me quedé de piedra al ver el precio, "I was stunned when I saw the price".
11. no queda más remedio (sentence 12): no quedar más remedio que plus an infinitive means there is no other option. Example: No queda más remedio que esperar, "there is no choice but to wait".
12. ¿Te estás quedando conmigo? (sentence 14): familiar in Spain, it means to be teasing someone, to pull their leg. The literal sense is "are you staying with me?", and the idiom is the same with the verb in the present, as in the line.
13. quedan dos días (sentence 15): quedar as "to be left", said of time and of things. The verb agrees with what is left. Example: Quedan tres semanas para las vacaciones, "there are three weeks left until the holidays".
14. quedarse en blanco (sentence 17): to go blank, to lose one's mind for a moment; en blanco means blank, as a page is blank. Foundation, lesson 61, has the same pattern with quedarse and an adjective. Example: Me quedé en blanco en el examen, "my mind went blank in the exam".
15. se han quedado dormidos (sentence 22): quedarse dormido means "to fall asleep", and the adjective agrees with the people in the sentence, so dormidos is plural. Foundation, lesson 61, has quedarse with an adjective. Example: Me quedé dormida en el tren, "I fell asleep on the train" (said by a woman).

== 10 B2
numeral: Lección décima
title: El puente
title.en: The long weekend

dialogue:
- En este país el año se planifica según el calendario de festivos, y en enero todo el mundo ya cuenta los puentes.
  en: In this country the year is planned around the holiday calendar, and in January everybody is already counting the bridges.
- Un puente es un día libre que une un festivo con el fin de semana: si el festivo cae en jueves, el viernes se convierte en puente.
  en: A bridge is a day off that links a holiday to the weekend: if the holiday falls on a Thursday, the Friday becomes a bridge.
- Un puente de cuatro días es un puente largo, y uno de cinco ya es un acueducto.
  en: A bridge of four days is a long bridge, and one of five is already an aqueduct.
- En enero nadie habla del sueldo: todos hablan de dónde cae el puente.
  en: In January nobody talks about salary: everyone talks about where the bridge falls.
- A lo mejor hacemos puente el viernes, dice el jefe de ventas, y todo el departamento se pone a sacar cuentas.
  en: Maybe we'll take Friday off and make a long weekend, says the sales manager, and the whole department starts working out the sums.
- Quizá nos den el viernes, dice otro, aunque nadie ha visto el decreto.
  en: Perhaps they will give us Friday, says another, though nobody has seen the decree.
- Puede que el Gobierno lo apruebe, pero también puede que no, y en la oficina ya hay apuestas.
  en: The government may approve it, but it may also not, and there are already bets in the office.
- Igual nos dan el viernes, dice la secretaria, que lleva veinte años en la empresa y nunca se equivoca.
  en: Maybe they'll give us Friday, says the secretary, who has been with the firm for twenty years and is never wrong.
- Lo más seguro es que el decreto llegue el último día de enero, cuando ya no quede tiempo para nada.
  en: The most likely thing is that the decree will arrive on the last day of January, when there is no time left for anything.
- Se prevé que el año tenga once puentes y dos acueductos, según las cuentas de Recursos Humanos.
  en: It is forecast that the year will have eleven bridges and two aqueducts, according to the figures from Human Resources.
- El director presenta la previsión anual con una gráfica en forma de arco, y todos aplauden la forma.
  en: The director presents the annual forecast with a graph in the shape of an arch, and everyone applauds the shape.
- Los trabajadores suman los días que les tocan: diez días de vacaciones, cuatro puentes y un acueducto, si Dios quiere.
  en: The workers add up the days they are due: ten days of holiday, four bridges and an aqueduct, God willing.
- Las carreteras se llenan los jueves por la tarde, y los hoteles de la costa se preparan con un mes de antelación.
  en: The roads fill up on Thursday afternoons, and the hotels on the coast get ready a month in advance.
- Un pueblo de la costa vive del puente, y tiene un cartel que dice: «Llegue el puente que llegue, aquí le esperamos».
  en: A coastal village lives off the bridges, and has a sign that says: "Whatever bridge comes, we will be waiting for you here."
- Solo hay un empleado que nunca se toma un puente. Se llama Ernesto, y su mesa siempre está ordenada.
  en: There is only one employee who never takes a bridge. His name is Ernesto, and his desk is always tidy.
- Sus compañeros lo miran con respeto, pero no entienden por qué no quiere un día libre cuando se lo ofrecen.
  en: His colleagues look at him with respect, but they do not understand why he does not want a day off when it is offered to him.
- En diciembre, el jefe anuncia que este año habrá un acueducto de verdad, y nadie sabe qué significa.
  en: In December the boss announces that this year there will be a real aqueduct, and nobody knows what he means.
- El viernes, Ernesto llega a las ocho, como siempre, y encuentra la oficina cerrada: el acueducto de verdad era para todos, también para él.
  en: On Friday Ernesto arrives at eight, as always, and finds the office shut: the real aqueduct was for everyone, him included.

notes.en:
1. hacer puente (line 5): to take the day between a holiday and the weekend off, so that the break runs on from the holiday. The noun puente is the bridge, the day itself. Example: Este año hacemos puente en mayo, "this year we are taking a bridge in May".
2. acueducto (line 3): literally an aqueduct, the Roman structure that carries water across a valley; in this text it is a playful word for a very long bridge, and un puente largo is the bridge of four days. Example: Un acueducto de tres kilómetros lleva agua a la ciudad, "a three-kilometre aqueduct brings water to the city".
3. cae en jueves (line 2): caer en plus a day means that a holiday falls on that day. Example: La fiesta cae en martes este año, "the holiday falls on a Tuesday this year".
4. a lo mejor hacemos (line 5): a lo mejor means "maybe" and it is followed by the indicative, never the subjunctive. Example: A lo mejor llego tarde, "I may arrive late".
5. quizá nos den (line 6): quizá, quizás and tal vez can take either mood: the indicative for what is likely, and the subjunctive for what is still open. Foundation, lesson 54, has quizás ganen. Example: Quizá vengan mañana, "maybe they will come tomorrow".
6. puede que lo apruebe (line 7): puede que takes the subjunctive, as in Foundation, lesson 54, because the event is not certain. Example: Puede que llueva esta tarde, "it may rain this afternoon".
7. igual nos dan (line 8): igual is the familiar word for "maybe" in Spain, and it is often followed by the indicative in speech. Example: Igual llego tarde, "I might be late" (to a friend).
8. lo más seguro es que llegue (line 9): lo más seguro es que means "the most likely thing is that", and the verb after que takes the subjunctive, since the event is not yet known. Example: Lo más seguro es que el tren llegue con retraso, "the train is most likely to be late".
9. se prevé que tenga (line 10): se prevé que means "it is forecast that", and the verb after que takes the subjunctive. Example: Se prevé que el precio de la luz suba en invierno, "the price of electricity is expected to rise in winter".
10. sacar cuentas (line 5): sacar cuentas means to work out the sums, close in sense to echar cuentas, lesson 1, note 12. Example: Saca cuentas antes de comprar el coche, "she works out the sums before buying the car".
11. llegue el puente que llegue (line 14): the verb comes twice, after the noun and after que, and both are in the subjunctive, as in pase lo que pase, Foundation, lesson 85. Example: Pase lo que pase, nos vemos el sábado, "whatever happens, we will see each other on Saturday".
12. en forma de arco (line 11): en forma de plus a noun means "in the shape of". Example: El edificio tiene forma de barco, "the building is shaped like a ship".

ex1:
- A lo mejor llego un poco tarde, no me esperéis.
  en: Maybe I'll be a little late, don't wait for me (to friends).
- Quizá vengan mañana, pero no estoy seguro.
  en: Perhaps they will come tomorrow, but I'm not sure.

  en: It may be cold tonight, take a jacket (to a friend).

  en: The train is most likely to be late.
- Se prevé que el precio de la luz suba en invierno.
  en: The price of electricity is expected to rise in winter.

  en: This year the holiday falls on a Tuesday.

  en: It may rain this afternoon.

  en: Most likely the boss will give us Friday.

  en: It is forecast that the year will have eleven bridges.

  en: There is a saying about meigas, witches. In Galician: "Eu non creo nas meigas, mais habelas, hainas". In Spanish: "No creo en las brujas, pero haberlas, haylas": I don't believe in witches, but there are some.
- Del mar sale el marisco, y el pulpo á feira es uno de los platos más famosos: cocido, cortado a tijera y aliñado con pimentón y aceite.
  en: The sea provides the shellfish, and pulpo á feira is one of the most famous dishes: boiled, cut with scissors and dressed with paprika and oil.
- La empanada gallega, de atún o de zamburiñas, es un pan relleno que se come en casi cualquier bar.
  en: Galician empanada, with tuna or with zamburiñas, is a filled bread eaten in almost any bar.
- Para beber, el albariño, un vino blanco de las Rías Baixas, que va muy bien con el marisco.
  en: To drink, there is albariño, a white wine from the Rías Baixas, which goes very well with shellfish.
- En la costa, las casas de piedra tienen hórreos, graneros elevados sobre pilares para que la humedad no llegue al grano.
  en: On the coast, the stone houses have hórreos, granaries raised on pillars so that the damp does not reach the grain.
- En Santiago, la catedral recibe cada año a peregrinos de todo el mundo.
  en: In Santiago, the cathedral welcomes pilgrims from all over the world every year.
- En Ourense, el agua sale caliente de la tierra, y hay termas junto al río Miño.
  en: In Ourense, the water comes up hot out of the ground, and there are thermal baths beside the River Miño.
- Dicen que aquí se come bien, y que uno nunca sabe si va a llover.
  en: They say the food here is good, and that you never know whether it is going to rain.
- Y para terminar, el queso de tetilla, que lleva el nombre de su forma.
  en: And to finish, tetilla cheese, which takes its name from its shape.

notes.en:
1. A Coruña (line 1): the Galician name of the city, which in Spanish is La Coruña. Lugo and Pontevedra are the same in both languages. Example: Viajamos a La Coruña en tren, "we travelled to A Coruña by train".
2. Camino de Santiago (line 2; Foundation, lesson 57): the pilgrims' route, which Foundation, lesson 57, describes; this text only names its end. Example: Hacer el Camino de Santiago lleva semanas, "walking the Camino de Santiago takes weeks".
3. faro (line 3): a lighthouse, the Spanish word for the same thing. Example: El faro marca la entrada del puerto, "the lighthouse marks the entrance to the harbour".
4. casi completa (line 4): casi plus an adjective means "almost", and completa agrees with the feminine muralla. Example: La casa está casi terminada, "the house is almost finished".
5. rías (line 5): a ría is a long inlet where the sea runs into a river valley; English has the word ria from it. Example: Vivimos junto a la ría, "we live beside the inlet".
6. cooficial (line 6): co-official, shared by two languages in the same region. Galician is the language of Galicia, close to Portuguese, and it is not a dialect of Spanish. Example: En Galicia, el gallego y el castellano son cooficiales, "in Galicia, Galician and Spanish are both official".
7. rúa, calle (line 7): rúa is the Galician word and calle the Spanish, both meaning street. Example: Vivo en una calle tranquila, "I live on a quiet street" (in Galician, rúa).
8. morriña (line 8): a Galician word for homesickness, longing for one's own land, now used in Spanish too. Example: Tiene morriña de su tierra, "she is homesick for her land".
9. Eu non creo nas meigas (line 9): a Galician saying. Mais is Galician for pero, and habelas, hainas is haberlas, haylas, which the Spanish line gives. Example: Pero haberlas, haylas, "but there are some", the Spanish form of the saying.
10. pulpo á feira (line 10): octopus of the fair, a Galician dish; in Spanish menus it is often called pulpo a la gallega. Example: Pedimos pulpo a la gallega de primero, "we ordered Galician-style octopus to start".
11. empanada (line 11): a filled bread, here with tuna or zamburiñas, a small scallop. Example: Una empanada de atún, "a tuna empanada".
12. albariño (line 12): a white wine from the Rías Baixas, in Spanish Rías Bajas. Example: Un albariño bien frío, "a well-chilled albariño".
13. hórreos (line 13): granaries raised on stone or wood pillars, so that the damp does not reach the grain. Example: El hórreo guarda el maíz, "the granary keeps the maize".
14. dicen que, aquí se come (line 14): dicen que means "they say that", and aquí se come is the impersonal se, "people eat here", for a general truth. Example: Aquí se habla gallego en la calle, "here people speak Galician in the street".
15. tetilla (line 17): literally "small nipple", and the cheese takes its name from its rounded shape. Example: Un trozo de queso de tetilla, "a piece of tetilla cheese".

- Me encantan las empanadas de atún, sobre todo recién hechas.
  en: I love tuna empanadas, especially freshly made.
- Después de un mes en Madrid, tenía morriña de Galicia.
  en: After a month in Madrid, I was homesick for Galicia.
- Dicen que en Galicia se come de maravilla.
  en: They say you eat wonderfully in Galicia.
- Ayer probamos el albariño en un bar junto a la ría.
  en: Yesterday we tried the albariño in a bar beside the inlet.
- Me gusta el pulpo a la gallega, sobre todo con un poco de pimentón.
  en: I like Galician-style octopus, especially with a little paprika.

ex2:
- En Galicia se habla gallego, una lengua [cooficial].
  en: In Galicia people speak Galician, a co-official language.
- La [torre] de Hércules es un faro de origen romano.
  en: The Tower of Hercules is a lighthouse of Roman origin.
- Me gusta el vino [blanco] de las Rías Baixas.
  en: I like the white wine from the Rías Baixas.
- Dicen que aquí [se] come bien.
  en: They say you eat well here.
- Lugo tiene una muralla [romana] casi completa.
  en: Lugo has an almost complete Roman wall.

== 12 B2
numeral: Lección doce
title: Acogedor y luminoso
title.en: Cosy and bright

dialogue:
- Pasen, pasen. Este es el piso del que les hablé por teléfono. Es muy acogedor.
  en: Come in, come in. This is the flat I told you about on the phone. It is very cosy.
- ¿Acogedor? Es muy pequeño, ¿no?
  en: Cosy? It's very small, isn't it?
- No es pequeño, sino práctico. Cada metro cuenta.
  en: It's not small, it's practical. Every metre counts.
- Bueno. ¿Y hay mucha luz? Aquí está todo un poco oscuro.
  en: Well. And is there a lot of light? It's all a bit dark in here.
- Es muy luminoso. No tiene cortinas, así que de noche la farola de la calle lo ilumina todo.
  en: It's very bright. It has no curtains, so at night the street lamp lights up the whole place.
- Ya veo. ¿Y las paredes? Están un poco feas.
  en: I see. And the walls? They're a bit ugly.
- Es un piso para reformar: no hace falta arreglar nada, sino empezar de cero.
  en: It's a flat to renovate: there's no need to repair anything, but to start again from scratch.
- Nuestro presupuesto es muy justo. No podemos gastar mucho.
  en: Our budget is very tight. We can't spend much.
- Tranquilos. Con lo que pueden pagar, no hay muchos pisos así.
  en: Don't worry. With what you can pay, there aren't many flats like this.
- ¿Y el ruido? Se oye mucho desde la calle.
  en: And the noise? You can hear a lot from the street.
- No solo es céntrico, sino también muy animado: los bares cierran a las tres, y nunca estarán solos.
  en: Not only is it central, it is also very lively: the bars close at three, and you will never be alone.
- ¿Y cuántas habitaciones tiene?
  en: And how many bedrooms does it have?
- No tiene habitaciones, sino un espacio muy versátil. Aquí cabe todo lo que no usan.
  en: It doesn't have bedrooms, but a very versatile space. Everything you don't use fits in here.
- ¿Y dónde dormimos?
  en: And where do we sleep?
- Pues ahí, entre las cajas. Ya verán cómo se acomodan.
  en: Well, there, among the boxes. You'll see how well you settle in.
- ¿Y la cocina? Es muy estrecha.
  en: And the kitchen? It's very narrow.
- No es estrecha, sino compacta. Todo lo que necesitan está a un paso.
  en: It's not narrow, it's compact. Everything you need is one step away.
- Bueno, la verdad es que nos encanta. Nos lo quedamos.
  en: Well, the truth is we love it. We'll take it.
- Me alegro, porque es lo único que pueden pagar. Es un trastero.
  en: I'm glad, because it's the only thing you can afford. It's a storage room.

notes.en:
1. acogedor (line 1): cosy, warm and welcoming. In property adverts the word is a common euphemism, and it can hide a small size, which is why the couple asks in line 2. Example: Es un hotel muy acogedor, "it is a very cosy hotel".
2. sino (line 3): after a negative, sino corrects the first part: no es pequeño, sino práctico, "it is not small but practical". Plain pero would keep both parts: es pequeño pero práctico, "it is small but practical". English "but" does both jobs, so the choice is only clear in Spanish.
3. luminoso (line 5): bright, full of light. The agent uses it for a flat lit only by the lamp in the street. Example: Nuestra cocina es muy luminosa, "our kitchen is very bright".
4. farola (line 5): a street lamp, the post with a lamp on the top. Example: La farola de la esquina se ha fundido, "the street lamp on the corner has gone out".
5. sino empezar (line 7): no hace falta plus an infinitive is "you do not need to", and sino then gives what is needed instead. Example: No hace falta reservar, sino llegar pronto, "you do not need to book, but to arrive early".
6. para reformar (line 7): a flat for renovating; reformar means to renovate, and para plus the infinitive says what the flat is for. Example: Una casa para reformar, "a house to be done up".
7. no solo ... sino también (line 11): the correlative pair for "not only ... but also". Example: No solo habla bien inglés, sino también francés, "he speaks not only good English but also French".
8. versátil (line 13): versatile, able to be used for many purposes. Example: Un mueble versátil sirve de mesa y de estantería, "a versatile piece of furniture works as a table and a shelf".
9. Aquí cabe todo (line 13): caber means to fit, and the thing that fits is the subject. Example: En la maleta no cabe nada más, "nothing more fits in the suitcase".
10. nos encanta (line 18): encantar works like gustar, so the thing liked is the subject and the person is the object pronoun. Example: Me encanta este barrio, "I love this neighbourhood".
11. Me alegro, porque (line 19): alegrarse means to be glad; me alegro followed by a reason with porque, "I'm glad because". Example: Me alegro de que hayas venido, "I'm glad you came".
12. trastero (line 19): a storage room, often a small one in a block of flats, where people keep things they do not use every day. Example: Guardo las bicis en el trastero, "I keep the bikes in the storage room".

ex1:
- No es un coche caro, sino uno práctico.
  en: It is not an expensive car, but a practical one.
- No solo es simpático, sino también muy trabajador.
  en: He is not only friendly, but also very hard-working.
- Esta cocina es pequeña, pero funciona muy bien.
  en: This kitchen is small, but it works very well.
- Nos encanta el barrio, aunque es muy ruidoso.
  en: We love the neighbourhood, although it is very noisy.
- Guardamos las bicis en el trastero.
  en: We keep the bikes in the storage room.

ex2:
- No hace falta llegar pronto, [sino] llegar a tiempo.
  en: You do not need to arrive early, but rather on time.
- Es caro, [pero] vale la pena.
  en: It is expensive, but it is worth it.
- [No] solo es simpático, sino también muy listo.
  en: He is not only friendly, but also very clever.
- Me [encanta] el barrio, aunque hay mucho ruido.
  en: I love the neighbourhood, although there is a lot of noise.
- Ya no [cabe] nada en el armario.
  en: There is no room left in the wardrobe.

== 13 B2
numeral: Lección trece
title: A ver si quedamos
title.en: We must meet up some time

dialogue:
- «¿Qué tal?» es la pregunta que más se hace en España, y la que menos pide respuesta.
  en: "¿Qué tal?" is the question most often asked in Spain, and the one that asks for the least answer.
- Alguien te la hace en el ascensor, tú contestas «bien», y ahí termina todo.
  en: Someone asks you in the lift, you say "bien", and that is the end of it.
- Otras frases son más difíciles, porque no significan lo que dicen.
  en: Other phrases are harder, because they do not mean what they say.
- «A ver si quedamos» es la más famosa. Se dice al despedirse de un amigo al que hace tiempo que no se ve.
  en: "A ver si quedamos" is the most famous. It is said when saying goodbye to a friend you have not seen for a long time.
- Todo el mundo lo dice, y casi nadie lo cumple: puede repetirse durante años sin que nadie proponga una fecha.
  en: Everybody says it, and almost nobody keeps it: it can be repeated for years without anyone proposing a date.
- «Ya te llamaré» no quiere decir que vaya a llamar, sino que la conversación ha terminado.
  en: "Ya te llamaré" does not mean that the caller will ring, but that the conversation is over.
- «En cinco minutos estoy» suele significar veinte, y «ahora voy» significa dentro de un rato.
  en: "En cinco minutos estoy" usually means twenty minutes, and "ahora voy" means in a while.
- «Lo que tú digas» quiere decir que no estás de acuerdo, y «como quieras» que lo vas a lamentar.
  en: "Lo que tú digas" means that I don't agree, and "como quieras" that you will regret it.
- Nadie lo dice en voz alta: el tono lo dice todo.
  en: Nobody says it out loud: the tone says it all.
- Lo que sí se dice con claridad es «cuando puedas» o «donde prefieras», que dejan la elección abierta.
  en: What is said clearly is "cuando puedas" or "donde prefieras", which leave the choice open.
- «Ahora mismo» puede querer decir dentro de un rato, y «luego» puede querer decir cualquier otro día.
  en: "Ahora mismo" can mean in a while, and "luego" can mean any other day.
- Se aprende a escuchar estas frases como se aprende un idioma: poco a poco, y acabas por entender el tono antes que las palabras.
  en: You learn to listen to these phrases the way you learn a language: little by little, and in the end you understand the tone before the words.
- Las palabras que más se usan son las que más esconden.
  en: The words used most are the ones that hide the most.
- Por eso, cuando alguien dice una fecha, la frase suele ser sincera.
  en: So when someone gives a date, the phrase is usually sincere.
- Hay excepciones, claro: el amigo que de verdad quiere quedar dice el día y la hora.
  en: There are exceptions, of course: the friend who really wants to meet says the day and the time.
- Entre tanta frase vacía, esa sí cuenta.
  en: Among so many empty phrases, that one does count.
- Hace diez años, un viejo amigo me escribió: «A ver si quedamos un día». Todavía lo estoy esperando, y acabo de mandarle el mismo mensaje.
  en: Ten years ago an old friend wrote to me: "Let's see if we can meet up one day". I am still waiting, and I have just sent him the same message.

notes.en:
1. ¿Qué tal? (line 1): a greeting that asks a question nobody waits to have answered in full; "bien" closes it. Example: ¿Qué tal el fin de semana? "how was the weekend?" (to a friend), when the answer is short.
2. a ver si quedamos (line 4): a ver si followed by the indicative is "let's see whether", and in this phrase it is a wish with no plan behind it. Quedar means to meet up, as in lesson 9, note 1. Example: A ver si nos vemos el sábado, "let's see if we can meet on Saturday".
3. ya te llamaré (line 6): the future for a vague promise; the speaker does not commit to a day. Example: Ya lo veremos, "we'll see".
4. sino que (line 6): after a negative clause, sino que introduces a verb or a clause. Example: No te llamo para pedirte nada, sino que quiero hablar, "I'm not calling to ask you for anything, but because I want to talk".
5. en cinco minutos estoy (line 7): estar used for a future arrival, "I'll be there", and the time given is often not the time meant. Example: Estoy en la puerta, "I'm at the door".
6. ahora voy (line 7): ir in the present with ahora, "I'm coming", and here it means soon, later. Example: ¿Vienes? Ahora voy, "are you coming? In a bit".
7. lo que tú digas (line 8): the subjunctive of the open choice, the same pattern as pase lo que pase in Foundation, lesson 85. Example: Lo que tú digas, "whatever you say".
8. como quieras (line 8): the same subjunctive of the open choice, here with como. Example: Como quieras, yo me voy, "as you like, I'm going".
9. cuando puedas, donde prefieras (line 10): cuando and donde with a subjunctive leave the choice to the other person. Example: Ven cuando puedas, "come when you can".
10. ahora mismo, luego (line 11): ahora mismo is "right now", and luego is "later". In speech both are used loosely, and the time they name is not always the time meant. Example: Luego te llamo, "I'll call you later".
11. quedar (line 17): the verb of lesson 9, to meet up. Example: He quedado con Marta el martes, "I've arranged to meet Marta on Tuesday".

ex1:
- A ver si quedamos un día de estos, ¿vale?
  en: Let's see if we can meet up one of these days, OK? (to a friend)
- Ya te llamaré, pero no sé cuándo.
  en: I'll call you, but I don't know when.
- Como quieras, pero yo creo que es mejor ir mañana.
  en: As you like, but I think it's better to go tomorrow.
- Pásate por casa cuando puedas.
  en: Drop by the house when you can (to a friend).
- Ahora mismo voy, que me estoy cambiando.
  en: I'm coming right away, I'm getting changed (to a friend).

ex2:
- [A ver si] nos vemos esta semana.
  en: Let's see if we can see each other this week.
- Lo que tú [digas], yo acepto.
  en: Whatever you say, I accept.
- Ya te [llamaré] esta semana.
  en: I'll call you this week.
- Ven [cuando] puedas.
  en: Come when you can.
- Ahora [mismo] te lo traigo.
  en: I'll bring it to you right away.

- Le da vergüenza seguir allí sin pedir nada, y le da igual lo que cueste: pide otro café, y luego otro.
  en: He is embarrassed to stay there without ordering anything, and it does not matter to him what it costs: he orders another coffee, and then another.

  en: Irene writes again: "I'm almost there." But she does not arrive, and she does not write again.

  en: He searches for his phone in the pocket of his coat, and when he finally finds it, he is afraid to look at it.

  en: He puts it face down on the table and goes out to the square for a stroll.

  en: When he comes back, he turns the chair round to face the door.

  en: At the next table a young man poses as an expert on beer, and explains to his friends which one is the best in the world.

  en: The waitress walks past with a tray and stops for a moment: her daughter has given birth this week, and she feels sorry to see him so alone.

  en: "My shift ends at ten. If you have been stood up, here is my number."

notes.en:
1. da a una plaza (sentence 1): dar a with a place means to look onto it or open onto it, as the window faces the square. Example: Mi cuarto da al patio, "my room looks onto the courtyard". The word ventana is in Foundation (Foundation, lesson 12: ventana).
2. Me doy prisa (sentence 4): darse prisa means to hurry, and the reflexive pronoun stays in every form: me doy, te das, se da. The literal sense is "to give oneself haste". Example: Date prisa, que llegamos tarde, "hurry up, we're late" (to a friend).
3. me ha dado guerra (sentence 4): dar guerra means to be a handful, to cause trouble, usually said of a child. The perfect says the afternoon is not over. Example: Este niño da mucha guerra, "this child is a real handful".
4. darle la lata (sentence 6): dar la lata means to pester or bore someone. Lata is a tin can, and a rattling can is a nuisance. Example: Deja de darme la lata, "stop pestering me" (to a friend).
5. No me da la gana irme (sentence 7): no me da la gana with an infinitive means "I don't feel like it, I'm not going to". The literal sense is "it does not give me the wish": gana is desire, as in tener ganas de. It is familiar and a little blunt. Example: No me da la gana ir al gimnasio hoy, "I don't feel like going to the gym today".
6. se da cuenta de que (sentence 8): darse cuenta de means to realise, and the literal sense is "to give oneself account of". Do not use realizar for it: that is a false friend (Foundation, lesson 96: realizar). Example: Me di cuenta de que llovía, "I realised it was raining".
7. Le da vergüenza (sentence 9): dar vergüenza means to make someone feel embarrassed or ashamed, and English says "to be embarrassed". The thing that causes the feeling is the subject, so the verb is in the third person. Example: Me da vergüenza pedir ayuda, "I'm embarrassed to ask for help". The noun is in Foundation (Foundation, lesson 38: vergüenza).
8. le da igual lo que cueste (sentence 9): dar igual means it does not matter, and the literal sense is "to give the same". Lo que cueste is "whatever it costs". Example: Me da igual, "it doesn't matter to me, I don't mind".
9. da con él (sentence 11): dar con means to find something after looking for it, often after some effort or by chance. Example: Por fin doy con las llaves, "I finally find the keys".
10. le da miedo mirarlo (sentence 11): dar miedo means to frighten, and English says "to be afraid of". The feared thing is the subject, here the infinitive mirarlo. Example: Me da miedo la oscuridad, "I'm afraid of the dark".
11. dar una vuelta (sentence 12): dar una vuelta means to go for a walk or a stroll, and the literal sense is "to give a turn". Example: Doy una vuelta antes de cenar, "I'm going for a stroll before dinner".
12. da la vuelta a la silla (sentence 13): dar la vuelta a means to turn something round or over. The same phrase with a plate is in Foundation (Foundation, lesson 34: dale la vuelta con un plato). Example: Da la vuelta a la tortilla con un plato, "turn the omelette over with a plate".
13. se las da de experto (sentence 14): dárselas de means to pose as something, to put on airs of being it. The pronouns go with it: me las doy de, te las das de, se las da de. The literal sense is "to give oneself them of", where las stands for the airs. Example: Se las da de gracioso, pero nadie se ríe, "he poses as the funny one, but nobody laughs".
14. ha dado a luz (sentence 15): dar a luz means to give birth, and the literal sense is "to give to the light". The perfect is used because the week is not over. Example: Mi hermana dio a luz en marzo, "my sister gave birth in March".
15. le da pena verlo (sentence 15): dar pena means that something makes you sad or sorry, and pena also means a pity. The word is in Foundation (Foundation, lesson 38: pena). Example: Me da pena que se vaya, "it's a pity he's leaving", with the subjunctive after the feeling.
16. le ha dado plantón (sentence 18): dar plantón means to stand someone up: to leave them waiting and not turn up. The literal sense is to leave someone standing. Example: Me dio plantón en la puerta del cine, "she stood me up at the cinema door".
17. doy en el clavo (sentence 18): dar en el clavo means to hit the nail on the head, to guess right. The literal sense is the same. Example: Has dado en el clavo, "you've hit the nail on the head".
18. dan calabazas (sentence 19): dar calabazas means to turn someone down, mostly in love: the one who refuses a suitor gives calabazas. The literal sense is "to give pumpkins". Example: Le dio calabazas en la primera cita, "she turned him down on the first date".
19. dar las diez (sentence 20): dar las plus an hour means the clock strikes that hour, and the chimes are the campanadas. Example: El reloj de la iglesia da las siete, "the church clock strikes seven".

  en: He travelled to Italy twice.

  en: In Rome, in 1650, he painted a portrait of Pope Innocent X.

  en: It is a nude seen from behind, and her face is reflected in a mirror.

  en: In 1659, late in his life, Philip IV made him a knight of the Order of Santiago.

  en: His most famous work, Las meninas, is still in the Prado and has given rise to a great many studies.

  en: In the painting the painter himself appears, standing before a large canvas.

  en: On his chest a red cross stands out, that of the Order of Santiago.

  en: Apparently the cross was not there at the start: it was painted after he received the order.

  en: According to legend, the king himself painted it.

notes.en:
1. Sería su suegro (sentence 2): the conditional here is the future seen from the past. At that time Pacheco was not yet his father-in-law, so sería says "would be". Example: Sabía que sería difícil, "he knew it would be difficult".
2. Según los historiadores (sentence 3): según plus a source means "according to", and the source follows it. Example: Según el periódico, mañana llueve, "according to the paper, it will rain tomorrow".
3. se conserva hoy (sentence 4): se conserva is the reflexive form for something that is kept, and hoy shows that it is still true today, so the present is used. Example: Este castillo se conserva muy bien, "this castle is very well preserved".
4. está en Londres (sentence 5): estar says where a thing is, and ser says where an event takes place (Foundation, lesson 7: ser and estar). Example: El cuadro está en el museo, "the painting is in the museum".
5. se casó con (sentence 6): casarse con means to marry, and the reflexive pronoun is part of the verb. Example: Se casó con su novio de la universidad, "she married her boyfriend from university". Maestro here means his teacher, the master in whose workshop he trained.
6. Al parecer (sentence 7): al parecer means "apparently", and it reports what seems to be so from the evidence, more carefully than dicen que. Example: Al parecer, el tren llega con retraso, "apparently the train is late".
7. se conoce como (sentence 8): se conoce como means "is known as", and the name after como is the one people use. Example: Ese postre se conoce como tarta de Santiago, "that dessert is known as tarta de Santiago".
8. Viajó dos veces (sentence 9): viajó is the preterite, because the two journeys are over. The perfect, ha viajado, would be for a time that is not over. Example: Viajé a Roma el año pasado, "I went to Rome last year".
9. retrató (sentence 10): retratar means to paint or draw a portrait of someone, from retrato. Example: Un pintor me retrató en su estudio, "a painter made a portrait of me in his studio".
10. de espaldas (sentence 12): de espaldas means with the back turned, seen from behind. Example: Se sentó de espaldas a la ventana, "she sat with her back to the window".
11. lo hizo caballero (sentence 13): hacer plus a noun or an adjective means to make someone into it, and lo is the pronoun for the man. Example: El jefe lo hizo responsable del proyecto, "the boss made him responsible for the project".
12. Las meninas (sentence 14): the title keeps its Spanish form and the painting is in the Prado (Foundation, lesson 29: Las Meninas). Example: Vi Las Meninas en el Prado, "I saw Las Meninas in the Prado".
13. después de recibir la orden (sentence 17): después de plus an infinitive means "after doing" something, and the infinitive has no subject of its own: here it is the painter who receives the order. Example: Después de comer, salimos a dar un paseo, "after eating, we went out for a walk".
14. Según la leyenda (sentence 18): según la leyenda means "according to legend", the form for a story told as a legend. The conditional of rumour, habría pintado, is the newspaper form for what is reported but not confirmed. Example: Según la prensa, el cuadro habría sido retocado, "according to the press, the painting would have been touched up".
15. Reporting what is said (this lesson): según, al parecer and según la leyenda are the forms above; por lo visto (familiar) means "apparently" in speech, as in Por lo visto, no ha venido nadie, "apparently nobody has come". Se dice que and dicen que mean "it is said that" and "they say that", and se cuenta que means "it is told that". The speaker does not vouch for what follows.

  en: Apparently the museum has a new room.

  en: They say the wine from this area is very good.

  en: The worst, according to the neighbours, comes every morning at seven: someone sings in the shower, and sings very badly.

  en: They say it starts before the alarm goes off. I never hear it, because at that time I'm already in the shower.

  en: One day, in the entrance hall, I hear two neighbours talking about the same problem.

  en: "I heard him yesterday singing something from the radio, and he didn't stop for half an hour", says the man from the fourth floor.

  en: "Well, nobody says anything to him, and he goes on singing without anyone stopping him."

  en: "Well, we'll all say it at the meeting", replies Pilar.

  en: The meeting is on Thursday at eight, in the entrance hall, and all the neighbours come.

  en: They arrive with their complaints: the couple opposite, the lady with the cats and the man from the first floor.

  en: "I don't listen to it, I hear it, which is different", says the man from the first floor, and nobody answers him.
- Román, el presidente de la comunidad, pide la palabra y pregunta quién es el cantante.
  en: Román, the chair of the residents' association, asks for the floor and wants to know who the singer is.

  en: Nobody knows: the sound goes round and round the patio, so nobody can tell which floor it comes from.
- «Pues yo lo oigo desde el segundo, y el otro día lo vi bajando la escalera con la toalla al hombro: es el del quinto», dice la señora Pilar.
  en: "Well, I hear him from the second floor, and the other day I saw him coming down the stairs with a towel over his shoulder: he's the one on the fifth", says Pilar.

  en: "Then we'll have to write him a letter", Román suggests, and everyone nods at once.

  en: I nod too, and write in the minutes: "Letter to the man on the fifth".

  en: Before the meeting ends, Pilar looks at me and asks: "And which floor are you on?"

  en: I take a moment to answer, with the pen in my hand.
- «Pues yo vivo en el quinto.»
  en: "Well, I live on the fifth floor."

notes.en:
1. Oigo a los vecinos (sentence 2): oír is to hear, the sound reaching you without any choice on your part. The neighbours are people, so the object takes the a. Example: Oigo la lluvia en el tejado, "I hear the rain on the roof".
2. Oigo a los vecinos de enfrente discutir (sentence 2): oír with a person and an infinitive means you hear them doing something, and the infinitive is the action. Example: Oí a mi hermana reír en la cocina, "I heard my sister laughing in the kitchen".
3. antes de que suene (sentence 4): antes de que always takes the subjunctive. The alarm has not gone off yet, so the verb is suene, not suena. Example: Llámame antes de que salgas, "call me before you leave".
4. sin que nadie lo pare (sentence 7): sin que takes the subjunctive, because the thing that does not happen has not happened yet. The sense is "without anyone doing it". Example: Lo hago sin que nadie me lo pida, "I do it without anyone asking me".
5. Yo no lo escucho, lo oigo (sentence 11): escuchar means to listen on purpose, while oír means to hear what reaches you. Example: Escucho lo que dices, pero no te oigo bien, "I listen to what you say, but I can't hear you well".
6. pide la palabra (sentence 12): pedir la palabra means to ask to speak at a meeting, and the literal sense is "to ask for the word". Example: Pido la palabra, "I would like to say something".
7. de qué piso viene (sentence 13): de qué plus a noun asks which one, and here the sound comes from some floor. Example: ¿De qué lado viene el viento? "which direction is the wind coming from?"
8. lo vi bajando la escalera (sentence 14): ver with a gerund shows you saw the action in progress, caught halfway through, and English says "I saw him coming down". Example: Te vi cruzando la calle, "I saw you crossing the street".
9. habrá que escribirle (sentence 15): habrá que plus an infinitive means "we will have to", and the pronoun le joins the infinitive at its end, so it means "to write him". Example: Habrá que llamar al fontanero, "we will have to call the plumber".
10. el del quinto (sentences 14 and 16): el del plus a floor or a place names the person by where they live: el del quinto is "the one on the fifth floor". Example: El del tercero toca el piano, "the one on the third floor plays the piano".
11. antes de que acabe (sentence 17): the second time antes de que takes the subjunctive, because the meeting has not ended yet. Pilar uses usted, the form for someone you do not know well. Example: Me voy antes de que empiece la lluvia, "I'm leaving before the rain starts".

6. pa amb tomàquet (sentence 11): in Catalan, pa amb tomàquet means "bread with tomato"; in Spanish it is pan con tomate. Example: En el bar pedí pan con tomate, "in the bar I ordered bread with tomato".

8. Girona, Lleida, Gerona, Lérida (sentence 15): Girona and Lleida are the official Catalan names; in Spanish the names are Gerona and Lérida. Example: Vamos a Gerona en tren, "we're going to Gerona by train".

  en: The expert says it is a good sign, although the species is still very rare.

  en: Finally, the weather: the rains arrive in the north, and in the south the heat continues.

  en: Tomorrow the union confirms the meeting with the company, and if there is no agreement, the strike will go on.

  en: Experts warn that the rain may come with hail.

notes.en:
1. Empieza el telediario, llegan las lluvias, sigue el calor (sentences 1 and 13): in news Spanish the verb often comes before its subject when the subject is the new information, while English keeps the order subject and verb. Example: Llega el tren con retraso, "the train is late" (said on the platform).
2. Lo anuncia el alcalde (sentence 2): anunciar means to announce, a reporting verb for official news, and it names the source. Example: La empresa anuncia nuevos trenes para el verano, "the company announces new trains for the summer".
3. asegura que (sentences 2, 6 and 12): asegurar que means to state something firmly as a fact, with the speaker standing behind it. Example: Asegura que no ha visto nada, "he says he has seen nothing".
4. informa de que (sentence 4): informar de que means to report that something is the case. The preposition de is needed. Example: El colegio informa de que las clases empiezan el lunes, "the school reports that classes begin on Monday".
5. declara que (sentence 5): declarar que means to state officially, in a public statement. Example: La directora declara que el colegio cerrará el viernes, "the headteacher states that the school will close on Friday".
6. desmiente que haya recortes (sentence 6): desmentir means to deny, and the thing denied is followed by que and usually the subjunctive. Example: Desmiento que haya dicho eso, "I deny that I said that".
7. ha subido el alquiler (sentence 7): news word order puts the verb first when the thing that happened is the new information. Spanish says ha subido el alquiler, not el alquiler ha subido. Example: Ha llegado el paquete, "the parcel has arrived".
8. se han registrado (sentence 8): passive se in a report: the verb takes se and agrees with the thing registered, which comes after it, so it is se han registrado contratos. Example: Se han vendido muchas entradas, "many tickets have been sold".
9. Se espera que (sentence 9): passive se with esperar takes que and the subjunctive, and English says "is expected to". Example: Se espera que llueva mañana, "rain is expected tomorrow".
10. filmado (sentence 10): a participle after a noun works like a clause with "that was": un lince filmado cerca de una autovía is "a lynx that was filmed near a dual carriageway". Note the trap: an autovía is a dual carriageway, while an autopista is a motorway (Foundation, lesson 67: autopista). Example: Un coche aparcado en la acera, "a car parked on the pavement".
11. lo vio cruzar (sentence 11): ver plus an infinitive shows the whole action, here the lynx crossing the hard shoulder. Example: La vi cruzar la calle, "I saw her cross the street".
12. confirma la reunión (sentence 14): confirmar means to confirm, and it is used for meetings, flights and appointments. Example: Confirmo la cita para el martes, "I confirm the appointment for Tuesday".
13. advierten de que (sentence 15): advertir means to warn, not to advertise, which is anunciar in Spanish. The preposition is de, as with informar. Example: Te advierto de que el café está muy caliente, "I warn you that the coffee is very hot".

  en: I confirm that the meeting is on Monday.

  en: The weather is expected to improve tomorrow.

  en: The spokesman assures us there will be no power cuts.

notes.en:
1. en el descuento (sentence 1): the stoppage time, the minutes the referee adds at the end of each half. The phrase is used for goals and results decided at that point. Example: El gol llegó en el descuento, "the goal came in stoppage time".
2. perdía por un gol (sentence 2): perder por plus a number of goals means to be losing by that many, and the imperfect describes the state during the match. Example: Ganábamos por dos goles al descanso, "we were winning by two goals at half-time".
3. no tira la toalla (sentence 3): tirar la toalla means to give up, to throw in the towel. The literal sense comes from boxing, where a trainer throws in the towel to stop a fight. Example: Después de tres derrotas, el equipo tiró la toalla, "after three defeats the team gave up".
4. en la recta final (sentence 3): en la recta final means in the final stretch, the last straight part of a race. Example: Estamos en la recta final del curso, "we are in the final stretch of the school year".
5. salvado por la campana (sentence 4): salvado por la campana means saved at the last moment, literally saved by the bell that ends a boxing round. Example: Aprobé el examen salvado por la campana, "I passed the exam by the skin of my teeth".
6. le metió un gol (sentence 5): meter un gol a alguien, literally to score a goal against someone, means to put one over on them, to trick them. Example: Me metió un gol con la excusa del tráfico, "he put one over on me with the excuse of the traffic".
7. Detenido un hombre (sentence 6): headline grammar. The participle stands alone, with no auxiliary and no article, for an event that has happened. Detenido un hombre means "a man has been arrested". Example: Detenido el conductor del camión, "the lorry driver has been arrested".
8. Muere un perro (sentence 7): headlines use the present for a past event, so Muere is "has died". Example: Cae la nieve en Soria, "snow has fallen in Soria".
9. llevaba quince años (sentence 7): llevar plus a period means to have been somewhere for that long, and the imperfect says it was still so at the time spoken of (Foundation, lesson 39: llevo tres años). Example: Llevaba tres horas esperando, "he had been waiting for three hours".
10. entra en vigor (sentence 8): entrar en vigor means a law comes into force, a set phrase for legal texts. Example: La norma entra en vigor el lunes, "the rule comes into force on Monday".
11. la cota de nieve (sentence 11): the snow line, the height above which snow falls. Example: La cota de nieve está en los mil metros, "the snow line is at a thousand metres".
12. aguanta un día más (sentence 12): aguantar means to hold out, to last. The sun lasts one more day, and the thing that holds out is the subject. Example: El buen tiempo aguanta hasta el viernes, "the good weather holds until Friday".
13. ha durado tres días (sentence 14): the perfect says the period counts up to now, so the carnival is spoken of as having lasted. The reason follows porque with the indicative. Example: Ha llovido tres días seguidos, "it has rained three days in a row".

  en: A parcel has arrived for you this morning.

  en: I have been waiting for the answer for two weeks.

  en: The reform of the neighbourhood has been passed.

notes.en:
1. siempre que (sentence 2): provided that. The subjunctive follows it for a condition not yet met (podamos). With the indicative it means whenever, for a habit: Siempre que llueve, me duele la rodilla, "whenever it rains, my knee hurts". Example: Te ayudo siempre que tengas tiempo, "I'll help you provided you have time".
2. el que pone (sentence 4): el que stands for a noun already named, here el sueldo, so the phrase means "the one that the advert gives". Example: Quiero el que está en el escaparate, "I want the one in the window".
3. Si no me lo suben (sentence 6): suben has no subject, which is the usual way to say "they raise" without naming anyone. The pronouns me lo come before the verb, so it means "they raise it for me". Example: Si no me lo arreglan, lo cambio, "if they don't fix it for me, I'll change it".
4. con tal de que (sentence 7): provided that, firmer than siempre que, and always with the subjunctive. It sets the terms of a deal. Example: Te acompaño con tal de que me dejes elegir la película, "I'll come with you as long as you let me choose the film".
5. coja (sentence 9): coger in Spain means to take, as in coger las vacaciones, to take a holiday, and coja is its subjunctive after siempre que. In much of Latin America people avoid coger and say tomar. Example: Cogí dos días de vacaciones en mayo, "I took two days' holiday in May".
6. a no ser que (sentence 10): unless, a fixed phrase that always takes the subjunctive. Example: Iremos en tren, a no ser que llueva, "we'll go by train, unless it rains".
7. salvo que (sentence 11): unless, used for a rule with an exception: the thing is usual, except when the condition holds. Example: Abrimos todos los días, salvo que sea festivo, "we open every day, unless it is a public holiday".
8. sitio (sentence 12): here sitio is a space or room, as in no hay sitio, "there is no room". The plaza de aparcamiento is the parking space, and aparcar is the verb to park. Example: No encuentro sitio para aparcar, "I can't find anywhere to park".
9. en caso de que (sentence 13): in the event that, and the verb after it is in the subjunctive (quede). Quede comes from quedar, to be left over: queda una plaza, "there is one space left". Example: En caso de que haya problemas, llámame, "if there are any problems, call me".
10. Si hubiera negociado (sentence 14): a mixed condition. Si with the pluperfect subjunctive gives the past that did not happen (Foundation, lesson 76), and the conditional tendría gives the result now. Example: Si hubiera estudiado derecho, ahora tendría más opciones, "if I had studied law, I would have more options now".
11. De haberlo sabido (sentence 15): the same meaning as si lo hubiera sabido, "had I known". Here de plus the infinitive haber plus the participle replaces si, and lo means the fact. Example: De haberlo sabido, no habría venido, "had I known, I wouldn't have come".
12. Como no me den (sentence 16): como plus the subjunctive gives a warning or a threat, and the result follows. Example: Como no vengas, me enfado, "if you don't come, I'll be cross" (to a friend). Compare como with the indicative, which gives a reason: Como llueve, no salimos, "as it's raining, we're not going out".
13. llevo dos años queriendo jubilarme (sentence 17): llevar plus a period plus the gerund says that something has been going on for that long (Foundation, lesson 62: Llevo dos horas esperando). The pronoun joins the gerund, so queriendo jubilarme. Jubilarse is to retire from work. Example: Llevo tres meses estudiando inglés, "I have been studying English for three months".
14. Cuanto antes, mejor (sentence 19): the sooner, the better. Spanish says it with cuanto antes, not with a word for "possible". Example: Lo necesito cuanto antes, "I need it as soon as possible".
15. puesto (sentence 21): post or job, a position in a company. En mi puesto means "in my job", the one she holds. Example: Me han ofrecido un puesto en Sevilla, "I have been offered a post in Seville".

  en: If you don't call me tonight, I'll be cross. (to a friend)

  en: Had I known, I would have brought the umbrella.

notes.en:
1. cartones (sentence 2): a cartoon here is the full-size design for a tapestry, not cardboard. Example: El cartón tiene el mismo tamaño que el tapiz, "the cartoon is the same size as the tapestry".
2. pintor de cámara (sentence 3): the painter of the royal household, who worked for the king. Cámara is the household of the king. Example: El pintor de cámara trabajaba para el rey, "the court painter worked for the king".
3. lo dejó sordo (sentence 5): dejar plus an adjective means to leave someone in a state. Example: La lluvia nos dejó empapados, "the rain left us soaked".
4. estampas (sentence 6): a print, the sheet that comes off an engraved plate. Example: Tengo una estampa antigua de la catedral, "I have an old print of the cathedral".
5. fueron pintados (sentence 8): ser plus a participle shows the act of painting, with the date. Example: La puerta fue pintada ayer, "the door was painted yesterday".
6. estaban pintadas (sentence 9): estar plus a participle shows the state that results, not the act. Example: La puerta está pintada de verde, "the door is painted green". Compare the note above: the same verb form with ser shows the action, and with estar the result.
7. pasó (sentence 11): pasar plus a period means to spend it. Example: Pasamos el verano en la costa, "we spent the summer on the coast".
8. óleo sobre lienzo (sentence 12): óleo is oil paint, lienzo is canvas, and the two make the usual phrase for an oil painting on canvas. Example: Es un óleo sobre lienzo, "it is an oil on canvas".
9. pinceladas (sentence 12): a pincelada is one stroke of the brush, from pincel, the brush. Example: Dio una pincelada roja en la esquina, "he put a red stroke in the corner".
10. plancha (sentence 13): a plate or a metal sheet. The word also means the iron for clothes, which is the usual sense in everyday speech. Example: Plancho la camisa antes de salir, "I iron my shirt before going out".
11. claroscuro (sentence 14): from claro, light, and oscuro, dark. It is the contrast of light and shade in a picture, and English uses the same word, chiaroscuro. Example: Este cuadro tiene mucho claroscuro, "this painting has a lot of chiaroscuro".
12. rojizo (sentence 15): colour adjectives in -izo, -áceo and -oso mean "tending to" the colour. Rojizo is reddish, grisáceo is greyish and verdoso is greenish. Example: Tiene el pelo rojizo, "she has reddish hair".
13. punto de partida (sentence 16): a starting point, the place where something begins. Example: Esta idea es el punto de partida del proyecto, "this idea is the starting point of the project".
14. sueño (sentence 18): one spelling with two senses. Sueño is sleep, the thing you do at night, and it is also a dream. Example: Tengo sueño, "I'm sleepy"; Tuve un sueño raro, "I had a strange dream".

7. Donostia (sentence 8): the Basque name of San Sebastián. Both names are official, and the Spanish text may use either, with the Basque name in Basque. Example: Donostia está en Guipúzcoa, "Donostia is in Guipúzcoa".

9. fue bombardeada (sentence 11): ser plus a participle for the event of the bombing, with a date. Example: El puente fue reparado el año pasado, "the bridge was repaired last year".
10. el Guernica (sentence 12): the painting keeps its Spanish title, Guernica, while the town is called Gernika in Basque. Example: Vimos el Guernica en el Reina Sofía, "we saw Guernica at the Reina Sofía".
11. parentesco (sentence 13): kinship between languages, and no tiene parentesco means it is not related to any other. Example: El español y el italiano tienen parentesco, "Spanish and Italian are related".
12. kaixo, agur, eskerrik asko (sentence 14): three Basque words quoted and named. In Basque, kaixo is hola, agur is adiós and eskerrik asko is gracias. Example: Kaixo, en Euskadi, es lo mismo que hola, "kaixo, in the Basque Country, is the same as hola".
13. txakoli (sentence 17): a Basque white wine, spelled with tx for the sound of the ch in the Spanish chico. Example: Pedí un txakoli para acompañar el pescado, "I ordered a txakoli to go with the fish".
14. al pil-pil (sentence 18): al plus a noun is a way of cooking, as in pollo al ajillo, chicken with garlic. The cod is cooked in oil and garlic, and the sauce is made by moving the pan. Example: Pollo al ajillo, "chicken with garlic".
15. gilda (sentence 19): a pincho, a snack on a stick, in the Spanish word. In Basque it is a pintxo, with the tx spelling. The name is said to come from the film Gilda, and según se cuenta marks it as a story told, not a proven fact. Example: Tomamos una gilda con la caña, "we had a gilda with the beer".

- Esa noche me pongo el pijama, pongo la tele y me quedo dormida en el sofá.
  en: That night I put on my pyjamas, switch the telly on and fall asleep on the sofa.
- El despertador lo pongo mañana, que hoy ya no me quedan fuerzas.
  en: I'll set the alarm tomorrow, because I have no energy left today.
- El sábado me despierto a las nueve y media, con el sol en la cara.
  en: On Saturday I wake up at half past nine, with the sun in my face.
- Laura me pone verde por teléfono, así que me pongo las pilas, y en cinco minutos ya estoy vestida.
  en: Laura tears me apart on the phone, so I get a move on, and in five minutes I am dressed.
- Mi hermano llegó a su fiesta sorpresa a las nueve en punto, y la única que llegó tarde fui yo, porque se me olvidó poner el despertador.
  en: My brother arrived at his surprise party at nine o'clock sharp, and the only one who arrived late was me, because I forgot to set the alarm.

notes.en:
1. poner los puntos sobre las íes (sentence 2): literally to put the dots on the i's, that is to say exactly what one means, with nothing left vague. Example: Pongamos los puntos sobre las íes: ¿quién paga la tarta?, "let's be clear: who pays for the cake?".
2. poner el grito en el cielo (sentence 3): literally to raise a cry to heaven, to be outraged or to make a fuss. Example: Cuando sube el precio del café, todos ponen el grito en el cielo, "when the price of coffee goes up, everybody makes a fuss".
3. se pone nerviosa (sentence 4): ponerse with an adjective means to become or to get, a change in the person (Foundation, lesson 61). Example: Me pongo nerviosa cuando hablo en público, "I get nervous when I speak in public".
4. ponte las pilas (sentence 5): literally to put new batteries in, so to get a move on or to wake up and act. The pronoun joins the affirmative command, ponte. Example: Ponte las pilas, que llegamos tarde, "get a move on, we're late" (to a friend).
5. me pone de los nervios (sentence 6): poner de los nervios means to get on someone's nerves, and the person is the object of the verb. Example: Este ruido me pone de los nervios, "this noise gets on my nerves".
6. poner pegas (sentence 8): pegas are objections or problems, and poner pegas means to raise them. Example: Siempre pones pegas a todo, "you always raise objections to everything" (to a friend).
7. pone de su parte (sentence 9): poner de su parte means to do one's share or to help, literally to put something from one's side. Example: Si cada uno pone de su parte, terminamos hoy, "if everyone does their share, we'll finish today".
8. poner las cartas sobre la mesa (sentence 10): literally to lay the cards on the table, so to be open and say what you mean. Example: Mejor pongamos las cartas sobre la mesa, "we'd better be open with each other".
9. se pone a pensar (sentence 11): ponerse a plus an infinitive means to begin to do something. The reflexive pronoun stays with ponerse. Example: Me puse a estudiar a las cinco, "I started studying at five".
10. una multa (sentence 12): poner una multa is to give a fine, said of the police or the authority. The fine is the object and the one fined is the indirect object. Example: Le pusieron una multa por aparcar en doble fila, "he was given a fine for double parking".
11. pongo en marcha (sentence 13): poner en marcha means to set in motion, to start a project or a machine. Example: Vamos a poner en marcha un servicio nuevo, "we are going to launch a new service".
12. pone a prueba (sentence 14): poner a prueba means to put to the test, to try someone's patience or ability. Example: El examen pone a prueba lo que sabes, "the exam tests what you know".
13. Me pongo en el lugar de Javier (sentence 15): ponerse en el lugar de alguien means to put oneself in someone else's place. Example: Ponte en mi lugar, "put yourself in my place" (to a friend).
14. las meto (sentence 16): meter means to put something inside a container or a slot, where poner puts something on or down. Example: Mete la llave en la cerradura, "put the key in the lock".
15. me pongo el pijama (sentence 17): with clothes, poner becomes ponerse, to put on, and the reflexive pronoun goes with it. Example: Me pongo el abrigo, que hace frío, "I'm putting my coat on, it's cold".
16. pongo la tele (sentence 17): with a machine or a light, poner means to switch on, not to put. Example: Pon la radio, por favor, "turn the radio on, please".
17. El despertador lo pongo (sentence 18): with an alarm, poner means to set it. The alarm comes first as the topic, and lo repeats it before the verb. Example: Pon el despertador a las siete, "set the alarm for seven".
18. pone verde (sentence 20): poner verde a alguien means to criticise someone harshly, literally to make them green. Example: Lo puso verde por llegar tarde, "he gave him a dressing-down for arriving late".

  en: The sign on the door says No parking, and there is always a motorbike in front of it.

  en: If the door says Push, push, even if your hands are full of tapas.

  en: One orders what one wants, not what the menu says.

  en: People at the bar are known by their drink: whoever orders a tinto de verano is already a regular.

  en: When the waiter announces the last round, order another.

  en: And if nobody orders it, you order it, because there is always one more.

  en: Rule number one: there is never a last round, and the last round is always called the penultimate.

notes.en:
1. aquí no se espera a nadie (sentence 1): se with the third person and no subject is the generic statement: it describes what is done here, by anyone. Example: Aquí se cena tarde, "here people have dinner late".
2. Entras, te acercas y pides (sentence 2): the tú form used for anyone, as in "you" in English. Spanish manuals and advice use it all the time. Example: Entras en el pueblo y ves la plaza, "you come into the village and see the square".
3. ¡Oiga! and ¡Perdona! (sentence 4): both are used to call a waiter. Oiga is the usted command of oír, and perdona is the tú form of perdonar. Example: ¡Oiga, por favor! "excuse me, please!" (to a waiter).
4. caña (sentence 5): a small glass of draught beer, the usual order in many bars in Spain. Example: Dos cañas, por favor, "two small beers, please".
5. comer de pie, sentarse (sentence 6): the infinitive can be the subject of a sentence, as a noun. English uses the -ing form for this, but Spanish never says comiendo here. Example: Fumar aquí está prohibido, "smoking is forbidden here".
6. para que toda la barra se entere (sentence 7): para que always takes the subjunctive, because the result has not happened yet. Se entere is the reflexive enterarse, to find out. Example: Lo digo para que lo sepas, "I'm saying it so that you know".
7. El hablar bajo (sentence 8): the article el with an infinitive makes a noun, and it can be followed by an adjective. Example: El comer temprano es sano, "eating early is healthy".
8. discute la cuenta (sentence 9): discutir la cuenta means to argue about the bill, the thing that comes after the meal. Who pays the round is the subject of lesson 3. Example: Pedimos la cuenta al final, "we asked for the bill at the end".
9. penúltima (sentence 10): penúltimo means second to last, from último. It is a formal word for the round before the last one. Example: Esta es la penúltima parada, "this is the second-last stop".
10. No aparcar (sentence 11): a sign uses the infinitive, with no subject and no need for a command. Example: No fumar, "no smoking".
11. Empujar (sentence 12): the same infinitive form on a door, where it tells you the action without naming a person. Example: Tirar, "pull".
12. Uno pide (sentence 13): uno is a generic pronoun for "one" or "a person", and the verb after it is in the third person singular. Example: Uno nunca sabe cuándo va a llover, "one never knows when it is going to rain".
13. La gente de la barra (sentence 14): la gente is a singular noun for "people" in general, so the verb is singular. Example: La gente de este pueblo es muy amable, "the people of this village are very kind".
14. tinto de verano (sentence 14): tinto is red wine in Spain, and tinto de verano is red wine mixed with a soft drink, a summer drink. Example: Un tinto de verano, por favor, "a tinto de verano, please".
15. la última ronda (sentence 15): a ronda is a round of drinks bought for the table, and última is the last. Example: Esta es la última ronda, "this is the last round".

  en: You walk in and sit wherever you like. (to a friend)

  en: Eating fast is not healthy.

  en: One always learns something new abroad.

  en: You speak quietly so that they don't hear you. (to a friend)

notes.en:
1. Coyoacán (sentence 1): a district of Mexico City, in the south of the city. Example: Vivo en Coyoacán, "I live in Coyoacán".
2. prefería decir (sentence 2): the imperfect of preferir, the preference that held for a time, followed by an infinitive. Example: Prefería viajar de noche, "she preferred to travel at night".
3. Debe de ser (sentence 3): deber de plus an infinitive makes a likely guess, while deber without de is an obligation. Example: Debe de ser tarde, ya está oscuro, "it must be late, it is already dark".
4. mayor (sentence 8): for a person, mayor means older, and it is the usual word in Spain for someone's age. Example: Mi hermana es mayor que yo, "my sister is older than me".
5. la Casa Azul (sentence 11): the name the house is known by, and the adjective azul is the colour of its walls. Example: La casa es azul por fuera, "the house is blue on the outside".
6. ahorita (sentence 12): a Mexican word for in a moment. In Spain you would say ahora mismo or en un momento. Example in Spain: Ahora mismo voy, "I'm coming right now".
7. ¿mande? (sentence 12): a Mexican way to ask someone to repeat. In Spain you would say ¿perdón? or ¿cómo? Example in Spain: ¿Cómo?, "pardon?".
8. padre (sentence 12): a Mexican word for great or very good, from the everyday use. In Spain padre means father, and you would say genial or estupendo. Example in Mexico: Esa película está padre, "that film is great".
9. debe cuidar (sentence 13): deber plus an infinitive means must or should, an obligation. Example: Debes cuidar tu salud, "you must look after your health".
10. no puede ser (sentence 16): no puede ser says that something is impossible. Example: No puede ser verdad, "it can't be true".
11. tiene que ser (sentence 17): tener que plus the infinitive for a conclusion that the evidence leaves no other choice for. Example: Tiene que ser Ana, nadie más tiene esa voz, "it must be Ana, nobody else has that voice".
12. tendría unos veinte (sentence 18): the conditional of probability refers to a past time, as a guess. Example: Tendría unos treinta años cuando se mudó, "she would have been about thirty when she moved".
13. Viva la vida (sentence 19): viva is the form of vivir used in the phrase long live, and vida is life. Example: ¡Viva la libertad!, "long live freedom!".

notes.en:
1. usted (sentence 2): in Spain today tú is the form for most adults, among friends, family and colleagues, while usted is kept for older people, for formal service and to keep a distance (Foundation, lesson 13). In much of Latin America usted is used more widely, even between friends, and ustedes is the plural for everyone (Foundation, lesson 93). Example: ¿Es usted el dueño del bar?, "are you the owner of the bar?"
2. hablar de usted, tutear (sentence 3): hablar de usted, or tratar de usted, means to use the usted form with someone, and tutear means to use tú with them. Example: Mi jefe me trata de usted, pero a mi compañera la tutea, "my boss uses usted with me, but he uses tú with my colleague".
3. Tutéame (sentence 3): the tú command of tutear, with me attached at the end. The stress falls two syllables before the end, so it takes an accent, as in llámame. Example: Llámame mañana, "call me tomorrow".
4. Le ayudo (sentence 4): with usted the verb takes the third person, and the object pronoun is le. The tú form would be te ayudo. Example: ¿Le llevo la maleta, señora?, "shall I carry your case, madam?".
5. perdone, perdona (sentence 4): the same verb in two forms. Perdone is the usted form and perdona the tú form, and the ending shows which one the speaker has chosen (Foundation, lesson 13, note 3).
6. Déjalo, Siéntate (sentence 5): tú commands with the pronoun joined on the end. Déjalo is leave it, and siéntate is sit down, from sentarse, with the reflexive pronoun on the end (Foundation, lesson 34). Example: Siéntate aquí, que ya llega el cocido, "sit here, the stew is almost here".
7. Estás contento, no eres contento (sentence 7): estar describes how someone feels or is today, and ser describes what someone is, in a lasting way (Foundation, lesson 7, and lesson 3 for estar). Example: Soy inglés, pero hoy estoy cansado, "I'm English, but today I'm tired".
8. Ya le ha salido el usted (sentence 9): salir with a pronoun means something comes out of someone or turns out for them. The subject is el usted, so the verb stays singular. Example: Me ha salido bien el examen, "the exam went well for me".
9. no hay usted ni tú (sentence 10): English has one word, you, for both the familiar and the polite form. Spanish chooses between tú and usted, and the verb form shows the choice. The rule is the one in note 1.
10. Me cuesta (sentence 11): costar with a pronoun means something is hard for someone, and cuesta agrees with the singular thing, el tú. Example: Me cuesta madrugar, "getting up early is hard for me".
11. Mi suegro... trataba de usted hasta la boda (sentence 15): a suegro is a father-in-law, and the parents of a partner are the suegros. Trataba is the imperfect, a habit kept up over a period, and hasta la boda is until the wedding. Example: Mi suegra cocina de maravilla, "my mother-in-law cooks wonderfully".
12. Ahora sí (sentence 20): ahora sí is said when someone finally gets something right, and uno de la familia is one of the family. Example: Ahora sí, así se hace, "now that's the way to do it".

  en: They keep on laughing, and she goes on with her phrases, as if nothing had happened.

notes.en:
1. frase fija (sentence 1): a set phrase, the same words each time. Example: Tiene una frase fija para despedirse, "he has a set phrase for saying goodbye".
2. ¡Que aproveche! (sentence 2): said before a meal, and que with the subjunctive makes a wish (Foundation, lesson 90). Aproveche is the subjunctive of aprovechar. Aunque takes the subjunctive when the speaker does not state the fact as true, as with la comida todavía esté (Foundation, lesson 75). Example: ¡Que aproveche, que la sopa está buena!, "enjoy your soup, it's good!" (to a friend).
3. ¡Que te vaya bien! (sentence 3): que with the subjunctive, for a wish to someone who is leaving. Vaya is the subjunctive of ir. Example: ¡Que te lo pases bien en Sevilla!, "enjoy yourself in Seville!" (to a friend).
4. ¡Así sea! (sentence 4): so be it, a wish that someone answers with the subjunctive of ser. It is the same kind of wish as ojalá (Foundation, lesson 51). Example: Ojalá llegue a tiempo, "I hope he gets there on time".
5. ¡Válgame Dios! (sentence 5): an exclamation of surprise, from the subjunctive of valer, valga, with the pronoun me joined on: válgame. Example: ¡Válgame, qué calor!, "good heavens, how hot it is!".
6. ¡Vaya por Dios! (sentence 6): vaya comes from ir and is a frozen subjunctive. The same is true of ¡Vaya!, ¡Venga! and ¡Viva!, which each keep an old subjunctive form and no longer change. Example: ¡Vaya, qué lástima!, "oh, what a pity!".
7. sea como sea (sentence 7): be that as it may. Sea is the subjunctive of ser, and English has the same fossils: long live the King, God save the King, be that as it may. Example: Sea como sea, lo hacemos hoy, "be that as it may, we'll do it today".
8. que en paz descanse (sentence 8): the phrase is said for someone who has died, and in English it is may he rest in peace. Example: Que en paz descanse, "may he rest in peace".
9. a sus espaldas (sentence 9): literally at his or her back, so behind someone's back. Example: Lo dijo a mis espaldas, "he said it behind my back".
10. a coro (sentence 18): in chorus, all saying the same thing at once. Example: Cantaron a coro, "they sang in chorus".
11. ¡Jesús! (sentence 18): the word the family says to someone who has just sneezed, as Foundation, lesson 90, explains.
12. Amén (sentence 18): the word that ends a prayer, and English uses it in the same way.
13. por una vez (sentence 18): for once, this one time. Example: Por una vez, tú tienes razón, "for once, you are right".

2. pensaba que iba a ser (sentence 2): the imperfect for a thought held over a period, and iba a ser is the plan he had then, "was going to be". Example: Pensaba que iba a ser médico, hasta que descubrí los idiomas, "I used to think I would be a doctor, until I discovered languages".
3. la Residencia de Estudiantes (sentence 3): a student residence in Madrid, where Dalí and Buñuel also studied. Example: Vivía en una residencia de estudiantes, "he lived in a student residence".
4. trabó amistad (sentence 4): trabar amistad is a formal way to say make friends. Example: Trabé amistad con mis vecinos, "I made friends with my neighbours".
5. romances (sentence 5): a romance is a Spanish verse form of the ballad kind, with lines of eight syllables and assonance on the even lines. The same word is used for a love affair. Example: Vivieron un romance de juventud, "they had a youthful romance".
6. salió (sentence 6): salir de means to come out of, here a book coming out of a stay abroad. Example: De esa idea salió un proyecto, "a project came out of that idea".
7. dirigió La Barraca (sentence 8): dirigir means to direct or to lead a group, and La Barraca was a group that performed plays. Example: Dirige el coro del colegio, "he conducts the school choir".
8. obras de teatro (sentence 10): English keeps the Spanish titles or translates them: Blood Wedding for Bodas de sangre, The House of Bernarda Alba for La casa de Bernarda Alba, and Yerma stays the same.
9. se escribió (sentence 11): with se and a thing as the subject, the verb agrees with the thing and means was written. Example: Se escribió hace mucho tiempo, "it was written a long time ago".
10. fue fusilado (sentence 12): ser with a participle for an act, with a date, as in lesson 23. Example: Fue detenido el lunes, "he was arrested on Monday".
11. llenos de colores (sentence 15): lleno de means full of. Example: La plaza está llena de gente, "the square is full of people".
12. verlo todo negro, quedarse en blanco, ponerse morado (sentence 16): each idiom keeps its colour. Verlo todo negro is literally to see everything black, and means to see only the bad side. Quedarse en blanco is to go blank, and ponerse morado is to stuff oneself. Example: Después de la cena me puse morado, "after dinner I stuffed myself".
13. verde que te quiero verde (sentence 17): the first line of the poem Romance sonámbulo, in the Romancero gitano. The line means green, how I love you green.
14. poner verde a alguien (sentence 18): to criticise someone harshly, as in lesson 25, note 18. Example: Lo puso verde por llegar tarde, "he tore him apart for arriving late".

notes.en:
1. llevar (sentence 1): the basic sense is to carry or take something from one place to another, and llevar is the verb for taking a thing away from where the speaker is. Example: Llevo la maleta al coche, "I'm carrying the case to the car".
2. llevar la contraria (sentence 3): to contradict someone, literally to carry the opposite to them. Example: Nunca le llevo la contraria a mi jefe, "I never contradict my boss".
3. llevarse bien con (sentence 5): to get on well with someone, with the person after con. Example: Me llevo bien con mis compañeros de trabajo, "I get on well with my colleagues".
4. llevarse mal con (sentence 6): the opposite, to get on badly with someone or with something. Example: Mi padre se lleva mal con los móviles, "my father doesn't get on with mobile phones".
5. llevar la cuenta (sentence 7): to keep count of something. Example: Llevo la cuenta de los kilómetros, "I'm keeping count of the kilometres".
6. llevar las de perder (sentence 8): to be at a disadvantage, to be the one who will lose. Example: Con este equipo llevamos las de perder, "with this team we are in for a losing game".
7. llevarse un susto (sentence 9): to get a fright. Example: Me llevé un susto con el trueno, "the thunder gave me a fright".
8. llevarse un chasco (sentence 10): to be let down or disappointed. Example: Nos llevamos un chasco: el museo estaba cerrado, "we were disappointed: the museum was closed".
9. llevar razón (sentence 11): to be right, literally to carry the reason. Example: Llevas razón, "you're right".
10. se lleva el amarillo (sentence 12): when something is in fashion, it is worn, and with se the verb agrees with the thing. Example: Este año se llevan los pantalones anchos, "wide trousers are in this year".
11. traes, traigo (sentence 13): traer means to bring towards the speaker, and llevar takes a thing away from the speaker. Example: ¿Me traes un vaso de agua?, "can you bring me a glass of water?".
12. voy yo... ¿Vienes? (sentence 14): ir goes away from the speaker, and venir comes towards the speaker or along with them. Example: Voy al cine esta tarde, ¿vienes?, "I'm going to the cinema this afternoon, are you coming?".
13. llevo dos horas esperando (sentence 15): llevar with a period of time and a gerund says how long something has been going on (Foundation, lesson 62). Example: Llevo tres meses estudiando inglés, "I have been studying English for three months".
14. para llevar (sentence 15): a takeaway, something taken away instead of eaten in the place. Example: Dos cafés para llevar, por favor, "two coffees to take away, please".
15. llevo leídas (sentence 17): llevar with a participle says how much of something has been done so far, and the participle agrees with the thing. Example: Llevo escritas cinco páginas, "I have written five pages so far".
16. llevas los pantalones (sentence 18): to be the one in charge at home. Example: En mi casa lleva los pantalones mi madre, "in my house my mother is in charge".
17. llevarse el gato al agua (sentence 19): to win through, to get one's way, literally to take the cat to the water. Example: Se llevó el gato al agua con su propuesta, "he got his proposal through".
18. llevar a cabo (sentence 20): to carry out, to put a plan into action. Example: Vamos a llevar a cabo el proyecto en verano, "we are going to carry out the project in summer".
19. llevar adelante (sentence 20): to keep something going, to see it through. Example: Llevó adelante la empresa durante años, "he kept the firm going for years".

notes.en:
1. Se acabó ya la comida (line 1): the verse has eight syllables. Se and a meet, and vowel to vowel they make one syllable, so the line is sea-ca-bó-ya-la-co-mi-da. This join is called sinalefa.
2. sinalefa (line 12): the same join happens in ya se alarga, where se and a form one syllable, sea-lar-ga. Example: Ya se acaba, "it's ending now" (the same join, se acaba).
3. assonance (the even lines, 2 to 16): the even lines share only the stressed vowel and the last vowel, so levanta, montaña, tarta, calla, ventana, alarga, sala and acaba all have á and a. A full rhyme would also need the same consonants after the vowel, and this poem does not do that. The odd lines are free.
4. the last-stress rule (line 7): a line ending on a word stressed on the second-to-last syllable counts as it is written; one ending on a word stressed on the last syllable counts one more, and one ending on an esdrújula counts one less. La tía habla de política has nine syllables as written and ends on política, so it counts eight.
5. diphthongs (lines 5 and 9): ie, ua, ue and ui count as one syllable in verse. So cual in cada cual is one, and so is rui in ruido. Frío in line 11 is different: the stress is on the í, so the two vowels are two syllables, frí-o.
6. la tía habla (line 7): tía is two syllables, tí-a, but its last a joins the a of habla, whose h is silent: tí-a_ha-bla.
7. y el abuelo (line 8): three words, and in verse three vowels meeting can join into one sound: ye-la-bue-lo. The line counts eight.
8. la sobremesa no acaba (line 16): the sobremesa is the time spent at the table talking after a meal, often longer than the meal itself. No and acaba join: no_a-ca-ba.
9. Ya se ha dormido el abuelo (line 13): the h of ha is silent, so se and ha join into one syllable, and so do the o of dormido and the e of el.
10. La sobremesa terminó a las nueve (line 17): this line is prose, not verse, so it has no fixed count. It closes the poem plainly, with the time and grandad's denial.

notes.en:
1. murió, entró (sentence 1): the preterite for events that are over, a death and the start of a new stage. The present se llama is used for the name the period has today. Example: Mi abuelo murió en invierno, "my grandfather died in winter".
2. el 20 de noviembre de 1975 (sentence 1): in Spanish the day comes first, then de and the month, then de and the year, which is read in full: mil novecientos setenta y cinco. Example: El 3 de mayo de 2004 nació mi hija, "my daughter was born on 3 May 2004".
3. dos días después (sentence 2): después with a time phrase means later, and it takes no de. Example: Llegamos tres horas después, "we arrived three hours later".
4. las Cortes (sentence 2): the Cortes is the Spanish parliament, and it is used in the plural with las. English keeps the name. Example: Las Cortes aprobaron el presupuesto, "the Cortes passed the budget".
5. la aprobación, la legalización (sentences 3, 5 and 10): nominalisation. A verb becomes a noun with a suffix (aprobar gives aprobación, legalizar gives legalización), and history uses these nouns to name events. Example: La publicación del libro fue en mayo, "the publication of the book was in May".
6. se celebraron (sentence 6): the passive se with a plural noun as the subject, so the verb agrees with the elections, as in Foundation, lesson 59. Example: Se vendieron todas las entradas, "all the tickets were sold".
7. desde 1936 (sentence 6): desde with a year gives the point from which something has not happened since. Example: No hemos tenido noticias desde el martes, "we have had no news since Tuesday".
8. Ningún partido (sentence 7): ningún is the short form of ninguno before a masculine singular noun, and it comes before the noun, where English has "no". Example: Ningún alumno llegó tarde, "no student arrived late".
9. el consenso (sentence 7): a masculine noun, the word of the period: an agreement that the parties reach by giving way to each other. Example: Llegamos a un consenso, "we reached a consensus".
10. Para salir de la crisis (sentence 8): para with an infinitive gives the purpose. The Pactos de la Moncloa take their name from the Moncloa palace in Madrid, where the government met. Example: Lo hice para terminar a tiempo, "I did it to finish on time".
11. el hecho de que los partidos cedieran (sentence 9): el hecho de que takes the subjunctive when the clause is a reaction or a judgement, and the indicative is also heard for a plain fact. Here the main verb is in the past, so the past subjunctive cedieran follows, as in Foundation, lesson 74. Example: El hecho de que llegara tarde no me sorprendió, "the fact that he arrived late did not surprise me".
12. el 23-F (sentence 12): a coup is known by its date, 23 February 1981, written 23-F. Example: Recuerdo el 23-F por la televisión, "I remember 23-F from the television".
13. reconoce, sigue en vigor, se recuerda (sentences 13, 17 and 16): the text is in the past for events, and the present is used only for what still holds today. Example: La casa sigue en pie, "the house is still standing".
14. ese puente (sentence 18): a puente is a day off that joins a holiday to the weekend, explained in lesson 10. Example: Este año hacemos puente el viernes, "this year we are taking the Friday off as a bridge".

notes.en:
1. Pasamos tres días (sentence 2): pasar means to spend time, when the subject is the time spent somewhere. Example: Pasamos el verano en la costa, "we spent the summer at the coast".
2. ¿Y qué te pasa? (sentence 3): ¿qué te pasa? asks what is wrong with someone, or what is happening to them. Example: ¿Qué te pasa? Estás muy callado, "what's wrong with you? You're very quiet".
3. pasamos la noche en blanco (sentence 4): to stay awake all night, literally to spend the night in white. Example: Pasé la noche en blanco estudiando, "I stayed up all night studying".
4. lo pasasteis bien (sentence 5): pasarlo bien means to have a good time, and pasarlo mal means to have a bad time. Never say tener un buen tiempo for this. Example: Lo pasé mal en el dentista, "I had a bad time at the dentist's".
5. se lo pasaron en grande (sentence 6): pasárselo en grande means to have a brilliant time. Here se lo is the pronoun for the group, so the phrase is se lo pasaron. Example: Nos lo pasamos en grande en la fiesta, "we had a brilliant time at the party".
6. Se pasó con los deberes (sentence 7): pasarse with a thing means to overdo it, to give too much of it. Example: Te has pasado con la sal, "you've overdone the salt".
7. Se pasó de la raya (sentence 8): pasarse de la raya means to go beyond the limit, literally over the line. Example: Eso ya se pasa de la raya, "that is going too far".
8. Pasé del móvil (sentence 10): pasar de algo, in everyday speech, means not to care about something or not to bother with it. It is familiar. Example: Paso de las rebajas, "I'm not bothered about the sales".
9. Pasó por el hostal (sentence 12): pasar por means to call in or drop by. Example: Pasa por casa cuando quieras, "drop by the house whenever you like".
10. le pasó lo de siempre (sentence 12): pasarle a alguien means something happens to someone. Example: ¿Qué le ha pasado al coche?, "what has happened to the car?"
11. que pasara página (sentence 14): pasar página means to move on from something, literally to turn the page. The verb after que is in the past subjunctive, because the teacher spoke in the past, as in Foundation, lesson 74. Example: Ya es hora de pasar página, "it's time to move on".
12. lo paso por alto (sentence 15): pasar por alto means to overlook something, to let it go without comment. Example: Pasó por alto el error, "he overlooked the mistake".
13. me paso el día durmiendo (sentence 18): pasarse el día with a gerund means to spend the whole day doing something. Example: Me paso el día leyendo, "I spend the whole day reading".
14. Pasado mañana (sentence 17): the day after tomorrow. Example: Nos vemos pasado mañana, "see you the day after tomorrow".
15. me he pasado la noche (sentence 19): pasarse la noche with an adjective means to spend the night in that state. Example: Me pasé la noche despierta, "I spent the night awake".
16. no pasó nada (sentence 20): with no, pasar means that nothing happened, or that nothing matters. Example: No pasa nada si llegas tarde, "it doesn't matter if you're late".
17. Paso de contarlo (sentence 22): pasar de with an infinitive is familiar, meaning not to be bothered to do something. Example: Paso de ir a la fiesta, "I'm not bothering to go to the party".
18. Expressions of this lesson, with the sentence and the note:

pasárselo en grande (sentence 6, note 5)

  en: We spent the weekend at the beach in Cadiz.

  en: I had a really good time at my sister's wedding.

title: Tres chistes

dialogue:
- Javier, cuéntame un chiste, que hoy no tengo ganas de nada.
  en: Javier, tell me a joke, I don't feel like anything today.
- Te cuento tres, y tú me dices cuál es el peor.
  en: I'll tell you three, and you tell me which is the worst.
- Venga, empieza.
  en: Go on, then.
- Van un inglés, un francés y un español a una apuesta: el que no se mueva durante una hora gana mil euros.
  en: An Englishman, a Frenchman and a Spaniard make a bet: whoever keeps still for an hour wins a thousand euros.
- Ya me huele a trampa.
  en: I can smell a trick already.

  en: The Englishman lasts five minutes and then scratches his nose. The Frenchman lasts ten, and then starts arguing with the judge.

  en: And the Spaniard?

  en: That doesn't count, he moved to lie down.

  en: That was before the signal. When it was given, he was already asleep, and someone who is asleep does not move. So he wins.

  en: All right, that one is poor, but let's hear the second.

  en: What's the height of misfortune for a dog? Queuing, and having someone tread on its tail.

  en: Oh, I hadn't heard that one.

  en: Then the third one is the best, and it's about soldiers.

  en: Go on, you're keeping me in suspense.
- Le dice un sargento a un recluta: oye, ¿por qué tienes la frente tan arrugada?
  en: A sergeant says to a new recruit: hey, why is your forehead so wrinkled?
- Pues no sé. ¿Y qué le contesta el recluta?
  en: Well, I don't know. And what does the recruit answer?

  en: Well, I've been at the front for three years, sergeant.

notes.en:
1. Van un inglés (sentence 4): the usual way to open a joke about three nationalities. Van is ir in the third person, and the subject comes after it, as in Van dos amigos a la playa, "two friends are going to the beach". The frame Le dice un ... a otro ... (sentence 16) is the same kind of opening.
2. apuesta (sentence 4): a bet. Hacer una apuesta means to make a bet, and apostar is the verb. Example: Hicimos una apuesta sobre el partido, "we made a bet on the match".
3. Ya me huele a trampa (sentence 5): oler with a and a noun means to smell of something, and figuratively to suspect it. Example: Esto me huele mal, "this smells fishy to me".
4. aguanta cinco minutos (sentence 6): aguantar means to hold out, to put up with something, or to stay still. Example: No aguanto más este calor, "I can't take this heat any more".
5. se rasca la nariz (sentence 6): rascarse is a reflexive verb, and the part of the body takes the article, not a possessive, when the subject does it to himself. Example: Se rasca la cabeza cuando está nervioso, "he scratches his head when he is nervous".
6. se pone a discutir (sentence 6): ponerse a with an infinitive means to start doing something. Example: Se puso a llorar, "he started to cry".
7. ya estaba dormido (sentence 10): ya with the imperfect means already at that moment, and the state had begun before. Example: Cuando llegué, ya estaba dormido, "when I arrived, he was already asleep".
8. antes de la señal (sentence 10): señal here is the signal that starts a race or a contest, as in the English start signal. Example: Cuando suene la señal, empezad, "when the signal sounds, start".
9. el colmo de (sentence 12): el colmo de with a noun means the height of, the worst or the most extreme. Example: Es el colmo de la paciencia, "it is the height of patience".
10. cola (sentence 12): one word with two meanings: the tail of an animal, and a queue of people. Both are feminine. Examples: El gato mueve la cola, "the cat moves its tail"; Hay cola en la farmacia, "there is a queue at the chemist's".
11. que le pisen (sentence 12): pisar means to tread on, and the verb after y que is in the subjunctive when the one doing the action is not named. Le is the pronoun for the dog, the one with the tail. Example: Me pisó el pie en el autobús, "he trod on my foot on the bus".
12. la frente, el frente (sentence 16): the article decides the meaning: la frente is the forehead and el frente is the front of a battle. Examples: Tiene la frente sudada, "his forehead is sweaty"; Estuvo en el frente, "he was at the front".
13. arrugada (sentence 16): the adjective agrees with frente, which is feminine when it means forehead. Example: Tiene la frente arrugada cuando piensa, "his forehead wrinkles when he thinks".
14. llevo tres años (sentence 18): llevar with a period of time says how long something has gone on, as in Foundation, lesson 62. Example: Llevo dos años aquí, "I have been here two years".

- Mi hermano siempre se rasca la cabeza cuando no sabe la respuesta.
  en: My brother always scratches his head when he doesn't know the answer.
- Hicimos una apuesta: el que llegue último paga el café.
  en: We made a bet: whoever arrives last pays for the coffee.

  en: I can't stand the noise from the building work.
- La cola del perro se mueve cuando ve a su dueño.
  en: The dog's tail wags when it sees its owner.
- Cuando sonó la señal, ya estaba corriendo.
  en: When the signal sounded, he was already running.

ex2:

  en: My father scratches his head when he is confused.

  en: The dog wags its tail when it arrives.

The sixth week of this course is finished. Nothing here needs learning by heart: the tables put in order what you have read in lessons 36 to 41, and those texts are your store of examples.
If a point still feels unclear, go back to the example line in its lesson and read it aloud; it will be clearer each time. Then do the ten exercises at the end.
1. should have, needn't have, could have (lesson 38): the past of obligation, of need and of possibility, each with its own form.
| Meaning | What it does | Example from the week | In English |
|---|---|---|---|
| should have | tendría que haber + participle | Tendría que habértelo dicho antes (lesson 38) | I should have told you before |
| should have, short form | debiste + infinitive | Debiste hacerlo desde el principio. (lesson 38) | You should have done that from the start. |
| needn't have | no hacía falta que + imperfect subjunctive | no hacía falta que frenaras (lesson 38) | you needn't have braked |
| needn't have, with a reason | no tenías por qué + infinitive | no tenías por qué mirar el velocímetro cada dos segundos (lesson 38) | you didn't need to look at the speedometer every two seconds |

2. nominalisation (lesson 36): a verb becomes a noun, and history uses these nouns to name events.
| Verb | Noun | Suffix and gender | Example from the week | In English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aprobar | aprobación | -ción, f. | La aprobación de la Constitución por referéndum (lesson 36) | The approval of the Constitution by referendum |

The endings -miento (m.) and -dad (f.) work in the same way, but the week's texts give no example of them, so learn them from the dictionary when you meet them.
3. telling a life (lesson 39): the connectors that move a biography from one event to the next.
| Form | What it does | Example from the week | In English |
|---|---|---|---|
| tras | after, a more formal word than después de, used with a noun | Tras la batalla siguió en el ejército (lesson 39) | After the battle he stayed in the army. |
| al cabo de | after a period of time | Al cabo de los años, lo encarcelaron en Sevilla (lesson 39) | After some years he was put in prison in Seville. |
| una vez + participle | once something has happened; the participle agrees with the subject | Una vez cautivo, intentó escapar cuatro veces. (lesson 39) | Once a captive, he tried to escape four times. |
| hasta que + preterite | the end point of an action in the past | Siguió escribiendo hasta que murió. (lesson 39) | He went on writing until he died. |
A biography may use the historic present from the first line to the last, but it must not switch tenses halfway. Lesson 39 keeps the past for the life itself, and its last lines use the present only for what still holds today.
