== 1 B2
numeral: Lección primera
title: Echar
title.en: To throw

dialogue:
- Lunes, siete y cuarto. Lucía se despierta tarde y, con los ojos cerrados, busca el móvil.
  en: Monday, quarter past seven. Lucía wakes up late and, with her eyes shut, looks for her phone.
- Su madre no llama desde el jueves, y Lucía la echa de menos.
  en: Her mother has not rung since Thursday, and Lucía misses her (she throws her of less).
- Se levanta, tropieza con la maleta y le echa la culpa al gato.
  en: She gets up, trips over the suitcase and blames the cat (she throws the fault to the cat).
- La leche del desayuno se ha echado a perder, así que la tira por el fregadero.
  en: The milk from breakfast has gone off (it has thrown itself to lose), so she pours it down the sink.
- En la oficina echa un vistazo al correo: cuarenta mensajes, y tres son del jefe.
  en: At the office she has a quick look at her emails: forty messages, three of them from the boss (she throws a glance).
- Pablo, su compañero, no encuentra el informe, y Lucía le echa una mano.
  en: Pablo, her colleague, cannot find the report, so Lucía lends him a hand (she throws him a hand).
- El informe estaba en el cajón, pero el jefe, que lo ve todo, le echa en cara que llegó tarde.
  en: The report was in the drawer, but the boss, who sees everything, reproaches her for arriving late (he throws it in her face that she arrived late).
- Lucía echa humo por las orejas, pero no dice nada.
  en: Lucía is furious, but says nothing (she throws smoke out of her ears).
- A mediodía, Pablo le cuenta un chiste, y ella se echa a reír por primera vez.
  en: At midday Pablo tells her a joke, and she bursts out laughing for the first time (she throws herself to laugh).
- Después de comer, echa una siesta de diez minutos en el sofá de la sala de reuniones.
  en: After lunch she has a ten-minute nap on the sofa in the meeting room (she throws a siesta).
- Por la tarde, el jefe echa chispas por un correo mal escrito.
  en: In the afternoon the boss is furious about a badly written email (he throws sparks about it).
- Al final del día, Lucía echa cuentas: el sueldo no llega a fin de mes.
  en: At the end of the day Lucía works out the sums: the salary does not stretch to the end of the month (she throws accounts).
- Lleva cinco años en la empresa y ya ha echado raíces: nadie la imagina en otra oficina.
  en: She has been at the firm for five years and has put down roots: nobody can picture her in another office (she has thrown roots).
- Si sigue así, el jefe la echa del trabajo.
  en: If this goes on, the boss will fire her (he throws her out of work).
- Ha quedado con una amiga para cenar, pero a última hora se echa atrás.
  en: She has arranged to have dinner with a friend, but at the last minute she backs out (she throws herself back).
- Camino de casa echa una carta al buzón, la de la renovación del gimnasio.
  en: On the way home she posts a letter, the one about renewing her gym membership (she throws a letter into the postbox).
- En la gasolinera echa gasolina: el depósito estaba casi vacío.
  en: At the petrol station she fills up: the tank was almost empty (she throws petrol in).
- Al arrancar, el coche no se mueve, y ella le echa la bronca.
  en: When she starts it, the car does not move, and she tells it off (she throws it the row).
- Por fin se acuesta. Mañana es martes, y el gato, que ya no tiene la culpa de nada, duerme a sus pies.
  en: At last she goes to bed. Tomorrow is Tuesday, and the cat, no longer to blame for anything, sleeps at her feet.
- Y aquí echamos el cierre.
  en: And that is where we pull the shutter down for today (we throw the closing).

notes.en:
1. Echar: English splits this verb into throw, pour, post, fire, miss and nap; Spanish uses one verb, and the object tells you which sense is meant. Its basic sense is "to throw" or "to put in": echa sal a la sopa, "she adds salt to the soup", and echo el correo, "I post the mail".
2. echa de menos (sentence 2): to miss someone or something; literal "to throw of less", as if something has gone missing from where it should be. The person comes before the verb: la echa de menos, "she misses her", and Te echo de menos, "I miss you". In much of Latin America you will hear extrañar more often: Te extraño.
3. le echa la culpa al gato (sentence 3): to blame; literal "to throw the fault to". The person blamed is named twice, once as le and once as al gato. Example: No me eches la culpa a mí, "don't blame me".
4. se ha echado a perder (sentence 4): to spoil or go off, said of food, plans and the weather; literal "to throw oneself to lose". Example: La fruta se ha echado a perder, "the fruit has gone off". The pronoun se stays in every form.
5. echa un vistazo (sentence 5): to have a quick look; literal "to throw a glance". Echar un ojo means the same and is more familiar. Example: Echa un vistazo a esto, "have a quick look at this".
6. le echa una mano (sentence 6; Foundation, lesson 87): to lend a hand; literal "to throw a hand". Foundation lesson 87 has the question ¿Y no me vas a echar una mano? The person helped takes le. Example: ¿Me echas una mano con las cajas?, "can you give me a hand with the boxes?"
7. le echa en cara (sentence 7): to reproach someone for something; literal "to throw in the face". What is reproached comes after en cara, as a noun or as a que clause. Example: Siempre me echa en cara lo del coche, "he always brings up the car business with me".
8. echa humo por las orejas (sentence 8): to be furious; literal "to throw smoke out of the ears". The shortened form echa humo, without por las orejas, is also used in speech. Example: Mi padre echaba humo por las orejas, "my father was furious".
9. se echa a reír (sentence 9): to burst out laughing. Echarse a followed by an infinitive means to start suddenly, and the English "burst out" has the same sense. Example: Se echó a llorar al oír la noticia, "she burst into tears when she heard the news".
10. echa una siesta (sentence 10): to have a nap. Spanish says echar una siesta or echarse una siesta, and the nap is usually taken after lunch. Example: Después de comer me echo una siesta, "after lunch I have a nap".
11. echa chispas (sentence 11): to be furious, from the image of sparks; it is close in sense to note 8, and both are about anger. Example: Cuando se enteró, echó chispas, "when he found out, he hit the roof".
12. echa cuentas (sentence 12): to work out the sums, to see whether money will stretch; literal "to throw accounts". Cuenta means both "account" and "bill", and it also means "count". Example: Echa cuentas antes de comprar un coche, "she works out the sums before buying a car".
13. ha echado raíces (sentence 13): to put down roots, to settle somewhere. The English uses the same image, so this one is easy to keep. Example: Los abuelos han echado raíces en ese pueblo, "my grandparents have settled in that village".
14. la echa del trabajo (sentence 14): to dismiss someone, to throw them out of a job. The place or group comes after de, as in del trabajo, "from work". Example: Lo echaron del equipo por llegar tarde, "they threw him off the team for being late".
15. se echa atrás (sentence 15): to back out, to change one's mind about something agreed. The reflexive pronoun stays: se echa atrás, me eché atrás. Example: Me eché atrás en el último momento, "I backed out at the last moment".
16. echa una carta al buzón (sentence 16): to post a letter; literal "to throw a letter into the postbox". Buzón is the postbox, and the same verb is used for putting in a card or a leaflet. Example: Echa la carta al buzón antes de las cinco, "post the letter before five".
17. echa gasolina (sentence 17): to put petrol into a car. Figuratively, echar gasolina al fuego means to make an argument worse. Example: No le eches gasolina al fuego, "don't make things worse".
18. le echa la bronca (sentence 18): to scold or tell someone off; bronca is the telling-off itself and also a noisy row. Example: Mi madre me echó la bronca por llegar tarde, "my mother told me off for being late".
19. echamos el cierre (sentence 20): to close a shop or a business for the day; literal "to throw the closing". Example: La panadería echa el cierre a las tres, "the bakery closes at three".
20. Expressions of this lesson, with the sentence and the note:

echar la culpa (sentence 3, note 3)
echar a perder (sentence 4, note 4)
echar un vistazo (sentence 5, note 5)
echar una mano (sentence 6, note 6)
echar en cara (sentence 7, note 7)
echar humo por las orejas (sentence 8, note 8)
echarse a reír (sentence 9, note 9)
echar una siesta (sentence 10, note 10)
echar chispas (sentence 11, note 11)
echar cuentas (sentence 12, note 12)
echar raíces (sentence 13, note 13)
echar a alguien del trabajo (sentence 14, note 14)
echarse atrás (sentence 15, note 15)
echar una carta al buzón (sentence 16, note 16)
echar gasolina (sentence 17, note 17)
echar la bronca (sentence 18, note 18)
echar el cierre (sentence 20, note 19)

ex1:
- Te echo mucho de menos cuando viajas.
  en: I really miss you when you travel (to a friend).
- ¿Me echas una mano con las maletas?
  en: Can you give me a hand with the suitcases (to a friend)?
- No me eches la culpa a mí.
  en: Don't blame me (to a friend).
- El jefe me ha echado en cara el retraso.
  en: The boss has reproached me for being late.
- Después de comer, siempre me echo una siesta.
  en: After lunch I always have a nap.

ex2:
- Mi hermana echa [de] menos a sus amigos de Valencia.
  en: My sister misses her friends from Valencia.
- Lo siento, no quería [echar] la culpa a nadie.
  en: I'm sorry, I didn't want to blame anyone.
- Cuando vio al gato, ella [se echó] a reír.
  en: When she saw the cat, she burst out laughing.
- La leche se ha echado [a] perder.
  en: The milk has gone off.
- El director [le] echó la bronca a Pablo.
  en: The manager told Pablo off.

== 2 B2
numeral: Lección segunda
title: Lo que te espera
title.en: What lies ahead

dialogue:
- Este curso es para quien ya habla bien español y quiere hablarlo mejor, sin prisa.
  en: This course is for someone who already speaks Spanish well and wants to speak it better, without hurry.
- Aquí vas a leer textos de todo tipo: un chiste, una carta al ayuntamiento, un cuento, un poema.
  en: Here you will read texts of every kind: a joke, a letter to the town hall, a story, a poem.
- Viajaremos por España y por el mundo hispano, de Sevilla a Buenos Aires.
  en: We will travel around Spain and the Spanish-speaking world, from Seville to Buenos Aires.
- Conocerás a pintores, a escritores y a gente corriente, y a todos les daremos la palabra.
  en: You will meet painters, writers and ordinary people, and we will give each of them the floor (we will give each of them the word).
- Habrá modismos a docenas, de esos que parecen sencillos y no lo son.
  en: There will be idioms by the dozen, the kind that look simple and are not.
- Cada lección es un texto completo, y nadie te pedirá que lo recites de memoria.
  en: Each lesson is a complete text, and nobody will ask you to recite it from memory.
- Mientras tanto, la segunda vuelta del curso anterior sigue en paralelo: cada lección, hasta la cuarenta y nueve, te trae de vuelta una frase de ella.
  en: Meanwhile the second wave of the earlier course goes on alongside: each lesson up to the forty-ninth brings back a sentence from it (brings back: literally brings of return).
- Si empiezas aquí directamente, la segunda vuelta de este curso empieza en la lección cincuenta.
  en: If you start here directly, this course's own wave begins at lesson fifty.
- Hay textos que hacen reír, otros que hacen pensar, y algunos hacen las dos cosas.
  en: Some texts make you laugh, others make you think, and some do both.
- Si lees una lección al día, dentro de un mes habrás leído treinta textos.
  en: If you read one lesson a day, in a month's time you will have read thirty texts.
- Para leer bien no hace falta entender cada palabra: basta con captar la idea.
  en: To read well you do not need to understand every word: it is enough to grasp the idea.
- Lee en voz alta, para que el oído se acostumbre al ritmo de la frase.
  en: Read aloud, so that your ear gets used to the rhythm of the sentence.
- Y cuando dudes, usa las notas para comprobar tu respuesta sin volver al texto.
  en: And when you are unsure, use the notes to check your answer without going back to the text.
- Hace falta paciencia, pero no hace falta correr.
  en: You need patience, but you do not need to rush.
- Aquí nadie te pide la cuenta.
  en: Here nobody asks you for the bill.
- Mañana, dos amigos se pelearán por pagarla.
  en: Tomorrow, two friends will fight over paying it.

notes.en:
1. habrás leído (sentence 10): the future perfect, habrás plus the past participle, for an action that will be finished by a future point. Example: Mañana a esta hora habré terminado el libro, "by this time tomorrow I will have finished the book".
2. dentro de un mes (sentence 10): dentro de plus a time means "in a month's time, from now", while en un mes gives the length of time something takes. Example: Lo termino en un mes, "I will finish it within a month".
3. para comprobar (sentence 13): para plus an infinitive is used when the same person does both actions, here the one who checks. Example: Vino para hablar conmigo, "he came to talk to me".
4. para que el oído se acostumbre (sentence 12; Foundation, lesson 52): para que plus the subjunctive is used when the subject changes, here the ear is the one that gets used to the rhythm. Example: Lo escribo para que lo entiendas, "I write it so that you understand it".
5. te trae de vuelta (sentence 7): traer de vuelta means "to bring back" in the sense of recalling a memory, not carrying anything physically. Example: Esta canción me trae de vuelta mi infancia, "this song brings my childhood back to me".
6. les daremos la palabra (sentence 4): dar la palabra means to give someone the floor so that they can speak; literal "to give the word". Example: El presidente le dio la palabra a la secretaria, "the chairman gave the secretary the floor".
7. a docenas (sentence 5): in large numbers, by the dozen, with no exact count. Example: Hay libros a docenas en esa librería, "there are books by the dozen in that bookshop".
8. no lo son (sentence 5): lo replaces an adjective already said, and it does not change with the noun. Example: ¿Es fácil? No, no lo es, "Is it easy? No, it is not".
9. hacen reír, hacen pensar (sentence 9): hacer plus an infinitive means to make someone do something, here make people laugh or think. Example: Esa película me hizo llorar, "that film made me cry".
10. basta con (sentence 11): to be enough, followed by a noun or an infinitive. Example: Basta con decir que sí, "saying yes is enough".
11. la cuenta (sentence 15): the bill, in a restaurant; cuenta also means "account" and "count", as in lesson 1, note 12. Example: Pedimos la cuenta, "we asked for the bill".
12. se pelearán por pagarla (sentence 16): pelearse por means to fight over something, and por introduces what they fight over. Example: Se pelean por el último trozo de tarta, "they are fighting over the last piece of cake".

ex1:
- Dentro de dos semanas habré acabado el capítulo.
  en: In two weeks' time I will have finished the chapter.
- Para que lo entiendas, te lo explico otra vez.
  en: So that you understand it, I will explain it to you again (to a friend).
- Me trajo de vuelta muchos recuerdos de la infancia.
  en: It brought back a lot of childhood memories to me.
- No hace falta llegar pronto, basta con llegar a tiempo.
  en: You do not need to arrive early, it is enough to arrive on time.
- Hay cuadros a docenas en ese museo de Sevilla.
  en: There are paintings by the dozen in that museum in Seville.

ex2:
- Mañana a estas horas [habré] terminado el informe.
  en: By this time tomorrow I will have finished the report.
- Esa novela me [hizo] reír mucho.
  en: That novel made me laugh a lot.
- Te lo digo para [que] lo sepas a tiempo.
  en: I am telling you so that you know in time.
- Pedimos la [cuenta] después del postre.
  en: We asked for the bill after dessert.
- Mis primos se [pelean] por el último trozo de pastel.
  en: My cousins are fighting over the last piece of cake.

== 3 B2
numeral: Lección tercera
title: ¡Que pago yo!
title.en: No, I'm paying!

dialogue:
- Dos cafés con leche y dos churros, por favor.
  en: Two white coffees and two churros, please.
- Ni hablar, Javier. Esta ronda la pago yo.
  en: No way, Javier. I'm paying for this round.
- ¡Que no, Rubén, que esta ronda la pago yo, que la última vez pagaste tú!
  en: No, Rubén, I'm paying for this round, you paid last time!
- Qué va, hombre. Eso fue en diciembre, y la próxima la pagas tú.
  en: Come off it, mate. That was in December, and you can pay next time.
- De eso nada. Tú a mí me invitarás cuando te toque, y hoy me toca a mí.
  en: No way. You'll treat me when it's your turn, and today it's my turn.
- Anda, déjate de tonterías y cómete el churro antes de que se enfríe.
  en: Come on, stop being silly and eat your churro before it goes cold.
- No es ninguna tontería. El que invita se acuerda, y yo me acuerdo de todo.
  en: It is no silly matter. The one who treats remembers, and I remember everything.
- Pues yo me acuerdo de más cosas, y tu madre, que nos mira desde el fondo, también.
  en: Well, I remember more than you do, and so does your mother, who is watching us from the back.
- Mi madre no se mete en esto, y no la mires, que se va a dar cuenta.
  en: My mother stays out of this, and don't look at her, she will notice.
- Entonces, a medias, que es lo justo.
  en: Then we split it, which is only fair.
- Perdonad, pero la máquina de la tarjeta se está quedando sin paciencia.
  en: Sorry, but the card machine is running out of patience.
- Venga, vale. A medias, pues, y que conste que la próxima la pagas tú.
  en: Fine, then. Half each, and let it be noted that you pay next time.
- Ya lo sé, que lo has dicho tres veces.
  en: I know, you have said it three times.
- Perfecto. Pero hay un problema.
  en: Perfect. But there's a problem.
- ¿Qué problema? ¿No funciona la tarjeta?
  en: What problem? Doesn't the card work?
- Que ya está pagado. Lo ha pagado la señora del fondo, que dice que es la madre de Javier.
  en: It's already paid. The lady at the back has paid for it, and she says she is Javier's mother.

notes.en:
1. ¡Que no!, que esta ronda la pago yo (line 3): a spoken way of saying "no, I tell you", with que before each clause to press the point. Example: ¡Que sí, que lo tengo!, "yes, I tell you, I have it!".
2. Ni hablar (line 2): literally "not to speak", a flat no to a suggestion or a request. Example: ¿Me prestas el coche? Ni hablar, "can I borrow the car? No way".
3. esta ronda la pago yo (line 2): the thing paid comes first and the pronoun la repeats it. English keeps the order, I'm paying for this round, but Spanish puts the round first. Example: El café lo pago yo, "I'm paying for the coffee".
4. Qué va, hombre (line 4): qué va means "not at all", and hombre is said between friends, whatever their sex, the way English says "mate". Example: Qué va, hombre, no te preocupes, "not at all, mate, don't worry".
5. la próxima la pagas tú (line 4): la próxima means "the next time" (la próxima vez), and the pronoun la repeats the round, as in note 3. Example: La próxima la invito yo, "next time I'll treat you".
6. Tú a mí me invitarás (line 5; Foundation, lesson 54): invitar means "to treat someone", to pay for their food or drink, as in Si gana el Madrid, me invitas tú. Te invito is "it's my treat". The a mí me doubles the pronoun to put the stress on the person.
7. Venga, vale (line 12): venga is "come on" or "all right", and vale is "okay". Together they mark a quick agreement. Example: Venga, vamos, que llegamos tarde, "come on, let's go, we're late".
8. Anda, déjate de tonterías (line 6): anda is a push to get on with it, and dejarse de plus a noun means to stop with a nonsense. Example: Déjate de rodeos, "stop beating around the bush". Compare dejar de plus an infinitive, which means to stop doing something.
9. El que invita se acuerda (line 7): el que plus a verb means "the one who", and here it names the friend who pays. Example: El que llega tarde paga el café, "the one who arrives late pays for the coffee".
10. Pues yo me acuerdo (line 8): pues starts the reply, as "well" or "so" does in English, and it links the answer to what was just said. Example: ¿Te acuerdas del taxi? Pues sí, "do you remember the taxi? Well, yes".
11. la señora del fondo (lines 8 and 16): del fondo means "at the back", the far end of the room or the bar. Example: Nos sentamos en la mesa del fondo, "we sat at the table at the back".
12. a medias (lines 10 and 12): half each, split equally between two people. Example: Lo pagamos a medias, "we pay half each".
13. Que ya está pagado (line 16): an answer can start with que when it gives the reason asked for, "the thing is, it's already paid". Example: ¿Qué pasa? Que no tengo llaves, "what's up? I haven't got my keys".
14. dice que es la madre de Javier (line 16): decir que plus the indicative reports what someone says about themselves, here the woman claims to be Javier's mother. Example: Dice que es médico, "she says she is a doctor".

ex1:
- Esta vez lo pago yo, y la próxima lo pagas tú.
  en: This time I'm paying, and next time you pay (to a friend).
- Ni hablar, no te presto el coche.
  en: No way, I won't lend you the car (to a friend).
- Que sí, que te lo devuelvo mañana.
  en: Yes, I tell you, I'll give it back to you tomorrow (to a friend).
- Pagamos a medias y ya está.
  en: We pay half each and that's that.
- Te invito a un café, venga.
  en: Let me treat you to a coffee, come on (to a friend).

ex2:
- Esa cuenta la [pago] yo, no discutas.
  en: I'm paying that bill, don't argue (to a friend).
- Esta [ronda] la pago yo, de verdad.
  en: Honestly, I am paying for this round (to a friend).
- Nos sentamos en la mesa del [fondo].
  en: We sat at the table at the back.
- Ya me [invitarás] cuando quieras.
  en: You will treat me whenever you like (to a friend).
- [Qué] va, no es para tanto.
  en: Not at all, it's not that bad.

== 4 B2
numeral: Lección cuarta
title: Andalucía
title.en: Andalusia

dialogue:
- Andalucía es la comunidad más al sur de la España peninsular, y en ella se encuentran el mar, la montaña y las llanuras.
  en: Andalusia is the region furthest south on the Spanish mainland, and the sea, the mountains and the plains all meet there.
- Tiene ocho provincias: Almería, Cádiz, Córdoba, Granada, Huelva, Jaén, Málaga y Sevilla.
  en: It has eight provinces: Almería, Cádiz, Córdoba, Granada, Huelva, Jaén, Málaga and Seville.
- Su capital es Sevilla, situada a orillas del Guadalquivir, que atraviesa la ciudad.
  en: Its capital is Seville, set on the banks of the Guadalquivir, which runs through the city.
- Su símbolo más conocido es la Giralda, que fue el minarete de una mezquita y hoy es el campanario de la catedral.
  en: Its best-known symbol is the Giralda, once the minaret of a mosque and now the bell tower of the cathedral.
- En Córdoba, la Mezquita conserva un bosque de columnas, y en medio se levanta una catedral construida dentro del antiguo edificio.
  en: In Córdoba, the Mezquita keeps a forest of columns, and in the middle stands a cathedral built inside the old building.
- En Granada, la Alhambra fue el palacio de los reyes nazaríes, y está situada sobre una colina, frente al Albaicín.
  en: In Granada, the Alhambra was the palace of the Nasrid kings, and it stands on a hill facing the Albaicín.
- El dos de enero de mil cuatrocientos noventa y dos, Granada fue entregada a los Reyes Católicos.
  en: On 2 January 1492, Granada was handed over to the Catholic Monarchs.
- Jaén, Córdoba y Sevilla tienen olivares que llegan hasta el horizonte, y el aceite es una de sus grandes riquezas.
  en: Jaén, Córdoba and Seville have olive groves that stretch to the horizon, and olive oil is one of their great riches.
- Al suroeste, Doñana es un parque nacional de marismas, donde descansan muchas aves migratorias.
  en: To the south-west, Doñana is a national park of marshes, where many migratory birds rest.
- En la Sierra Nevada, cerca de Granada, está el Mulhacén, que supera los tres mil metros: es el pico más alto de la península ibérica.
  en: In the Sierra Nevada, near Granada, stands the Mulhacén, which is over three thousand metres high: it is the highest peak in the Iberian Peninsula.
- En Jerez de la Frontera, cerca de Cádiz, se elabora el jerez, que lleva el nombre de la ciudad.
  en: In Jerez de la Frontera, near Cádiz, sherry is made, and it takes its name from the town.
- En la mesa aparecen el gazpacho, el salmorejo cordobés y el jamón de Jabugo, de la provincia de Huelva.
  en: On the table there are gazpacho, salmorejo from Córdoba and the ham from Jabugo, in the province of Huelva.
- La Feria de Abril es en Sevilla, en abril, y en sus casetas se baila hasta la madrugada.
  en: The April Fair is held in Seville, in April, and people dance in its tents until the early hours.
- Pero la verdadera tradición de la región es la sobremesa: se come tarde, se habla mucho y nadie tiene prisa.
  en: But the real tradition of the region is the long talk after lunch: people eat late, talk a lot and nobody is in a hurry.
- Y para terminar, un plato de pescaíto frito, cuyo nombre ya ha perdido la d por el camino.
  en: And to finish, a plate of pescaíto frito, small fried fish, whose name has already lost its d on the way.

notes.en:
1. Andalucía (line 1): the Spanish name of the region, which the English calls Andalusia. The regions of Spain are called comunidades, and this one is in the south.
2. ocho provincias (line 2): a province is a smaller division inside a region, and Andalusia has eight of them. Sevilla is spelled without a c, as Seville is in English.
3. situada a orillas del Guadalquivir (line 3): a participle phrase, which describes a place without a verb of its own. The participle agrees with the noun, so situada is feminine for Sevilla. Example: Una casa situada en el centro, "a house situated in the centre".
4. fue el minarete (line 4): ser with fue says what something was, a role that has ended, while hoy es says what it is now. Example: Antes era una escuela y hoy es un museo, "it used to be a school and today it is a museum".
5. construida dentro del antiguo edificio (line 5): another participle phrase, with the same agreement as note 3. Example: Una carta escrita a mano, "a letter written by hand".
6. está situada sobre una colina (line 6): estar with a participle describes where something is and how it stands now. Example: El pueblo está situado en un valle, "the village is set in a valley".
7. fue entregada (line 7): ser with a participle is the passive, and it reports an action that happened. Example: La carta fue enviada ayer, "the letter was sent yesterday". Compare the state with estar in note 6.
8. olivares (line 8): an olivar is an olive grove, from olivo, "olive tree". The -ar ending names the place where a plant grows, as naranjal is an orange grove. Example: Pasamos por un naranjal, "we went past an orange grove".
9. marismas (line 9): marshes, low flat land that floods when the rivers rise and the sea comes in. Example: Las marismas están inundadas en invierno, "the marshes are flooded in winter".
10. supera los tres mil metros (line 10): supera means "is over" or "goes beyond", and it is used for a height as well as a number. Example: El avión supera los diez mil metros, "the plane flies above ten thousand metres".
11. el jerez (line 11): jerez, with a small letter, is the wine, and Jerez with a capital is the town it comes from. The English word sherry comes from the same name. Example: Me tomo un jerez seco, "I'll have a dry sherry".
12. el salmorejo cordobés (line 12): salmorejo is a thick cold soup of tomato and bread, from Córdoba. Gazpacho is thinner, so the two are not the same soup. Example: El salmorejo es más espeso que el gazpacho, "salmorejo is thicker than gazpacho".
13. La Feria de Abril es en Sevilla (line 13): where an event takes place takes ser, while where a thing is takes estar. So Sevilla está en Andalucía, but la feria es en Sevilla. Example: La reunión es en mi casa, "the meeting is at my house".
14. la sobremesa (line 14): the time spent at the table after a meal, talking, and not hurrying to leave. Example: Después de la comida hubo una buena sobremesa, "after the meal there was a long talk at the table".
15. pescaíto frito (line 15): pescaíto is the diminutive of pescado, "fish", and it is used for small fried fish. The d of -ado is dropped in speech, as in the Andalusian accent of Foundation, lesson 94. Example: Un plato de pescaíto, "a plate of small fried fish".

- Andalucía es la región más al sur de la España peninsular.
  en: Andalusia is the region furthest south on the Spanish mainland.
- La Giralda fue el minarete de una mezquita.
  en: The Giralda was the minaret of a mosque.
- La catedral está construida dentro de la antigua mezquita.
  en: The cathedral is built on the site of the old mosque.
- La boda es en Sevilla, en una iglesia del centro.
  en: The wedding is in Seville, in a church in the centre.
- Las marismas están inundadas en invierno.
  en: The marshes are flooded in winter.

ex2:
- El pueblo [está] situado en un valle.
  en: The village is set in a valley.
- La carta [fue] enviada ayer.
  en: The letter was sent yesterday.
- Una casa [situada] en el centro.
  en: A house situated in the centre.
- La feria [es] en abril.
  en: The fair is in April.

  en: Today the building is a museum.

numeral: Lección quinta
title: Las doce uvas
title.en: The twelve grapes

dialogue:
- Durante años, Ramón no consiguió comerse las doce uvas de Nochevieja: cada año se atragantaba antes de la última campanada.
  en: For years Ramón failed to eat the twelve grapes on New Year's Eve: every year he choked before the last chime.
- Su mujer lo conoció en una cena de Nochevieja y, desde entonces, se ríe de sus propósitos.
  en: His wife met him at a New Year's Eve dinner, and she has laughed at his resolutions ever since.
- Ese año, por fin, decidió que lo iba a conseguir, y se preparó durante una semana entera.
  en: That year, at last, he decided he would manage it, and he trained for a whole week.
- Compró uvas sin pepitas, las pelaba una a una y las contaba en un cuenco azul.
  en: He bought seedless grapes, peeled them one by one and counted them into a blue bowl.
- Su hija se reía de él, y su mujer le preguntaba si el cuenco era para la ensalada.
  en: His daughter laughed at him, and his wife asked whether the bowl was for the salad.
- La noche del treinta y uno, la familia se sentó delante de la tele, y Ramón puso el cuenco en la mesita.
  en: On the night of the thirty-first the family sat down in front of the telly, and Ramón put the bowl on the little table.
- Su hija miraba el móvil, pero él no le hizo caso.
  en: His daughter was looking at her phone, but he paid her no attention (he did not make any case of her).
- Faltaban cinco minutos. Ramón se acomodó en el sofá con las uvas en la mano.
  en: There were five minutes to go. Ramón settled into the sofa with the grapes in his hand.
- Cuando empezaron las campanadas, se metió la primera uva en la boca.
  en: When the chimes began, he put the first grape in his mouth.
- A la tercera ya tenía la boca llena, y a la sexta empezó a sudar.
  en: By the third he had his mouth full, and by the sixth he had started to sweat.
- Justo cuando se metía la octava, su mujer le ofreció un vaso de agua, y él lo rechazó: no quería perder el ritmo.
  en: Just as he was putting in the eighth, his wife offered him a glass of water, and he turned it down: he did not want to lose the rhythm.
- En ese momento se le cayó una uva debajo del sofá, y tuvo que agacharse a buscarla sin dejar de contar.
  en: At that moment a grape fell under the sofa, and he had to bend down to find it without stopping his count.
- Al terminar la undécima, tenía la cara roja, pero no quiso parar.
  en: After the eleventh he had a red face, but he refused to stop.
- De repente, la tele mostró la plaza llena de gente, y las campanadas sonaron por todo el salón.
  en: Suddenly the telly showed the square full of people, and the chimes rang out through the whole living room.
- Ramón se metió la última uva en la boca, levantó la copa y por fin pudo gritar que lo había conseguido.
  en: Ramón put the last grape in his mouth, raised his glass and at last managed to shout that he had done it.
- Su hija, que había dejado el móvil, supo enseguida lo que pasaba, y lo miró sin decir nada.
  en: His daughter, who had put down her phone, found out at once what was going on, and looked at him without a word.
- Papá, eso es la repetición del año pasado. Las campanadas de verdad ya sonaron hace un minuto.
  en: Dad, that is last year's rerun. The real chimes rang out a minute ago.

notes.en:
1. no consiguió (line 1): the preterite gives the result that never came, so he never managed it. se atragantaba, in the same line, is the imperfect, because it was what happened every year.
2. conoció (line 2): conocer in the preterite means "met", the moment of getting to know someone. In the imperfect it means "knew", as in sabía. Example: Conocí a mi vecino en una boda, "I met my neighbour at a wedding".
3. decidió (line 3): the preterite gives the moment of the decision, while the imperfect iba a conseguir describes what he was going to do. Example: Decidí que iba a cambiar de trabajo, "I decided I was going to change jobs".
4. pelaba, contaba (line 4): the imperfect describes the way he was doing it, one grape after another, as background, while compró is a single event. Example: Compró el pan y lo llevaba en una bolsa, "he bought the bread and was carrying it in a bag".
5. se reía, preguntaba (line 5): the imperfect for the scene, what was going on around the event. Example: Su hermana se reía mientras yo cocinaba, "my sister was laughing while I cooked".
6. no le hizo caso (line 7): hacer caso means to pay attention to someone, and the pronoun le shows who was ignored. Example: No le hagas caso, "don't pay any attention to him".
7. Justo cuando se metía la octava (line 11): justo cuando plus the imperfect sets what was happening at the very moment another event took place. Example: Justo cuando salía de casa, sonó el teléfono, "just as I was leaving the house, the phone rang".
8. En ese momento (line 12): a fixed phrase that points to the moment of an event. Example: En ese momento se apagó la luz, "at that moment the light went out".
9. tuvo que agacharse (line 12): tener que in the preterite means "had to" and it was done. Example: Tuve que esperar una hora, "I had to wait an hour".
10. Al terminar la undécima (line 13): al plus an infinitive means "on finishing", and the subject of both actions is the same person. Example: Al llegar, lo primero fue la catedral, "on arrival, the first thing was the cathedral" (Foundation, lesson 58).
11. no quiso parar (line 13): the preterite of querer with no means "refused", as a decision not taken. The imperfect quería means "wanted". Example: No quise decirle nada, "I refused to tell him anything".
12. De repente (line 14): all of a sudden, an event that comes without warning. Example: De repente empezó a llover, "suddenly it began to rain".
13. por fin pudo gritar (line 15): the preterite of poder means "managed", as the result of an effort. Example: Por fin pude terminar el libro, "at last I managed to finish the book".
14. lo había conseguido (line 15): the pluperfect gives what had already happened before the shout. Example: Cuando llegué, ya habían cenado, "when I arrived, they had already eaten" (Foundation, lesson 53).
15. supo enseguida (line 16): saber in the preterite means "found out" or "learned", a single moment; the imperfect sabía means "knew". Example: Lo supe por la radio, "I heard about it on the radio" (Foundation, lesson 42).

- Conocí a mi marido en una fiesta de Nochevieja.
  en: I met my husband at a New Year's Eve party.
- Justo cuando salía, empezó a llover.
  en: Just as I was leaving, it started to rain.
- Por fin pude terminar el libro.
  en: At last I managed to finish the book.
- Lo supe por la radio, esta mañana.
  en: I found out about it on the radio this morning.
- No quise decirle nada.
  en: I refused to tell him anything.

ex2:
- Ayer [supe] que habían llegado los primos.
  en: Yesterday I found out that the cousins had arrived.
- Cuando llegamos, la tele ya [estaba] encendida.
  en: When we arrived, the telly was already on.
- Al [terminar] la cena, nos fuimos a dormir.
  en: After finishing dinner, we went to bed.
- Mi hijo [se echó] a reír cuando vio al gato.
  en: My son burst out laughing when he saw the cat.
- Por fin [pudo] salir de casa a las diez.
  en: At last he managed to leave the house at ten.

numeral: Lección sexta
title: ¡Qué calor!
title.en: So hot!

dialogue:
- Nada más salir del hotel, un turista inglés decide dar un paseo por Sevilla: hace un calor de muerte, son las tres de una tarde de agosto.
  en: As soon as he leaves the hotel, an English tourist decides to go for a walk in Seville: it is roasting, three o'clock on an August afternoon (it is a heat of death).
- Las calles están vacías, las persianas bajadas y hay un bochorno que se pega a la piel.
  en: The streets are empty, the shutters are down and there is a muggy heat that sticks to the skin.
- Nada más doblar la esquina, se da cuenta de que no hay nadie.
  en: As soon as he turns the corner, he realises there is nobody about.
- Por fin ve a otro hombre paseando, y es otro inglés: los dos se miran la mar de sorprendidos.
  en: At last he sees another man out walking, and he is another Englishman: the two of them look at each other, really surprised.
- En Madrid, una mujer no se fía del hombre del tiempo, sino de la cruz verde de la farmacia de la esquina.
  en: In Madrid, a woman does not trust the weatherman, but the green cross of the chemist's on the corner.
- En cuanto sale de casa, mira la cruz, y la cruz decide si lleva abanico o paraguas.
  en: As soon as she leaves home, she looks at the cross, and the cross decides whether she takes a fan or an umbrella.
- Un día, el hombre del tiempo anuncia el fin de la ola de calor: veintidós grados por la tarde.
  en: One day the weatherman announces the end of the heatwave: twenty-two degrees in the afternoon.
- Pero a las cuatro y pico ella mira la cruz y lee un cuarenta y uno.
  en: But at just after four she looks at the cross and reads forty-one.
- Vuelve a casa, baja las persianas y pasa la tarde con el abanico.
  en: She goes back home, pulls down the blinds and spends the afternoon with her fan.
- Por la noche, una vecina le cuenta que la tarde ha sido de lo más agradable, y que la cruz, a esa hora, no daba los grados: daba la hora, las cuatro y cuarenta y uno.
  en: In the evening a neighbour tells her that the afternoon was as pleasant as can be, and that at that moment the cross was not showing the degrees: it was showing the time, forty-one minutes past four.
- Un visitante llega a Burgos en mayo, con una camiseta de manga corta y bastante optimismo.
  en: A visitor arrives in Burgos in May, in a short-sleeved shirt and with quite a lot of optimism.
- Apenas ha dejado la maleta, el encargado del hotel le recita el viejo refrán castellano: nueve meses de invierno y tres de infierno.
  en: He has barely put down his suitcase when the hotel manager recites the old Castilian saying: nine months of winter and three of hell.
- El visitante se pone serio y pregunta en cuál de las dos estaciones ha llegado.
  en: The visitor looks serious and asks which of the two seasons he has arrived in.
- A la mañana siguiente, a las ocho, hay escarcha en las ventanas del comedor, y él desayuna con el abrigo puesto.
  en: The next morning, at eight, there is frost on the dining-room windows, and he has his breakfast with his coat on.
- Vuelve a preguntarlo, y el camarero mira el sol, que ya asoma, y contesta sin dudar.
  en: He asks again, and the waiter looks at the sun, which is already coming out, and answers without a moment's doubt.
- Hoy tiene usted suerte: los dos.
  en: Today you are in luck: both of them.

notes.en:
1. Nada más salir (line 1), nada más doblar (line 3): nada más plus an infinitive means "as soon as", and the same person does both actions. Example: Nada más llegar, me puse a trabajar, "as soon as I arrived, I got down to work".
2. hace un calor de muerte (line 1): literally "it makes a heat of death", the set phrase for extreme heat. The same pattern works for cold: hace un frío de muerte, "it is bitterly cold".
3. bochorno (line 2): a close, muggy heat with no breeze, the kind that sticks to the skin. Example: Hay mucho bochorno antes de la tormenta, "it's very muggy before the storm".
4. chispea, chaparrón, refresca: chispea is a light drizzle, a chaparrón is a sudden heavy shower, and refrescar used of the weather means that it gets cooler. Example: Ha caído un chaparrón y ahora refresca, "a heavy shower came down and now it's cooler".
5. la mar de sorprendidos (line 4): la mar de plus an adjective is familiar and emphatic, "really" or "awfully", and it can be used for good things too. Example: Estoy la mar de contento, "I'm really pleased".
6. En cuanto sale (line 6): en cuanto plus the present indicative describes what happens each time; the future form takes the subjunctive (Foundation, lesson 73). Example: En cuanto llega el verano, se va a la playa, "as soon as summer comes, she goes to the beach".
7. la ola de calor (line 7): a heatwave, where ola is a wave, the same word as in the sea. Example: Hay una ola de calor en toda la península, "there is a heatwave across the whole peninsula".
8. de lo más agradable (line 10): de lo más plus an adjective means "as much as can be", a strong intensifier. Example: Es de lo más amable, "he is as kind as can be".
9. bastante optimismo (line 11): bastante before a noun means "quite a lot". It also means "enough", so the context decides: Tenemos bastante dinero, "we have enough money". Before an adjective, bastante caliente means "rather hot", not "very hot".
10. Apenas ha dejado (line 12): apenas with the perfect means "hardly", and it is followed by the next event at once. Example: Apenas he llegado, ya quieren irse, "I have hardly arrived and they already want to leave".
11. el viejo refrán castellano (line 12): a refrán is a proverb, and castellano refers to Castile, the old kingdom at the heart of the peninsula. Example: Hay un refrán para cada cosa, "there is a saying for everything".
12. en cuál de las dos estaciones (line 13): cuál asks which one of a small set, here two seasons. Example: ¿Cuál de los dos prefieres?, "which of the two do you prefer?"
13. Hoy tiene usted suerte: los dos (line 16): the waiter uses usted with the visitor, the form for a stranger, and the answer turns the saying around, so that both seasons arrive in one day. Example: Tiene usted razón, "you are right" (formal).

ex1:

  en: As soon as we went in, we noticed the muggy heat.
- Hace un frío de muerte en enero.
  en: It is bitterly cold in January.
- Estoy la mar de contento con el resultado.
  en: I'm really pleased with the result (to a friend).
- En cuanto llega el verano, nos vamos a la playa.
  en: As soon as summer comes, we go to the beach.
- Hay bastante gente en la plaza esta noche.
  en: There are quite a lot of people in the square tonight.

ex2:
- Nada más [llegar], me puse a trabajar.
  en: As soon as I arrived, I got down to work.
- Hay una [ola] de calor en toda la península.
  en: There is a heatwave across the whole peninsula.
- Es de lo más [amable], siempre tiene una sonrisa.
  en: He is as kind as can be; he always has a smile.
- Apenas he [llegado], ya quieren irse.
  en: I have hardly arrived, and they already want to leave.
- ¿[Cuál] de los dos prefieres?
  en: Which of the two do you prefer?
