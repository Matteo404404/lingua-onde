notes.en:
1. actualmente (sentence 1): currently, at present. It is a false friend: it does not mean actually, which is en realidad, as in Foundation, lesson 96. Example: Actualmente vivo en Sevilla, "I currently live in Seville".
2. en realidad (sentence 2): in fact, actually, the English sense of the word. Example: En realidad no tengo hambre, "actually I'm not hungry".
3. Es que (sentence 6): a way of starting an explanation, "it's just that" or "the thing is". Example: Es que llevo dos días sin dormir, "it's just that I haven't slept for two days".
4. Por cierto (sentence 7): by the way, when a new point is added to the talk. Example: Por cierto, ¿has llamado a tu madre?, "by the way, did you ring your mother?"
5. no es que ... es que (sentence 10): it is not that ..., it is that ...; the first verb takes the subjunctive, here the perfect haya leído, and the second has the indicative. Example: No es que no quiera ir, es que tengo trabajo, "it's not that I don't want to go, it's that I have work".
6. Total, que (sentence 11): a familiar way of summing up, "so in the end" or "so, in short". Example: Total, que no vino nadie, "so in the end nobody came".
7. En fin (sentence 12): an end to the talk, "well, in short", often with a note of resignation. Example: En fin, ya veremos, "well, we'll see".
8. De hecho (sentence 14): in fact, to confirm something already said. Example: De hecho, ya lo sabía, "in fact I already knew it".
9. A decir verdad (sentence 15): to be honest, before a frank opinion. Example: A decir verdad, no me gusta el café, "to be honest, I don't like coffee".
10. O sea (sentence 16): that is to say, so; the usual way to restate a point. Example: O sea, que no vienes, "so you're not coming".
11. eventualmente (sentence 16): a false friend: it means possibly, or at some point, not eventually in the sense of at last, which is al final. Example: Eventualmente podemos cenar juntos, "we may have dinner together at some point".
12. Vamos, que (sentence 17): a familiar way of drawing a conclusion, "so, in short", often a little ironic. Example: Vamos, que no hay prisa, "so, there's no hurry".
13. sin que nadie se extrañe (sentence 19): sin que takes the subjunctive, as in lesson 17, note 4. Example: Lo hago sin que nadie me lo pida, "I do it without anyone asking me to".

notes.en:
1. comparece y, por medio del presente escrito, EXPONE (item 1): the opening of a Spanish instancia, a formal request to the town hall. It gives the name, the age, the work and the address, then comparece, appears, and EXPONE, sets out, the facts. Example: El interesado comparece y EXPONE, "the applicant appears and sets out".
2. Que el día 14 (item 2): each fact in an EXPONE section begins with Que, and the dates come in full. Example: Que el pedido llegó el martes, "that the order arrived on Tuesday".
3. según la ordenanza municipal (item 3): the ordenanza is the bylaw of the town, and según names the source of the rule. Example: Según la ordenanza, el aparcamiento está prohibido de noche, "according to the bylaw, parking is forbidden at night".
4. la abajo firmante (item 4): the person who signs below. A man says el abajo firmante. Example: El abajo firmante declara que es cierto, "the undersigned declares that it is true".
5. ni molestia a ningún vecino (item 5): after a negative verb, Spanish adds the negative words again: no se ha producido daño alguno ni molestia, with alguno and ningún after the verb. Example: No vino ningún vecino, "no neighbour came".
6. me indicó que (item 6): indicar que reports what the officer said; the indicative is used because it is a plain report of a fact. Example: Me indicó que el plazo terminaba el viernes, "he told me the deadline was Friday".
7. no cita el artículo (item 7): citar means to cite or quote. A fine usually names the article it applies, so that the person can check it. Example: La sentencia cita la ley de 1995, "the judgment cites the law of 1995".
8. conforme a lo dispuesto (item 8): conforme a means in accordance with, and lo dispuesto is what the rule lays down. Example: Conforme a lo acordado, pagamos en dos plazos, "in accordance with what was agreed, we pay in two instalments".
9. en relación con (item 9): in relation to, the formal way of saying about or regarding. Example: En relación con su carta, le informo de lo siguiente, "with regard to your letter, I inform you of the following".
10. abonado puntualmente (item 10): abonar is the formal verb for to pay, and tasas are fees charged by the town. Example: Abono el recibo a su vencimiento, "I pay the bill when it falls due".
11. en virtud de (item 11): by virtue of, by reason of a right or a rule. Example: En virtud de este contrato, el alquiler se revisa cada año, "by virtue of this contract, the rent is reviewed each year".
12. SOLICITA: que se anule (item 12): the request section of an instancia, with que and the present subjunctive. Subsidiariamente means as a fallback, if the first request is refused. Example: SOLICITA: que se admita la prueba, "REQUESTS: that the evidence be admitted".
13. Sírvase tener por presentado (item 13): sírvase is the formal please, from servir, followed by an infinitive. Tener por presentado means to consider as submitted. Example: Sírvase enviarnos el justificante, "please send us the receipt".
14. Ruego que se me notifique (item 14): ruego que is a formal request with the subjunctive, here passive se with notificar. Example: Ruego que me confirmen la cita, "I ask you to confirm the appointment".
15. Sin otro particular, le saluda atentamente (item 15): the close of a formal letter, as in Foundation, lesson 89, where atentamente is "yours faithfully".

  en: The argument about the amount is also rejected, since the fine conforms to the scale for minor offences.

  en: Whoever breaches what the bylaw provides will be fined in line with its scale, where there is ground for it.

  en: Having looked at the applicable rules, it is agreed to dismiss the appeal and to keep the fine of 150 euros.

  en: The present decision is to be served, with notice of the appeals that may be lodged against it.

  en: An appeal for reconsideration may be lodged against this decision within one month.
- Lo que se comunica a los efectos oportunos.
  en: This is communicated for the appropriate purposes.
- Por delegación del alcalde, firma el jefe del Servicio de Sanciones.
  en: By delegation of the mayor, the head of the Sanctions Department signs.
- Firmado digitalmente: el jefe del Servicio de Sanciones.
  en: Digitally signed: the head of the Sanctions Department.

  en: Please note that, in the event of non-payment, the debt will be enforced by the executive procedure.

  en: The applicant is to be notified at the address on record in the file.

notes.en:
1. Visto el expediente (item 1): a participle at the start of a decision, which sets out the grounds before the decision itself. Visto and vistas agree with what they describe. Plain Spanish: después de examinar el expediente, "after examining the file".
2. Considerando que (item 2): the gerund opens the reasons, as in Visto el informe. Plain Spanish: como el escrito se presentó a tiempo, "since the letter was sent on time". Example: Considerando que la obra está terminada, se paga, "considering the work is finished, it is paid".
3. Consta en el expediente (item 3): consta means is on record, and it is the usual way to say that something appears in the file. Plain Spanish: está en el expediente. Example: Consta en el acta que llegó tarde, "it is on record that he arrived late".
4. Se desestima (item 4): passive se with desestimar, to reject a claim or an argument. Plain Spanish: se rechaza, "is refused". Example: Se desestima la solicitud, "the application is refused".
5. Se desestima asimismo (item 5): asimismo is a formal word for también, "also". Example: Se aprueba asimismo el presupuesto, "the budget is also approved".
6. En cuanto a (item 6): the formal way to start a new point, "as for" or "with regard to". Plain Spanish: sobre la falta de cita. Example: En cuanto a los plazos, no hay problema, "as for the deadlines, there is no problem".
7. Quien infringiere (item 7): the future subjunctive of infringir. It is kept in laws and rules, and it survives in a few set phrases. Plain Spanish: quien incumpla, "whoever breaks". The -re forms are explained in lesson 49.
8. si hubiere lugar (item 7): the future subjunctive of haber, hubiere, kept in this set legal phrase. Plain Spanish: si procede, "if it applies". Example: Si hubiere dudas, llámenos, "if there are any doubts, call us".
9. Vistos los preceptos (item 8): preceptos are the articles of the law. Vistos, again a participle, sets out the reason. Plain Spanish: después de leer las normas que se aplican, "after reading the rules that apply". Example: Vistos los documentos, firmo, "having seen the documents, I sign".
10. Procédase (item 9): the passive imperative of proceder, which tells the office what to do next. Plain Spanish: notifíquese, "notify". Example: Procédase al pago de la factura, "the invoice is to be paid".
11. cabe recurso (item 10): caber means there is room for, so cabe recurso means an appeal can be lodged. Plain Spanish: puede presentar un recurso. Example: Contra esta decisión cabe recurso, "an appeal can be made against this decision".
12. Por delegación (item 12): by delegation: the head signs for the mayor, who has passed the power to him. Example: Por delegación de la directora, firma el secretario, "by delegation of the director, the secretary signs".
13. vía de apremio (item 14): the procedure to collect an unpaid debt by force, through the authorities. Plain Spanish: se cobrará de forma obligatoria, "it will be collected compulsorily". Example: La multa pasó a la vía de apremio, "the fine went to enforcement".
14. Notifíquese (item 15): the imperative of notificar with se, here written as a command to the office. Example: Notifíquese al interesado, "the applicant is to be notified".
15. Posdata (item 16): the postscript, written after the signature, as in Foundation, lesson 89. It is the last line of the letter, and it has the news.

  en: Whoever breaks the contract will pay a fine. (formal)

  en: It is on record in the file that the invoice was paid in May.

notes.en:
1. el Caribe (sentence 1): Spanish puts the article before the name of the sea and the region, el Caribe, where English says the Caribbean. Example: Pasamos el invierno en el Caribe, "we spent the winter in the Caribbean".
2. la ciudad más antigua de América (sentence 2): after a superlative Spanish says de for "of" or "in", as in Foundation, lesson 47. Example: Es el edificio más alto de la ciudad, "it is the tallest building in the city".
3. Patrimonio de la Humanidad (sentence 3): the name Spain uses for UNESCO's World Heritage list. Example: La Alhambra es Patrimonio de la Humanidad, "the Alhambra is a World Heritage site".
4. territorio, estadounidenses (sentence 4): a territory is neither an independent country nor a state, but its people are US citizens. Estadounidense is the word Spain uses for someone from the United States, where English says American. Example: Mi profesora de inglés es estadounidense, "my English teacher is American".
5. canoa, hamaca, huracán (sentence 5): words from Taíno, the language of the people of these islands before Columbus, which came into Spanish and from there into English, as lesson 37 explains for the words from Nahuatl (lesson 37, note 5). Example: Dormimos en una hamaca, "we slept in a hammock".
6. la s al final de sílaba (sentence 7): a feature of the Caribbean, named as such. In Spain the s is pronounced clearly; in Caribbean speech it weakens or is dropped, but the spelling does not change. Example: Los niños juegan en la calle, "the children are playing in the street" (said in Spain, with the s clear).
7. ¿Qué tú quieres? (sentence 8): in Spain the subject pronoun is usually dropped and used only to contrast or stress (Foundation, lesson 7). In the Caribbean it can stay in a question, after the question word. Example: ¿Qué quieres beber?, "what do you want to drink?" (said in Spain).
8. arroz con frijoles (sentence 9): the same dish under different names. Habichuelas is used in Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic; congrí and moros y cristianos are used in Cuba. Example: Pido arroz con habichuelas en Puerto Rico, "I order rice and beans in Puerto Rico".
9. mofongo (sentence 10): a dish of green plantain, fried and mashed with garlic. Example: Quiero probar el mofongo, "I want to try the mofongo".
10. ustedes (sentence 11): the plural you of the Caribbean, as in the Canaries (lesson 30, note 8) and in Mexico (lesson 37, note 13); in Spain, vosotros. Example: Ustedes llegan temprano, "you all arrive early" (said to a group, in Mexico).
11. La Habana (sentence 12): the Spanish name of the city, which English writes as Havana. Una de las with a superlative means one of the most or one of the largest. Example: Es uno de los mejores restaurantes de la ciudad, "it is one of the best restaurants in the city".
12. las grandes ligas (sentence 13): the Spanish name for the major leagues of baseball in the United States. Example: Juega en las grandes ligas, "he plays in the major leagues".
13. tostones (sentence 15): slices of green plantain, flattened and fried twice. Example: Me sirvieron tostones con el pescado, "they served me tostones with the fish".
14. Nueva York (sentence 16): the Spanish name of the city, which English spells New York. Example: Mi hermana vive en Nueva York, "my sister lives in New York".
15. guagua (sentence 18): a word that changes meaning: the bus in Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Canaries (lesson 30, note 7), and a baby in Chile. In Spain the bus is the autobús. Example: Tomo la guagua al trabajo, "I take the bus to work" (said in the Canaries).

notes.en:
1. convivían (sentence 1): convivir is to live together, and the imperfect shows the house as it was for years. Example: En aquella casa convivían tres generaciones, "three generations lived together in that house".
2. Según contó él mismo (sentence 4): contar is to tell, and según contó él mismo gives the source of a fact when the source is the person himself. Example: Según cuenta mi madre, nevó mucho en su pueblo, "according to my mother, it snowed a lot in her village".
3. con quien escribió (sentence 8): for a person after a preposition Spanish uses quien, as in English with whom. Example: El amigo con quien viajé es periodista, "the friend I travelled with is a journalist".
4. un cargo en el cual (sentence 9): el cual after a preposition, as in Foundation, lesson 80. In speech en el que is more usual. Example: Es la ciudad en la cual nací, "it is the city in which I was born".
5. casi no veía (sentence 10): the imperfect describes a state that went on, and casi no means hardly. Example: Casi no oía nada con tanto ruido, "I could hardly hear anything with all that noise".
6. lo cual sorprendió (sentence 12): lo cual refers to the whole fact in the sentence before, not to a noun, and it means which. Example: No vino a la reunión, lo cual me extrañó, "he did not come to the meeting, which surprised me".
7. El Aleph, el cuento que da título (sentence 13): the commas make the clause add information about a thing already named. Without commas, the clause picks out one group: Los alumnos que estaban cansados se fueron, "the students who were tired left", against Los alumnos, que estaban cansados, se fueron, "the students, who were tired, left", where all of them left. English does the same with commas.
8. cuya obra (sentence 14): cuyo agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner, as in Foundation, lesson 80. Obra is feminine and singular, so it is cuya. Example: Es una profesora cuyos alumnos la admiran, "she is a teacher whose students admire her".

notes.en:
1. un éxito enorme (sentence 2): éxito means success, not exit; it is a false friend. Example: El concierto fue un éxito, "the concert was a success".
2. Federico García Lorca (sentence 3): the poet of lesson 32, who was a friend of Neruda in Madrid. Example: Lorca escribió obras de teatro, "Lorca wrote plays".
3. riquísima (sentence 6): the absolute superlative in -ísimo, added to the adjective without its last vowel, as in Foundation, lesson 47. Example: El pastel es riquísimo, "the cake is delicious".
4. había ido coleccionando (sentence 7): ir with a gerund means a process that goes on bit by bit, as in Foundation, lesson 62. Example: Ha ido perdiendo peso poco a poco, "he has been losing weight bit by bit".
5. ¡Qué casa tan bonita! (sentence 8): with a noun, Spanish says ¡qué + noun + tan + adjective!, and tan is dropped when the adjective stands alone: ¡qué bonita! Example: ¡Qué día tan bonito!, "what a lovely day!"
6. ¡Cuántos objetos! (sentence 9): cuánto agrees with the noun it counts: cuántos objetos, cuántas casas. Example: ¡Cuántas flores hay en el jardín!, "what a lot of flowers there are in the garden!"
7. tiernísimos, intensísima (sentence 10): the spelling changes with the superlative. Rico becomes riquísimo (c to qu), feliz becomes felicísimo (z to c), and the stress falls on the í. Tierno becomes tiernísimo, and intenso becomes intensísimo. Example: La película es larguísima, "the film is extremely long".
8. lo bonita que era (sentence 11): lo + adjective + que means how, as in Foundation, lesson 67, where lo is the neuter article. Example: No imaginas lo difícil que es, "you have no idea how hard it is".
9. el Premio Nobel de Literatura (sentence 12): Spanish keeps the article in the name of the prize, where English says the Nobel Prize in Literature. Example: Ganó el Premio Nobel de Física, "he won the Nobel Prize in Physics".

notes.en:
1. tirar la casa por la ventana (sentence 1): to spare no expense, to go all out. The literal sense is to throw the house out of the window. Example: Para la boda tiraron la casa por la ventana, "for the wedding they spared no expense".
2. tirar a la basura (sentence 3): tirar means to throw, and to throw something away is also tirar, where English says put it in the bin. Example: Tira esos periódicos a la basura, "throw those newspapers in the bin".
3. tirar de la lengua (sentence 4): to get someone to talk and tell you what they know. The literal sense is to pull on the tongue. Example: Le tiré de la lengua y me contó todo, "I got him talking and he told me everything".
4. tira a su padre (sentence 5): tirar a means to resemble, to take after, or to tend towards a colour: tira a verde is greenish. Example: Tu hija tira a tu madre, "your daughter takes after your mother".
5. tirar los tejos (sentence 6): familiar, to make a pass at someone. Tejo is a metal disc thrown at a target in the game of tejo. Example: ¿Me estás tirando los tejos?, "are you making a pass at me?"
6. tirarse un farol (sentence 7): to bluff, to pretend to know or to be able to do something. Farol also means lantern. Example: No te creo, te estás tirando un farol, "I don't believe you, you're bluffing".
7. tirarse a la piscina (sentence 8): to take the plunge, to decide to do something without much hesitation. The literal sense is to dive into the pool. Example: Por fin me tiré a la piscina y me apunté al curso, "at last I took the plunge and signed up for the course".
8. tirarse de los pelos (sentence 9): to despair, to be at one's wits' end. The image is the English tear one's hair out. Example: Me tiro de los pelos con este ordenador, "I'm tearing my hair out with this computer".
9. tira y afloja (sentence 10): give and take, a dispute in which each side gives a little. The literal sense is pull and slacken. Example: Hubo mucho tira y afloja antes de firmar, "there was a lot of give and take before we signed".
10. a todo tirar (sentence 11): familiar, very good, great. Example: El viaje estuvo a todo tirar, "the trip was great".
11. tirar por la borda (sentence 12): to waste, to throw away money or effort. The literal sense is to throw overboard. Example: Con ese error tiramos por la borda todo el trabajo, "with that mistake we threw all the work away".
12. Tirar on a door (sentence 13): on a door, tirar means pull, and empujar means push. The sign Tirar tells you to pull. Example: Tira de la puerta, que se abre hacia ti, "pull the door, it opens towards you".
13. tirar de las orejas (sentence 14): to pull someone's ears, and figuratively to scold. Here the family joke takes the literal sense. Example: Me tiraron de las orejas por llegar tarde, "they told me off for being late".
14. tirar la toalla (sentence 15): to give up, as in lesson 20, note 3, where the image is of a boxing trainer throwing in the towel. Example: No tires la toalla, que ya casi está, "don't give up, it's almost done".
15. ir tirando (sentence 22): to get by, to manage with what there is. Voy tirando is the usual answer to ¿qué tal? when things are neither good nor bad. Example: Voy tirando con el trabajo, "I'm getting by with work".

notes.en:
1. me dijo que lo apuntara (sentence 3): a reported command takes the subjunctive, as in Foundation, lesson 74: decir que with a past verb gives the imperfect subjunctive, apuntara. A plain fact would take the indicative: me dijo que lo apuntaba. Example: Me dijo que llamara a su madre, "he told me to ring his mother".
2. ni dónde está Sevilla (sentence 4): in an indirect question the question word keeps its accent (dónde, quién, cuándo, qué), and the verb is indicative. The ni means not even. Example: No sé dónde está la estación, "I don't know where the station is".
3. no le hice caso (sentence 5): hacer caso a means to pay attention to or to take notice of someone, and the person takes a before them. Example: Nunca me hace caso cuando hablo, "he never listens when I speak".
4. pero / sino (sentence 8): pero joins a positive clause, and sino comes after a negative one: Las meninas no es de Goya, sino de Velázquez. Example: No es caro, sino barato, "it isn't expensive, it's cheap".
5. sevillano (sentence 9): the adjective for someone from Seville, and for the city in general. Example: Mi amiga es sevillana, "my friend is from Seville".
6. me preguntó que si (sentence 12): in reported questions que si is common in speech and familiar; the plain form is me preguntó si. Example: Me preguntó que si quería café, "he asked me whether I wanted coffee" (familiar).
7. Le pregunté a él si (sentence 13): preguntar si means to ask whether, and the person asked takes le, with a after it for emphasis. Example: Le pregunté si venía, "I asked him whether he was coming".
8. había sido en 1991 (sentence 13): a reported fact keeps the indicative, and the past moves back, so fue becomes había sido, as in Foundation, lesson 65. Example: Me dijo que había llamado ayer, "he told me he had rung yesterday".
9. Sídney (sentence 16): Spanish spells the city Sídney, with an accent on the í, and English writes Sydney. Example: Mi hermana vive en Sídney, "my sister lives in Sydney".
10. fijarse en (sentence 19): to notice, to pay attention to something, and the person takes se. Example: ¿Te has fijado en el precio?, "did you notice the price?"
11. por un punto (sentence 20): por shows the margin of a win or a loss. Example: Perdimos por un gol, "we lost by a goal".

notes.en:
1. he suspendido (sentence 1): in Spain suspender means to fail an exam, and aprobar means to pass it. The perfect is used because the result still stands today, a time not over. Example: He aprobado el examen de conducir, "I passed the driving test".
2. No hay mal que por bien no venga (sentence 2): the literal sense is that there is no harm which does not come for some good. Venga is in the subjunctive, because the proverb speaks of any harm, and no hay mal que ... is a fixed form. Foundation, lesson 95 gave six other proverbs, and these are new. Example: No hay mal que por bien no venga: la avería nos dio una tarde libre, "every cloud has a silver lining: the breakdown gave us a free afternoon".
3. hija (sentence 2): a grandmother, or any older woman, calls a young woman hija, and it means dear, as in Foundation, lesson 17. Example: ¿Qué quieres, hija?, "what do you want, dear?"
4. Quien mucho abarca poco aprieta (sentence 4): the literal sense is that whoever embraces a lot grips little. Abarcar is to take in, to cover, and apretar is to squeeze, to hold tight. Example: Quien mucho abarca poco aprieta: tienes tres trabajos y ninguno bien hecho, "whoever takes on too much does none of it well: you have three jobs and not one is done properly".
5. A caballo regalado no le mires el diente (sentence 6): the literal sense is do not look at a horse's teeth when it was given to you, since the teeth show its age. In a negative command the pronoun comes before the verb, no le mires, as no + pronoun + verb. Example: No lo mires tanto, que es un regalo, "don't look at it so hard, it's a present".
6. Llévate el abrigo (sentence 6): llevarse means to take something with you, and the pronoun joins onto the affirmative command: llévate. Example: Llévate las llaves, que vuelvo tarde, "take the keys with you, I'm coming back late".
7. Camarón que se duerme (sentence 8): the literal sense is that a shrimp which falls asleep is carried away by the current. It is said of someone who waits too long to act. Example: Apúntate hoy al curso, que camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente, "sign up for the course today, because if you wait you miss it".
8. Lo llamo yo (sentence 9): llamar takes a before a person, llamar a Nacho, and the pronoun lo stands for him. Example: Lo llamé ayer y no me contestó, "I rang him yesterday and he didn't answer".
9. sin dar señales (sentence 9): dar señales de vida means to get in touch, or to show that you are still alive. Example: Hace meses que no da señales de vida, "he has not been in touch for months".
10. Ojos que no ven (sentence 10): the nearest English is "out of sight, out of mind", and the proverb is completed by corazón que no siente. Tú verás means it is your decision, you'll see. Example: Tú verás lo que haces, "it's your decision what you do".
11. a buen entendedor (sentence 12): ya sabes introduces a saying the speaker only starts, and the listener is expected to finish it. The full proverb is a buen entendedor, pocas palabras bastan, "a word to the wise is enough". Example: Ya sabes, a buen entendedor, pocas palabras bastan, "you know, a word to the wise is enough".
12. estás en los huesos (sentence 12): the literal sense is that you are in the bones; it means very thin, skin and bone. Example: Después de la gripe estaba en los huesos, "after the flu he was skin and bone".
13. Sancho Panza (sentence 13): the squire of Don Quijote in Cervantes' novel, famous for speaking in proverbs, as in Foundation, lesson 97. Example: Mi tío habla como Sancho, con un refrán para cada cosa, "my uncle talks like Sancho, with a proverb for everything".
14. ningún refrán / ninguno (sentence 15, 16): ninguno and ningún mean none, and before a masculine noun the form is shortened to ningún. Ninguno stands on its own. Example: No tengo ninguno, "I haven't got one".
15. a buena abuela (sentence 17): buena agrees with abuela, a feminine noun, as buen agrees with entendedor in the proverb, and buen is the short form before a masculine noun. Example: Es una buena idea, "it's a good idea".

  en: Whoever takes on too much holds very little, so choose just one subject. (to a friend)

  en: I'm starting to study seriously tomorrow, honestly. (to a friend)

notes.en:
1. corrigiendo (sentence 1): corregir means to correct, and it changes e to i in the stem: corrige, corrigiendo. Example: Corrige los errores del texto, "correct the mistakes in the text".
2. pienso de que (sentence 3): with pensar, the usual form is pensar que, without de, when the verb means to think that. The test is to replace the clause with eso: pienso eso gives pienso que, not pienso de que. Example: Pienso que tienes razón, "I think you're right".
3. habían muchas personas (sentences 4 and 5): impersonal haber is always singular, so había muchas personas, "there were many people", and hubo problemas, "there were problems". Example: Había mucha gente en la plaza, "there were a lot of people in the square".
4. me alegro que (sentence 7): alegrarse takes de, so the form is me alegro de que venga, "I'm glad he's coming". Test it as before: me alegro de eso gives me alegro de que. Example: Me alegro de que hayas venido, "I'm glad you came".
5. haber si / a ver si (sentence 9): a ver si means "let's see whether", and it is written as two words, with ver as the verb. Example: A ver si llegamos a tiempo, "let's see whether we get there on time".
6. sí / si (sentence 11): sí with an accent means yes, and si without an accent means if. Example: Sí, quiero, "yes, I do". Si llueve, nos quedamos, "if it rains, we stay".
7. porqué / por qué / porque (sentence 13): por qué is two words in a question, porqué is a noun meaning the reason, and porque is one word and joins a reason to a clause. Example: Llegué tarde porque perdí el autobús, "I was late because I missed the bus".
8. encargar (sentence 15): encargar means to order or to ask for something to be made, as in encargar una tarta. Example: He encargado el pan para el sábado, "I have ordered the bread for Saturday".
9. jubilarse (sentence 17): jubilarse means to retire from work, and the reflexive pronoun stays with the verb. Example: Mi padre se jubila el año que viene, "my father retires next year".
10. a propósito (sentence 19): a propósito means on purpose, and it also means by the way when it starts a sentence. Example: No lo hizo a propósito, "he didn't do it on purpose".

  en: At the end of the Civil War he crossed the border with his mother, since thousands of people were fleeing to France at that time.

  en: In the pocket of his coat a sheet of paper was found, with one last line: Estos días azules y este sol de la infancia.

notes.en:
1. Sin embargo (sentence 5): a connector of contrast, "even so", with a comma after it. It joins two sentences where the second goes against what the first led you to expect, as in Foundation, lesson 81. Example: Llovía; sin embargo, salimos a pasear, "it was raining; even so, we went out for a walk".
2. en cambio (sentence 7): on the other hand, it sets one thing against another and sits between commas after the first part. Foundation, lesson 81 has it too. Example: Mi hermano vive en Madrid; yo, en cambio, vivo en Sevilla, "my brother lives in Madrid; I, on the other hand, live in Seville".
3. No obstante (sentence 9): a more formal way to say even so, as sin embargo is. Written texts use it more than speech does. Example: Es caro; no obstante, lo compraré, "it is expensive; even so, I'll buy it".
4. Por consiguiente (sentence 10): therefore, a formal connector that shows a result. It takes commas, and in speech people say por eso. Example: Llovió toda la semana; por consiguiente, se suspendió la excursión, "it rained all week; therefore the trip was cancelled".
5. se hace camino al andar (sentence 10): the verse comes from his Proverbios y cantares, a section of his poetry. Al + infinitive means on or while doing something. Example: Al llegar, llamé a mi madre, "on arriving, I rang my mother".
6. pues (sentence 11): in written style pues means since or for and gives the reason. It comes after the main clause, never at the start of the sentence in this use. The spoken pues at the start, as in Pues vale, means well. Example: No vino a la cena, pues estaba enfermo, "he did not come to dinner, since he was ill".
7. Colliure (sentence 12): Spanish writes the village Colliure, and English writes Collioure. It is in France, on the coast near the Spanish border.
8. Es decir (sentence 12): that is to say, it restates the sentence before in other words, with commas. Example: Vive en Oviedo, es decir, en Asturias, "he lives in Oviedo, that is, in Asturias".
9. Así pues (sentence 13): so, therefore. It concludes a passage, and it can open the sentence. Example: Así pues, no hay más que hablar, "so there is nothing more to say".

numeral: Lección sesenta
title: Cara
title.en: Face

dialogue:
- Inés y Gonzalo llevaban tres horas sentados cara a cara en la mesa de la cocina, sin decidir dónde pasar el verano.
  en: Inés and Gonzalo had been sitting face to face at the kitchen table for three hours, without deciding where to spend the summer.
- Hugo, el hijo mayor, no quería dar la cara en la discusión, y se quedó en el sofá con el móvil.
  en: Hugo, the eldest son, did not want to face up to the argument, and stayed on the sofa with his mobile.
- Inés quería la playa, Gonzalo quería la sierra, y el hotel de la montaña, que acababa de recibir un lavado de cara, costaba un ojo de la cara.
  en: Inés wanted the beach, Gonzalo wanted the mountains, and the mountain hotel, which had just had a facelift, cost an arm and a leg.
- Gonzalo, con cara de póquer, dijo que nadie hace nada por su cara bonita, y que el hotel le parecía una broma.
  en: Gonzalo, with a poker face, said that nobody does anything for nothing, and that the hotel seemed a joke to him.
- Inés le contestó que tenía mucha cara: el hotel lo había encontrado ella.
  en: Inés told him he had a nerve: she was the one who had found the hotel.
- Gonzalo le echó en cara que siempre se salía con la suya.
  en: Gonzalo reproached her for always getting her own way.
- Inés tenía cara de pocos amigos, y Hugo lo notó desde el sofá.
  en: Inés looked unfriendly, and Hugo noticed it from the sofa.
- Hugo dijo que él no iba a plantar cara a nadie, y que le daba igual el destino.
  en: Hugo said he was not going to stand up to anyone, and that he did not mind where they went.
- Entonces Inés propuso jugárselo a cara o cruz, y Gonzalo puso buena cara, aunque no le hacía ninguna gracia.
  en: Then Inés suggested tossing a coin for it, and Gonzalo put a good face on it, though he did not find it funny at all.
- Hugo se levantó, sacó del bolsillo una moneda de cinco euros y la enseñó a los dos.
  en: Hugo got up, took a five-euro coin out of his pocket and showed it to both of them.
- Inés y Gonzalo se vieron las caras, y los dos supieron que ya no había vuelta atrás.
  en: Inés and Gonzalo faced each other, and both knew there was no going back.
- De cara a las vacaciones, lo de menos era quién ganaba; lo importante era no volver a discutir.
  en: With a view to the holidays, who won did not matter; what mattered was not to argue again.
- Hugo lanzó la moneda, que dio vueltas en el aire, y nadie se atrevió a mirar mientras caía.
  en: Hugo tossed the coin, which spun in the air, and nobody dared to look while it fell.
- Gonzalo se quedó callado: se le cayó la cara de vergüenza al recordar la apuesta del año pasado.
  en: Gonzalo fell silent: his face fell with shame when he remembered last year's bet.
- La moneda cayó de canto sobre la mesa.
  en: The coin landed on its edge on the table.

notes.en:
1. cara a cara (sentence 1): face to face, the two people facing each other in person. Example: Hablamos cara a cara en la oficina, "we talked face to face in the office".
2. dar la cara (sentence 2): to face up to something, to take the responsibility, or to stand up for someone. The literal sense is to give one's face. Example: Siempre das la cara por tus amigos, "you always stand up for your friends".
3. costar un ojo de la cara (sentence 3): to cost a fortune. The literal sense is to cost an eye from the face. As in Foundation, lesson 87. The adjective cara is a different word, meaning expensive: la gasolina está muy cara, "petrol is very expensive".
4. lavado de cara (sentence 3): dar or recibir un lavado de cara is to give something a superficial makeover, such as a new coat of paint on a building, without changing what is inside. Example: Le han dado un lavado de cara al bar, "they've given the bar a facelift".
5. cara de póquer (sentence 4): a face that shows no feeling, a poker face. Example: Tiene cara de póquer: nunca sabes lo que piensa, "he has a poker face: you never know what he thinks".
6. por su cara bonita (sentence 4): for nothing, without a reason. It is used with a negative, nadie hace nada por su cara bonita, "nobody does anything for nothing". The literal sense is for his pretty face.
7. tener cara / ser un caradura (sentence 5): tener mucha cara means to have a lot of nerve, and a caradura is a shameless person. Example: Tiene mucha cara: llega tarde y todavía se queja, "he has a nerve: he arrives late and still complains".
8. echar en cara (sentence 6): to reproach someone for something, as in lesson 1, note 7. The literal sense is to throw in someone's face. Example: Me echó en cara que no la había llamado, "she reproached me for not ringing her".
9. cara de pocos amigos (sentence 7): to look unfriendly, to look as if you do not want company. Example: Hoy tienes cara de pocos amigos, "you look unfriendly today".
10. plantar cara (sentence 8): to stand up to someone, to face them and not give way. Example: Por fin le planté cara al jefe, "at last I stood up to the boss".
11. cara o cruz (sentence 9): heads or tails, the two sides of a coin. Cara is the face side, and cruz is the other. Example: ¿Cara o cruz?, "heads or tails?"
12. poner buena cara (sentence 9): to put a good face on something, to accept it politely even when you do not like it. Example: Puso buena cara y aceptó el regalo, "he put a brave face on it and accepted the present".
13. verse las caras (sentence 11): to face each other in a confrontation, to come to grips. Example: Los dos se vieron las caras en la reunión, "the two of them came to grips at the meeting".
14. de cara a (sentence 12): with a view to, in preparation for. Example: De cara al examen, repaso cada día, "with a view to the exam I revise every day".
15. caérsele a uno la cara de vergüenza (sentence 14): to be so ashamed that one blushes or looks crestfallen. The pronoun comes before the verb, as in se le cayó. Example: Se me cayó la cara de vergüenza cuando lo supe, "I was mortified when I found out".
16. de canto (sentence 15): standing on its edge, not lying flat. Example: Puso el libro de canto en la estantería, "he stood the book on its edge on the shelf".
17. Expressions of this lesson, with the sentence and the note:

costar un ojo de la cara (sentence 3, note 3)
recibir un lavado de cara (sentence 3, note 4)
cara de póquer (sentence 4, note 5)
por su cara bonita (sentence 4, note 6)
tener mucha cara (sentence 5, note 7)
echar en cara (sentence 6, note 8)
cara de pocos amigos (sentence 7, note 9)
plantar cara (sentence 8, note 10)
cara o cruz (sentence 9, note 11)
poner buena cara (sentence 9, note 12)
verse las caras (sentence 11, note 13)
de cara a (sentence 12, note 14)
caérsele a uno la cara de vergüenza (sentence 14, note 15)
de canto (sentence 15, note 16)

ex1:
- Nos sentamos cara a cara en el bar.
  en: We sat face to face in the café.
- Ese bolso cuesta un ojo de la cara, así que no lo compro.
  en: That handbag costs a fortune, so I'm not buying it.
- Mi hermana tiene cara de póquer cuando juega.
  en: My sister has a poker face when she plays.
- Tienes mucha cara, después de lo que dijiste. (to a friend)
  en: You've got a nerve, after what you said. (to a friend)
- Dejé el libro de canto en la estantería.
  en: I put the book on its edge on the shelf.

ex2:
- Nos sentamos [cara a cara] en el bar.
  en: We sat face to face in the café.
- Hoy tienes [cara] de pocos amigos, ¿qué te pasa?
  en: You look unfriendly today, what's wrong?
- Me [echó en cara] que no la llamé.
  en: She threw in my face that I hadn't rung her.
- [De cara a] las vacaciones, ahorro cada mes.
  en: With a view to the holidays, I save every month.
- Puso [buena cara] y aceptó la oferta.
  en: He put a brave face on it and accepted the offer.

== 61 C1
numeral: Lección sesenta y una
title: En el banco del parque
title.en: On the park bench

dialogue:
- Ya está otra vez la chica del abrigo rojo, hablando por el móvil.
  en: There's the girl in the red coat again, talking on her phone.
- Acaba de salir de la farmacia, y ya vuelve a llamar.
  en: She has just come out of the chemist's, and she's calling again already.
- ¿Tendrá novio?
  en: Do you think she has a boyfriend?
- Pues yo creo que sí: tiene un novio que no deja de llamarla.
  en: Well, I think so: she has a boyfriend who won't stop ringing her.
- ¿Y tú cómo lo sabes?
  en: And how do you know?
- Porque su madre anda diciendo por el bar que el novio es de Valladolid.
  en: Because her mother keeps going round the bar saying the boyfriend is from Valladolid.
- ¿Y será buen chico?
  en: And is he a good lad, do you think?
- Eso espero, que ya era hora de que alguien la quisiera.
  en: I hope so, because it was high time somebody loved her.
- Ya lo sabía yo. Tiene cara de estar enamorada desde el verano.
  en: I knew it. She has looked like someone in love since the summer.
- Pues aquel señor de la esquina también lo parece, y no deja de mirarla.
  en: Well, that man on the corner seems so too, and he won't stop looking at her.
- ¿Ese? Ese viene a ser como el panadero: mucha pinta y poco más.
  en: That one? He's more or less like the baker: all show and not much else.
- Bueno, ¿y tú no ibas a ir al mercado?
  en: Well, weren't you going to the market?
- Ya no. Tengo la cena hecha desde las dos, así que no tengo prisa.
  en: Not any more. I've had dinner ready since two, so I'm in no hurry.
- Pues yo me quedo contigo un rato más, que hace buena tarde.
  en: Then I'll stay with you a bit longer, it's a lovely afternoon.
- Oye, ese señor del sombrero, ¿qué tal te cae? Lleva media hora mirándonos.
  en: Listen, that man in the hat, what do you make of him? He's been looking at us for half an hour.
- No me gusta nada ese señor del sombrero.
  en: I don't like that man in the hat at all.
- A mí tampoco.
  en: Nor do I.
- Es mi marido.
  en: He's my husband.
- Por eso.
  en: That's why.

notes.en:
1. Ya está otra vez (sentence 1): said with some irritation, "there he goes again" or "here she goes again". Example: Ya está otra vez con lo mismo, "here he goes again with the same thing".
2. acaba de salir (sentence 2): acabar de + infinitive means to have just done something. Example: Acabo de llegar a casa, "I've just got home".
3. vuelve a llamar (sentence 2): volver a + infinitive means to do something again. Example: Vuelve a llamar cada hora, "she rings again every hour".
4. creo que sí (sentence 4): a short answer. Say creo que sí or creo que no instead of repeating the clause. Example: ¿Viene mañana? Creo que sí, "is he coming tomorrow? I think so".
5. no deja de (sentences 4 and 10): dejar de + infinitive means to stop, and with no it means to keep on doing something. Example: No deja de llover, "it won't stop raining".
6. anda diciendo (sentence 6): andar + gerund is familiar, and it describes someone going about doing something. Example: Anda diciendo que se va a casar, "he's going round saying he's getting married".
7. Eso espero (sentence 8): "I hope so", a short answer to a hope. Example: ¿Llegaremos a tiempo? Eso espero, "will we get there on time? I hope so".
8. Ya lo sabía yo (sentence 9): ya + imperfect means I already knew. Example: Ya lo sabía yo: te lo dije ayer, "I knew it: I told you yesterday".
9. lo parece (sentence 10): lo stands for an adjective said before, as English says so. It works with ser, estar and parecer. Example: ¿Es simpático? Lo parece, "is he nice? He seems so".
10. viene a ser (sentence 11): venir a + infinitive means roughly, more or less. Example: Viene a costar unos treinta euros, "it comes to about thirty euros".
11. tengo la cena hecha (sentence 13): tener + participle describes a thing in a finished state. The participle agrees with the thing: hecha agrees with cena. Example: Tengo las maletas hechas desde ayer, "I have had the cases packed since yesterday".
12. no me gusta nada (sentence 16): with gustar-type verbs the thing liked is the subject, and nada makes the negative stronger. Example: No me gusta nada el frío, "I don't like the cold at all".
13. A mí tampoco (sentence 17): tampoco answers a negative, and a mí adds emphasis. In English it is "nor do I" or "me neither". Example: A mí tampoco me gusta el café, "I don't like coffee either".
14. Por eso (sentence 19): that is why, it gives the cause of what was just said. Example: Llovía, por eso no salimos, "it was raining, that's why we didn't go out".

ex1:
- Acabo de hablar con mi madre.
  en: I've just spoken to my mother.
- Vuelve a llover, y no tengo paraguas.
  en: It's raining again, and I have no umbrella.
- ¿Viene esta tarde? Creo que no.
  en: Is he coming this afternoon? I don't think so.
- Mi hermano no deja de hablar de la boda.
  en: My brother won't stop talking about the wedding.
- Tengo la maleta hecha desde ayer.
  en: I've had the case packed since yesterday.

ex2:
- Acabo [de] llegar a casa.
  en: I've just got home.
- Vuelve [a] llamar cada hora.
  en: She rings again every hour.
- ¿Es simpático? Lo [parece].
  en: Is he nice? He seems so.
- No me [gusta] nada el frío.
  en: I don't like the cold at all.
- Viene [a] costar unos cien euros.
  en: It comes to about a hundred euros.

== 62 C1
numeral: Lección sesenta y dos
title: Como el perro y el gato
title.en: Like cat and dog

dialogue:
- Ayer me tocó la reunión del claustro, y fue un gallinero.
  en: Yesterday I had to sit through the staff meeting, and it was a complete free-for-all.
- ¿Y eso? ¿Quién estaba?
  en: What happened? Who was there?
- Éramos cuatro gatos: el director, Esteban, Nieves y yo.
  en: There were four of us: the head, Esteban, Nieves and me.
- ¿Y Esteban? Ese siempre está como pez en el agua con el micrófono.
  en: And Esteban? He's always in his element with a microphone.
- Pues hoy hizo el ganso todo el rato, y el director no le dijo nada.
  en: Well, today he clowned around the whole time, and the head said nothing to him.
- ¿Y Nieves? ¿Le buscó tres pies al gato a todo?
  en: And Nieves? Did she nitpick everything?
- Ella es un lince: no se le escapa ni una coma del acta, aunque sí le busca tres pies al gato a todo lo demás.
  en: She's sharp: not a single comma of the minutes gets past her, though she does nitpick everything else.
- ¿Y Esteban y Nieves se llevan bien?
  en: And do Esteban and Nieves get on?
- Se llevan como el perro y el gato, y el director es un pez gordo que no escucha a nadie.
  en: They get on like cat and dog, and the head is a big shot who doesn't listen to anyone.
- ¿Y qué propuso el pez gordo?
  en: And what did the big shot propose?
- Nos ha dado gato por liebre: dijo que el horario era voluntario, y no lo es.
  en: He has palmed us off: he said the timetable was voluntary, and it isn't.
- ¿Y tú tuviste la mosca detrás de la oreja?
  en: And did you smell a rat?
- Desde el primer minuto. Entonces cogí el toro por los cuernos y propuse que cada departamento hiciera su propio horario.
  en: From the first minute. So I took the bull by the horns and proposed that each department make its own timetable.
- Eso es matar dos pájaros de un tiro: acabas con el lío y nadie se queda sin horario.
  en: That's killing two birds with one stone: you end the mess and nobody is left without a timetable.
- Ya, pero Samuel no vino, y es la oveja negra del departamento. Además es un gallina cuando hay que hablar en público.
  en: Yes, but Samuel didn't come, and he is the black sheep of the department. He is also a coward when he has to speak in public.
- ¿Y Lorenzo? Ese siempre paga el pato cuando algo sale mal.
  en: And Lorenzo? He always takes the blame when something goes wrong.
- Pues hoy le ha tocado otra vez pagar el pato, sin tener nada que ver.
  en: Well, today it was his turn to take the blame again, though he had nothing to do with it.
- Pues en la sala de profesores dicen que tú estás como una cabra.
  en: Well, in the staff room they say you're off your head.

notes.en:
1. gallinero (sentence 1): a henhouse, and figuratively a noisy place where everyone talks at once. Example: La oficina es un gallinero los lunes, "the office is a madhouse on Mondays".
2. cuatro gatos (sentence 3): a handful of people, literally four cats. Example: Solo vinieron cuatro gatos a la charla, "only a handful of people came to the talk".
3. como pez en el agua (sentence 4): to be in one's element, literally like a fish in water. Example: En la cocina está como pez en el agua, "in the kitchen she is in her element".
4. hacer el ganso (sentence 5): to clown around, to play the fool. The literal sense is to do the goose. Example: Deja de hacer el ganso y ponte serio, "stop clowning around and be serious".
5. buscar tres pies al gato (sentences 6 and 7): to nitpick, to look for complications where there are none. The literal sense is to look for three feet on a cat. Example: No le busques tres pies al gato: el plan es bueno, "don't nitpick: the plan is good".
6. ser un lince (sentence 7): to be sharp and observant. A lince is a lynx, which is said to have sharp eyes. Example: Eres un lince para encontrar errores, "you're sharp at spotting mistakes".
7. como el perro y el gato (sentence 9): to get on very badly, like cat and dog. The English is also an animal pair. Example: Mis dos hermanos se llevan como el perro y el gato, "my two brothers get on like cat and dog".
8. pez gordo (sentence 9): a big shot, an important person. The literal sense is a fat fish. Example: En la empresa es un pez gordo, "in the company he's a big shot".
9. dar gato por liebre (sentence 11): to pass off something worse as something better. The literal sense is to give cat for hare. The English equivalent is to sell someone a pig in a poke, which means buying something without seeing it first. Example: Nos dieron gato por liebre con el coche usado, "they sold us a dud as a good car".
10. tener la mosca detrás de la oreja (sentence 12): to be suspicious. The literal sense is to have the fly behind the ear, and the English is to smell a rat. Example: Tengo la mosca detrás de la oreja, "I smell a rat".
11. coger el toro por los cuernos (sentence 13): to face a difficulty head on. The literal sense is to take the bull by the horns. Example: Hay que coger el toro por los cuernos y hablar con él, "you have to face it head on and talk to him".
12. matar dos pájaros de un tiro (sentence 14): to kill two birds with one stone. Example: Con una sola llamada matamos dos pájaros de un tiro, "with one call we killed two birds with one stone".
13. gallina (sentence 15): a coward, usually as un gallina. The literal sense is a hen. Example: No seas gallina y pide el aumento, "don't be a chicken, ask for the rise".
14. la oveja negra (sentence 15): the black sheep of a family or group. Example: Mi tío es la oveja negra de la familia, "my uncle is the black sheep of the family".
15. pagar el pato (sentences 16 and 17): to take the blame or the cost for something one did not do. The literal sense is to pay for the duck. Example: Siempre pago el pato de los errores de los demás, "I always take the blame for other people's mistakes".
16. estar como una cabra (sentence 18): to be mad, off one's head. The literal sense is to be like a goat. Example: Con este calor estoy como una cabra, "this heat is driving me mad".
17. Expressions of this lesson, with the sentence and the note:

  en: The party was chaos from the start.
- Mi hermana está como pez en el agua en la piscina.
  en: My sister is in her element in the swimming pool.
- No le busques tres pies al gato: el plan es bueno. (to a friend)
  en: Don't nitpick: the plan is good. (to a friend)

  en: That price makes me smell a rat.

== 63 C1
kind: revision
numeral: Lección sesenta y tres
title: Repaso y matices
title.en: Revision and finer points

notes.en:
The ninth week of this course is finished. Nothing here needs learning by heart: the tables put in order what you have read in lessons 57 to 62, and those texts are your store of examples.
If a point still feels unclear, go back to the example line in its lesson and read it aloud; it will be clearer each time. Then do the ten exercises at the end.
1. connectors of argument and their position (lesson 59): the words that link one reason or result to the next, and whether they can open a sentence.
| Connector | What it does | Can it open the sentence? | Example from the week |
|---|---|---|---|
| sin embargo | even so, a contrast with what went before | yes, with a comma after it | Leonor murió en 1912. Sin embargo, ese mismo año se publicó Campos de Castilla, un libro sobre la tierra de Soria. (lesson 59) |
| no obstante | even so, more formal than sin embargo | yes, with a comma after it | No obstante, muchos los repiten sin saber de quién son. (lesson 59) |
| en cambio | on the other hand, setting one thing against another | usually after the first part, between commas | Soria, en cambio, quedó para siempre en sus poemas. (lesson 59) |
| por consiguiente | therefore, a result that follows | yes, with a comma after it | Por consiguiente, el camino no existe antes de recorrerlo. (lesson 59) |
| es decir | that is to say, a restatement | yes, when it restates the sentence before | Es decir, murió lejos de Castilla. (lesson 59) |
| así pues | so, a conclusion at the end of a passage | yes | Así pues, el poeta de los caminos acabó su vida en Francia. (lesson 59) |
| pues | since, for, giving a reason in written style | never first in this use | cruzó la frontera con su madre, pues miles de personas huían hacia Francia en aquellos días. (lesson 59) |
2. the norm and usage (lessons 58 and 59): the forms that spoken Spanish blurs, and the test that settles them.
| Form | Standard | Example from the week |
|---|---|---|
| pienso de que / pienso que | pensar takes que without de when it means to think that; test with eso: pienso eso gives pienso que | A la prima. Ha escrito pienso de que vendrá, y dice que se escribe pienso que vendrá, sin de. (lesson 58) |
| me alegro que / me alegro de que | alegrarse takes de; test with eso: me alegro de eso gives me alegro de que | y papá le ha corregido: me alegro de que. (lesson 58) |
| habían / había | impersonal haber is always singular | porque el haber impersonal siempre va en singular (lesson 58) |
| haber si / a ver si | a ver si means let's see whether, and it is two words | Que es a ver, en dos palabras (lesson 58) |
| Pair | What it means | Example from the week |
|---|---|---|
| tú / tu | tú is the pronoun, with the accent, and tu (no accent) is the possessive | Sancho era muy sabio, hija. Y tú, ¿qué has comido hoy? (lesson 57) |
| él / el | él is the pronoun for a man, and el is the article | ¡Él, que corrige a todo el mundo! (lesson 58) |
| sí / si | sí means yes or the pronoun after a preposition, and si means if | que sí, que voy, con tilde en el sí (lesson 58) |
| por qué / porqué / porque | por qué is two words in a question, porqué is a noun meaning the reason, and porque joins a reason to a clause | la pregunta lleva por qué, en dos palabras (lesson 58); Llegué tarde porque perdí el autobús (lesson 58, note 7) |
3. periphrases and short answers (lesson 61): a verb with another verb in front of it, and the answers that do not repeat the clause.
| Periphrasis | Meaning | Example from the week | In English |
|---|---|---|---|
| acabar de + infinitive | to have just done something | Acaba de salir de la farmacia (lesson 61) | She has just come out of the chemist's |
| volver a + infinitive | to do something again | ya vuelve a llamar (lesson 61) | she is calling again already |

notes.en:
1. Felipe II (sentence 1): Philip II, the king who moved the court to Madrid in 1561. The court stayed there, and Madrid has been the capital since. Example: Felipe II construyó El Escorial, "Philip II built El Escorial".
2. kilómetro cero (sentence 2): the point from which the distances from the capital are measured. Madrid's plaque is in the Puerta del Sol. Example: Vivo a veinte kilómetros del kilómetro cero, "I live twenty kilometres from kilometre zero".
3. madroño (sentence 3): the strawberry tree, whose red fruit looks like a strawberry. It is on the city's coat of arms with the bear. Example: El madroño tiene un fruto rojo y áspero, "the strawberry tree has a red, bumpy fruit".
4. en cambio (sentence 5): on the other hand, it sets one thing against another and sits between commas after the first part, as in lesson 59, note 2. Example: Yo, en cambio, prefiero el mar, "I, on the other hand, prefer the sea".
5. A diferencia de (sentence 6): unlike, followed by a noun. Example: A diferencia de mi hermana, yo nunca llego tarde, "unlike my sister, I am never late".
6. Mientras que (sentence 8): whereas. It sets two facts side by side in one sentence and is followed by a full clause. Example: Mientras que tú lees, yo preparo la cena, "whereas you read, I cook dinner".
7. verbena (sentence 9): a night-time street party, with music and dancing, held for a local festival. Example: Esta noche hay verbena en la plaza, "there is a street party in the square tonight".
8. chulapos and chulapas (sentence 9): the people of Madrid's traditional festival dress, worn at San Isidro. Example: Mi abuela se puso un mantón de Manila para la fiesta, "my grandmother put on a Manila shawl for the festival".
9. chotis (sentence 10): the dance of Madrid, whose name comes from the schottische, the dance from Scotland. It is danced in couples. Example: Bailaron el chotis hasta medianoche, "they danced the chotis until midnight".
10. la dije (sentence 10): the standard form for telling someone is le dije, "I told her". In Madrid you will hear la dije, but it is not standard, and the text keeps le. Example: Le dije que sí, "I told her yes".
11. cocido (sentence 11): a stew of chickpeas, vegetables and meat, usually eaten in winter. In Madrid the broth comes first, then the chickpeas and the meat. Example: Hace frío: hoy hacemos cocido, "it is cold: today we're making cocido".
12. le vieron (sentence 14): with a man as direct object, central Spain says le, not lo, and the Academy accepts this. Lo vieron is also correct. Example: Al profesor le vi ayer, "I saw the teacher yesterday".
13. Barcelona and Madrid (sentence 15): the two cities have a famous rivalry, most of all in football, where Real Madrid and Barcelona meet in El Clásico. Example: Mi padre es del Barça y mi hermano, del Madrid, "my father supports Barça and my brother supports Madrid".
14. por el contrario (sentence 16): on the contrary, it introduces the opposite of what has just been said. Example: No es caro; por el contrario, es muy barato, "it is not expensive; on the contrary, it is very cheap".
15. no ... sino (sentence 17): after a negative, sino gives the correct alternative. It is the same pattern as lesson 12, note 2. Example: No es Londres, sino Madrid, "it is not London, but Madrid".

  en: The first visit was a couple from Valencia: a chair could be heard being dragged across the floor upstairs.

  en: The third visit was on a Friday afternoon, and as Nuria, the estate agent, went up the stairs, her phone went dead.

  en: Nuria did not want to talk about the noises, but in the end she told the husband, in a low voice.

  en: They left in a hurry, and the husband forgot his umbrella in the hall.

  en: That night, as on almost every night, Grandfather got up at two and went down to the kitchen.

  en: Marta, his granddaughter, who slept in the next room, heard the footsteps and thought they came from the house.

  en: In the morning Grandfather complained that he had not slept, and nobody asked him why.
- Los compradores llamaron a la agencia y dijeron que no volverían.
  en: The buyers rang the agency and said they would not come back.

  en: As she left, Nuria said, "This house is alive", and went off without her commission.

  en: In the end, Marta's mother said that the house was not for sale.

  en: Grandfather smiled for the first time in months, and ate the whole cake.

  en: The day the family took the house off the market, the noises stopped, and Grandfather slept like a baby.

notes.en:
1. se puso a la venta (sentence 1) and se oían pasos (sentence 3): a passive with se, used when the thing is the subject. The verb agrees with it, so one house is se vende and several houses are se venden. Example: Se venden pisos en esta calle, "flats are for sale in this street".
2. se oyó arrastrar (sentence 4): impersonal se, with no subject, used when someone hears something and the speaker does not say who. The verb stays singular whatever is heard, because there is no subject for it to agree with. Example: Se oyó un grito en la plaza, "a shout was heard in the square" (Foundation, lesson 59).
3. se moría de miedo and se rió (sentence 5): morirse de miedo means to be scared to death, an intensifier and not a real death, and the pronoun is part of the verb. Reírse is the same: it means to laugh, and it takes se. Example: Me reí mucho con el chiste, "I laughed a lot at the joke".
4. se abrió sola (sentence 6): a door or window that opens or closes by itself, with no person doing it. The pronoun shows that the change just happens. Example: La ventana se cerró sola con el viento, "the window closed by itself in the wind".
5. se miraron (sentence 6): reciprocal, the two of them look at each other. Example: Se quieren desde niños, "they have loved each other since they were children".
6. se le apagó el móvil (sentence 8): involuntary se: the thing happens to the person, who did not mean it, and the person is the indirect object, le. Example: Se me cayó el vaso, "I dropped the glass" (Foundation, lesson 57).
7. se lo dijo (sentence 9): when le and lo come together, le becomes se, so le dijo lo becomes se lo dijo (Foundation, lesson 18). Example: Se lo di a Ana ayer, "I gave it to Ana yesterday".
8. se levantó (sentence 11): reflexive, the subject gets himself up. Levantarse does not take an object, but levantar does. Example: Me levanto a las siete, "I get up at seven".
9. se fue sin cobrar (sentence 15): irse means to go away, and the pronoun is part of the verb, so it is me voy, not voy. Example: Me voy ya, "I'm going now".
10. se comió la tarta entera (sentence 17): the se of completion. Comerse means to eat up, and the pronoun shows that the thing is finished. Example: Se bebió la botella entera, "he drank the whole bottle".

notes.en:
1. Cerrado por vacaciones (sentence 2): the sign on a shop door, closed for holidays. Example: Cerrado por vacaciones hasta el lunes, "closed for holidays until Monday".
2. no está mal (sentence 4): literally it is not bad. It is said of something good, but also of something poor when the speaker does not want to complain, and the tone decides which. Here the tone is the joke. Example: ¿Qué tal el hotel? No está mal, "how is the hotel? It's not bad".
3. chiringuito (sentence 5): a simple bar on the beach, often with tables under a roof of reeds or cloth. Example: Comimos en un chiringuito junto al mar, "we ate at a beach bar by the sea".
4. cañas (sentence 5): a small glass of draught beer, the usual thing to order in a bar. Example: Pidió dos cañas y una tapa, "he ordered two small beers and a snack".
5. ¡Menudo calor! (sentence 6): menudo, in an exclamation, means what a, and it is often used for something big or annoying. Example: ¡Menudo lío! "what a mess!"
6. verbena (sentence 7): a night-time street party with music and dancing, the same word as lesson 64, note 7. Example: Esta noche hay verbena en la plaza, "there is a street party in the square tonight".
7. ¡Qué bien! (sentence 9): said with irony, "how lovely!" means the opposite. The speaker is annoyed, and con la sonrisa apretada means with a tight smile, the teeth held together. Example: ¡Qué bien, otra vez llueve!, "how lovely, it's raining again!" (said as a complaint).
8. ¡Vaya día! (sentence 10): what a day! It can be a sigh about bad weather, or a joke, and the context shows which. Example: ¡Vaya tormenta! "what a storm!"
9. Ojalá + imperfect subjunctive (sentence 11): a wish that cannot come true now, (Foundation, lesson 72). Example: Ojalá tuviera más tiempo, "I wish I had more time".
10. ¡Quién pudiera! (sentence 12): if only I could, literally who could. Quién is followed by the imperfect subjunctive of the verb. Example: ¡Quién tuviera tu suerte! "if only I had your luck!"
11. ¡Si al menos ...! (sentence 13): if only at least, a wish with the imperfect subjunctive and no main verb. Example: ¡Si al menos tuviera tiempo! "if only I had time, at least!"
12. atasco (sentence 17): a traffic jam, a word of the road. Example: Hubo un atasco de dos horas, "there was a two-hour jam".
13. síndrome postvacacional (sentence 18): the low mood people feel after the holidays, a term often heard in Spain. The English is post-holiday blues. Example: Después de las fiestas tengo el síndrome postvacacional, "after the festive season I have the post-holiday blues".

  en: There is a terrible jam on the motorway.

  en: I hope it rains tomorrow, it's far too hot. (to a friend)

  en: If only I had your luck with the exams! (to a friend)

1. rodó cortos (sentence 2): rodar means to shoot a film, and the shoot itself is el rodaje. Short films are cortos. Example: Rodamos la escena en una playa, "we shot the scene on a beach".
2. la Movida madrileña (sentence 3): the lively cultural scene of Madrid in the 1980s, with music, fashion and film. Movida means a stir or a scene, and the word stays in Spanish in English texts. Example: Vivió la Movida desde dentro, "he lived through the Movida from the inside".
3. nominada al Oscar (sentence 4): a nomination means the film was chosen among the finalists, not that it won. Example: La película fue nominada, pero no ganó, "the film was nominated, but did not win".
4. habla no inglesa (sentence 5): the Oscar's English name is Best Foreign Language Film; the Spanish phrase, de habla no inglesa, is literally of speech not English. Example: Es una película de habla francesa, "it is a film in French".
5. guion original (sentence 6): the script. An original script is one written for the film, not adapted from a book. Example: El guion está escrito en español, "the script is written in Spanish".
6. rodada (sentence 7): the past participle agrees with the film, which is feminine, so rodada, shot. Example: La película, rodada en Madrid, se ve muy bien, "the film, shot in Madrid, looks very good".
7. rodaje (sentence 8): the shoot, the time when a film is filmed. El equipo is the crew, and it moves between the locations. Example: El rodaje duró tres meses, "the shoot lasted three months".
8. propios guiones (sentence 9): propio means own, and it stands before the noun here. Example: Escribe sus propias canciones, "he writes his own songs".
9. banda sonora (sentence 11): the soundtrack, the music written for a film. Example: La banda sonora de esta película es preciosa, "the soundtrack of this film is lovely".
10. gran director (sentence 12): gran before a singular noun means great, and grande, after the noun, means big or large. Example: Es un hombre grande: mide dos metros, "he is a big man: he is two metres tall".
11. versión original (sentence 12): the film with its original language and soundtrack, usually with subtitles. Example: Vi la película en versión original con subtítulos, "I watched the film in the original version with subtitles".
12. la misma actriz (sentence 13): the same actress, which means one who is also in other films, not one who is the same person. La actriz misma means the actress herself, as in La actriz misma lo dijo, "the actress herself said it". Example: Vi la misma película que el año pasado, "I saw the same film as last year".
13. un único papel (sentence 14): un único means only one, a single role. Un papel único means a unique role, one of a kind. Example: Tuvo un único papel en la obra, "he had only one role in the play".
14. cine antiguo (sentence 15): cine antiguo means old films, the films of an earlier time; el antiguo cine is the former cinema, the building that is no longer there. Example: El antiguo cine del barrio cerró hace años, "the former cinema in the neighbourhood closed years ago".

notes.en:
1. dado que (sentences 2, 4, 10 and 18): the expression is followed by the indicative and means since or given that. It is formal, and it belongs to a written or formal register, as in an exam. Example: Dado que hay niebla, el vuelo sale más tarde, "since there is fog, the flight leaves later".
2. en primer lugar, por un lado ... por otro (sentences 2, 4, 10 and 18): the order of an argument: first, one side, then the other. English uses "on the one hand ... on the other", but in Spanish the first point comes with en primer lugar, and the second with por otro lado. Example: Por un lado, es caro; por otro, es muy cómodo, "on the one hand it is expensive; on the other, it is very comfortable".
3. ya que (sentence 2 and sentence 12): since, as, a reason that the listener already knows, with the indicative. It is less formal than dado que. Example: Ya que estás aquí, ayúdame, "since you're here, help me".
4. en el supuesto de que and suponiendo que (sentences 2, 8, 12 and 18): both mark an assumption and take the subjunctive. En el supuesto de que is formal and written, while suponiendo que is common in speech. Example: Suponiendo que no llueva, vamos a la playa, "assuming it does not rain, we'll go to the beach".
5. en definitiva and a fin de cuentas (sentence 2): both mean all in all, in the end, the final word of an argument. They are often used together, but one is enough. Example: En definitiva, no vale la pena, "all in all, it is not worth it".
6. puesto que (sentences 4 and 16): since, because, a formal reason with the indicative, the same as dado que and ya que. Example: Puesto que no hay tren, vamos en coche, "since there is no train, we'll go by car".
7. como (sentences 6 and 14): a cause that must open its sentence, with a verb in the indicative, followed by the main clause. Inside a sentence, como means like or as, as in Habla como su padre, "he talks like his father". Example: Como llovía, nos quedamos en casa, "as it was raining, we stayed at home".
8. Mientras que (sentence 8): whereas, the contrast from lesson 64, note 6. Example: Mientras que tú lees, yo cocino, "whereas you read, I cook".
9. porque (sentences 4 and 10): one word, it joins a reason to a clause, as in lesson 58, note 7. Porque is not por qué, the question. Example: Lo hice porque me lo pidió, "I did it because she asked me to".
10. le había preguntado (sentence 19): the pluperfect, had asked, for an action finished before another past moment. The examiner had asked only about the water, before all the long answers (Foundation, lesson 53). Example: Ya lo había dicho antes, "I had already said it before".
