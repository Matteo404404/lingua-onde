# How Assimil builds a course: the progression map

Measured lesson by lesson in four books (details and per-lesson rows in
`work/analysis/`): Lo Spagnolo (Spanish for Italians, 100 lessons), German
with ease (German for English speakers, lessons 1–50), Espagnol
perfectionnement (60 lessons), and the introduction of Perfectionnement
Allemand. Counts come from scanned text and are approximate (±1 line, ±15%
words). This file is the reference for planning and writing every Lingua
Onde course; METHOD.md is its short public version.

## Foundation

### Size of a lesson

| Lessons | Numbered lines | Words of target text | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 5–6 | 20–30 | 4–8 |
| 4–14 | 5–8 | 35–60 | 6–9 |
| 15–25 | 6–9 | 50–70 | 5–10 |
| 26–35 | 8–10 | 45–90 | 7–9 |
| 36–50 | 9–15 | 70–150 | 7–12 |
| 51–75 | 8–20 (about 13) | 70–150 (about 105) | 4–14 (about 8.5) |
| 76–100 | 6–20 (about 15) | 85–190 (about 125) | 3–10 (about 7) |

- Lines are short: 3–9 words, rarely 12–14.
- Growth is slow and uneven on purpose. From lesson 26, lessons of 8–10
  lines alternate with one of 13–18. The lesson right after a revision
  lesson is a long one; the last lesson before a revision is short or
  medium. The author warns before an unusually long lesson and makes the
  next one light.

### The scene

- Two voices (sometimes three), one speaker per line. Mostly unnamed (a
  customer, a waiter, a couple, two friends, "sir", "madam"); first names in
  about a third of lessons. A few names may come back, but there is never a
  plot running across lessons. A story in two parts ("continued") happens two
  or three times in a course.
- Settings: everyday and public-service situations (bar, shop, post office,
  doctor, station, taxi, customs, swimming pool, office, home, phone).
- Kinds over the course: dialogues throughout. From about lesson 65 some
  lessons are short descriptive or informative texts with no speakers (a
  beach at dawn, festivals); from about lesson 92 they become most lessons
  (the country, its language). Lessons 99–100 are the author's farewell to
  the learner: how to keep in touch with the language, study advice.
- Humour: lessons 1–5 end plainly. Humour starts around lesson 6 and from
  about lesson 9 nearly every dialogue ends with a twist in the last line or
  two: a deadpan reversal, the literal reading of a phrase, a speaker's faulty
  logic, a misunderstanding. Domestic, gentle, never satirical. The joke can
  be understood from the translation; sometimes a note helps. Informative
  texts end plainly.
- Titles: about half plain nouns or places ("At the post office", "The
  photo"); about a quarter character traits or situations ("A cautious taxi
  driver", "Absent-minded"); the rest questions, proverbs or idioms, often
  paid off in the last line.

### Grammar: how a point travels through the course

1. **It appears first in a dialogue**, often as a fixed phrase long before it
   is a topic: a past tense in lesson 3 ("I have slept"), the imperfect in
   lesson 8, a future in lesson 12, a hidden subjunctive in lesson 13
   (Spanish); the dative inside fixed phrases from lesson 2, explained only
   in lesson 37 (German).
2. **A note glosses it the same day** in 70–90% of cases: a short "first
   example of ..." with the form explained just enough, often with a promise
   ("we'll come back to this", "see the revision lesson").
3. **It is used again** in the following dialogues and exercises.
4. **The next revision lesson puts it in order**, 1–7 lessons after the note:
   the first full table of forms, the rule, a few completions.
5. **Hard topics come in two or three instalments** with longer gaps:
   first sighting with a short note, a fuller note later, the table in a
   revision lesson 15–35 lessons after the first sighting (German cases:
   nominative named in 14, accusative 16 → 21 → 28, dative 37 → 42, genitive
   47 → 49; Spanish opinion verbs 29 → 49; se lo 34 → 48 → 49).
6. From about lesson 76 forms already met are no longer annotated; after
   about lesson 85 the course stops adding tenses and adds vocabulary, set
   expressions and word contrasts.

Load per lesson: 1–25: 2–4 new grammar points, about 15–20 new words.
26–75: 5–9 new forms visible in the dialogue, of which 2–4 get a grammar
note; about 15–25 new words, of which notes comment 4–8. 76–100: 1–3 new
constructions. German, harder for its learners, brings 1–2 truly new
structures a lesson.

Order of introduction, as models (adapt to the language and the learner):
- Spanish for Italians: gender and articles, ser, the perfect, personal a,
  plural, usted and ir (1–5) → ir a + infinitive, imperfect seen, -ísimo,
  gustar, por/para, future seen, hours, hay que / tener que, acabar de (6–19)
  → imperfect, preterite seen, diminutives, reflexive se (22–25) → possessives,
  dative pronouns, imperatives, demonstratives, conditional, subjunctive in
  requests, negative imperative, preterite, future, gerund, pluperfect
  (26–50) → subjunctive after purpose, preterite in narrative and its table,
  irregular verb groups, relatives, como si (51–75) → polite subjunctive,
  sequence of tenses, aunque, cuyo, future subjunctive, cleft sentences
  (76–100).
- German for English speakers: present and Sie/wir (1) → verb second and
  questions, gender (2–4) → negation, possessives, plural, du, infinitive
  last (3–8) → kein, ihr, imperative, first separable verb (9–13) → es gibt,
  accusative (15–16), numbers, adjectives (16–18) → perfect seen (20) →
  modals in dialogues (26–27, table 35) → subordinate clauses (31 seen,
  33–34 noted, 35 table) → dative (37–42) → perfect formed (36–46) → simple
  past of sein/haben (45–46) → genitive (47–49).

### Notes

- Each note hangs on a word or phrase of the dialogue, in order: the
  meaning, how it works, a contrast with the learner's language, two to five
  short examples with translation, often a family of related words or a
  synonym list (3–6 items), often ending with a rule of thumb or a
  reassurance ("no need to learn this by heart").
- Grammar terms are kept few and explained in one sentence when first used.
- Share of notes by kind, early → late: grammar 45–60% → 25%; word meaning
  20% → 40–60%; idioms 5–10% → 20%; contrast with the learner's language and
  false friends 6–12% (a few every week); usage and register 8%; culture and
  pronunciation a few percent (culture has its own paragraph).
- Back references ("seen in lesson N", "remember") start around lesson 8
  and are in 60–70% of lessons after 15, usually 1–10 lessons back. Forward
  references, about one lesson in two early on, one in four later, mostly
  "see the revision lesson".

### Pronunciation

- Observation boxes ("Remember:") in nearly every lesson of the first week,
  then in some lessons up to about 35 (1–2 points on one sound, or the
  stress), then almost none.
- Figured pronunciation: a full line under every sentence up to lesson 11
  (Spanish, regular spelling) or 28 (German); after that only new or hard
  words, the rest of the line left out.

### Exercises

- Exercise 1: five items to translate into the learner's language. Lesson 1:
  single words and greetings; then short sentences of 3–8 words; 5–10 later.
  Built from the dialogue's words in new combinations and from the previous
  two or three lessons.
- Exercise 2: five items (four in lessons 1–2): the learner's sentence and
  the target sentence with 1–3 blanks of one or two words, one dot per
  letter. The blanks test the lesson's notes (a form just explained) and
  sometimes one older point.

### Cultural note

- In about half the ordinary lessons (13 of the first 22, 10 of 21 later),
  5–12 lines after the exercises, practical and tied to the scene (how
  things work in the country: bars, meals, address, coins, phones, post,
  roads, holidays). Often closes with a short phrase or proverb in the
  target language. None in revision lessons.

### The author's voice

- A short aside every 4–6 lessons: encouragement ("you are making
  progress", "a third of the course done"), the announcement of the next
  revision, a warning before a long lesson, a reassurance after a hard one.
  Lesson 49 announces the second wave; lesson 50 gives its instructions;
  lessons 99–100 are the farewell.

### Revision lessons (7, 14, 21, ...)

- Open with a few lines of reassurance (no rules to learn by heart; doubts
  are normal; aim at communicating).
- Sections: 8–9 in the first one (pronunciation, stress, articles, gender,
  plural, conjugations, the key verbs), then 3–5. Each collects points from
  the last six lessons plus one or two older ones; full tables; completions
  the lessons had not given (the whole verb, all numbers, the full pronoun
  table); hard topics come back here 15–20 lessons after their first note.
- The recap dialogue: 10–16 short lines, about one and a half times the
  lessons just before it, almost every line tagged with 1–4 lesson numbers;
  the span reaches further back as the course goes on (lessons 1–6 at first,
  40–55 lessons back from lesson 70).
- A closing line in the early ones ("the mechanisms are starting to work").
- No exercises (a rare short drill), no cultural note.

### The second wave

Announced at the end of lesson 49; from lesson 50 each lesson names an
earlier one to rebuild (lesson n − 49: 50 → 1); the learner covers the
target text and translates the learner's-language text aloud. It continues
after lesson 100 to the end.

## Perfectionnement

- No growth: each lesson is a text complete in itself, 12–36 numbered
  sentences, 150–450 words, the kind varying from day to day.
- Rotation (Espagnol perfectionnement, 60 lessons): a verb or keyword and all
  its idioms about every 8 lessons (1, 15, 36, 45, 53, 59), with nearly all
  notes on idioms and an expression list as its exercise; a portrait of a
  region or city next to each revision lesson, on a fixed template
  (situation and character, cities, a historical remark, landscape, crafts,
  food, a closing speciality); portraits of artists (literature, painting,
  music); dialogues and street scenes; jokes; character sketches; social
  satire; news broadcasts in two parts; a poem; a business letter in two
  parts; proverbs; a thematic list of idioms; a farewell letter as the last
  lesson, sending the learner to authentic texts with a dictionary.
- Humour is the second aim of the course: satire, wordplay on the lesson's
  keyword, an absurd last line that undoes the premise; titles are idioms,
  places, names, proverbs.
- Notes: median 7–8, range 4–17 (30+ in a verb lesson): word families and
  the many senses of a word about 40%, grammar 20–30%, idioms 17–20%,
  culture 10–15%, register; each with the headword, the meaning, one to
  three examples, "remember" back references (3–30 lessons back). No
  pronunciation.
- Grammar: no new basics; 1–3 constructions a lesson that the learner knows
  but uses badly, one explained in depth, the same point returning in 3–5
  notes with back references.
- Exercises: 5 + 5 (translate; fill in), 5–20 words a sentence; every seventh
  lesson or so a vocabulary review list of the last lessons instead.
- Revision lessons: grammar only, two to five sections with tables and
  examples, sometimes a short exercise, no text.
- Translation deliberately free, idioms given literally first. No
  stand-alone cultural note: culture lives in the texts and in notes.
- Espagnol perfectionnement has no second wave; Perfectionnement Allemand
  has one from lesson 56 back to lesson 1, paragraph by paragraph.

## What Lingua Onde takes from this

- Foundation follows every point above. Lesson sizes, the grammar route,
  notes, exercises, culture, asides and revision lessons are planned against
  the tables here, lesson by lesson.
- Figured pronunciation: a full line in lessons 1–28, then only the words
  new in each lesson (English for Italians, whose spelling is irregular,
  needs it as long as German does).
- The author's asides get their own section in a lesson (`aside.it:`).
- Perfectionnement keeps 70 lessons (between the Spanish book's 60 and the
  German one's length) and the active wave from lesson 50.
- Mastery, Lingua Onde's own, keeps the Perfectionnement shape at C1–C2.
