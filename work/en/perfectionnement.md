# English for Italian speakers · Perfectionnement plan (70 lessons)

For learners who have finished Foundation (B2 at the start, C1 at the end).
British English; everything else in Italian. Levels: B2 1–45, C1 46–70.
Read `work/PATTERNS.md` (section "Perfectionnement") and `work/BRIEF.md`
(section "Perfectionnement") first.

How the course works (from Assimil's Perfectionnement):
- Each lesson is a text complete in itself; the kind changes from day to
  day. No growth in length: 14–25 numbered sentences, 150–400 words.
- Humour is the second aim: wordplay on the lesson's keyword, satire, an
  absurd last line. Informative texts may end plainly or with a wink.
- A "word" lesson about every eight lessons: one verb and its idioms; nearly
  every note is an idiom (15–30 notes) and the notes end with an expression
  list (each idiom with its sentence and note number).
- A portrait of a place of the English-speaking world about every seven
  lessons, on one template: where and what it is like, its towns, a remark
  of history, the landscape, what is made there, food and drink, a last
  line on a speciality.
- Artists and figures of the past: only well-established facts; quote only
  titles or a few words of public-domain works.
- Notes 8–15 (more in word lessons): word families and senses, idioms with
  their literal sense first, grammar the learner "knows but uses badly",
  register, false friends; "ricordate" back references.
- Exercises: 5 + 5 (translate; fill in), sentences of 5–20 words.
- No figured pronunciation; stress is noted where it is irregular. No
  separate cultural note: culture lives in the texts and the notes.
- Revision lessons (7, 14 ... 70) have no text: numbered sections on finer
  points, with tables and examples, then a short practice of five items
  whose answers close the lesson (in ex1 and ex2).
- From lesson 50 the active wave brings back lesson n − 49.
- Foundation's active wave goes on alongside: for a learner who finished
  Foundation, lessons 1–49 each bring back one of Foundation 52–100 (the app
  does it). Lesson 2 (the course presents itself) says so in a sentence, and
  says that a learner who starts here directly simply begins the course's
  own wave at lesson 50.

## The book around the lessons

As in the Assimil Perfectionnement volumes: the introduction is written
(`content/en/appendix-perfectionnement.it.txt`, `== intro`). After lesson 70,
before the release: the grammar appendix (`== grammar`, the finer points of
the revision lessons, with tables) and the grammar index (`== index`, topic →
lessons), plus the expressions of the word lessons (`== phrases`).

## Lessons

### 1 · B2 · word · Take
- A humorous reflection on the verb take: take a break, take after, take it
  easy, take for granted, take part, take place, take off, take someone in;
  the last line: "take your time".
- Focus: phrasal verbs with take; take against bring (Foundation 66).

### 2 · B2 · introduction · What lies ahead
- The course presents itself: a tour of the English-speaking world in texts
  of every kind, from a pub joke to a letter to the bank.
- Focus: future continuous and future perfect (by the end you will have
  read...); so that, in order to.

### 3 · B2 · dialogue · Your call is important to us
- A customer on hold with a service line: recorded messages, music, options.
  After forty minutes: "Our offices are now closed."
- Focus: formal passive formulas, polite modals, phone vocabulary.

### 4 · B2 · portrait · Cornwall
- The template: the far south-west, fishing towns, tin mining, cliffs and
  coves, the Cornish language, the pasty.
- Focus: participle clauses (Surrounded by the sea, Cornwall...), relative
  where and whose.

### 5 · B2 · story · The wrong train
- A commuter falls asleep on the 6.15 and wakes up in Edinburgh; the guard is
  unmoved; he phones the office to say he will be "slightly late".
- Focus: narrative tenses, past perfect continuous; understatement.

### 6 · B2 · anecdotes · Three stories about queues
- Three short original anecdotes about queuing, each with a twist.
- Focus: hardly ... when, no sooner ... than (first look); queue vocabulary.

### 7 · revision
- Sections: 1 narrative tenses together; 2 future continuous and future
  perfect; 3 participle clauses; 4 the take family (list). Practice: five
  sentences to translate, five to complete.

### 8 · B2 · character · Mr Pemberton is never late
- A man who has not been late in forty years. The first day he is late is the
  day of his retirement party.
- Focus: would and used to for past habits; adverbs of frequency and degree.

### 9 · B2 · word · Put
- put up with, put off, put up (a guest), put your foot in it, put two and
  two together, put on (weight, a show).
- Focus: the put family; separable or not.

### 10 · B2 · satire · The forecast
- The British and the weather forecast: "a chance of showers, sunny spells,
  possibly heavy at times", the art of never being wrong.
- Focus: probability (likely to, bound to, may well, could possibly);
  hedging.

### 11 · B2 · portrait · The Lake District
- The template: lakes and fells, Wordsworth's daffodils (only the title),
  walkers and rain, slate, Kendal mint cake.
- Focus: descriptive adjectives and their order; compound adjectives.

### 12 · B2 · dialogue · Full of character
- An estate agent shows a flat: "cosy" means tiny, "full of character" means
  falling down, "close to transport" means above the railway.
- Focus: euphemism; both, either, neither, each.

### 13 · B2 · reflection · Sorry
- The many uses of sorry: apology, request, complaint, surprise, anger.
- Focus: indirectness and politeness; I'm afraid, I was wondering if.

### 14 · revision
- Sections: 1 would and used to; 2 probability and hedging; 3 euphemism
  and understatement; 4 the put family; 5 both, either, neither, each.
  Practice.

### 15 · B2 · word · Go
- go off, go without, go for it, go downhill, go Dutch, it goes without
  saying, have a go.
- Focus: the go family.

### 16 · B2 · artist · Charles Dickens
- His life, the London of his novels (titles only), his readings, Christmas.
- Focus: reporting passives (he is said to have..., it is believed that).

### 17 · B2 · anecdote · The parrot
- A parrot that repeats everything it hears betrays its owner at a dinner
  party.
- Focus: verbs of perception (hear someone say / saying).

### 18 · B2 · portrait · Wales
- The template: Welsh on road signs, castles, valleys and coal, rugby and
  choirs, the longest place name, Welsh cakes.
- Focus: inversion of here/there (Here comes...); prefixes.

### 19 · B2 · news · The six o'clock news (1)
- Headlines: politics, an accident, an economic report.
- Focus: the language of news: passive, reporting verbs, headline grammar.

### 20 · B2 · news · The six o'clock news (2)
- Sport, weather and the "and finally" story (a sheep that takes the bus).
- Focus: present tenses for past events in headlines; idioms of sport.

### 21 · revision
- Sections: 1 reporting verbs (deny, admit, accuse of, insist on); 2 it is
  said that / he is thought to; 3 verbs of perception; 4 the language of
  news; 5 the go family. Practice.

### 22 · B2 · dialogue · The job offer
- A phone negotiation: salary, holidays, a parking space; the candidate gets
  everything, then realises the job is in Aberdeen.
- Focus: mixed conditionals; polite bargaining.

### 23 · B2 · artist · J. M. W. Turner
- The painter of light: storms, steam, sunsets; the Thames.
- Focus: describing art; adjectives of colour and light.

### 24 · B2 · portrait · Dublin
- The template: the Liffey, Georgian doors, writers (names only), the pub,
  Irish English (grand, craic, "I'm after eating").
- Focus: varieties of English.

### 25 · B2 · word · Come
- come across, come round, come off, come into, how come, come to think of
  it.
- Focus: the come family.

### 26 · B2 · satire · The art of the queue
- A mock manual: rules nobody wrote and everybody knows.
- Focus: articles with abstract and generic nouns (the British, queuing is).

### 27 · B2 · story · The tortoise
- A family's tortoise disappears every winter and reappears every spring;
  one year it reappears twice.
- Focus: past modals of deduction (must have, can't have, might have).

### 28 · revision
- Sections: 1 mixed conditionals; 2 past modals of deduction; 3 articles,
  generic and abstract; 4 the come family; 5 varieties of English. Practice.

### 29 · B2 · dialogue · Dinner with the in-laws
- A first dinner with a partner's parents; every question a trap.
- Focus: remember, forget, regret, try + -ing or + to (meaning changes).

### 30 · B2 · portrait · Yorkshire
- The template: dales and moors, York, plain speaking, tea, Yorkshire
  pudding.
- Focus: dialect words and plain speech.

### 31 · B2 · character · The gardener next door
- A neighbour who measures his lawn with a ruler.
- Focus: emphatic do/did; intensifiers (utterly, absolutely, totally).

### 32 · B2 · artist · Jane Austen
- Her life, Bath, irony, her novels (titles only).
- Focus: formal inversion in conditionals (Had she..., Were he...).

### 33 · B2 · word · Turn
- turn down, turn up, turn out, turn over a new leaf, turn a blind eye, a
  turn for the worse.
- Focus: the turn family.

### 34 · B2 · poem · Rain
- An original short poem in ballad stanzas about rain in an English town.
- Focus: stress and rhythm; poetic word order and ellipsis.

### 35 · revision
- Sections: 1 verbs + -ing or + to with a change of meaning; 2 emphasis (do,
  intensifiers); 3 formal inversion in conditionals; 4 the turn family; 5
  rhythm and stress. Practice.

### 36 · B2 · essay · Class
- The British and social class: accents, words (lunch, dinner, tea), houses.
- Focus: nominalisation; abstract nouns.

### 37 · B2 · portrait · New York
- The template: from New Amsterdam to the five boroughs, American English
  (sidewalk, subway, check), the bagel.
- Focus: British against American English (revisits Foundation 93).

### 38 · B2 · dialogue · The last lesson
- A driving instructor's last day before retirement, with his worst pupil.
- Focus: needn't have against didn't need to.

### 39 · B2 · biography · Florence Nightingale
- Scutari, the lamp, statistics, nursing as a profession.
- Focus: participle clauses of time and reason; time linkers.

### 40 · B2 · word · Run
- run into, run late, run the show, in the long run, run in the family, run
  out of steam.
- Focus: the run family.

### 41 · B2 · jokes · Three short ones
- Three original jokes built on double meanings.
- Focus: puns, homophones, words with two senses.

### 42 · revision
- Sections: 1 needn't have, didn't need to; 2 participle clauses; 3
  nominalisation; 4 the run family; 5 double meanings. Practice.

### 43 · B2 · portrait · Liverpool
- The template: the docks, the Mersey, football, the Beatles (facts only),
  Scouse.
- Focus: dialect and accent (Scouse).

### 44 · B2 · story · The inheritance
- A great-aunt leaves her fortune to whoever can find it.
- Focus: the future in the past (was going to, would, was about to).

### 45 · B2 · word · Break
- break down, break up, break the ice, break even, break the news, break a
  record.
- Focus: the break family.

### 46 · C1 · dialogue · The book club
- Readers who have not read the book discuss it.
- Focus: discourse markers (admittedly, mind you, to be fair, as it happens).

### 47 · C1 · letter · Dear Sir or Madam (1)
- A formal letter to a bank about a mistaken charge.
- Focus: formal register: structure and phrases.

### 48 · C1 · letter · Dear Ms Hart (2)
- The bank's reply, very polite and saying nothing; a postscript gives the
  game away.
- Focus: formal phrases; the art of saying no politely.

### 49 · revision
- Sections: 1 formal letters (structure, openings, closings); 2 the future
  in the past; 3 discourse markers; 4 the break family. Practice.
- Aside: tomorrow the active wave of this course begins.

### 50 · C1 · portrait · Australia
- The template: cities, distances, history (careful facts), Australian
  English (arvo, no worries), the barbecue.
- Focus: abbreviations and informal word formation.

### 51 · C1 · artist · The Brontës
- Haworth, the moors, three sisters and their pen names (titles only).
- Focus: relative clauses with prepositions (in which, of whom).

### 52 · C1 · satire · Going forward
- An office meeting in pure jargon (touch base, circle back, low-hanging
  fruit); nobody knows what was decided.
- Focus: jargon and plain English; dependent prepositions.

### 53 · C1 · word · Keep
- keep up with, keep an eye on, keep a straight face, keep your head, keep
  it up.
- Focus: the keep family.

### 54 · C1 · dialogue · Quiz night
- A pub quiz team argues over every answer and wins with the wrong one.
- Focus: indirect questions and embedded clauses.

### 55 · C1 · portrait · English in India
- The template: history (careful facts), Indian English (prepone, do the
  needful), railways, cricket, chai.
- Focus: varieties of English.

### 56 · revision
- Sections: 1 relative clauses with prepositions; 2 jargon and dependent
  prepositions; 3 the keep family; 4 indirect questions. Practice.

### 57 · C1 · proverbs · Every cloud
- British proverbs and what they really mean in daily life, with a comic
  example for each.
- Focus: proverbs; the literal and the figurative.

### 58 · C1 · character · He never throws anything away
- A man whose house is full of things "that might come in handy".
- Focus: so and such; compound nouns.

### 59 · C1 · biography · Charles Darwin
- The Beagle, Down House, twenty years of doubt before publishing.
- Focus: hedging in scientific style; the passive.

### 60 · C1 · word · Mind
- mind you, never mind, mind your own business, in two minds, slip your
  mind, mind the gap.
- Focus: the mind family.

### 61 · C1 · dialogue · The removal men
- Moving house with two removal men who answer everything with "I hope so"
  and "I'm afraid not".
- Focus: ellipsis and substitution (so, not, do so).

### 62 · C1 · idioms · Body language
- Idioms with parts of the body (cost an arm and a leg, pull someone's leg,
  keep your chin up), told as a story.
- Focus: body idioms.

### 63 · revision
- Sections: 1 ellipsis and substitution; 2 so and such; 3 hedging; 4 the
  mind family; 5 body idioms. Practice.

### 64 · C1 · portrait · The Caribbean
- The template: islands, history (careful facts), Windrush (1948),
  Jamaican English and Patois, rice and peas.
- Focus: varieties of English.

### 65 · C1 · story · The ghost of the bed and breakfast
- A guest complains of a ghost; the landlady charges extra for it.
- Focus: narrative inversion (No sooner had..., Never before had...).

### 66 · C1 · satire · The British summer
- Barbecues in the rain, sunburn in an hour, the hosepipe ban.
- Focus: irony and understatement; wish and if only (advanced).

### 67 · C1 · artist · Edward Elgar
- A self-taught musician from Worcester, the Enigma Variations, a march
  everyone knows (titles only).
- Focus: word formation (prefixes and suffixes).

### 68 · C1 · dialogue · The oral exam
- A candidate answers every question with a conditional; the examiner
  replies in kind.
- Focus: all conditionals in use.

### 69 · C1 · letter · Dear reader
- The author's farewell: how to read English now (newspapers, novels,
  radio), and what Mastery will bring; the active wave goes on, one lesson
  a day, to lesson 70, also once Mastery has begun.

### 70 · revision
- Sections: 1 word formation; 2 the verb families of the course (take,
  put, go, come, turn, run, break, keep, mind): one list; 3 inversion and
  emphasis; 4 register from formal to informal. Practice, and a closing
  line.
