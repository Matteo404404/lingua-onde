# English for Italian speakers · Foundation plan (100 lessons)

British English (standard southern pronunciation, British spelling and words:
flat, lift, queue, -ise, colour). Translations, notes and everything else in
Italian. Levels: A1 1–25, A2 26–50, B1 51–75, B2 76–100.

This plan applies `work/PATTERNS.md` (how Assimil builds a course, measured in
the books) to English for Italians. Read it first, then `work/BRIEF.md`.

## How to read a lesson block

- **Lines**: the dialogue's numbered lines (the size the map gives).
- **Scene**: a small scene complete in itself, its people and its last line.
  The twist is the idea to keep; sharpen the wording, keep it gentle. Voices
  are mostly unnamed; a name in a block belongs to that lesson only. Lessons
  1–5 end plainly; humour starts at 6.
- **Grammar**: *seen* = appears in the dialogue for the first time as a
  fixed phrase; its note says only what it is and that it will come back
  ("lo vedremo più avanti / nella lezione di ripasso"). *Explained* = a note
  explains it properly. *Recycle* = earlier forms to use again. A revision
  lesson lists its sections: the first full tables.
- **Words**: the lesson's vocabulary field; **F** = a false friend or a
  trap for Italians to note.
- **Sounds**: pronunciation observations (`sounds.it`).
- **Culture**: the cultural note's subject, or none.
- **Aside**: the author's words (`aside.it`), when there are some.

Throughout: subject pronouns are never dropped; adjectives come before the
noun and never take -s; do-support, the -s of the third person, articles,
his/her and the present perfect are the great Italian traps and come back
again and again in notes ("ricordate: ...", "come nella lezione N").

## Week 1

### 1 · A1 · 5 lines · A cup of tea
- Scene: a café. A customer asks for a cup of tea; "Milk?" "Yes, please."
  "Here you are." "Thank you." Plain ending.
- Grammar: seen: a (article), "Here you are". Explained: a + noun; please and
  thank you (prego is not please).
- Words: good morning, a cup of tea, milk, yes, please, here you are, thank
  you. Ex1 = words and phrases of the dialogue.
- Sounds: long ii (tea, please), th (thank), h always sounded (here), ə (a,
  of).
- Culture: tea with milk, "a cuppa".

### 2 · A1 · 6 lines · Nice to meet you
- Scene: a party. "Hello, I'm Anna." "Hi, I'm Luca." "Are you Italian?" "Yes,
  I am. And you?" "I'm English, from York." "Nice to meet you." Plain.
- Grammar: seen: I'm, Are you...?, short answer "Yes, I am". Explained: the
  pronoun is never dropped (I'm, not 'm); "Yes, I am" never "Yes, I'm";
  nationality words with a capital letter.
- Words: hello, hi, Italian, English, from, and, nice to meet you.
- Sounds: the English r (not rolled), aa vs a.
- Culture: hello and hi, first names straight away.

### 3 · A1 · 5 lines · How are you?
- Scene: morning, two flatmates. "Morning! How are you?" "Tired. I slept
  badly." "Oh dear. Coffee?" "Yes, please. A big one." Plain.
- Grammar: seen: "I slept" (a first past, only a phrase); "a big one".
  Explained: How are you? (a greeting more than a question); adjective before
  the noun, never plural; one instead of the noun.
- Words: morning, tired, badly, oh dear, coffee, big.
- Sounds: ou (oh, no), æ (badly).
- Culture: none.

### 4 · A1 · 6 lines · Where's the station?
- Scene: a street. "Excuse me, where's the station?" "It's on the left, next
  to the bank." "Is it far?" "No, it isn't. Five minutes." "Thanks a lot."
  "You're welcome." Plain.
- Grammar: seen: where's, it's, isn't. Explained: it for things (always a
  subject); the; contractions; Is it...? No, it isn't.
- Words: excuse me, station, left, right, next to, bank, far, minute,
  numbers 1–5, thanks a lot, you're welcome. F: "you're welcome" is the
  "prego" of thanks.
- Sounds: weak forms (to, the), the linking r (where's it).
- Culture: excuse me and sorry: the British apologise constantly.

### 5 · A1 · 6 lines · Do you live in London?
- Scene: a train. "Do you live in London?" "No, I don't. I live in Leeds."
  "Do you like London?" "Yes, I do. But it's very expensive." "Like Milan."
  Plain.
- Grammar: seen: do in questions, Yes I do / No I don't (fixed answers; the
  revision lesson of week 2 explains them). Explained: very; but; like as
  "come" (like Milan).
- Words: live, like, very, expensive, but, London, Leeds.
- Sounds: the soft dh (the), z (does, lives).
- Culture: London and the North.

### 6 · A1 · 6 lines · Two returns
- Scene: a station ticket office. "Two tickets to Oxford, please." "Single or
  return?" "Return, please." "That's forty-two pounds." "Forty-two pounds? We
  want to go to Oxford, not buy it!"
- Grammar: seen: we want to + verb. Explained: plural -s (tickets, pounds)
  and its three sounds; numbers by tens; want to (never "want that").
- Words: ticket, single, return, pound, to + place, buy, numbers 10–50.
- Sounds: -s as s, z, iz.
- Culture: trains and railcards; pounds and pence.
- Aside: tomorrow a revision lesson: nothing new, everything in order.

### 7 · revision · 10 lines
- Recap scene: two strangers in a café: tea, names, where they live, the
  price; small twist (the tea is the most expensive thing of the day).
- Sections: 1 vowels long and short (ii/i, uu/u, aa/a), ə; 2 th, dh, h, the
  English r; 3 stress and weak forms (a, the, to, of); 4 the verb to be:
  full present, contractions, negative, questions, short answers; 5 subject
  pronouns, always there; it; 6 a, an (an before a vowel sound: completion),
  the; 7 plural -s; 8 numbers 1–20 (completion). Encouragement at the start,
  a closing line.

## Week 2

### 8 · A1 · 7 lines · A room for tonight
- Scene: hotel reception. "Have you got a room for tonight?" "For one
  person?" "Yes. Has it got a bathroom?" "Yes, and it's got a view." "A view
  of the sea?" "Of the car park." "Lovely. I'll take it."
- Grammar: seen: I'll take it (will, as a phrase). Explained: have got / has
  got (British possession); has for he, she, it (the first -s).
- Words: room, tonight, person, bathroom, view, sea, car park, lovely. F:
  room is "camera" (camera = macchina fotografica).
- Sounds: the dark l (lovely), ai (tonight).
- Culture: hotels and B&Bs.

### 9 · A1 · 7 lines · What does she do?
- Scene: two friends. "What does your sister do?" "She works in a bank."
  "Does she like her job?" "No, she doesn't." "Why not?" "She likes money.
  But it's always other people's money."
- Grammar: seen: other people's (the 's). Explained: the -s of he/she/it
  (works, likes); does in questions, doesn't; What do you do? = what job;
  her (first look at his/her: it agrees with the owner).
- Words: sister, work, job, money, why not, always, other people.
- Sounds: -s after a hiss (watches) as iz.
- Culture: none.

### 10 · A1 · 7 lines · Is there a chemist's?
- Scene: a tourist and a local. "Is there a chemist's near here?" "Yes,
  there's one in the High Street." "Is it open on Sundays?" "On Sundays?
  Nothing is open here on Sundays." "Nothing?" "Only the pub."
- Grammar: seen: nothing. Explained: there is / there are (c'è, ci sono); on
  + day; 's for shops (the chemist's).
- Words: chemist's, near here, High Street, open, closed, Sunday, pub, only.
- Sounds: the ng that ends in the nose (nothing).
- Culture: Sunday opening hours; the high street.

### 11 · A1 · 7 lines · Can you cook?
- Scene: a room in a shared flat. "Can you cook?" "Yes, I can." "Can you
  clean?" "Yes, I can." "Can you play the guitar?" "No, I can't." "Perfect.
  The last one played the guitar all night."
- Grammar: seen: played (another past, a phrase). Explained: can, can't (no
  do, no -s); short answers with can; play the + instrument.
- Words: cook, clean, play, guitar, perfect, the last one, all night.
- Sounds: can (kən / kæn) and can't (kaant).
- Culture: flat-sharing.
- Aside: two weeks done; do not try to remember everything.

### 12 · A1 · 7 lines · A pink umbrella
- Scene: a lost umbrella at a door. "Is this your umbrella?" "No, it's my
  husband's." "It's pink!" "Yes. Pink is his favourite colour." "And what's
  your favourite colour?" "Black. Like his umbrellas."
- Grammar: explained: the possessive 's (my husband's); his and her agree
  with the owner, not the thing (Italian "suo"); my, your.
- Words: umbrella, husband, wife, pink, black, colours, favourite. F: the
  British spelling colour, favourite.
- Sounds: the u of umbrella (a), stress of favourite.
- Culture: umbrellas and the British weather.

### 13 · A1 · 8 lines · Half past eight
- Scene: two people at a shop door. "It's cold, isn't it?" "Very. What time
  is it?" "Half past eight." "The shop opens at nine." "Let's have a coffee,
  then." "Good idea. There's a café over there." "It opens at ten."
- Grammar: seen: isn't it? (a tag, as a phrase), let's. Explained: telling
  the time (half past, quarter past/to, o'clock); at + time; then at the end.
- Words: cold, time, half, past, quarter, o'clock, open, good idea, over
  there, numbers to 60.
- Sounds: the ou of o'clock, linking (half past eight).
- Culture: none.

### 14 · revision · 11 lines
- Recap scene: a hotel breakfast; a guest asks the time, the bank, the
  chemist's; twist on Sunday closing.
- Sections: 1 the present simple: I/you/we/they and he/she/it with -s (its
  three sounds); 2 do/does in questions, don't/doesn't, short answers
  (table); 3 have got (table); 4 my, your, his, her, its, our, their and the
  's (the owner rule); 5 there is / there are; 6 can; 7 days of the week,
  numbers to 100 (completion).

## Week 3

### 15 · A1 · 8 lines · On the phone
- Scene: a husband calls home. "Hi, it's me. What are you doing?" "I'm
  cooking." "What are you cooking?" "I don't know yet. I'm reading the
  recipe." "What's for dinner, then?" "Sandwiches, probably."
- Grammar: seen: yet. Explained: the present continuous (sto facendo); it's
  me (sono io).
- Words: phone, cook, read, recipe, dinner, probably, sandwich. F: recipe is
  "ricetta" of cooking; the doctor's ricetta is a prescription.
- Sounds: -ing (ng), ə in probably.
- Culture: none.

### 16 · A1 · 7 lines · Every morning
- Scene: two colleagues. "What time do you get up?" "At six." "Every day?"
  "Usually. I always go for a run before work." "Do you ever sleep in?"
  "Never. My dog doesn't let me."
- Grammar: seen: get up (a verb in two words), let me. Explained: always,
  usually, never before the verb; ever in questions; before work (no
  article).
- Words: get up, every day, run, before, after, work, sleep in, dog.
- Sounds: the r that disappears (before, never).
- Culture: the full English breakfast (and who has time for it).

### 17 · A1 · 8 lines · At the doctor's
- Scene: a surgery. "What's the matter?" "My back hurts." "Lie down, please.
  Does it hurt here?" "Yes." "And here?" "A lot." "You should rest. No
  gardening for two weeks." "But doctor, I'm a gardener."
- Grammar: seen: you should. Explained: the imperative (lie down, don't
  worry); What's the matter?; the doctor's.
- Words: doctor, back, hurt, lie down, rest, here, a lot, garden, gardener,
  weeks.
- Sounds: the strong h of hurt, öö (hurt, work).
- Culture: the NHS and the GP.
- Aside: three weeks; things are starting to settle by themselves.

### 18 · A1 · 8 lines · Let's go out
- Scene: a couple. "Let's go out tonight." "Where?" "Why don't we invite Tom
  and Sue?" "Them? He talks about his car all evening." "And her?" "She
  talks about his car too."
- Grammar: explained: let's / let's not; why don't we...?; me, him, her,
  us, them after a verb or a preposition; too at the end (anche).
- Words: go out, invite, talk about, car, all evening, too.
- Sounds: ə in about, the weak them.
- Culture: none.

### 19 · A1 · 8 lines · Happy birthday!
- Scene: an office. "Happy birthday, Mary! How old are you?" "That's a
  secret." "When were you born?" "On the third of May." "Which year?" "That's
  another secret."
- Grammar: seen: were you born / I was born (a past of be and a passive, as
  a phrase). Explained: be for age (I'm thirty: never "I have"); dates said
  "the third of May"; ordinals 1st–3rd; which.
- Words: birthday, old, secret, born, year, months, another. F: born: "I was
  born", never "I am born".
- Sounds: th in third, birthday (öö).
- Culture: birthday cards and the office cake.

### 20 · A1 · 8 lines · Have we got any bread?
- Scene: a kitchen before shopping. "Have we got any bread?" "No, we haven't.
  And there isn't any milk." "Is there any cheese?" "Some." "How much?" "Not
  much. And it's moving."
- Grammar: explained: some and any; bread, milk, cheese have no plural and
  no a (un pane = a loaf); how much.
- Words: bread, loaf, milk, cheese, shopping, any, some, not much.
- Sounds: ii and i (cheese, milk).
- Culture: none.

### 21 · revision · 12 lines
- Recap scene: a phone call: what are you doing, a birthday, an invitation
  out; twist with the cheese.
- Sections: 1 the present continuous (form, -ing spelling) and the present
  simple against it (first table); 2 imperative, don't, let's, why don't
  we; 3 pronouns: subject, object, possessive (table); 4 always, usually,
  never: where they go; 5 was and were (table), "I was born"; 6 months,
  dates, ordinals (completion); 7 some/any, words without a plural.

## Week 4

### 22 · A1 · 9 lines · Yesterday
- Scene: two friends. "What did you do yesterday?" "I stayed at home." "All
  day?" "Yes. I cleaned the kitchen, I washed the car, I watched a film."
  "Very busy!" "Well, I watched the film first. Then I didn't have time."
- Grammar: explained: the past simple of regular verbs (-ed: t, d, id); did
  in questions, didn't; back to lesson 3 (slept) and 11 (played).
- Words: yesterday, stay at home, clean, wash, watch, film, busy, first,
  then, well.
- Sounds: -ed as t, d, id.
- Culture: none.

### 23 · A1 · 9 lines · A weekend in Bath
- Scene: Monday at work. "Did you have a nice weekend?" "Lovely. We went to
  Bath." "Did you see the Roman baths?" "Yes, we did. We saw them from the
  café." "Didn't you go in?" "No, it was too cold. We had tea."
- Grammar: seen: too cold. Explained: irregular pasts (go/went, see/saw,
  have/had, be/was); short answers Yes, we did; have tea (have = eat,
  drink).
- Words: weekend, Roman, baths, go in, café, nice, lovely.
- Sounds: aa in Bath (and the North's short a: a note).
- Culture: Bath and its Roman baths.

### 24 · A1 · 8 lines · Lost property
- Scene: a lost property office. "I lost my bag on the bus." "What colour is
  it?" "Black." "Is this yours?" "No, mine is bigger." "And this one?" "That's
  mine! But it isn't my bag." "Whose is it, then?" "My wife's."
- Grammar: seen: bigger (a comparative), whose. Explained: mine, yours,
  his, hers against my, your; this one, that one.
- Words: lose/lost, bag, bus, mine, yours, whose, bigger.
- Sounds: the u of bus (a), z in whose.
- Culture: none.
- Aside: a month with English; read the numbers aloud as you go.

### 25 · A1 · 8 lines · Some more cake?
- Scene: tea at a grandmother's. "Would you like some more cake?" "I'd love
  some, thank you." "Do you like it?" "I love it." "I know. You've already
  had four pieces." "Five, actually."
- Grammar: seen: you've already had (a first present perfect, as a phrase).
  Explained: would like (vorrei) against like (mi piace); I'd love; some in
  offers.
- Words: cake, piece, more, love, already, actually. F: actually = "in
  realtà", never "attualmente".
- Sounds: the weak would, the ou of know.
- Culture: afternoon tea.

## Week 5

### 26 · A2 · 8 lines · Ten years ago
- Scene: old school friends meet. "Mark! I last saw you ten years ago!" "Did
  you? I didn't have a beard then." "And I had hair." "Where did you go after
  school?" "I moved to Canada." "Why did you come back?" "I missed the rain."
- Grammar: explained: ago (after the time: fa); wh- questions with did;

- Words: ago, last, beard, hair, school, move, come back, miss, rain. F: miss

- Sounds: iə in beard, eə in hair.
- Culture: none.

### 27 · A2 · 8 lines · What's the weather like?
- Scene: a phone call between Bologna and London. "What's the weather like

  course. It's a tradition."
- Grammar: seen: colder than (comparatives), is it going to (going to).
  Explained: What's it like?; it for the weather (no subject in Italian);
  still against again.
- Words: weather, rain, sunny, warm, cold, still, again, tomorrow, of course.
- Sounds: the ei of rain, linking.
- Culture: talking about the weather.

- Scene: two friends. "What are you going to do on Saturday?" "I'm going to

- Grammar: explained: going to for plans (and "gonna" when people speak);

- Sounds: the weak going to (gòuing tə).
- Culture: DIY.

### 30 · A2 · 9 lines · Smaller but dearer
- Scene: a flat viewing. "It's smaller than the old one." "But it's nearer
  the centre." "And more expensive." "It's quieter, too." "Quieter? There's a
  nightclub downstairs." "Yes, but it's closed on Mondays."
- Grammar: explained: -er and more (by syllables), than; quiet against
  quite.
- Words: flat, small, near, centre, expensive, dear, quiet, downstairs,
  nightclub. F: dear = caro (both meanings).
- Sounds: stress in comparatives, ə in than.
- Culture: renting a flat.

### 31 · A2 · 10 lines · Have you ever...?
- Scene: a bus stop. "Have you ever been to Scotland?" "Yes, I have. I went
  there last year." "Did you like it?" "I loved it. Have you ever eaten
  haggis?" "No, I haven't. What is it?" "You don't want to know."
- Grammar: explained: the present perfect for experience (ever, never,
  been to); the past simple with a time (last year): the great Italian trap
  (passato prossimo for both).
- Words: ever, never, been, Scotland, last year, eat/ate/eaten.
- Sounds: the weak have, been (bin/biin).
- Culture: Scotland and haggis.

### 32 · A2 · 9 lines · Rules are rules
- Scene: a swimming pool. "Excuse me, sir. You must wear a cap." "A cap? I
  haven't got any hair." "Sorry, you have to wear one. It's the rule." "And I
  mustn't run, I mustn't jump..." "You don't have to swim either." "Then I'll
  sit in the café."
- Grammar: explained: must, have to; mustn't (vietato) against don't have to
  (non è necessario): Italian "non devi" covers both; either at the end.
- Words: wear, cap, rule, run, jump, swim, sit, either.
- Sounds: the silent t in mustn't (masnt).
- Culture: none.

### 33 · A2 · 10 lines · The best in town
- Scene: a tourist and a local. "Where's the best fish and chips in town?"

- Grammar: explained: -est and most; good/better/best, bad/worse/worst.
- Words: best, worst, popular, cheap, fish and chips, town, come in.
- Sounds: the st that closes best, worst.
- Culture: fish and chips.

### 34 · A2 · 8 lines · Too small

- Sounds: the ju of beautiful.
- Culture: none.
- Aside: a third of the course done.
