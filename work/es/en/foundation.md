# Spanish for English speakers · Foundation plan (100 lessons)

The Spanish of Spain as Madrid speaks it: c and z said th (distinción), ll and
y the same sound (yeísmo), vosotros for "you all", the perfect for what
happened today, coche, ordenador, móvil, zumo. Latin American Spanish appears
only in notes that name it and in lessons 92–94. Translations, notes and
everything else in English, British spelling and words (colour, realise, flat,
mobile, holiday, queue, the underground). Levels: A1 1–25, A2 26–50, B1 51–75,
B2 76–100.

This plan applies `work/PATTERNS.md` (how Assimil builds a course, measured in
the books) to Spanish for English speakers. Read it first, then
`work/BRIEF.md` and `work/es/en/writer.md`. The model for this pair is
Assimil's Spanish with Ease (Anton and Smellie, the English edition), read by
the planner and never by writers. From it the plan takes:

- the pace: a first lesson of about seven very short lines, about ten lines by
  lesson 12, twelve to fourteen from lesson 40, fourteen or more after 75; the
  lines stay short (3–9 words);
- the grammar order, faster than Lo Spagnolo's: ser and estar, pronoun
  dropping, tú and usted in the first week; hay, the time, demonstratives,
  para and por in the second; pronouns joined to the infinitive and the
  imperative, reflexive verbs, the imperfect and the perfect with se lo in the
  third; gustar-type verbs, saber and conocer, comparisons and the gerund in
  the fourth; the preterite from 29; soler, the future, cuál and the
  conditional of courtesy before 50; the subjunctive in use from 51;
- the note style: short, plain, practical, set against English ("Spanish does
  not need the 'I'", "literally 'less badly'; the nearest English is 'just as
  well'"), sounds explained through English words (the ch of Scottish loch,
  the ni of onion, the tt of American better), a promise when something will
  come back ("we will see the whole table in the revision lesson");
- the translation convention: natural English first, the literal sense in
  (parentheses) where Spanish builds the sentence differently, and [square
  brackets] around English words the Spanish does not have: "Are [you]
  English?", "Here you are. (Here [you] have.)". Dialogue lines only, never in
  the exercises;
- the revision lesson as "Revision and notes": plain numbered sections
  (pronunciation and stress early on, then ser and estar, para and por, the
  time, the pronoun table), reassurance that no rule need be learnt by heart;
- the figured pronunciation's signs (content/es/pronunciation.md, English
  readers): é, ou, ss, H, c and ç, gn, tch, ï, the stressed syllable in bold.

The order of places, cultural topics and some grammar steps reuses the good
ideas of the old plan (`work/es/syllabus.md`, whose cast, story and numbering
are not used). Pronunciation key: `content/es/pronunciation.md`.

## How to read a lesson block

- **Lines**: the dialogue's numbered lines (the size the map gives).
- **Scene**: a small scene complete in itself, its people and its last line.
  The sketched Spanish lines are the plan's proposal: keep their idea and
  their grammar, improve the wording where it is unnatural, and say so.
  Voices are mostly unnamed; a name in a block belongs to that lesson only.
  Lessons 1–5 end plainly; humour starts at 6. No continuing story, no
  recurring cast.
- **Grammar**: *seen* = appears in the dialogue for the first time as a fixed
  phrase; its note says only what it is and that it will come back ("we will
  come back to this", "see the revision lesson"). *Explained* = a note
  explains it properly. *Recycle* = earlier forms to use again. A revision
  lesson lists its sections: the first full tables.
- **Words**: the lesson's vocabulary field; **F** = a false friend or a trap
  for English speakers, to be named in a note.
- **Sounds**: pronunciation observations (`sounds.en`).
- **Culture**: the cultural note's subject, or none.
- **Aside**: the author's words (`aside.en`), when there are some.

Throughout: subject pronouns are dropped unless they contrast or stress (soy
inglés; yo soy inglés, ella no); every noun is masculine or feminine and its
words agree with it; the stress follows the written accent or the default rule.
The English speaker's great traps come back again and again in notes
("remember: ...", "as in lesson N"): ser against estar; gender and agreement
(also of "it": la lista → la olvidas); tú, usted and vosotros; the perfect for
today against the preterite for yesterday, and the preterite against the
imperfect; gustar and its family turned round ("the cinema pleases me"); por
against para; the subjunctive, where English has none; the tapped r against
the rolled rr; full, pure vowels with no "uh"; a s never said z; tener for age,
hunger and cold (tengo treinta años, tengo hambre); pedir and preguntar for
"ask"; the false friends (largo, simpático, constipado, embarazada, éxito,
librería, carpeta, actual, sensible, asistir, realizar, molestar, decepción).
The easy side, to point out with pleasure: thousands of cognates (-ción =
-tion, -dad = -ty, -mente = -ly, -oso = -ous) and a spelling that is read as
it is written. From lesson 76 forms already met are no longer annotated; each
lesson adds one to three constructions and many expressions, with more texts
without speakers.

## Week 1

### 1 · A1 · 7 lines · En el bar (At the bar)
- Scene: a bar counter in the morning. "¡Buenos días!" "Buenos días. ¿Qué le
  pongo?" "Un café con leche, por favor." "¿Algo más?" "Sí, una tostada con
  tomate." "Aquí tiene." "Muchas gracias." Plain ending. (Written: 001.txt.)
- Grammar: seen: ¿Qué le pongo? (le, "for you": later); aquí tiene (usted and
  the dropped pronoun: lesson 2). Explained: un and una, every noun masculine
  or feminine; ¿ and ¡ at the start; mucho agreeing (muchas gracias).
- Words: buenos días, café con leche, por favor, algo más, sí, tostada,
  tomate, aquí, muchas gracias. Ex1 = words and phrases of the dialogue.
- Sounds: the figured line (bold, é, ou, ss); pure vowels never reduced
  (leche, tomate); c before i as th (gracias), v said b (favor), the tapped r.
- Culture: breakfast at the bar: café con leche, tostada con tomate, café
  solo, cortado; paying at the end; ¿Cuánto es?

### 2 · A1 · 7 lines · ¿Cómo te llamas? (What's your name?)
- Scene: a language exchange in a Madrid bar. "Hola. ¿Cómo te llamas?" "Me
  llamo Fiona. ¿Y tú?" "Yo soy Javier. ¿Eres inglesa?" "No, soy escocesa. De
  Edimburgo." "Yo soy de aquí, de Madrid." "Encantada." "Encantado." Plain.
  (Written: 002.txt.)
- Grammar: explained: me llamo, te llamas; soy, eres (ser); the subject
  pronoun dropped, and kept only for contrast (yo soy Javier); tú against
  usted (back to lesson 1); a question in the same order as the statement;
  nationality words agree and take no capital (inglés, inglesa).
- Words: hola, cómo, llamarse, yo, tú, y, ser, inglés, escocés, de, aquí,
  Madrid, encantado/encantada.
- Sounds: j as H (Javier); h silent, ll as y; the stress rule (escocesa,
  Madrid, cómo).
- Culture: tú from the first sentence; dos besos; intercambios.

### 3 · A1 · 6 lines · ¿Qué tal? (How are things?)
- Scene: two flatmates in the kitchen in the morning. "Buenos días. ¿Qué
  tal?" "Regular. He dormido muy mal." "¿Estás cansado?" "Sí, estoy muy
  cansado." "¿Quieres un café?" "Sí, por favor. Uno grande." Plain.
- Grammar: seen: he dormido (a first perfect, "I have slept", as in English:
  lesson 18). Explained: estoy, estás (estar): the second "to be", for how
  you are today, against soy (lesson 2) for who you are; the revision lesson
  puts them side by side; quieres (querer, "to want"); uno standing alone for
  un (uno grande).
- Words: qué tal, regular, dormir, muy, mal, cansado, estar, querer, grande.
  F: regular = so-so, not "regular"; cansado agrees (cansada for a woman).
- Sounds: the r at the start of regular is rolled, though the figured line
  writes it r (régou*lar*); qu as k before e and i (*kié*réss).
- Culture: none.

### 4 · A1 · 6 lines · ¿Dónde está el metro? (Where's the underground?)
- Scene: a street; a visitor and a passer-by, usted. "Perdone, ¿dónde está
  el metro?" "¿El metro? Está allí, a la derecha." "¿Está lejos?" "No, no.
  Cinco minutos." "Muchas gracias." "De nada." Plain.
- Grammar: explained: está for where something is; el and la ("the") with
  masculine and feminine nouns (el metro, la derecha); perdone (usted)
  against perdona (tú); no twice (no, no; no + verb later).
- Words: perdone, dónde, metro, allí, derecha, izquierda, lejos, cerca,
  minuto, de nada, numbers 1–5.
- Sounds: d between vowels soft, like the th of this (nada); j again (lejos);
  the tapped r of derecha.
- Culture: the Madrid metro (opened 1919), the diamond logo, the stations
  with names of squares and saints.

### 5 · A1 · 7 lines · ¿Hablas inglés? (Do you speak English?)
- Scene: a train from Madrid to Toledo; two young travellers. "¿Hablas
  inglés?" "Sí, un poco. ¿Eres inglés?" "No, soy irlandés. Vivo en Dublín."
  "¿Y qué haces en España?" "Estudio español en Madrid." "¡Hablas muy bien!"
  "Gracias. Hablo un poco." Plain.
- Grammar: seen: ¿qué haces? (hacer: lesson 15). Explained: the present of
  -ar and -ir verbs, first look: hablo, hablas, habla; vivo, vives, vive; the
  ending says the person, so no pronoun is needed; a question by the voice
  alone; muy bien.
- Words: hablar, inglés and español (the languages, no capital), un poco,
  irlandés, vivir, en, España, estudiar, muy bien.
- Sounds: the ñ of España, written gn (the ni of onion); the -s of hablas
  always sounded (ss), never z.
- Culture: Spanish in Spain is called español or castellano; why both.

### 6 · A1 · 6 lines · Dos chocolates (Two hot chocolates)
- Scene: a churrería in the afternoon; one customer alone. "Buenas tardes.
  ¿Qué le pongo?" "Dos chocolates con churros, por favor." "¿Para dos
  personas?" "No, para una." "¿Dos chocolates para una persona?" "Sí: uno
  para hoy y otro para mañana."
- Grammar: explained: the plural in -s (churros, chocolates, personas);
  numbers 1–10; para, "for" (para una persona; more in lesson 14); otro with
  no un ("another" is otro, never un otro).
- Words: buenas tardes, chocolate, churro, dos, persona, uno, hoy, otro,
  mañana (tomorrow), numbers 6–10.
- Sounds: ch as tch (churros, chocolate); the rolled rr of churros.
- Culture: chocolate con churros (San Ginés, near the Puerta del Sol, since
  1894), after a night out or on Sunday morning; porras.
- Aside: tomorrow, a revision lesson: nothing new, everything in order.

### 7 · revision · 10 lines
- Recap scene: two strangers at a bar counter near the Puerta del Sol order,
  give their names, say where they live and what they do, all in careful
  Spanish; twist: both are English, from the same town, and only find out at
  the end, and decide to go on in Spanish.
- Sections (Revision and notes): 1 pronunciation: the five pure vowels and
  how the figured line writes them (é, ou); c and z as th, j and g as H, ll
  and y, ñ, h, b and v, r and rr, s never z; 2 stress and the written accent:
  the rule, accents on question words (cómo, dónde, qué), tú/tu, sí/si,
  más; 3 gender: un, una, el, la; -o and -a, café and leche; nationality
  words (inglés, inglesa); 4 the plural: -s after a vowel, -es after a
  consonant (completions: tren, trenes; ciudad, ciudades), los and las; 5
  the subject pronouns (table: yo, tú, usted, él, ella, nosotros, nosotras,
  vosotros, vosotras, ustedes, ellos, ellas), when to drop them, tú, usted
  and vosotros; 6 ser and estar: the full present of both (table), first
  rule: ser for who and what, estar for where and how; 7 the present of
  hablar and vivir in full (completion), with comer for -er; 8 numbers 0–20
  (completion). Encouragement at the start, a closing line.

## Week 2

### 8 · A1 · 9 lines · Una farmacia (A chemist's)
- Scene: Sunday evening, a street. "Perdone, ¿hay una farmacia por aquí?"
  "Sí, hay una en la plaza." "¿Y está abierta?" "Hoy es domingo. Está
  cerrada." "¿Hay otra?" "Hay una de guardia en la calle Mayor." "¿Está
  lejos?" "No. Pero hay una cola muy larga." "¿Muy larga?" "Sí. Empieza
  aquí." (They are standing in it.)
- Grammar: seen: empieza (empezar, e → ie: lesson 9). Explained: hay, one
  form for "there is" and "there are", for something new (hay una
  farmacia), against está for where a known thing is (la farmacia está en la
  plaza); estar with abierto, cerrado (a state); otra again with no una.
- Words: farmacia, por aquí, plaza, abierto, cerrado, domingo, de guardia,
  calle, cola, largo. F: largo = long (large is grande).
- Sounds: gu before a keeps its u, as an ou glide (*gouar*dia); ll as y
  (*ca*yé).
- Culture: the green cross; the farmacia de guardia, open at night and on
  Sundays, listed on every chemist's door.

### 9 · A1 · 7 lines · ¿Qué hora es? (What time is it?)
- Scene: a student running in the street. "Perdona, ¿qué hora es?" "Son las
  nueve y cuarto." "¡Las nueve y cuarto! Tengo que estar en clase a las
  nueve." "Entonces llegas tarde." "No, no. La clase empieza cuando llego
  yo." "¿Por qué?" "Porque el profesor soy yo."
- Grammar: explained: the time: es la una, son las dos; y cuarto, y media,
  menos cuarto; a las nueve, "at nine"; tengo que + infinitive, "I have to"
  (tener: tengo, tienes, tiene); empezar (e → ie), first look; por qué and
  porque.
- Words: hora, cuarto, media, menos, clase, tener que, llegar, tarde,
  entonces, cuando, profesor, numbers 11–12.
- Sounds: qu in que, cuarto: ké, but *couar*to (the u sounded before a).
- Culture: Spanish timetables: shops open late and close late; many small
  shops shut for a few hours after lunch.

### 10 · A1 · 8 lines · Mi hermana (My sister)
- Scene: two friends look at a photo on a phone. "¿Quién es la chica de la
  foto?" "Es mi hermana Lucía. Es médica." "¿Y dónde vive?" "Vive en
  Londres. Trabaja en un hospital." "¿Es simpática?" "Muy simpática. Pero
  siempre está cansada." "¿Por el trabajo?" "No. Por sus tres niños."
- Grammar: explained: ser and estar side by side (es médica, es simpática /
  está cansada; dónde vive); a job after ser with no article (es médica, "she
  is a doctor"); mi, tu, su; por, "because of" (more in lesson 14).
- Words: quién, chica, foto (la foto), hermana, hermano, médico, vivir,
  Londres, trabajar, hospital, simpático, siempre, trabajo, niño, niña. F:
  simpático = nice, friendly (sympathetic is comprensivo).
- Sounds: the H of trabajo; the stressed syllable of médica and simpática
  (written accent on the third from last).
- Culture: two surnames, the father's and the mother's; women keep theirs
  when they marry.

### 11 · A1 · 7 lines · ¿Adónde vas? (Where are you going?)
- Scene: Saturday morning, two flatmates. "¿Adónde vas?" "Voy al mercado.
  ¿Vienes conmigo?" "No puedo. Voy a estudiar." "¿Estudiar? ¡Pero hoy es
  sábado!" "Mañana tengo un examen." "¿De qué?" "De cocina. ¡Espera! Voy
  contigo."
- Grammar: seen: no puedo (poder, o → ue: lesson 13); espera (an imperative:
  lesson 16). Explained: ir: voy, vas, va...; a + el = al; ¿adónde? (where
  to) against ¿dónde? (where); voy a + infinitive, "I'm going to", as in
  English; vienes (venir); conmigo, contigo.
- Words: ir, mercado, venir, estudiar, sábado, examen, cocina, esperar. F:
  esperar = to wait and to hope.
- Sounds: x as k + s (examen, ék*sa*mén); the stressed syllable of sábado.
- Culture: the neighbourhood market (mercado de abastos): stalls for fish,
  meat, fruit; Saturday morning shopping.

### 12 · A1 · 9 lines · Este es el salón (This is the living room)
- Scene: an estate agent shows a woman a flat. "Este es el salón." "¡Qué
  pequeño!" "Es pequeño, pero tiene mucha luz." "¿Y esta puerta?" "Esa es la
  cocina." "¿Y aquella ventana?" "Aquella es del vecino." "¿Y el
  dormitorio?" "Señora, esto es todo el piso."
- Grammar: explained: este, esta, ese, esa, aquel, aquella (three distances
  where English has two: this, that, that over there); esto, eso for a
  thing not named; ¡qué + adjective!; de + el = del; mucho, mucha.
- Words: salón, pequeño, luz, puerta, cocina (the room, back to lesson 11's
  cooking), ventana, vecino, dormitorio, piso, todo, señora. F: piso = flat
  and floor.
- Sounds: ñ again (pequeño); the z of luz as ç (louç).
- Culture: Madrid flats: interior and exterior, the portero, the floors
  (bajo, primero...).
- Aside: there is a lot of groundwork in these first lessons; it gets
  easier.

### 13 · A1 · 8 lines · De usted (Formally)
- Scene: a bar in the evening; a young woman and her grandfather. "Buenas
  noches, señora. ¿Qué va a tomar?" "¿Señora? ¡Tengo treinta años!" "Perdona.
  ¿Qué vas a tomar?" "Una caña, por favor." "¿Y tu amigo?" "No es mi amigo.
  Es mi abuelo." "Perdone, señor. ¿Usted qué va a tomar?" "Un vino tinto,
  joven. Y a mí, de usted."
- Grammar: explained: usted with the verb of él (va, tiene), tú with its own
  (vas, tienes), and why; tener for age (tengo treinta años: never "soy
  treinta"); numbers by tens to 100; poder (o → ue) and querer (e → ie):
  the stem changes, first look (back to lessons 3 and 11).
- Words: buenas noches, tomar, años, caña, amigo, abuelo, señor, vino tinto,
  joven, numbers 20–100 by tens.
- Sounds: ñ again (*ca*gna); the H of joven (*Ho*bén).
- Culture: tú and usted in Spain today: tú almost everywhere, usted to older
  people, in formal settings, to show respect; hablar de usted.

### 14 · revision · 11 lines
- Recap scene: a night queue at a farmacia de guardia; a young man and a
  lady of ninety chat: the time, where they live, her family; twist: she is
  not ill: she comes every night because in the queue she meets her friends
  ("aquí están mis amigas").
- Sections: 1 diphthongs and syllables: strong vowels (a, e, o) and weak
  ones (i, u, y), when two vowels make one syllable, when an accent splits
  them (bien, bueno; día); 2 hay and estar (table, examples from 8, 12); 3
  ser and estar, second step: profession, origin, character with ser; place,
  state, mood with estar (examples from the lessons); 4 the time (table:
  es la una, son las..., y/menos cuarto, y media; a las...); 5 ir and venir
  (full present), al and del, ir a + infinitive; 6 para and por, first look
  (para for "for" a person or a goal, por for "because of"); 7 tú, usted,
  vosotros, ustedes with their verb forms (table), tener for age; numbers
  20–100 (completion). Pronunciation is no longer a section after this:
  difficult words get a note in their lesson.

## Week 3

### 15 · A1 · 10 lines · La lista de la compra (The shopping list)
- Scene: a couple at the door. "Voy a hacer la compra. ¿Necesitas algo?"
  "Sí. ¿Puedes traerme pan?" "Claro. ¿Algo más?" "Leche, huevos y tomates."
  "¿Por qué no me escribes una lista?" "Porque siempre la olvidas en casa."
  "Bueno, pues dímelo otra vez." "Pan, leche, huevos y tomates." "Pan,
  leche, huevos... ¿y qué más?" "Mira, mejor voy yo."
- Grammar: seen: dímelo, mira (imperatives: lesson 16). Explained: the
  object pronoun joined to the infinitive (traerme, "to bring me"); la for
  "it" when the thing is feminine (la lista → la olvidas): "it" has a gender;
  hacer (hago, haces...) and hacer la compra, "do the shopping"; necesitar;
  pues as a filler ("well then").
- Words: hacer la compra, necesitar, traer, pan, claro, huevo, escribir,
  lista, olvidar, casa, bueno (as "OK, well"), otra vez, mejor.
- Sounds: the hue of huevo as oué; the v of olvidas.
- Culture: the daily barra de pan; shopping little and often.

### 16 · A1 · 9 lines · ¡Levántate! (Get up!)
- Scene: a mother wakes her son. "¡Javi! Son las siete y media.
  ¡Levántate!" "Cinco minutos más, mamá..." "¡Ya! Vas a llegar tarde." "Ya
  me levanto, ya me levanto." "¿Y la ducha?" "Me ducho y me visto en cinco
  minutos." "¿Y el desayuno?" "Desayuno en el autobús." "Ay, Javi...
  perdona. Hoy es sábado."
- Grammar: explained: reflexive verbs: levantarse, ducharse, vestirse (e →
  i): me levanto, te levantas, se levanta; the tú imperative (levanta,
  levántate) with the pronoun joined and an accent to keep the stress; ya,
  "already, now".
- Words: levantarse, mamá, ya, ducha, ducharse, vestirse, desayuno,
  desayunar, autobús, ay.
- Sounds: the stressed syllable of levántate (the accent added when the
  pronoun is joined).
- Culture: none.

### 17 · A1 · 8 lines · Antes (In the old days)
- Scene: a grandfather and his granddaughter. "Abuelo, ¿cómo era Madrid
  antes?" "Era más pequeño. No había tantos coches." "¿Y qué hacías los
  domingos?" "Iba al Retiro con mis padres." "¿Y tenías móvil?" "No, hija.
  No había móviles." "Entonces, ¿cómo llamabas a la abuela?" "No la llamaba.
  Vivía en el piso de arriba."
- Grammar: explained: the imperfect, the past of description and habit
  ("was", "used to", "was ...ing"): -aba for -ar verbs (llamaba), -ía for
  -er and -ir (tenía, vivía, hacía); era, iba; había from hay; the other
  past, the preterite, comes later (lesson 29); más, tanto.
- Words: abuelo, abuela, antes, más, tanto, coche, los domingos, el Retiro,
  padres, móvil, hija (as a fond address), llamar, arriba. F: padres =
  parents.
- Sounds: the stress of hacías, había, tenía (í splits the vowels).
- Culture: the Retiro, a royal park opened to the public in 1868: the lake,
  the boats, the Crystal Palace.

### 18 · A1 · 10 lines · Se lo he dicho (I've told her)
- Scene: two colleagues at the office. "¿Has visto a Marta hoy?" "Sí, la he
  visto esta mañana." "¿Le has dado el informe?" "Sí, se lo he dado." "¿Y le
  has dicho que es para el jefe?" "Sí, se lo he dicho." "¿Y qué ha dicho?"
  "Nada. Lo ha leído y se lo ha llevado." "¿Adónde?" "Al jefe. Con su
  nombre."
- Grammar: explained: the perfect: he, has, ha... + participle in -ado,
  -ido; irregular participles (visto, dicho, hecho); in Spain it is the tense
  for today (hoy, esta mañana), used much as in English; lo, la (him, her,
  it), le (to him, to her); le + lo becomes se lo; a before a person (ver a
  Marta), first look.
- Words: ver, dar, decir, informe, jefe, esta mañana (F: mañana = morning
  and tomorrow), leer, llevarse, nada, nombre.
- Sounds: ch as tch (*di*tcho); the silent h of hecho (*é*tcho).
- Culture: none.
- Aside: when you have read a sentence, see if you can say it again without
  looking: that is how you begin to think in Spanish.

### 19 · A1 · 10 lines · ¿Te gusta el cine? (Do you like the cinema?)
- Scene: a first date. "¿Te gusta el cine?" "Me encanta. ¿Y a ti?" "A mí
  también. ¿Te gustan las películas de miedo?" "No, no me gustan nada." "¿Y
  las comedias?" "Tampoco." "Entonces, ¿qué te gusta?" "Las películas
  largas, en blanco y negro, en sueco..." "¿Con subtítulos?" "No, sin
  subtítulos. Así duermo mejor."
- Grammar: explained: gustar turned round: "the cinema pleases me" (me gusta
  el cine), me gustan with a plural; encantar; a mí, a ti for stress;
  también and tampoco ("me neither"); no ... nada, "not at all"; dormir (o →
  ue): duermo.
- Words: gustar, cine, encantar, película, miedo, comedia, también,
  tampoco, blanco, negro, sueco, subtítulo, sin, así.
- Sounds: the hard g of gusta (*gouss*ta) against the H of Javier (lesson
  2).
- Culture: dubbed films in Spain; versión original (VO) cinemas.

### 20 · A1 · 10 lines · Me duele la cabeza (I've got a headache)
- Scene: at the doctor's. "Buenos días, doctora." "Buenos días. ¿Qué le
  pasa?" "Me duele la cabeza y me duele la garganta." "¿Tiene fiebre?" "Un
