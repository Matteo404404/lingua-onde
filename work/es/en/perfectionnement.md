# Spanish for English speakers · Perfectionnement plan (70 lessons)

For learners who have finished Foundation (B2 at the start, C1 at the end).
The Spanish of Spain as Madrid speaks and writes it (distinción, vosotros,
the perfect for today, coche, ordenador, móvil, zumo), spelled by the Real
Academia. Other varieties only in the portraits that are about them
(Canarias, México, the Río de la Plata, the Caribbean, the United States) and
in notes that name them; Catalan, Galician and Basque only in their portraits
and in notes that name them, each word quoted and named. Everything else in
English, British spelling and words (you to the learner). Levels: B2 1–45, C1
46–70 (as in content/FORMAT.md and the other Perfectionnement courses). Read
`work/PATTERNS.md` (section "Perfectionnement") and `work/BRIEF.md` (section
"Perfectionnement") first, then the writer brief `work/es/en/perf-writer.md`.

How the course works (from Assimil's Espagnol perfectionnement, read by the
planner for structure only, never by writers):
- Each lesson is a text complete in itself; the kind changes from day to
  day. No growth in length: 14–25 numbered sentences or lines, 150–400
  words. No continuing story, no recurring cast: a name belongs to one
  lesson.
- Humour is the second aim: wordplay on the lesson's keyword, satire, an
  absurd last line that undoes the premise. Informative texts and the
  serious figures (Cervantes, Machado) may end plainly.
- A "word" lesson about every eight lessons, as the book opens with andar
  and comes back to ver, pedir, decir and dar: one verb or noun and its
  idioms (echar, quedar, dar, poner, llevar, pasar, sacar, tirar, cara);
  nearly every note is an idiom (15–30 notes) and the notes end with the
  expression list (each idiom with its sentence and note number), as the
  book's first lesson ends with its list.
- A portrait of a place of the Spanish-speaking world about every seven
  lessons, on one template: where and what it is like, its towns, a remark
  of history, the landscape, what is made there, food and drink, a last line
  on a speciality. Spain first (Andalucía, Galicia, Cataluña, Euskadi,
  Canarias), then the Americas (México, the Río de la Plata, the Caribbean,
  the United States), and Madrid last.
- Artists, writers and figures of the past: only well-established facts;
  legends told as legends ("según la leyenda", "se cuenta que"); quote only
  titles or a few words of public-domain works (authors who died before
  1946); never quote a living or recent author's text.
- Notes 8–15 (more in word lessons): word families and senses, idioms with
  their literal sense first, grammar the learner "knows but uses badly",
  register, false friends; "remember" back references to Foundation
  ("Foundation" in the English) and to earlier lessons of this course.
- Grammar is never basic again. Foundation explained ser and estar, the
  perfect, preterite and imperfect, the pluperfect, the present subjunctive
  and its triggers (51–56), involuntary se (57, 78), impersonal and passive
  se (59), ser and estar with adjectives that change meaning (60), verbs of
  change (61), llevar, seguir and ir + gerund (62), the passive with ser
  (64), reported speech (65), relatives with the subjunctive (66), lo +
  adjective (67), the future of probability (68), the imperfect subjunctive
  and si (71, 76), como si (72), time clauses (73), the sequence of tenses
  (74), aunque (75), cuyo and el cual (80), connectors (81), verbs with
  prepositions (83), por and para (84), the repeated subjunctive (85),
  diminutives (86), idioms (87), the formal letter (89), que + subjunctive
  for wishes (90), Spain and Latin America (93), the Andalusian accent (94),
  proverbs (95), false friends (96), Don Quijote (97). These numbers are the
  Foundation plan's: the writer checks each in the written lesson. This
  course takes those points deeper and adds what Foundation left out (see
  each Focus).
- The English speaker's traps come back in notes whenever a text meets them:
  ser and estar; the subjunctive, which English has almost lost; por and
  para; the preterite against the imperfect (aspect, and the verbs whose
  meaning changes: supe, conocí, quise, pude); pronoun order and doubling
  (se lo, a mí me, esta la pago yo); sino against pero; llevar against
  traer; the -ing form that Spanish says with an infinitive (fumar está
  prohibido); false friends and calques.
- Exercises: 5 + 5 (translate; fill in), sentences of 5–20 words.
- No figured pronunciation lines and no culture section: culture lives in
  the texts and the notes. A figured form appears only inside a note, for a
  new or hard word (a foreign name, the x of México, a stress that decides
  the meaning: término, termino, terminó), copied from
  `python3 -I work/tools/figure_es.py --say "word"` (see the writer brief).
- Revision lessons (7, 14 ... 70) have no text: numbered sections on finer
  points, with tables and examples from the week, then a short practice of
  five + five items (in ex1 and ex2).
- From lesson 50 the active wave brings back lesson n − 49.
- Foundation's active wave goes on alongside: for a learner who finished
  Foundation, lessons 1–49 each bring back one of Foundation 52–100 (the app
  does it). Lesson 2 says so in a sentence, and says that a learner who
  starts here directly simply begins the course's own wave at lesson 50.

## The book around the lessons

As in the Assimil Perfectionnement volumes, after lesson 70 and before the
release: `content/es/appendix-perfectionnement.en.txt` with the
introduction (`== intro`), the grammar appendix (`== grammar`, the finer
points of the revision lessons, with tables), the grammar index (`== index`,
topic → lessons) and the expressions of the word lessons (`== phrases`).

## How to read a lesson block

- The heading gives the number, the level, the size of the text (numbered
  items) and the title, with its English in brackets. Revision lessons have
  no text and no size.
- The first line of the block names the kind (word, dialogue, portrait,
  story ...) and gives the scene, the facts and the last line.
- **Focus**: the grammar, register or vocabulary the notes must teach.

## Lessons

## Week 1

### 1 · B2 · 20 lines · Echar (To throw)
- Word lesson: a humorous reflection on echar, the verb Spanish uses where
  English throws, pours, posts, fires, misses and naps: echar de menos,
  echar una mano (Foundation 87), echar la culpa, echar un vistazo, echar
  una siesta, echar a perder, echar en cara, echar humo, echar chispas,
  echar la bronca, echar raíces, echar cuentas, echarse atrás, echarse a
  llorar / a reír, echar a alguien (del trabajo), echar una carta al buzón,
  echar gasolina, echarle cara. Told through one woman, Lucía, over one bad
  Monday. Last line: the author closes the lesson with "Y aquí echamos el
  cierre" (we pull down the shutter for today).
- Focus: echar as throw, pour, put in, expel; echarse a + infinitive (start
  suddenly: se echó a reír) against ponerse a (lesson 25 will bring it);
  echar de menos and extrañar, the usual verb in Latin America; the
  expression list.

### 2 · B2 · 16 lines · Lo que te espera (What lies ahead)
- Introduction: the course presents itself, tú to the learner: a tour of
  Spain and the Spanish-speaking world in texts of every kind, from Seville
  to Buenos Aires, from a joke to a letter to the town hall, painters and
  poets, idioms by the dozen. Foundation's wave goes on (one sentence); a
  learner who starts here begins this course's wave at lesson 50. Ends with
  a wink: in this course nobody asks for the bill, and tomorrow two friends
  will fight to pay it (tomorrow's lesson).
- Focus: the future perfect for what will be done by a time (dentro de un
  mes habrás leído treinta textos); para + infinitive (same subject) against
  para que + subjunctive (a different subject), back to Foundation 52.

### 3 · B2 · 20 lines · ¡Que pago yo! (No, I'm paying!)
- Dialogue: two old friends at a bar counter in Madrid fight to pay for the
  coffees and churros: "Ni hablar", "Que no, que esta la pago yo", "La
  próxima la pagas tú", "Ya me invitarás", "Déjate de tonterías", the
  waiter waiting with the card machine. They finally agree to pay half each.
  Last line: the waiter: "Ya está pagado. Lo ha pagado la señora del fondo,
  que dice que es su madre."
- Focus: emphatic que at the start (¡que no!, que pago yo); ni hablar,
  venga, anda, hombre / mujer, pues; a fronted object takes its pronoun
  (esta la pago yo, el café lo pago yo), against English word order; invitar
  as "treat, pay for" (te invito), pagar a medias, pagar a escote; the
  pronoun doubled with a person (le pago el café a mi amigo).

### 4 · B2 · 17 lines · Andalucía (Andalusia)
- Portrait, the template: the south of Spain, eight provinces (Almería,
  Cádiz, Córdoba, Granada, Huelva, Jaén, Málaga, Sevilla); Seville, the
  capital, on the Guadalquivir, and the Giralda, once the minaret of a
  mosque and now the cathedral's bell tower; the Mezquita of Córdoba, with a
  cathedral built inside it; the Alhambra of Granada, palace of the Nasrid
  kings, and 2 January 1492, when Granada surrendered to the Catholic
  Monarchs; the olive groves of Jaén, the Sierra Nevada with the highest
  peak of the Iberian Peninsula (Mulhacén), the marshes of Doñana; sherry
  from Jerez; gazpacho, salmorejo from Córdoba, ham from Jabugo; last line:
  pescaíto frito, the fried fish whose name has already lost its d
  (Foundation 94).
- Focus: descriptive written style: participle phrases (situada a orillas
  del Guadalquivir, construida sobre una mezquita); estar + participle for
  where a place is and what state it is in, ser + participle for what was
  done to it (fue conquistada en 1492); ser for where an event takes place
  (la feria es en abril, la boda es en Sevilla), the exception to "estar for
  place".

### 5 · B2 · 18 lines · Las doce uvas (The twelve grapes)
- Story: a man has never managed to eat the twelve grapes of New Year's Eve,
  one at each stroke of midnight from the clock of the Puerta del Sol. This
  year he trains for a week: seedless grapes, peeled, counted into a bowl;
  his family laughs. At midnight he eats all twelve, one per stroke, and
  raises his glass in triumph. Last line: his daughter tells him the channel
  he was watching was showing last year's broadcast; the real chimes had
  ended a minute before.
- Focus: the tenses of a story: the preterite for each event, the imperfect
  for the scene and for what was going on, the pluperfect for what had
  happened before (Foundation 53); verbs that change meaning in the
  preterite: supe (found out), conocí (met), quise (tried), no quise
  (refused), pude (managed); de repente, en ese momento, justo cuando, al +
  infinitive (Foundation 58).

### 6 · B2 · 18 lines · ¡Qué calor! (So hot!)
- Anecdotes: three short original anecdotes about Spanish weather, each with
  a twist: an English tourist who goes out for a walk in Seville at three in
  an August afternoon and finds the streets empty, the only other walker
  another Englishman; a woman in Madrid who believes the temperature on the
  green cross of the chemist's more than the forecast; a visitor in Burgos
  who arrives in May and is told the old Castilian saying about the
  climate, "nueve meses de invierno y tres de infierno", and asks which of
  the two he has come in. Last line: the answer, at a frosty breakfast
  before a scorching afternoon: "Hoy tiene usted suerte: los dos."
- Focus: nada más + infinitive (as soon as: nada más salir, empezó a
  sudar), en cuanto (Foundation 73), apenas; weather words: hace un calor
  de muerte, bochorno, chaparrón, chispea, refresca, la ola de calor; degree
  words: bastante (rather, quite: a false friend of "enough" too), algo,
  la mar de (familiar: la mar de contento), de lo más (de lo más agradable).

### 7 · revision
- Sections: 1 the words of conversation (table: ¡que no!, ni hablar,
  venga, anda, hombre, pues: effect, English, example from lesson 3); 2 the
  tenses of a story: preterite, imperfect, pluperfect (table with lesson 5)
  and the verbs that change meaning in the preterite (table); 3 a fronted
  object and its pronoun; the doubled indirect object (le pago el café a mi
  amigo); 4 ser and estar with places and events; participle phrases; 5 the
  echar family (list). Practice.

## Week 2

### 8 · B2 · 18 lines · El puntual (The punctual man)
- Character sketch: a man who has been on time all his life in a country
  where "a las nueve" means about nine (Foundation 68): he used to arrive at
  the cinema before it opened, at dinners while the host was still in the
  shower; it annoys him that people are late, it surprises him that nobody
  apologises. Last line: the one evening he arrives an hour late, everybody
  is already there, on time for once: it is his own surprise party.
- Focus: past habits: soler in the imperfect (solía llegar), the imperfect
  alone, acostumbrar a (formal); the subjunctive after feelings and
  judgements: le molesta que, le extraña que, no soporta que, le parece mal
  que + subjunctive, against the English "it annoys him that people are".

### 9 · B2 · 22 lines · Quedar (To meet, to stay, to fit)
- Word lesson: quedar con alguien (to meet up), quedar en (to agree to),
  ¿en qué quedamos?, quedar bien / mal, quedarse con (to keep), me queda
  grande / te queda bien (to fit, to suit), quedan dos días (be left),
  quedarse en blanco (Foundation 61), quedarse de piedra, quedarse tan
  ancho, no quedar más remedio, quedar por hacer, quedarse sin, quedarse con
  alguien (familiar, Spain: to pull someone's leg), quedarse dormido
  (Foundation 61). Told through a young couple who arrange the first meeting
  of their two families. Last line: everything agreed, the families arrive
  at nine; the couple do not: "se quedaron dormidos".
- Focus: quedar against quedarse (the se changes the meaning); quedar for
  meeting, which English says with "meet up" or "arrange"; the expression
  list.

### 10 · B2 · 18 lines · El puente (The long weekend)
- Satire: a country that plans its year around the calendar of holidays:
  in January everyone counts the puentes (a holiday on a Thursday makes the
  Friday a bridge, a longer one an acueducto); offices half empty, the
  roads full, a manager who forecasts the year's work by the number of
  bridges. Last line: the only employee who never takes a bridge goes in on
  the Friday and finds the office shut: the boss had made it an acueducto.
- Focus: probability and hedging: a lo mejor + indicative (a lo mejor
  hacemos puente: never the subjunctive), quizá(s) and tal vez with either
  mood, puede que + subjunctive (Foundation 54), igual (familiar, Spain:
  igual nos dan el viernes), lo más seguro es que + subjunctive, se prevé
  que.

### 11 · B2 · 17 lines · Galicia
- Portrait, the template: the north-west corner, four provinces (A Coruña,
  Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra); Santiago de Compostela, the capital, at the
  end of the Camino (Foundation 57–58: only a back reference); A Coruña and
  the Torre de Hércules, a Roman lighthouse still in use; Lugo inside its
  Roman walls; the rías, the rain and the green; Galician (galego),
  co-official, close to Portuguese; the saying about witches, told as a
  saying, "Eu non creo nas meigas, mais habelas, hainas" (I don't believe in
  witches, but they exist); seafood, pulpo á feira, empanada, Albariño wine
  from the Rías Baixas; last line: queso de tetilla, named after its shape.
- Focus: Galician words quoted and named, with their Spanish (rúa / calle,
  Xunta / Junta, A Coruña / La Coruña, Ourense / Orense); morriña,
  homesickness, a Galician word taken into Spanish; impersonal se and uno
  for general truths (aquí se come bien, uno nunca sabe), dicen que.

### 12 · B2 · 20 lines · Acogedor y luminoso (Cosy and bright)
- Dialogue: an estate agent shows a flat to a young couple: "acogedor"
  means tiny, "luminoso" means no curtains and a street lamp at the window,
  "para reformar" means everything must be redone, "céntrico" means the
  noise of the bars until three. To every flaw the agent answers not with
  "pero" but with "sino": "No es pequeño, sino práctico." They love it all
  the same. Last line: the agent: "Me alegro, porque es lo único que pueden
  pagar. Es un trastero."
- Focus: euphemism; sino against pero (English "but" does both: no es
  pequeño sino práctico; es pequeño pero práctico), sino que before a verb;
  correlative pairs: tanto ... como, no solo ... sino también, ni ... ni, o
  bien ... o bien, ya sea ... ya sea.

### 13 · B2 · 17 lines · A ver si quedamos (We must meet up some time)
- Reflection: the phrases that do not mean what they say: ¿qué tal? that
  wants no answer, "a ver si quedamos" said for years, "ya te llamaré", "en
  cinco minutos estoy" (twenty), "ahora voy" (in a while), "lo que tú
  digas" (I don't agree), "como quieras" (you will regret it). Last line:
  "A ver si quedamos un día", wrote an old friend to the author, ten years
  ago; the author is still waiting, and has just sent him the same message.
- Focus: pragmatics: what a phrase really means; a ver si + indicative (a
  ver si quedamos); the future for vague promises (ya te llamaré); the
  subjunctive of the open choice: lo que tú digas, como quieras, cuando
  puedas, donde prefieras (back to Foundation 85); ahora, ahora mismo, luego
  in Spain.

### 14 · revision
- Sections: 1 sino and pero; the correlative pairs (table); 2 probability
  and hedging (table: from sure to unlikely, with the mood each takes); 3
  past habits and the subjunctive of feelings (molestar, extrañar, parecer
  bien / mal + que); 4 phrases that mean something else, and the
  subjunctive of the open choice (lo que digas, como quieras); 5 the quedar
  family. Practice.

## Week 3

### 15 · B2 · 22 lines · Dar (To give)
- Word lesson: dar igual, no me da la gana, dar la lata, dar plantón, dar
  en el clavo, dar una vuelta, dar la vuelta, la ventana da al patio, dar
  con (to find), darse cuenta de, darse prisa, dar a luz, el reloj da las
  doce, dar pena / vergüenza / miedo, dárselas de (to pose as), dar guerra,
  dar calabazas. Told through a man waiting in a café for a first date who
  is late. Last line: the clock strikes ten, he has been stood up, and the
  waitress gives him her number.
- Focus: dar in idioms where English says "be", "find", "face" or nothing;
  darse cuenta de (realise: never realizar, Foundation 96); dar + a + place
  (look onto); the expression list.

### 16 · B2 · 18 lines · Velázquez
- Artist: Diego Velázquez (Seville 1599 – Madrid 1660): trained in Seville
  under Francisco Pacheco, who became his father-in-law; the kitchen scenes
  of his youth, one of them, an old woman cooking eggs (1618), now in
  Edinburgh, another, the water seller of Seville, in London (Apsley
  House); court painter to Philip IV from 1623 in Madrid; La rendición de
  Breda, also called Las lanzas; two journeys to Italy and the portrait of
  Pope Innocent X (1650) in Rome; the Rokeby Venus in the National Gallery,
  London; Las meninas (Foundation 64: only a back reference); a knight of
  the Order of Santiago in 1659. Last line: the red cross of Santiago on his
  chest in Las meninas was painted after he received the order, and,
  according to legend, by the king himself.
- Focus: reporting what is said: según + source, al parecer, por lo visto
  (familiar), se dice que, dicen que, se cuenta que; the conditional of
  rumour in the press (el cuadro habría sido retocado = reportedly), common
  in newspapers and frowned on by style guides.

### 17 · B2 · 18 lines · El del quinto (The man on the fifth floor)
- Anecdote: in a Madrid block of flats every sound travels through the
  patio de luces, the light well: a man hears his neighbours argue, a radio,
  a parrot, and every morning at seven somebody singing badly in the shower.
  At the residents' meeting he complains with the others; they all agree
  the singer must stop. Last line: an old lady explains it is the man on
  the fifth floor; he lives on the fifth floor.
- Focus: verbs of perception + infinitive (oigo a alguien cantar, la oí
  salir) and + gerund (la vi cruzando la calle: caught in the act); oír
  against escuchar (hear and listen); sin que + subjunctive (sin que nadie
  lo sepa).

### 18 · B2 · 18 lines · Cataluña (Catalonia)
- Portrait, the template: the north-east, four provinces (Barcelona,
  Girona, Lleida, Tarragona); two languages, Catalan and Spanish, both
  official; Barcelona, its capital: Gaudí's Sagrada Família, begun in 1882
  and built for well over a century since, the Park Güell, the Rambla, the Olympic Games of
  1992; the monastery of Montserrat on its mountain; the castells, human
  towers several storeys high; cava from the Penedès; pa amb tomàquet and
  crema catalana; last line: Sant Jordi, 23 April, when Catalans give each
  other a rose and a book.
- Focus: Catalan words quoted and named, with their Spanish (carrer /
  calle, plaça / plaza, bon dia, gràcies, adéu, pa amb tomàquet / pan con
  tomate, the Generalitat); place names official in Catalan (Girona,
  Lleida), still said Gerona and Lérida in Spanish; back to Foundation 88.

### 19 · B2 · 16 lines · El telediario (1) (The news, part 1)
- News: a news bulletin, invented and plausible: a new cycle lane in a
  city, a train strike announced for Monday, a report on rents, an Iberian
  lynx filmed near a motorway in Andalusia. No real events or people.
- Focus: word order of news: the verb before the subject for new
  information (Llegan las lluvias, Ha subido el alquiler), against English;
  passive se in reports (se han registrado, se espera); reporting verbs:
  anunciar, informar de que, confirmar, desmentir, asegurar, declarar,
  advertir (to warn: a false friend of "advertise").

### 20 · B2 · 16 lines · El telediario (2) (The news, part 2)
- News: sport (a match decided in stoppage time, en el descuento), a short
  culture item, and the weather (a borrasca from the Atlantic, chubascos,
  the snow line, la cota de nieve). Last line: the light story at the end:
  in a village of Teruel the carnival has lasted three days because nobody
  could find the key of the town hall to close it.
- Focus: headline grammar: no article, the present for the past, the
  participle alone (Detenido un hombre en ..., Muere ..., Aprobada la ley
  de ...); sport idioms in daily life: meter un gol a alguien (to put one
  over on someone), estar en fuera de juego, tirar la toalla, en la recta
  final, salvado por la campana.

### 21 · revision
- Sections: 1 reporting what is said (table: según, al parecer, por lo
  visto, se dice que, the conditional of rumour); 2 verbs of perception +
  infinitive or gerund; oír and escuchar; 3 word order: the verb first for
  new information; headline grammar (table); 4 Catalan and Galician words
  met (table: word, language, Spanish, English); 5 the dar family.
  Practice.

## Week 4

### 22 · B2 · 20 lines · Hablemos de condiciones (Let's talk terms)
- Dialogue: a job candidate negotiates with a polite head of personnel:
  salary, holidays, Fridays at home, a parking space; every answer a
  condition: "siempre que", "a no ser que", "con tal de que", "como no me lo
  suban, me voy". He gets it all. Last line: the head of personnel: "Muy
  bien. Empieza usted mañana, en mi puesto: con lo que sabe negociar, yo me
  jubilo."
- Focus: conditions beyond Foundation 71 and 76: the mixed type (si hubiera
  estudiado, ahora tendría ...), de + infinitive (de haberlo sabido = if I
  had known), como + subjunctive (a warning: como no vengas, me enfado),
  siempre que / siempre y cuando, con tal de que, a no ser que, salvo que,
  en caso de que + subjunctive.

### 23 · B2 · 18 lines · Goya
- Artist: Francisco de Goya (Fuendetodos, Aragón, 1746 – Bordeaux, 1828):
  cartoons for the royal tapestry factory, scenes of fairs and picnics;
  painter to the court, La familia de Carlos IV; the two majas, dressed and
  naked, in the Prado; deaf after an illness in 1792–1793; Los caprichos
  (1799); El dos de mayo and El tres de mayo de 1808, painted in 1814; Los
  desastres de la guerra; the Black Paintings on the walls of his house
  near Madrid, the Quinta del Sordo, now in the Prado (Saturno); his last
  years in exile in Bordeaux. Last line: the title of his most famous print,
  "El sueño de la razón produce monstruos", can be read two ways, because
  sueño is both sleep and dream.
- Focus: describing art: óleo, lienzo, grabado, pincelada, claroscuro;
  colour adjectives in -izo, -áceo, -oso (rojizo, grisáceo, verdoso); the
  passive with ser (fue pintado en 1814) against estar + participle (está
  pintado sobre la pared); sueño and the two senses.

### 24 · B2 · 18 lines · Euskadi (The Basque Country)
- Portrait, the template: three provinces (Álava, Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa; in
  Basque Araba, Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa); Vitoria-Gasteiz, seat of the Basque
  government; Bilbao and its Guggenheim Museum by Frank Gehry (1997);
  San Sebastián, Donostia in Basque, and the bay of La Concha; Gernika, its
  oak, symbol of Basque liberties, and the bombing of 26 April 1937 by
  German and Italian planes, painted by Picasso (Guernica, in the Reina
  Sofía in Madrid); euskera, a language related to no other known language;
  green hills, the sea, fishing ports; txakoli wine, bacalao al pil-pil,
  Idiazabal cheese; last line: the gilda, an olive, an anchovy and a green
  chilli on a stick, named, so it is said, after Rita Hayworth's Gilda.
- Focus: Basque words quoted and named (kaixo, agur, eskerrik asko; tx and
  k in Basque spelling); official double names (Vitoria-Gasteiz,
  Donostia-San Sebastián); relatives after a preposition, which English
  leaves at the end (the city I live in → la ciudad en la que vivo; el
  árbol bajo el cual, la persona con quien).

### 25 · B2 · 22 lines · Poner (To put)
- Word lesson: poner la mesa, poner la tele, ponerse el abrigo, ponerse
  nervioso (Foundation 61), ponerse a + infinitive, poner de los nervios,

  corazón que no siente; En casa del herrero, cuchillo de palo; Camarón que
  se duerme se lo lleva la corriente; A buen entendedor, pocas palabras
  bastan. Sancho Panza, who speaks in strings of proverbs, in a sentence.
  Last line: the granddaughter answers with one of her own invention.
- Focus: proverbs: the literal and the real sense, and the English match
  (a bird in the hand; every cloud has a silver lining; don't look a gift
  horse in the mouth; the cobbler's children; out of sight, out of mind);
  the subjunctive and old forms kept in them; the proverb said by half (ya
  sabes: a buen entendedor ...).

### 58 · C1 · 18 lines · El corrector (The know-it-all)
- Character sketch: a man who corrects everyone's Spanish, aloud and by
  message: "habían muchas personas" (había), "pienso de que" (pienso que),
  "me alegro que" (me alegro de que), "haber si vienes" (a ver), porque and
  por qué, the accent on tú, él, sí, más. His family has had enough. Last
  line: the cake for his sixtieth birthday reads "Felicidades. Haber si te
  jubilas", and the family wrote it so on purpose.
- Focus: the norm and common usage: impersonal haber always singular (había
  muchas personas, hubo problemas); dequeísmo and queísmo, and the test of
  replacing the clause with eso (pienso eso → pienso que; me alegro de eso →
  me alegro de que); a ver and haber; porque, por qué, el porqué, por que;
  the diacritic accent (tú / tu, él / el, sí / si, más / mas, sé / se, té /
  te) and the written accent that decides the meaning (término, termino,
  terminó).

### 59 · C1 · 17 lines · Antonio Machado
- Figure: Antonio Machado (Seville 1875 – Collioure, France, 1939), brother
  of the poet Manuel Machado; a teacher of French in Soria from 1907, where
  he married Leonor Izquierdo, who died in 1912; Campos de Castilla (1912),
  the Duero and the land of Soria; then Baeza and Segovia; the lines from
  his Proverbios y cantares, "se hace camino al andar"; at the end of the
  Civil War he crossed into France with his mother and died in Collioure in
  February 1939. Plain last line: in his coat pocket a last line was found,
  "Estos días azules y este sol de la infancia".
- Focus: connectors of argument and their place: sin embargo, no obstante,
  en cambio, por consiguiente, es decir, así pues; pues meaning "since, for"
  in written style, never first in the sentence (no vino, pues estaba
  enfermo); the commas around them.

### 60 · C1 · 22 lines · Cara (Face)
- Word lesson: cara a cara, dar la cara, echar en cara (lesson 1), tener
  cara / ser un caradura, ¡qué cara más dura!, plantar cara, poner buena /
  mala cara, tener cara de pocos amigos, cara de póquer, de cara a (with a
  view to), por su cara bonita (for nothing, just like that), verse las
  caras, caérsele a uno la cara de vergüenza, lavar la cara a algo (a quick
  facelift), cara o cruz (heads or tails), costar un ojo de la cara only as
  a back reference (Foundation 87). Told through a family that decides
  where to spend the summer by tossing a coin. Last line: the coin lands on
  its edge (de canto).
- Focus: cara and the body in idioms; cara (face) and the adjective cara
  (expensive), and the pun of "¡qué cara!"; the expression list.

### 61 · C1 · 20 lines · En el banco del parque (On the park bench)
- Dialogue: two elderly neighbours on a park bench comment on everyone who
  passes, in short answers: "Ya está otra vez", "Acaba de salir", "No deja
  de mirarla", "Anda diciendo que ...", "Viene a costar lo mismo", "Eso
  espero", "Ya lo sabía", "A mí tampoco". Last line: "No me gusta nada ese
  señor del sombrero." "A mí tampoco." "Es mi marido." "Por eso."
- Focus: verbal periphrases: volver a + infinitive (again, where English
  adds "again"), acabar de (just), dejar de (stop), andar + gerund
  (familiar: going around doing), venir a + infinitive (about, roughly),
  tener + participle (tengo hecha la cena); short answers and ellipsis: creo
  que sí / que no, eso espero, ya lo sabía, lo es / lo está (lo standing for
  an adjective, as English uses "so"); también and tampoco, a mí también /
  a mí tampoco with gustar-type verbs.

### 62 · C1 · 20 lines · Como el perro y el gato (Like cat and dog)
- Idioms: a teacher describes her colleagues at a staff meeting in animal
  idioms: está como una cabra, es un gallina, es la oveja negra, es un lince,
  está como pez en el agua, es un pez gordo, se llevan como el perro y el
  gato, le busca tres pies al gato, tiene la mosca detrás de la oreja,
  siempre paga el pato, hace el ganso, mata dos pájaros de un tiro, nos ha
  dado gato por liebre, coge el toro por los cuernos; at the meeting there
  were four people: cuatro gatos. Last line: the colleagues describe her
  with one as well.
- Focus: animal idioms and their English counterparts, often with another
  animal (dar gato por liebre against buy a pig in a poke; como el perro y
  el gato against like cat and dog; matar dos pájaros de un tiro, the same
  birds); register (friendly, sharp, rude).

## Week 10

### 64 · C1 · 18 lines · Madrid
- Portrait, the template: in the centre of the peninsula, the capital since
  Philip II settled the court there in 1561; the Puerta del Sol and the
  plaque of kilometre zero; the bear and the strawberry tree, el oso y el
  madroño, symbol of the city; the Prado, the Reina Sofía and the
  Thyssen-Bornemisza within a short walk of one another; the Retiro park;
  the Rastro market on Sunday mornings; San Isidro, the patron saint, 15 May,
  with chulapos and the chotis; cocido madrileño and the bocadillo de
  calamares; madrileños nicknamed gatos (the legend of a soldier who climbed
  the city wall like a cat, told as a legend); last line: "De Madrid al
  cielo".
- Focus: contrast: mientras que (whereas), en cambio, a diferencia de, por
  el contrario, no ... sino (lesson 12); leísmo of central Spain: le vi
  (a man) accepted for lo vi, while la dije for le dije is heard in Madrid
  and is not standard; the Madrid and Barcelona rivalry in the notes.

### 65 · C1 · 18 lines · La casa de los ruidos (The house of noises)
- Story: an old family house in a village is put up for sale after the
  grandmother's death; at every visit the buyers hear footsteps, a door
  that opens by itself, a pipe that sighs, the lights that go off; they
  leave in a hurry; the grandfather, who lives in the house and never wanted
  to sell it, says nothing. Last line: the day the family takes the house
  off the market, the noises stop, and the grandfather sleeps very well.
- Focus: se in all its uses: reflexive (se levanta), reciprocal (se miran),
  se for le before lo (se lo dijo), involuntary (se le apagó la luz,
  Foundation 57, 78), impersonal (se oye), passive (se vende), and the se of
  completion or intensity (irse, comerse la tarta entera, morirse de
  miedo); back to Foundation 59.

### 66 · C1 · 18 lines · Agosto (August)
- Satire: the Spanish August: "cerrado por vacaciones" on every shop, the
  towel on the beach at eight in the morning to keep a place, the
  chiringuito, the fiestas of the home village, the fight over the air
  conditioning, the whole country on holiday at once. Last line: in
  September everyone suffers from the "síndrome postvacacional" and needs a
  holiday to recover from the holiday.
- Focus: irony and understatement: no está mal, ¡menudo calor!, ¡vaya
  día!, ¡qué bien! said through the teeth; wishes: ojalá + imperfect
  subjunctive (Foundation 72), ¡quién + imperfect subjunctive! (¡quién
  pudiera! = if only I could), ¡si al menos + imperfect subjunctive!

### 67 · C1 · 18 lines · Pedro Almodóvar
- Artist: Pedro Almodóvar (born in 1949 in Calzada de Calatrava, in the
  province of Ciudad Real, La Mancha): Madrid as a young man, a job at the
  telephone company and short films at night; the Movida madrileña of the
  1980s; Mujeres al borde de un ataque de nervios (1988); Todo sobre mi
  madre (1999), Oscar for best foreign-language film; Hable con ella (2002),
  Oscar for best original screenplay; Volver (2006), filmed partly in La
  Mancha; strong colours, women at the centre, mothers. Plain, warm ending:
  a man from La Mancha who made Madrid his set. Only these facts; nothing
  about his private life.
- Focus: the position of the adjective changes its meaning: un gran
  director / un director grande, un pobre hombre / un hombre pobre, un viejo
  amigo / un amigo viejo, el antiguo cine / el cine antiguo, la misma
  actriz / la actriz misma, un único papel / un papel único; the language
  of film (rodar, el rodaje, la película, el guion, la banda sonora, en
  versión original).

### 68 · C1 · 20 lines · El examen oral (The oral exam)
- Dialogue: an English candidate at the oral of the DELE C1, the Instituto
  Cervantes exam, who has learnt every connector: to the examiner's first,
  very short question he answers with a full argument: en primer lugar, por
  un lado ... por otro, dado que, puesto que, ya que, en el supuesto de que,
  suponiendo que, en definitiva, a fin de cuentas. Last line: the examiner:
  "Muy bien. Pero solo le había preguntado si quería un vaso de agua."
- Focus: cause: porque, ya que, puesto que, dado que, and como, which must
  open the sentence (como llovía, nos quedamos); assumptions: suponiendo
  que, en el supuesto de que + subjunctive; the connectors that order an
  answer.

### 69 · C1 · 18 lines · Querido lector (Dear reader)
