# Dutch for Italian speakers, Mastery: what every week must follow

Read first, in this order:
1. work/BRIEF.md: the sections "The learner", "Mastery" and "Checks" (where
   it says English, read Dutch; mastery in place of foundation).
2. METHOD.md section 3, content/FORMAT.md (`tasks:`, `task.it:`,
   `rubric.it:`, `refs:`).
3. The plan: work/nl/it/mastery.md (rules at the top, then your week's table).
4. The model of a Mastery lesson with Italian notes, same format:
   work/en/mastery/001.txt and 007.txt (a revision), for the layout, the
   tasks and the rubrics only. Do not read the other English lessons, and
   never transpose an English text, joke or image (queues, "Mustn't
   grumble", British weather): the plan gives a Dutch subject, and every
   scene, joke and image comes from Dutch or Flemish life. Their notes are
   longer than yours may be (see the note limit below).
5. The approved lessons of this course written so far
   (work/nl/it/mastery/), all of the previous week. Before Mastery 8 exists,
   read the last approved week of Dutch Perfectionnement
   (work/nl/it/perfectionnement/, lessons 64–70 when they exist) for the
   Dutch voice, the Italian and the tone of the jokes.
6. Foundation and Perfectionnement are known to the learner:
   work/nl/it/foundation/*.txt and work/nl/it/perfectionnement/*.txt, with
   their plans work/nl/it/foundation.md and work/nl/it/perfectionnement.md,
   which name the lesson that explains each grammar point. Never teach again
   what they taught: point back to it and go further.

Files: work/nl/it/mastery/NNN.txt, one lesson each, starting:

    course: nl
    stage: mastery
    bases: it

    == N C1      (C2 from lesson 26)
    kind: revision    (only on revision lessons)
    numeral: Eerste les
    title: ...
    title.it: ...

`numeral:` the Dutch ordinal + les, exactly as Foundation's lesson of the
same number writes it (Eerste les, Eenentwintigste les, Tweeëntwintigste les
... Honderdste les): copy it from work/nl/it/foundation/NNN.txt. Mastery has
no figured pronunciation: no `numeral.pron.it:`, no `pron.it:` lines, no
`sounds.it:`, no culture section. Revision lessons: title "Herhaling",
title.it "Ripasso".

## Shape (BRIEF "Mastery" is the authority)

- `dialogue:` one item per numbered sentence of the text: 25–40 items,
  350–700 words, of the kind and with the ending the plan gives. Each item
  has `it:` with a free, natural Italian translation; for an idiom, the
  Italian meaning first, then the literal sense in brackets, only where it
  helps: «è al verde (siede su seme nero)».
- `notes.it:` 10–18 notes, in the order of the text, on register and its
  shifts, what is meant but not said, irony, cultural and historical
  references, idioms and collocations, the precise word against its
  near-synonyms, rhetoric; contrasts with Italian. Each note starts on its
  own line ("1. ...") with the words of the text, exactly as the dialogue
  has them. A note is at most about 120 words; a note that needs more is two
  notes. Do not re-tell the plot in the last note: say what the ending does,
  briefly.
- `ex1:` five demanding sentences or short passages to translate, where the
  nuance matters, each with `it:`; new sentences, never copies of text
  lines. From lesson 50 the learner rebuilds them from the Italian, so the
  Italian must point to one natural Dutch sentence.
- `ex2:` five collocations, idioms or connectors in context, one or two
  words per [blank], with `it:` the plain Italian sentence (never a hint, a
  gloss or a riddle such as "quella parola", "nella forma più formale").
  Each blank has only one right answer given the Italian. Never blank a word
  the Italian cannot tell from a near-synonym: je / jij, hoewel / ofschoon,
  omdat / doordat (unless the Italian makes the cause unmistakable), maar /
  echter, dus / daarom, toch / desondanks, waarschijnlijk / vermoedelijk,
  als / wanneer, overlijden / sterven.
- `tasks:` the plan's one or two tasks. Each item: the model answer as the
  item's text (Dutch, of the length the task asks), `task.it:` the
  instruction in Italian, `rubric.it:` four or five self-check points
  separated by `;` (meaning; register; structure; idiom; effect, adapted).
- Revision lessons (7, 14 ... 98): `kind: revision`; `dialogue:` the revision
  text, 20–30 items, each with `refs:` to the week's lessons its words or
  devices come from; `notes.it:` a few lines, then four to six numbered
  sections that put the week's focus in order, with tables (`| ... |` rows)
  and examples quoted from the week's texts; no ex1/ex2; one task.
- `aside.it:` only where the plan says (lesson 49).
- Free text is never hard-wrapped: each note, each line of a revision
  introduction and the aside are one line each; only table rows take one
  line per row.

## Rules

- Everything original. Do not open the reference course books (kept outside
  the repository). Literary pieces are "in the manner of" an era or genre,
  never imitating a living author's text; quote only titles or a few words
  of public-domain works; modern authors by name and title only. No premise
  or structure of a famous sketch, song, poem or book. Invented books,
  studies, papers, clubs and companies use invented names, never a real
  publisher, newspaper or institution, and never sit inside a real one.
- Real places, people, history: only well-established facts; when unsure,
  leave it out. Legends are told as legends ("secondo la leggenda"). Facts in
  the plan were checked: keep them. If you change or drop one, say so in the
  report with the reason; never replace a true fact with a guess, and never
  add a date, number or name you are not sure of. Things that may have
  changed recently (a law, an institution's name, where something is kept)
  are written so they stay true: "van oudsher", "traditioneel", or left out.
  Use the current name of a museum or institution. History and status facts
  need their exact stages.
- Legal and official documents fit Dutch practice; if unsure of a rule, keep
  the document generic and lawful (an association's own rules, not a law).
  A figure is right in its order of magnitude or left out.
- Standard Netherlands Dutch as people say and write it today, spelled by
  the Groene Boekje (pannenkoek, zo'n, ideeën, geüpdatet), of the register
  the plan asks for. Natural Dutch, nothing a Dutch speaker would find odd.
  Netherlands words in the text (patat or friet, slager, mobiel, leuk, zin),
  never Belgian ones. Every Dutch sentence must make sense in context as a
  native reader would read it; linkers must be logical ("maar" joins two
  things that really contrast).
- Flemish only in the lessons the plan sets in Flanders or about it (50,
  55, 56, 69 and notes that name Belgium): the narrator writes standard
  Dutch unless the plan says a character speaks tussentaal; each Flemish word
  is named as Flemish in the note ("in Belgio / nelle Fiandre si dice ...")
  with its Netherlands equivalent. A word of one city or region is presented
  as that, not as "the Belgian word". Never present a Flemish word or form
  as standard. The same for Brabant, Frisian and straattaal words: real ones
  only; if unsure of a dialect feature, leave it out.
- Address in Dutch: u to strangers, customers, older people, officials; je
  / jij among friends, family, colleagues; when the plan is about the switch,
  the switch is the point and must be visible. The Italian matches (lei /
  tu), one form per speaker through the lesson, also in ex1, ex2 and tasks;
  polite lei, le, la in lowercase; family members use tu with each other.
- The Dutch lines never contain Italian or English words, not even in quotes
  ("een kaartje is een biglietto" is wrong): explain in Dutch inside the
  text, and put the Italian only in the `it:` line and the notes. Loanwords
  that are standard Dutch (pizza, cappuccino, espresso, saldo, deadline) are
  Dutch words. Quoted Frisian or Flemish is allowed where the lesson is about
  it.
- Italian: natural, tu to the learner ("ricorda", "nota", "fai attenzione"),
  never lei or voi to the learner. No English words in Italian text (no
  "hedging", "timing", "understatement", "punchline": write attenuazione,
  tempi comici, litote or eufemismo, battuta finale). Italian prose stays
  Italian: quote a Dutch word as an example, never use it as an Italian word
  ("ha preso la bici", not "ha preso la fiets"); notes never contain a Dutch
  sentence that explains ("leggi het hardop" is wrong). Italian renders the
  Dutch exactly, without additions and without calques.
- The country is "i Paesi Bassi" (plural: "i Paesi Bassi sono"); "Olanda"
  only for the two provinces of Holland, when the text means them. The
  language and the people stay "l'olandese", "gli olandesi". Flanders is "le
  Fiandre", its speech "fiammingo". In translations use the Italian names of
  places where they exist (L'Aia, Groninga, Zelanda, Fiandre, Anversa, Gand,
  Lovanio, Bruxelles).
- Italian grammar: the congiuntivo where Italian wants it (penso che sia,
  sebbene fosse, prima che arrivi, affinché tu possa, sembra che abbia, è
  possibile che venga); se + congiuntivo imperfetto + condizionale (se
  avessi tempo, verrei), never "se avrei". Gender and number agree with the
  Italian word, never with the Dutch one: het meisje is "la ragazza ... lei",
  het huis "la casa ... la"; a het-word is not masculine or neuter in Italian.
  "ed è", "uno sfogo", "uno scostamento".
- In Italian text the earlier courses are "Fondamenti" and "Perfezionamento",
  never "Foundation" or "Perfectionnement"; this course is "Padronanza".
- References: "(lezione N)" or "(lezione N, nota M)" is this course;
  "(Perfezionamento, lezione N, nota M)" and "(Fondamenti, lezione N)" name
  the earlier courses; one course per bracket, references in brackets only
  ("(Fondamenti, lezione 3)", never "dai Fondamenti (lezione 3)"). N is the
  lesson whose DIALOGUE first has that word, found with
  `python3 -I work/tools/first_seen.py work/nl/it/foundation 'regex'` (or
  .../perfectionnement, .../mastery); for a grammar point, the lesson whose
  note explains it (the plans name it): open the lesson and check the note
  says it. Every "frase N", "nota N" and "lezione N, nota M" must point at
  the right sentence or note: open it and count. If no earlier note says it,
  give no reference. Every reference fits the course order.
- No recurring characters: a name used in an earlier lesson is another
  person; never write "lo conosci già (lezione N)" about a character. One
  name, one person, and a surname belongs to one family, within a week.
- Dialogues hold together line by line: each line answers the one before
  it, nobody asks what they have just said or been told, nobody repeats a
  question already answered, and a speaker knows only what their role knows.
  Read the dialogue aloud as two people before you write the notes. One
  speaker per line; speakers alternate unless a line says otherwise.
- A story keeps its own facts straight from line to line: the same person
  does the same thing; who pays whom, who knows what, what is already true at
  the start (a phone "on silent" cannot ring); places, days, times, clocks,
  amounts and means of transport stay put and add up. Any count the text
  states (eight opinions, three stanzas) is true: count before you write it.
  The twist must be plausible and follow from the whole text, the closing
  line pays off the premise, and the note explains the joke the text really
  tells.
- Tenses: a history text keeps the past (imperfect) for past events and the
  present only for what is still true today; no slip into the historical
  present halfway. A story told in the present (lesson 22) stays in it.
  People of another century stay in their century, with nothing anachronistic
  inside a period piece; modern people may quote them.
- Idioms and fixed phrases: only ones you are sure exist in standard Dutch,
  in their real shape (blijven zitten, not "zitten blijven"; "Wat is hier
  aan de hand?", not the invented "Wat loopt hier los?"), used in their real
  sense; explain the real sense first (op zwart zaad zitten = essere al
  verde), then the literal image, and give a word's literal origin only if
  it is right (a spuigat is a ship's scupper). Reflexive idioms keep their
  pronoun (wat haal je je in je hoofd?). If the scene does not fit the sense,
  change the scene; if you are not sure a phrase is said that way, write a
  plainer sentence. An invented blend (lesson 61) is made of two real idioms,
  both named in the note.
- Grammar and usage claims exact and true in general: no "sempre", "mai"
  where usage varies; say what is usual and what is also heard; if a rule
  has exceptions or you are not sure, describe only the example in the text.
  Keep the truths Foundation taught (verb second and verb last; the -t that
  drops after inverted je/jij; the adjective -e; zijn in the perfect for a
  change of place or state; 't kofschip; separable and inseparable
  prefixes). Check irregular forms you are not sure of (plegen → placht).
  wiens for masculine and het-words, wier for feminine words and plurals,
  both written language. "had niet hoeven + infinito" is "non c'era bisogno
  di", "had niet moeten" is "non avresti dovuto". Grammar labels are exact.
- Irony and implicature: say what is said, what is meant, and how the reader
  knows, only where implicature is the point of the note, not in every note.
  No disclaimers of the kind "il testo non dice che ..." unless a reader
  would really misread the sentence.
- Verse (lessons 59, 64–70 and any poem in a task): every line a full Dutch
  sentence or a clean fragment, no missing verb; rhymes really rhyme
  (a slant rhyme or an assonance is named as such, and is one); counts of
  stanzas, lines and feet match the text; stress claims follow the Dutch
  stress of the words. Facts about real poems are exact.
- Stress or a sound in a note only where it is the point. For a stress pair
  write the Dutch way, with accents on the spelling (vóórkomen / voorkómen).
  When the sound itself is the point (lessons 51, 54), never figure it by
  hand: copy the word's figure from a Foundation pron line, or run
  `python3 -I work/tools/figure_nl.py --say "word"` and copy its output with
  the key's signs exactly (content/nl/pronunciation.md); a one-syllable word
  never carries asterisks.
- A note explains only what is in its own sentence, and never claims
  something about another text without rereading it. When a note says who
  does what in the text, check it against the text. A table's columns hold
  what their headers say: check every row.
- Exercise sentences are small scenes, logical on their own, natural and
  self-contained; they restate the lesson's facts exactly (the same person
  does the same thing; a claim about a group holds for every member).
- A revision text introduces nothing the week did not have, reuses the
  week's people with their own names and places, never gives a character
  someone else's act, and repeats what happened exactly.
- Notes never mention the plan, the brief, the writer, copyright or
  originality ("il gag del piano", "testo originale", "non citiamo ...",
  "esempi inventati"), and make no remarks about the lesson itself or about
  writing it: the learner never sees the plan. Write "la battuta", "il
  finale". Never copy brief instructions into lesson text. No working notes
  to yourself in a lesson file ("(Note: ...)", "verificare: ...", "fix").
- Model answers in tasks are good, natural Dutch of the asked length and
  register, not a copy of the text; they do what their task asks and meet
  their own rubric (if the rubric asks for u, the model has u), and add no
  facts the rubric forbids.

## Checks before you finish

  with no problem and no `gap:` line, for each file.
- `python3 -I work/tools/extra_checks.py work/nl/it/mastery/*.txt` reports
  no repeated title (the revision lessons' "Herhaling" excepted) and no
  dialogue line repeated in another lesson.
- Count items and words of each text against the plan's ranges, and every
  count the text or a note states.
- Every "(Fondamenti, lezione N)", "(Perfezionamento, lezione N)" and
  "(lezione N)" verified with first_seen.py or by opening the note; every
  "frase N" and "nota N" counted.
- Search your files for any English, any Italian inside a Dutch line and any
  comment to yourself, and remove it.
- Reread every lesson once against this brief: the text coherent line by
  line, the ending and the joke work and the notes explain them correctly,
  natural Netherlands Dutch, natural Italian (congiuntivo, agreement, "i
  Paesi Bassi"), one answer per blank, no Flemish word unlabelled, no
  doubtful fact, notes not hard-wrapped.
- Touch nothing but your own lesson files. No git, no cargo. The commands
  you may run besides reading files and grep: the checker, extra_checks.py,
  first_seen.py and figure_nl.py --say above.

Report briefly: per lesson items, words, notes, tasks; checker output; facts
you were unsure of and what you did with them; any choice the plan did not
settle; anything in the plan you think is wrong (quote it, do not change it).
