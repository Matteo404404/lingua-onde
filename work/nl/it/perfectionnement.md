# Dutch for Italian speakers · Perfectionnement plan (70 lessons)

For learners who have finished Foundation (B2 at the start, C1 at the end).
Standard Netherlands Dutch (the words, forms and spelling of the
Netherlands, the Groene Boekje); Flemish only in the Flemish portraits and in
notes that name Belgium. Everything else in Italian (tu to the learner).
Levels: B2 1–45, C1 46–70 (as in content/FORMAT.md and the English and
Vietnamese Perfectionnement). Read `work/PATTERNS.md` (section
"Perfectionnement") and `work/BRIEF.md` (section "Perfectionnement") first,
then the writer brief `work/nl/it/perf-writer.md`.

How the course works (from Assimil's Perfectionnement):
- Each lesson is a text complete in itself; the kind changes from day to
  day. No growth in length: 14–25 numbered sentences or lines, 150–400
  words. No continuing story, no recurring cast: a name belongs to one
  lesson.
- Humour is the second aim: wordplay on the lesson's keyword, satire, an
  absurd last line that undoes the premise. Informative texts and the
  serious figures (Anne Frank) may end plainly.
- A "word" lesson about every eight lessons: one verb or noun and its idioms
  (zitten, houden, lopen, zetten, halen, hand, staan, trekken, water);
  nearly every note is an idiom (15–30 notes) and the notes end with the
  expression list (each idiom with its sentence and note number).
- A portrait of a place of the Dutch-speaking world about every seven
  lessons, on one template: where and what it is like, its towns, a remark
  of history, the landscape, what is made there, food and drink, a last line
  on a speciality.
- Artists and figures of the past: only well-established facts; legends told
  as legends ("si racconta", "secondo la leggenda"); quote only titles or a
  few words of public-domain works.
- Notes 8–15 (more in word lessons): word families and senses, idioms with
  their literal sense first, grammar the learner "knows but uses badly",
  register, false friends; "ricorda" back references to Foundation
  ("Fondamenti" in the Italian) and to earlier lessons of this course.
- Grammar is never basic again. Foundation explained de/het, verb second and
  verb last, separable verbs, the perfect and imperfect, the past perfect,
  modals and the double infinitive, er (all its uses, 98), the passive, zou
  and the unreal past, indirect speech, te + infinitive, laten,
  schijnen/blijken, the particles (72), the diminutive forms (47), compounds
  with -s-/-en- (91), formal letters (89). This course takes those points
  deeper and adds what Foundation left out (see each Focus).
- Exercises: 5 + 5 (translate; fill in), sentences of 5–20 words.
- No figured pronunciation and no culture section: culture lives in the
  texts and the notes. Stress is noted only where it is irregular or decides
  the meaning (voorkómen / vóórkomen), with the key's signs
  (content/nl/pronunciation.md).
- Revision lessons (7, 14 ... 70) have no text: numbered sections on finer
  points, with tables and examples from the week, then a short practice of
  five + five items (in ex1 and ex2).
- From lesson 50 the active wave brings back lesson n − 49.
- Foundation's active wave goes on alongside: for a learner who finished
  Foundation, lessons 1–49 each bring back one of Foundation 52–100 (the app
  does it). Lesson 2 says so in a sentence, and says that a learner who
  starts here directly simply begins the course's own wave at lesson 50.

## Lessons

## Week 1

### 1 · B2 · word · Zitten
- A humorous reflection on zitten, the verb the Dutch use where Italian says
  "essere", "trovarsi" or nothing at all: blijven zitten (repeat a school
  year: een zittenblijver), ergens mee zitten / daar zit ik niet mee, in de
  put zitten, op zwart zaad zitten, met de handen in het haar zitten, op
  hete kolen zitten, het zit wel goed, hoe zit dat?, daar zit wat in,
  ernaast zitten, dat zit er niet in, het zit hem in ..., zit niet zo te
  zeuren. Told through one man, Kees, over one bad week. Last line: the
  author closes the lesson with "Het zit erop" (that's it, we're done).
- Focus: zitten for where things are (de sleutels zitten in mijn jas),
  against liggen and staan; zitten te + infinitive (Foundation 62) in
  idioms; the expression list.

### 2 · B2 · introduction · Wat je te wachten staat
- The course presents itself: a tour of the Dutch-speaking world in texts of
  every kind, from Zeeland to Suriname, from a joke to a letter to the town
  hall. Foundation's wave goes on (one sentence); a learner who starts here
  begins this course's wave at lesson 50. Ends with a wink: at the end of
  every lesson there is no bill, and nobody will send you a Tikkie
  (tomorrow's lesson).
- Focus: the future perfect: zal hebben + participle (formal) and, more
  usual, the present perfect with a time phrase (volgende week heb je
  Zeeland gezien); zodat against om ... te (purpose with a different subject
  or the same one).

### 3 · B2 · dialogue · Een Tikkie
- Two friends after a dinner out: who paid, who owes what, splitting to the
  cent, "samsam doen", the payment request sent by app (a Tikkie, from the
  bank ABN AMRO's app; "een Tikkie sturen" is everyday Dutch). Last line:
  the friend who hosted the coffee afterwards sends a Tikkie for the coffee,
  and the other sends one back for the birthday present.
- Focus: modal particles in combination, beyond Foundation 72: maar even
  (stuur maar even), toch wel, nou eenmaal (dat is nou eenmaal zo), dan in
  questions (hoeveel krijg je dan?), ook alweer (hoe heette dat ook
  alweer?), joh; money verbs: betalen, terugbetalen, voorschieten, het
  verschil bijleggen.

### 4 · B2 · portrait · Zeeland
- The template: the south-west, islands and peninsulas between the Scheldt
  estuaries (Walcheren, Schouwen-Duiveland, Zeeuws-Vlaanderen against the
  Belgian border); Middelburg, the capital, and Vlissingen, where Michiel de
  Ruyter was born (1607); the motto "Luctor et emergo"; dikes, beaches, the
  storm-surge barriers (Foundation 88: the flood of 1953 and the Delta
  Works); New Zealand named after the province; mussels and oysters from
  Yerseke; last line: the Zeeuwse bolus with coffee.
- Focus: wiens and wier (formal "il cui, la cui": de stad wier haven ...);
  participle phrases in written style (omringd door water, gelegen aan de
  Schelde); de or het of place names.

### 5 · B2 · story · De stiltecoupé
- A man in the silent carriage of a Dutch train (the stiltecoupé, marked
  "stilte"): his phone rings with a loud tune; he cannot find it; the whole
  carriage glares; he finally whispers "Ik zit in de stiltecoupé." The voice
  answers: "Ik weet het. Ik zit twee rijen achter je": his wife.
- Focus: tenses in a story: the perfect to open and to comment (gisteren is
  er iets geks gebeurd), the imperfect to tell; toen as adverb ("toen ging
  de telefoon": verb second) against toen as conjunction ("toen de telefoon
  ging": verb last; Foundation 46); net, opeens, plotseling.

### 6 · B2 · anecdotes · Wisselvallig
- Three short original anecdotes about Dutch weather, each with a twist: a
  man who goes to the beach the moment the sun comes out and arrives with
  the rain; a woman who trusts the rain radar app and waits for the ten dry
  minutes, which come at night; a tourist who asks when summer starts and is
  told it was last Tuesday.
- Focus: nog niet ... of and nauwelijks ... of (no sooner ... than: hij was
  nog niet op het strand of het begon te regenen); weather words:
  wisselvallig, een bui, opklaringen, het klaart op, het giet.

### 7 · revision
- Sections: 1 modal particles in combination (table: particle, effect,
  Italian, example from lesson 3); 2 tenses in a story: perfect frame and
  imperfect narration; toen adverb and conjunction (table); 3 nog niet /
  nauwelijks ... of; zodat and om ... te; 4 wiens, wier and the written
  participle phrase; 5 the zitten family (list). Practice.

## Week 2

### 8 · B2 · character · Zuinig
- A man who is thrifty to perfection: one tea bag for three cups, the
  heating at fifteen degrees and a jumper (trui) instead, the shopping by
  the leaflet of special offers. Last line: he pays cash for a brand-new
  car, because he saved for it.
- Focus: past habits: vroeger + imperfect, placht te and was gewoon te
  (formal and old-fashioned, met in writing); degree adverbs: nogal,
  tamelijk, behoorlijk, vrij, ontzettend, hartstikke (familiar).

### 9 · B2 · word · Houden
- A reflection on houden: houden van (Foundation 9), rekening houden met,
  voor de gek houden, het hoofd koel houden, zich houden aan, ophouden,
  volhouden, bijhouden, het uithouden, iets voor zich houden, iemand aan het
  lijntje houden, zich groot houden, zich doof houden. Told through a couple
  and their New Year's resolutions. Last line: "Hou je taai!"
- Focus: houden as hold / keep / like / stop; zich houden + adjective
  (pretend); the expression list.

### 10 · B2 · satire · Code oranje
- The weather warning of the KNMI (the institute in De Bilt): code geel,
  code oranje, code rood; a breeze becomes a national event, the radio
  speaks of nothing else, offices close early, a man cycles to work as
  always. Last line: the next day the country is disappointed that the storm
  was only a storm.
- Focus: probability and hedging: waarschijnlijk, vermoedelijk, naar
  verwachting, de kans op, het zou kunnen, wel eens (het kan wel eens gaan
  stormen), mogelijk.

### 11 · B2 · portrait · Groningen
- The template: the north-east; the city and its university (1614), students
  on every street; the Martinitoren, almost a hundred metres; flat land with
  wierden (old dwelling mounds); the gas found near Slochteren in 1959 and
  the earthquakes its extraction caused (say no more than that); Gronings
  and the greeting "moi"; Groninger koek and mustard soup; last line: the
  city's slogan "Er gaat niets boven Groningen" (nothing goes above
  Groningen, because nothing is north of it).
- Focus: comparison beyond Foundation 37, 40: er gaat niets boven, niets is
  zo ... als, aller- (allerbeste, allerlaatst), zo ... mogelijk (zo snel
  mogelijk).

### 12 · B2 · dialogue · Sfeervol en knus
- An estate agent (makelaar) shows a flat to a young couple: "sfeervol"
  means dark, "knus" means tiny, "op loopafstand van het station" means
  above the railway, "veel potentie" means everything must be redone. They
  want it anyway: the housing shortage, the bidding above the asking price.
  Last line: twelve other couples already bid.
- Focus: euphemism; correlative pairs: zowel ... als, niet alleen ... maar
  ook, noch ... noch, hetzij ... hetzij (formal), of ... of.

### 13 · B2 · reflection · Een momentje
- The Dutch love of the diminutive: een uurtje, een weekje, een biertje, een
  rondje, een praatje, een ommetje, een dutje, een kleinigheidje, het
  zonnetje, eventjes, zachtjes, netjes; words that change meaning (een
  kaartje = a ticket, een toetje = a dessert, een potje voetbal = a game);
  the diminutive that belittles (een baantje, een mannetje). Last line: "Een
  momentje" on the phone, and forty minutes of music.
- Focus: what the diminutive does beyond "small" (friendly, vague, a
  portion, scorn), after the forms of Foundation 47; diminutive adverbs in
  -jes (zachtjes, eventjes, netjes, stilletjes); always het, plural -s.

### 14 · revision
- Sections: 1 the diminutive: forms (Foundation 47, short table) and
  meanings (table with the examples of lesson 13); 2 probability and hedging
  (table: from sure to unlikely); 3 correlative pairs; 4 past habits and
  degree adverbs; 5 the houden family. Practice.

## Week 3

### 15 · B2 · word · Lopen
- A reflection on lopen, which is "camminare", never "correre" (that is
  hardlopen, rennen): uit de hand lopen, tegen de lamp lopen, storm lopen,
  in het oog lopen, achter de feiten aanlopen, het loopt goed af, het loopt
  los, te hard van stapel lopen, op rolletjes lopen, de spuigaten uit lopen,
  weglopen met iemand, het contract loopt tot juni. Told through a small
  birthday party that gets out of hand. Last line: everything "liep op
  rolletjes", says the host, as the police leave.
- Focus: lopen and its false friend "correre"; lopen of things (a contract,
  a machine, a plan); the expression list.

### 16 · B2 · artist · Vermeer
- Johannes Vermeer of Delft (1632–1675): fewer than forty paintings known;
  quiet rooms, often lit from a window on the left, a woman reading a
  letter; Het melkmeisje (Rijksmuseum), Gezicht op Delft and Meisje met de
  parel (Mauritshuis, The Hague); he died in debt, was nearly forgotten, and
  was rediscovered in the 19th century. Last line: the pearl is said by some
  not to be a pearl at all (zou ... zijn), and still everyone calls her by
  it.
- Focus: reportative zou (hij zou een camera obscura hebben gebruikt = he is
  said to have used; the Italian "avrebbe usato" of the newspapers); naar
  verluidt, men neemt aan dat, het schijnt (Foundation 82).

### 17 · B2 · anecdote · De deurbel
- A man fits a video doorbell to catch the parcel thief of his street. He
  spends the day watching the app: he sees the neighbour walk past, hears
  the dog bark, sees a cat sit on the mat. Last line: on the recording he
  sees the courier ring, wait and leave with his parcel, while he was in the
  garden watching another recording.
- Focus: verbs of perception + infinitive (ik zie hem komen, ik hoor de hond
  blaffen) and their perfect with a double infinitive (ik heb hem zien
  komen, never "gezien komen"; back to Foundation 68).

### 18 · B2 · portrait · Antwerpen
- The template: Flanders' biggest city on the Scheldt, one of Europe's
  largest ports; the diamond quarter by the Central Station; Rubens's house
  and the cathedral of Our Lady; the legend of Brabo, who cut off the
  giant's hand and threw it in the river (told as a legend, the folk
  explanation of the name); the people called sinjoren, the city "'t Stad"
  to its own; frieten, a bolleke of beer; last line: the Antwerpse handjes,
  biscuits and chocolates shaped as a hand.
- Focus: Flemish and Netherlands usage, first look (the Belgian word next to
  the Dutch one: frieten / patat, beenhouwer / slager, gsm / mobieltje,
  content / tevreden, amai, allez), each named as Belgian; back to
  Foundation 93.

### 19 · B2 · news · Het journaal (1)
- A news bulletin, invented and plausible: a new cycle bridge, a ferry
  strike, a report on house prices, a rare bird seen in a polder. No real
  events or people.
- Focus: the usual order of the middle of the sentence: time before manner
  before place (hij fietst morgen met zijn zoon naar Utrecht), pronouns
  early; reporting verbs: melden, laten weten, bevestigen, ontkennen,
  verklaren.

### 20 · B2 · news · Het journaal (2)
- Sport (a football match decided at the last minute, op de valreep), a
  short culture item, and the weather (wind, westerly, showers). Last line:
  the light story at the end, a cow that wins a prize for punctuality at
  milking time.
- Focus: headline grammar (no articles, no verb: "Kabinet gevallen", "Brand
  in loods"); sport idioms in daily life: op de valreep, buitenspel staan,
  een eigen doelpunt, de bal misslaan (be wrong).

### 21 · revision
- Sections: 1 reportative zou and the language of hearsay (table: zou +
  infinitive, zou + hebben + participle, naar verluidt); 2 verbs of
  perception + infinitive and the double infinitive in the perfect; 3 the
  order of the middle field (table: subject, verb, time, manner, place,
  end); 4 headline and news style; 5 the lopen family. Practice.

## Week 4

### 22 · B2 · dialogue · Mag het ietsje meer zijn?
- A job candidate negotiates with a polite HR manager: salary, holidays, a
  lease bike, Fridays at home; to each offer he answers with the phrase of
  the butcher's counter, "Mag het ietsje meer zijn?", and gets it. Last
  line: when everything is agreed, he asks what the job actually is.
- Focus: conditionals beyond Foundation 73–74: mixed (als ik dat had
  geweten, zat ik nu niet hier), the condition without als (had ik dat
  geweten, dan ...), mocht u (should you: mocht u vragen hebben), stel dat,
  tenzij, mits.

### 23 · B2 · artist · Mondriaan
- Piet Mondriaan (1872–1944), born in Amersfoort: early landscapes and trees
  (De rode boom, De grijze boom), Paris, where he spelt his name Mondrian;
  De Stijl (1917, with Theo van Doesburg); straight black lines, red, yellow
  and blue, white; New York from 1940, Broadway Boogie Woogie; Victory
  Boogie Woogie, unfinished, in The Hague. Last line: half the world knows
  his paintings from dresses, mugs and shower curtains.
- Focus: describing art: colours and -achtig (roodachtig), vlak, lijn,
  rechthoek; compounds and their article (de kleur, het vlak → het
  kleurvlak: the last part decides; Foundation 91), and the "Engelse ziekte"
  of writing compounds apart (kunst museum for kunstmuseum).

### 24 · B2 · portrait · Leiden
- The template: the old university town on the Old Rhine and its canals; the
  siege by the Spanish and the relief of 3 October 1574, after the land was
  flooded; the university founded in 1575 (the story of the reward chosen by
  the city told as a legend); Rembrandt born here (1606); the Pilgrim
  Fathers who lived here before the Mayflower; the Hortus botanicus, where
  Clusius grew tulips at the end of the 16th century; keys in the city's
  arms; last line: every 3 October Leiden still eats hutspot, herring and
  white bread.
- Focus: inseparable prefixes and what they do: be- (belegeren), ont-
  (ontzetten, ontsnappen), ver- (verdrijven, verliezen), with no ge- in the
  participle (ontzet, verdreven).

### 25 · B2 · word · Zetten
- A reflection on zetten: koffie zetten, de wekker zetten, op touw zetten,
  in elkaar zetten, een punt achter iets zetten, op zijn kop zetten, op het
  spel zetten, voet aan wal zetten, iemand voor het blok zetten, zich over
  iets heen zetten, iemand op zijn plaats zetten, de puntjes op de i zetten,
  doorzetten, afzetten (drop off, switch off, rip off), zet 'm op. Told
  through a man who organises a surprise party. Last line: he has set
  everything, except the date in his own diary.
- Focus: zetten as "put upright, set" against leggen and stellen; the three
  senses of afzetten; the expression list.

### 26 · B2 · satire · Handleiding voor de Nederlandse fietser
- A mock manual of the rules nobody wrote: ride two abreast and talk, hold
  an umbrella, carry the children in the bakfiets, never ring the bell
  except in anger, lock the bike to anything that does not move; the law
  that since 2019 forbids holding a phone while cycling. Last line: rule
  one: the cyclist is always right, even when the cyclist is wrong.
- Focus: generic statements: de Nederlander, Nederlanders, men, je
  (Foundation 81); het + infinitive as a noun (het fietsen, het bellen):
  first look at nominalisation.

### 27 · B2 · artist · Escher
- M. C. Escher (1898–1972), born in Leeuwarden: twelve years in Italy
  (1923–1935), Italian villages and the coast in his early prints; the tiles
  of the Alhambra; impossible worlds: Klimmen en dalen (monks on an endless
  staircase), Waterval (water that flows down and comes back up), Tekenen
  (two hands drawing each other); the Escher museum in a palace in The
  Hague. Last line: a visitor looks for the start of the staircase and is
  still walking.
- Focus: modals of deduction: dat moet een trucje zijn, dat kan niet
  kloppen, het water moet ergens vandaan komen, hij moet het gezien hebben,
  dat zal wel (Foundation 82, 87).

### 28 · revision
- Sections: 1 conditionals: mixed, without als, mocht, stel dat, tenzij,
  mits (table with Italian); 2 modals of deduction (table: moet, kan niet,
  zal wel, with hebben + participle for the past); 3 inseparable prefixes
  be-, ont-, ver- and the participle without ge-; 4 compounds: the article
  from the last part, written as one word; 5 the zetten family. Practice.

## Week 5

### 29 · B2 · dialogue · De schoonouders
- An Italian meets his Dutch girlfriend's parents: he arrives at seven
  (dinner was at six, Foundation 75), calls the mother by the wrong name,
  takes a second biscuit; the mother answers each blunder kindly: "Dat komt
  in de beste families voor." Last line: the father: "Dat klopt. Maar in de
  beste families wordt het voorkómen."
- Focus: separable and inseparable verbs with the same particle, told apart
  by stress and participle: vóórkomen (happen: het komt voor, is
  voorgekomen) / voorkómen (prevent: hij voorkomt het, heeft voorkomen),
  óndergaan (de zon gaat onder) / ondergáán (een operatie ondergaan),
  óverkomen / overkómen (wat is je overkomen?).

### 30 · B2 · portrait · Den Haag
- The template: the seat of government, parliament and the king's working
  palace, while Amsterdam is the capital; the Binnenhof; Prinsjesdag, the
  third Tuesday of September, when the king reads the speech from the
  throne; the Peace Palace (1913) and the International Court of Justice;
  the Mauritshuis (lesson 16); the dunes and the beach at Scheveningen; last
  line: the Haagse bluf, a dessert of whipped egg white and berry juice, and
  the name for boastful talk.
- Focus: the language of politics: de Tweede Kamer, het kabinet, de
  minister-president, de troonrede, aftreden, een motie; hoofdstad against
  regeringszetel.

### 31 · B2 · character · De ceremoniemeester
- A man who speaks only in solemn formulas at every family party: "Lang leve
  het bruidspaar!", "Het zij zo", "Godzijdank", "Men neme een glas", "zij
  het met mate", "Hoe het ook zij", "Het ga je goed". Last line: at his own
  sixtieth birthday the family sings "Lang zal hij leven" and he, for once,
  has nothing to say.
- Focus: the remains of the subjunctive (aanvoegende wijs): leve, het zij
  zo, zij het, God zij dank / godzijdank, men neme, moge, hoe het ook zij,
  het ga je goed; fixed phrases only, against the Italian congiuntivo, which
  is alive.

### 32 · B2 · figure · Annie M.G. Schmidt
- Annie M.G. Schmidt (1911–1995), born in Kapelle in Zeeland: children's
  verse and books (Jip en Janneke, drawn by Fiep Westendorp; Pluk van de
  Petteflet; Abeltje; Minoes), the television series Ja zuster, nee zuster,
  songs and musicals; children who do what they like and grown-ups who are a
  little silly. Last line: "Jip-en-Janneketaal" is now the Dutch word for
  plain language, the highest praise a letter from the tax office could get.
- Focus: register: Jip-en-Janneketaal (short sentences, everyday words)
  against officialese (lesson 48 will bring it); light verse and its tone.

### 33 · B2 · word · Halen
- A reflection on halen: de trein halen, een voldoende halen, het niet
  halen, ophalen, inhalen, overhalen, uithalen (wat heb je nu weer
  uitgehaald?), iets in zijn hoofd halen, door elkaar halen, zijn hart
  ophalen, het bloed onder de nagels vandaan halen, de schouders ophalen,
  adem halen. Told through a student on exam day. Last line: he misses the
  train but gets the pass mark: "Je kunt niet alles halen."
- Focus: halen as fetch / catch / reach / get; the separable halen verbs;
  the expression list.

### 34 · B2 · poem · Tegenwind
- An original short poem in rhymed stanzas (four or five quatrains, a
  regular beat) about cycling against the wind, out and back, the Dutch
  belief that the wind always turns against you. Then one prose line to
  close.
- Focus: stress and rhythm; elision in verse and speech ('t, 'k, m'n, z'n,
  d'r); poetic word order.

### 35 · revision
- Sections: 1 separable and inseparable verbs with the same particle (table:
  verb, stress, present, participle, meaning); 2 the remains of the
  subjunctive (list with their everyday equivalents); 3 register from
  Jip-en-Janneketaal to formal (table); 4 elision and the weak forms ('t,
  'k, m'n, z'n, d'r, ie); 5 the halen family. Practice.

## Week 6

### 36 · B2 · essay · Verzuiling
- Pillarisation: until the 1960s Catholics, Protestants, socialists and
  liberals each had their own schools, newspapers, broadcasters, unions and
  sports clubs; the 1917 settlement that funds public and religious schools
  equally; the traces today (broadcasting associations with their own names
  on the public channels, schools "met de Bijbel"); ontzuiling from the
  1960s. Last line: today the Dutch are divided only by the question of the
  birthday circle.
- Focus: nominalisation and abstract nouns: -ing (verzuiling), -heid,
  -schap, -isme; het + infinitive (back to lesson 26); the noun style
  against the verb style.

### 37 · B2 · portrait · Suriname
- The template: on the north coast of South America, independent since 25
  November 1975; Paramaribo and its wooden inner city (UNESCO World
  Heritage); Dutch the official language, Sranan Tongo the language of the
  street, with Sarnami, Javanese and others; the 1667 settlement in which
  the Dutch kept Suriname and the English New Amsterdam; slavery abolished
  in 1863, remembered on 1 July (Keti Koti); rainforest and rivers; roti,
  pom; last line: a Surinamese word in every Dutch city: mattie (from Sranan
  mati, friend).
- Focus: varieties of Dutch (Netherlands, Flanders, Suriname); sinds,
  sindsdien, inmiddels; the past perfect in history.

### 38 · B2 · dialogue · De laatste rijles
- A driving instructor's last day before retirement, with his worst pupil,
  an hour before her fifth exam: "Je had niet hoeven remmen." "Ik had het je
  eerder moeten zeggen." Last line: she passes; he asks her to drive him to
  his farewell party, and she takes the wrong exit.
- Focus: had niet hoeven + infinitive (you needn't have: je had niet hoeven
  komen) against hoefde niet te (didn't need to); long verb clusters (ik had
  het hem moeten laten zien; dat ik het had kunnen weten) and the two orders
  at the end (dat hij gekomen is / dat hij is gekomen).

### 39 · B2 · biography · Anne Frank
- Anne Frank (1929–1945): born in Frankfurt, in Amsterdam from 1934; the
  diary she got for her thirteenth birthday (June 1942); the hiding place in
  the back annex at Prinsengracht 263 from July 1942; the arrest on 4 August
  1944; her death in Bergen-Belsen in early 1945; her father Otto, the only
  one of the family to survive, and the diary published in 1947 as Het
  Achterhuis. Plain, serious ending: the house is now a museum.
- Focus: telling history: the past perfect beside the imperfect, nadat,
  voordat, sindsdien, pas (only then, not before: pas in 1947), inmiddels.

### 40 · B2 · word · Hand
- A reflection on hand: wat is er aan de hand? (Foundation 17), iets bij de
  hand hebben, twee linkerhanden hebben, de handen vol hebben aan, de handen
  uit de mouwen steken, iemand de hand boven het hoofd houden, iets in de
  hand werken, van de hand doen, handen en voeten geven aan, de hand in
  eigen boezem steken, iemand op handen dragen, voor de hand liggen, uit de
  hand lopen (lesson 15). Told through a man who builds a cupboard from a
  flat pack. Last line: "Wat is er aan de hand?" asks his wife; the cupboard
  has two left doors.
- Focus: hand in idioms and compounds (handig, handdoek, handschoen,
  handtekening); the expression list.

### 41 · B2 · jokes · Drie moppen
- Three short original jokes: a Dutch joke about a Belgian and a Belgian
  joke about a stingy Dutchman (each country tells the same kind of joke
  about the other), with the same punchline; then a joke built on two words
  that sound the same.
- Focus: puns and homophones: ei and ij, au and ou sound the same (reizen /
  rijzen); words with two senses (bank: bench, sofa, bank; slot: lock,
  castle, end in ten slotte); how a mop is told.

### 42 · revision
- Sections: 1 had niet hoeven against hoefde niet te; long verb clusters
  (table: two, three and four verbs, main and subordinate clause); 2

## Week 7

### 43 · B2 · portrait · Gent
- The template: the Flemish city where the Leie meets the Scheldt;
  Gravensteen, the castle of the counts of Flanders; the Ghent Altarpiece
  (Het Lam Gods) by the Van Eyck brothers in St Bavo's cathedral (1432), one
  of its panels stolen in 1934 and never found; Charles V, born here in
  1500, who made the city's leaders walk with a noose round their necks
  (1540): the Gentenaars still call themselves stroppendragers; the
  university; Gentse waterzooi; last line: the cuberdons, the purple cones
  called "neuzekes".
- Focus: Flemish usage, second look: the diminutive in -ke (manneke,
  neuzeke), gij / ge in speech, tussentaal (the everyday spoken Dutch of
  Flanders between dialect and standard); Belgian standard words next to
  Netherlands ones; back to lesson 18.

### 44 · B2 · story · De erfenis van tante Truus
- Aunt Truus leaves her fortune to whichever nephew finds it; the clues send
  them through the house, the attic and finally the garden, which they dig
  over for a whole weekend. Last line: the notary reads the last clue: the
  money was in the bank all along; the garden needed digging.
- Focus: the future in the past: zou + infinitive (ze wisten niet dat ze het
  nooit zouden vinden), stond op het punt te (Foundation 87), ging +
  infinitive; prepositional phrases: op zoek naar, in de war, op goed geluk,
  op het nippertje, in het geheim, uit het hoofd, ten slotte.

### 45 · B2 · word · Staan
- A reflection on staan: ergens achter staan, voor schut staan, te kijk
  staan, op zijn strepen staan, het staat je goed, er alleen voor staan,
  stilstaan bij, het staat vast, op losse schroeven staan, hoe staat het
  ervoor?, iemand te woord staan, op eigen benen staan, op gespannen voet
  staan met, op het punt staan (Foundation 87). Told through a man trying on
  suits for a wedding. Last line: the suit "staat hem goed", says the shop
  assistant; the price, says his wife, a lot less.
- Focus: staan for things upright and for what is written (het staat in de
  krant); the expression list.

### 46 · C1 · dialogue · De leesclub
- A book club meets to discuss Max Havelaar (1860) by Multatuli, the pen
  name of Eduard Douwes Dekker (Latin for "I have suffered much"): the book
  against the abuses of colonial rule in Java, a classic of the school
  reading list; one member confuses it with the fair-trade coffee label
  named after it (1988); nobody admits not having read it. Last line: one
  member did read it: the first words, "Ik ben makelaar in koffie", and
  stopped there.
- Focus: discourse markers: weliswaar ... maar, overigens, trouwens, nou ja,
  enfin, hoe dan ook, al met al, eerlijk gezegd.

### 47 · C1 · letter · Bezwaarschrift (1)
- A formal objection (bezwaarschrift) to the municipality against a fine for
  putting the rubbish bin out one day early: header, "Betreft", the decision
  objected to, the grounds, the request; written within the six weeks the
  law allows; precise and furious under the politeness. The writer is a
  retired schoolteacher.
- Focus: formal written register beyond Foundation 89: ondergetekende,
  betreffende / inzake, hierbij, naar aanleiding van, derhalve, verzoeken
  om, dienen te, in afwachting van uw reactie.

### 48 · C1 · letter · Uw bezwaar (2)
- The municipality's reply in pure officialese: the objection is
  "ontvankelijk" but "ongegrond"; the fine stands; the sentences pile noun
  on noun. Last line: in a postscript, the new collection calendar: from
  next month the rubbish is collected one day earlier.
- Focus: officialese (ambtelijke taal): the noun style (het in behandeling
  nemen van), middels, alsmede, ten behoeve van, onverminderd, inzake; and
  how to say each in Jip-en-Janneketaal (lesson 32).

### 49 · revision

## Week 8

### 50 · C1 · portrait · De Waddeneilanden
- The template: the islands in the north between the Wadden Sea and the
  North Sea; the five inhabited Dutch ones, Texel, Vlieland, Terschelling,
  Ameland, Schiermonnikoog; the Wadden Sea, a UNESCO World Heritage site
  (2009); the tide, seals, birds; wadlopen, walking across the sea floor at
  low tide, only with a guide; Vlieland, where visitors may not bring a car;
  Texel sheep and their cheese; last line: on the islands the timetable is
  written by the tide, not by the ferry company.
- Focus: nature and landscape words (het wad, eb en vloed, het getij, de
  duinen, de kwelder); rules in the passive and impersonal (het is verboden
  te ..., er mag hier niet gefietst worden).

### 51 · C1 · figure · Spinoza
- Baruch (Benedictus) de Spinoza (1632–1677), born in Amsterdam in the
  Portuguese-Jewish community, expelled from it in 1656; he ground lenses
  for a living and lived in Rijnsburg, Voorburg and The Hague; the Tractatus
  theologico-politicus published anonymously (1670), the Ethica after his
  death (1677); God or Nature (Deus sive Natura), freedom of thought. Gentle
  last line: the man who ground lenses so that others could see better.
- Focus: relative clauses in formal style: hetgeen (= wat), welke (written
  relative), waarbij, waardoor, op grond waarvan; wiens / wier again (lesson
  4).

### 52 · C1 · satire · Even sparren
- An office meeting in Dutch-English jargon: even sparren, een call
  inplannen, terugkoppelen, afstemmen, draagvlak creëren, laaghangend fruit,
  op één lijn zitten. Nobody knows what was decided. Last line: the only
  decision is a follow-up meeting to "terugkoppelen".
- Focus: English verbs in Dutch: their conjugation by 't kofschip on the
  English sound and their spelling (updaten, geüpdatet; deleten, gedeletet);
  jargon and its plain Dutch; verbs with fixed prepositions (afstemmen op,
  terugkomen op).

### 53 · C1 · word · Trekken
- A reflection on trekken: aan het kortste eind trekken, zich iets
  aantrekken (trek het je niet aan), de stoute schoenen aantrekken,
  conclusies trekken, trek hebben in, aan de bel trekken, in twijfel
  trekken, tijd uittrekken voor, een gezicht trekken, zijn eigen plan
  trekken, aan de touwtjes trekken, de thee laten trekken, lootjes trekken.
  Told through a man who goes on a walking tour (trekken = hike). Last line:
  after ten days on foot, what he has most "trek" in is a chair.
- Focus: trekken as pull / draw / move / hike; the noun trek (appetite,
  migration); the expression list.

### 54 · C1 · dialogue · De kroegquiz
- A pub quiz team argues over every answer: who painted what, which river,
  what year; each sure of something different. Last line: they win the
  evening with a wrong answer, because the quizmaster had it wrong too.
- Focus: indirect questions and embedded clauses: weet jij toevallig wie
  ..., ik vraag me af of ..., het hangt ervan af wat ..., of against als
  (whether / if); the verb at the end in each.

### 55 · C1 · portrait · Afrikaans
- The template adapted to a language: Dutch settlers at the Cape from 1652
  (the VOC station of Jan van Riebeeck); a daughter language of Dutch,
  official in South Africa since 1925, also spoken in Namibia; verbs without
  person endings (ek is, jy is, hy is), the double negative (Ek praat nie
  Afrikaans nie); words a Dutch reader knows or half knows (baie dankie,
  lekker); words that went into English (trek, veld); braai, biltong,
  rooibos; last line: a Dutch reader understands most of a page, and laughs
  at the rest.
- Focus: Dutch and Afrikaans side by side (table in the notes); the Dutch
  negation niet / geen against nie ... nie; how a language changes.

## Week 9

### 57 · C1 · proverbs · Oost west, thuis best
- A grandmother answers every question of her student granddaughter with a
  proverb: oost west, thuis best; beter een goede buur dan een verre vriend;
  wie niet waagt, wie niet wint; wat de boer niet kent, dat eet hij niet;
  haastige spoed is zelden goed; al draagt een aap een gouden ring, het is
  en blijft een lelijk ding; wie het kleine niet eert, is het grote niet
  weerd. Pieter Bruegel's painting of Netherlandish proverbs (1559) in a
  sentence. Last line: the granddaughter answers with one of her own.
- Focus: proverbs: the literal and the real sense; old forms kept for the
  rhyme (weerd for waard); using a proverb in a modern sentence.

### 58 · C1 · character · De betweter
- A know-it-all who corrects everyone's spelling: pannenkoek with -n- since
  the 1995 reform, hij wordt with dt, het is gebeurd and not gebeurt. Last
  line: the sign over his own shop is misspelt.
- Focus: spelling: the tussen-n (pannenkoek, boekenkast: the first part has
  a plural in -en only; zonneschijn without -n, as there is one sun), the -d
  / -t of verbs (ik word, hij wordt, word je?, wordt je broer?; het gebeurt
  / het is gebeurd; hij vindt).

### 59 · C1 · figure · Erasmus
- Desiderius Erasmus of Rotterdam (born in the 1460s, died in Basel in
  1536): a scholar who wrote in Latin and travelled all of Europe; Lof der
  zotheid (Moriae encomium, printed 1511), written for Thomas More, with a
  pun on his name; the Greek New Testament (1516); his name now on a
  university, a bridge and a European exchange programme. Last line: Folly,
  who speaks in his book, has the last word: even Erasmus needed a little
  folly.
- Focus: conjunctive adverbs of argument and their place: echter (usually
  after the verb), immers, bovendien, daarentegen, namelijk (never first: ik
  kom niet, ik ben namelijk ziek), derhalve, dus; Latin words in Dutch.

### 60 · C1 · word · Water
- A reflection on water: water naar de zee dragen, het water staat hem tot
  de lippen, het hoofd boven water houden, boven water komen, in het water
  vallen, stille wateren hebben diepe gronden, als twee druppels water,
  watertanden, iemand het water niet kunnen aanreiken, een slag in het
  water, tussen wal en schip vallen, er zal nog heel wat water door de Rijn
  stromen; water bij de wijn doen only as a back reference (Foundation 95).
  Told through a family plan for a boat holiday. Last line: the holiday
  "valt in het water", literally: it rains for two weeks.
- Focus: water idioms in a country of water; the expression list.

### 61 · C1 · dialogue · De verhuizing
- Moving house with two removal men and their furniture lift through the
  window (the stairs are too steep): every question is answered with "Ik
  denk van wel", "Ik hoop het", "Dat dacht ik al". The new flat is in Den
  Bosch; one of the men says he has never been there, only to
  's-Hertogenbosch, twice. Last line: told that it is the same city, he
  answers: "Ik dacht van niet."
- Focus: ellipsis and substitution: ik denk van wel / van niet, dat hoop ik,
  ik ook (niet), dat dacht ik al, dat doe ik; the short answers that replace
  a whole sentence.

### 62 · C1 · idioms · De kat uit de boom
- A teacher describes her colleagues at a staff meeting in animal idioms: de
  kat uit de boom kijken, met de kippen op stok gaan, een kat in de zak
  kopen, als een kip zonder kop, het paard achter de wagen spannen, een
  gegeven paard niet in de bek kijken, slapende honden wakker maken, twee
  vliegen in één klap slaan, een vreemde eend in de bijt, van een mug een

- Focus: animal idioms and their Italian counterparts (often another
  animal); register (friendly, sharp).

### 64 · C1 · portrait · Rotterdam

### 65 · C1 · story · Het spookt hier
- "Er was eens" an old farmhouse where something knocks at night: the family
  hears footsteps, a door that opens, a pipe that sighs; a cousin swears he
  saw someone. The house is put up for sale. Last line: the estate agent
  writes "spook inbegrepen" in the advert, and the price goes up.
- Focus: er in depth: er doing two jobs at once, er liggen er drie (two

  werd geklopt), the fairy-tale "er was eens", het spookt hier (het, not

### 66 · C1 · satire · De Nederlandse zomer
- The Dutch summer: the barbecue lit in the rain, the sunburn in one
  afternoon, the heatwave as defined by the KNMI (at least five days of 25
  degrees or more in De Bilt, three of them 30 or more), the whole country
  at the beach on the same day, then rain. Last line: after three hot days
  everyone longs for "lekker fris" weather again.
- Focus: irony and understatement (niet verkeerd, lekker as irony: lekker
  dan!); wishes: was het maar, had ik maar, als het nou eens.

### 67 · C1 · artist · Van Gogh
- Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890), born in Zundert in Brabant: the art dealer's
  shop, the Borinage, painting from about 1880; De aardappeleters (Nuenen,
  1885); Paris with his brother Theo, Arles, Saint-Rémy, Auvers-sur-Oise,
  where he died; hundreds of letters, most of them to Theo, who paid for
  everything; hardly anything sold in his life; the Van Gogh Museum in
  Amsterdam and the Kröller-Müller in the Hoge Veluwe. Plain, warm ending.
- Focus: the language of letters and the spelling of his time (mensch, zoo,
  until the reforms of the 1940s); pas against alleen and maar (pas na zijn
  dood: only after, not before); nog steeds, ooit.

### 68 · C1 · dialogue · Het mondeling

  Last line: the examiner: "Prima. Maar ik vroeg alleen hoe laat het is."
- Focus: conditions and assumptions in argument; the connectors of a
  reasoned answer: ten eerste, daarnaast, kortom, al met al.
