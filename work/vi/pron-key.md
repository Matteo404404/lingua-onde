# Vietnamese figured-pronunciation key (northern, Hanoi)

Writers follow this key for every `pron.it`, `pron.en` and `pron.es` line of lessons 1–50 (see `work/PRON.md`).

## Rules for all readers

- One figured word per Vietnamese syllable, separated by spaces, also inside compounds (cảm ơn, Việt Nam).
- All lowercase. Keep the sentence's punctuation exactly (`! ? , . …`); add none (no ¡ ¿).
- Hanoi speech: d, gi, r all sound z; s and x both sound s; ch and tr both sound ch; â is a short ơ; ă is a short a.
- Tones: copy the Vietnamese tone mark onto the figured letter that replaces the marked letter. If that letter becomes a group
  (English ee, ah, aw, oh, oo, ay, eh, ow), the mark goes on the group's first letter: chị → en chẹe.
  No mark = ngang. No tone sandhi: each syllable keeps its own tone in any context.
- Old-style placement on a glide (hòa, khỏe, thủy, hủy) moves to the next vowel first (hoà, khoẻ, thuỷ, huỷ).
- Names and words in another language's spelling (Luca, Giulia, Bologna, pizza) stay as written, lowercase: luca.
  Vietnamese names (Lan, Hùng, Minh) are figured like any word.
- Digits are figured as spoken: 21 = hai mươi mốt, 25 = hai mươi lăm, 15 = mười lăm, 24 = hai mươi tư.
- Unreleased finals: p, t, c (and ch) are held, not exploded. After o, ô, u the finals ng and c close the lips
  (không ≈ khôngm, học ≈ họckp); write them plainly, the notes explain it.

### The six tones (Hanoi)

| mark | name | sound | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | ngang | level, medium-high, steady | ma (ghost) |
| à | huyền | low, slowly falling, relaxed and breathy | mà (but) |
| á | sắc | high and rising, tense | má (mum) |
| ả | hỏi | low, dips down and comes slightly back up | mả (grave) |
| ã | ngã | rising, broken by a catch in the throat halfway | mã (horse) |
| ạ | nặng | low, very short, choked off in the throat | mạ (rice seedling) |

**Stress.** No stress accents: every syllable is its own word with equal weight (a phrase's last syllable is a little longer).
Every accent on a figured vowel is a tone, never stress, so closed e and o are written ê and ô instead of é and ó.

**Special symbols (★).**
- ư: say u but with the lips spread as for i, as in a smile.
- ơ: a relaxed e between e and o, lips not rounded, like the final vowel of Neapolitan "mamm'".
- h: a breathed h as in English "house", never silent; th is t followed by that breath.
- kh: k made without touching the palate, a scratch like the Florentine c of "la casa" or German "Bach".
- ng: the n of "fango" or "panca" at the start of a word, with no g after it; also at the end (không).

**Connected speech.** No joining, doubling or elision between words: cảm ơn is cảm + ơn, never cả-mơn.
Add no vowel after final consonants (lan, not lane); hold p, t, c silently; close the lips at the end of ong, ông, ung, oc, ôc, uc.

**Tones.** The Vietnamese tone mark stays on the figured vowel (table "The six tones"): à low falling, á high rising,
ả dipping, ã rising with a break, ạ low and choked, no mark level. Change the pitch, not the loudness.

**Stress.** No CAPITALS and no hyphens: every syllable is a separate word with equal weight, and a phrase's last syllable
is a little longer. What carries meaning is the pitch, shown by the tone mark on the vowel.

**Special symbols (★).**
- ư: say "oo" with the lips spread in a smile instead of rounded.
- ơ: "uh" held a little longer, like British "her" with no r.
- kh: k without the tongue touching, a scrape like Scottish "loch" or German "Bach".
- th: t with a strong puff of air, as in "top!"; never as in "thin". Plain t has no puff.
- ng: the ng of "singer", used at the start of a syllable: say "singer" and drop the "si".

**Connected speech.** Do not link a final consonant to the next vowel: cảm ơn is kảhm ơn, never kah-mun; start each vowel
cleanly. Final p, t, k are held, not released; after aw, oh, oo the final ng and k end with closed lips. No r anywhere.

**Tones.** The Vietnamese tone mark sits on the first letter of the figured vowel (table "The six tones"): à low falling,
á high rising like a surprised "yes?", ả dipping, ã rising with a break, ạ low and choked, no mark level.
