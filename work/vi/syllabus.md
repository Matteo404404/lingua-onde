# Vietnamese (vi) course plan

Variety: northern standard (Hanoi). Base languages: it, en, es.

## Cast and setting

Setting: Hanoi. Mr Hùng's narrow tube house (nhà ống) in the Old Quarter near Hoàn Kiếm lake: phở shop on the ground floor in the mornings, the family upstairs, Luca's rented room on the third floor under the roof terrace. Nearby: Đồng Xuân market, the Hàng streets, the Temple of Literature. From B2 on, Lan's café Nhà Gỗ in a century-old wooden tube house in an alley ten minutes away, restored to Luca's design.

Characters (age at lesson 1):
- Luca (28): Italian architect from Turin on a contract with a heritage-restoration firm; Mr Hùng's lodger. He is em to Lan, anh to Minh and Mai, cháu to Mr Hùng. He stays on (072), marries Mai (173), and in C2 writes a newspaper column and then a book; his essays open C2 (201) and close the course (300).
- Lan (Nguyễn Thị Lan, 32): Mr Hùng's only child, a bank employee. She trains secretly as a barista (040), opens Nhà Gỗ (076–085), buys its house (176–178) and marries Đức (267).
- bác Hùng (Nguyễn Văn Hùng, 62): widower, Lan's father, landlord, phở cook since the 1980s, a tea drinker and Chinese-chess player who loves old Hanoi. He falls ill (108), turns seventy (197), stops selling phở (200) and dictates his memoirs to Luca (268–273).
- Minh (20): Lan's younger cousin (em họ), student of English and Italian, Luca's language-exchange partner; parents are farmers in Nam Định. He wins a scholarship (051), leaves for Bologna (068), writes his master's thesis on loanwords, stays in Italy (166) with Chiara, and visits with their son (260).
- Mai (27): Hanoian architect, Luca's colleague (011), girlfriend (097), wife (173), mother of An (223). Her father is a retired engineer; her mother sings the old lullabies.
- ông Tư (70): Mr Hùng's neighbour and chess partner, a retired literature teacher born in Saigon who moved north in the 1980s; Southern speech and memories of 1975. He dies in C1 (162–164); his children live in Saigon.
- Thảo (21): Minh's university friend from Huế; barista at Nhà Gỗ (087), then manager; opens a branch in Huế (181) and hosts Luca there (228).
- Phương (35): journalist and café regular; writes the article in 151, edits Luca's op-ed and column, writes the book's foreword (290).
- Đức (40): carpenter who restores the café's woodwork (080), Lan's partner (195) and husband (267).
- anh Quang: director of Luca's firm. cụ Lộc: elder of the village whose communal house Luca surveys (032–035, 262). cô Thu: fruit seller at Đồng Xuân. chú Thành: builder. bà Ngọc: the café house's owner. anh Y Kông: Ê Đê coffee grower in Đắk Lắk.
- Luca's family in Turin: his mother and his older sister Giulia, an English teacher, who visits in B2 (103–107). An: Luca and Mai's daughter, born in C2.

Story arcs:
- A1 (001–025): Luca's first weeks: the house, phở, family, time, the city, the new job, neighbours, weather, health, Mai's birthday; he resolves to shop alone at the market.
- A2 (026–050): overpaying and haggling, food and a clinic, the first project in a village, the rainy season and Mid-Autumn, Lan's secret barista classes, Minh's scholarship application, a trip to Ninh Bình, Luca's first motorbike.
- B1 (051–075): the helmet fine and Minh's scholarship; visas, Minh's village, phở lessons, the farewell and the empty house; Mai and Luca grow closer; Luca extends his contract; Lan plans her café and finds the wooden house.
- B2 (076–150): the café from family argument to first anniversary; coffee in the Central Highlands; Tết; Giulia's visit; Mr Hùng's fall; Mai's Saigon dilemma, the South and the Mekong Delta; ông Tư's memories; Vietnamese abroad; Luca's summer in Italy; meeting Mai's parents; the café grows and a journalist calls.
- C1 (151–200): sudden fame; a colonial villa and the heritage debate; Hanoi's history and Minh's thesis; ông Tư's death; Minh stays abroad; Luca and Mai's wedding; the café buys its house; Thảo goes to Huế; Luca's op-ed and TV appearance; Lan and Đức; Mr Hùng's seventieth and his last bowl of phở.
- C2 (201–300): ten years after his arrival Luca writes on the Vietnamese language: wordplay, idiom, classical literature, An's birth, the regions, the diaspora, the history of the scripts and the lexicon, translation; Lan's wedding and Mr Hùng's memoirs; the book, its launch at Nhà Gỗ, and a closing essay that returns to lesson 1.

## Grammar path

A1 (001–025)
1. Sounds and spelling: six northern tones and their marks; ph, d/gi/r, tr/ch, s/x in Hanoi.
2. Address: kinship pronouns chosen by age (anh, chị, em, bác, cháu, ông, bà, cô, chú), tôi in formal settings, ấy for the third person, ơi to call, dạ/ạ for respect.
3. Identity: là and không phải là; người + country; tiếng + language; adjectives and ages as predicates with no là.
4. Questions: có ... không?, à?, gì, ai, đâu, nào, mấy, bao nhiêu; word order unchanged.
5. Numbers to 99, age, clock time (mấy giờ rồi), days (thứ + number), dates (ngày, mồng, tháng, năm); time phrases from small to large.
6. Possession by word order; này/kia/đó/đấy; có "there is / have"; nhiều/ít.
7. Degree and frequency: rất, lắm, quá, hơi, khá, không ... lắm; thường, luôn, thỉnh thoảng, không bao giờ; cũng, đều, còn, cả hai.
8. Modal verbs: muốn, phải, thích, biết, định, verb + được (ability); ... được không? for permission and favours.
9. Place and movement: ở + place; trước, sau, cạnh, đối diện, giữa; gần/xa; đi thẳng, rẽ; mất bao lâu.
10. Requests and social formulas: mời, cho cháu ..., đừng, hãy, final đi, nhé, thôi, nhỉ, đâu, thế; tại sao ... vì ...; với; chúc, tặng.
Functions: greet, introduce, ask age and origin, order food, talk about family, tell the time, ask the way, describe a room and the weather, phone, say you are ill, invite and congratulate.

A2 (026–050)
1. Time and aspect: đang, đã, sẽ, sắp, vừa mới, rồi, chưa; đã ... bao giờ chưa?, chưa bao giờ, đã từng; past habit with hồi bé, ngày xưa, thường.
2. Classifiers: cái, chiếc, quả, con, viên, gói, chai; các, những, mỗi, cả.
3. Numbers in hundreds and thousands, lăm and mốt; prices (bao nhiêu tiền?); years.
4. Conditions and causes: nếu ... thì ...; vì ... nên ...; tại vì; đang ... thì ...
5. Permission, help and influence: được + verb, có thể, cho + person + verb, nhờ, giúp, hộ; nên/không nên.
6. Time clauses and purpose: trước khi, sau khi, từ ... đến ..., khoảng; để + verb.
7. Directional verbs ra, vào, lên, xuống, về, sang, alone and after other verbs.
8. Impersonal người ta; indefinites ai/gì/đâu + cũng; hay là, thế thì, không ... nữa, chỉ còn.
9. Sequence words for instructions: trước tiên, sau đó, cuối cùng; nhớ/quên + verb.
10. bị + symptom; health questions (từ bao giờ?, mấy lần một ngày).
Functions: bargain, report past events, describe symptoms, follow instructions, travel and book, express plans and hopes, ask for help, tell a short story about the past.

B1 (051–075)
1. được/bị passives with a positive or negative judgement; được + verb as a plain passive.
2. Comparison: hơn, bằng, nhất, như, giống, khác, không ... bằng; càng ngày càng, càng ... càng ...
3. Opinions: theo anh, tôi nghĩ là, tôi thấy, có vẻ, hình như, dường như.
4. Reported speech: nói rằng/là, bảo, hỏi ... có ... không.
5. Linking clauses: không những ... mà còn; mặc dù ... nhưng; vừa ... vừa; trong khi; thay vì; ngoài ra; dù ... cũng; một mặt ... mặt khác; vì thế, cho nên.
6. Result complements: xong, hết, kỹ, được, mất.
7. Relative clauses with mà; emphatic chính; kể từ khi; hóa ra.
8. Wishes and hypotheses: giá mà, ước, nếu được thì; liệu ... có ... không; thà ... còn hơn; trừ khi, miễn là; kẻo; nhỡ ... thì sao.
9. Large numbers (triệu, tỷ), quantity and sufficiency (đủ ... để ..., ít nhất, tối đa).
10. First Sino-Vietnamese abstract vocabulary; toasts and wishes; register with teachers.
Functions: give and justify opinions, compare, report what others said, narrate a personal history, give recipes, persuade and reassure, make decisions, negotiate a rent.

B2 (076–150)
1. Thành ngữ and tục ngữ inside ordinary sentences; rhetorical questions (ai chẳng ..., đâu phải ..., chẳng lẽ).
2. Particles of attitude: mới, lại ... rồi, ừ thì, chứ, cơ, mà, thì phải, đấy nhé.
3. Family and social registers: con/bố, thưa, indirect requests, face and tact.
4. Written forms: personal letters and emails, diary, blog, postcards, minutes, love letters.
5. Nominalisers sự, việc, cuộc, nỗi, niềm, tính; causatives làm cho, khiến; do ... gây ra.
6. Process and formal passives (được ... bởi/do), nhờ ... mà ...; tưởng ... hóa ra ...; đã ... lại còn ...; sao cho.
7. Narrative connectors: ngày xửa ngày xưa, tương truyền, bèn, liền, từ đó; đáng lẽ, lẽ ra, may mà, suýt.
8. News, radio and science register: theo, dự báo, ước tính, nhằm, cho thấy, tỷ lệ, chiếm.
9. Debate and speech: kính thưa, thứ nhất, tóm lại, đành rằng ... nhưng, cho dù ... thì, xét về.
10. Regional variety: Huế (mô, tê, răng, rứa), the South (hông, nè, nha, vô, ba/má, mắc, ký); Vietnamese abroad.
Functions: argue a case, mediate, write letters and reports, tell legends, present work, interview and be interviewed, complain and apologise formally, describe art, film and food.

C1 (151–200)
1. Press style: dense Sino-Vietnamese compounds with modifier first; press formulas; formal connectives song, nhằm, tuy nhiên, do đó, bởi lẽ, hơn thế nữa.
2. Administrative and legal language: Kính gửi, V/v, căn cứ, trân trọng; bên A, bên B, điều khoản, trong trường hợp, có quyền.
3. Academic register: luận điểm, giả thuyết, phương pháp, dẫn chứng; hedging (chưa đủ cơ sở, có thể cho rằng).
4. Debate rhetoric: giả sử, xét cho cùng, nói cách khác, không phải là ... mà là ..., chính vì ... mà ...
5. Ceremonial language: condolences, eulogy, betrothal and wedding speeches, longevity wishes.
6. Euphemism, implicitness and understatement; polite polemic and irony.
7. Historical narration and quoting classical texts in translation.
8. Economic, financial, scientific and technical vocabulary; calques and neologisms.
9. Essay forms: comparative, reflective, narrative essay; op-ed; feuilleton.
By 200 the learner has met the whole grammar of the language.

C2 (201–300)
1. Style: literary essay voice, irony, restraint, rhythm; editing and self-editing; parody and pastiche.
2. Register range: archaic and ritual formulas, royal speech, officialese, academic lecture, slang and teencode.
3. Idiom and wordplay: four-syllable idioms, từ láy, nói lái, couplets, proverbs, allusions to Truyện Kiều.
4. Literary language: ca dao and lullabies, lục bát, song thất lục bát, Đường luật, parallel prose (biền ngẫu), hịch, văn tế, memoir, modern prose.
5. Regional variety: Huế, Nghệ An, Quảng Nam, Saigon and the Mekong Delta, the northern uplands, the diaspora.
6. Historical variety: Hán văn, chữ Nôm, early Quốc ngữ, 19th–20th century prose, old Hanoi speech, Sino-Vietnamese strata and loanwords, spelling debates.
7. Translation and interpreting between cultures; culture-bound words (duyên, tình, nghĩa, thương).
